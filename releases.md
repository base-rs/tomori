# Releases

## Unreleased

- **Two people changing a wall at once no longer undo each other, and live charts cost far less.** A wall you change with its handles is now checked against the wall as you took hold of it: if someone else, or the agent, changed it meanwhile, your change is refused with the same cue as a refused tick, and the wall shows as it now is, to change again from there, rather than quietly undoing theirs. Ticks are checked the same way, and a tick made just as someone moved the panel is refused and looked at again. Carrying a wall, placing a panel, throwing one away and locking still simply happen, the latest winning. An environment's live chart now reaches each headset as the points it gained, a few hundred bytes a poll, rather than the whole chart again, and never makes anyone's tick or update be refused; the headset redraws the chart from them without laying its panel out again, knowing what changed by each panel's version rather than comparing it whole. For the agent: `tomori_panels` and `tomori_presence` give each panel's and wall's `version`, and `tomori_update_panel` and `tomori_set_wall` take an optional `version`, refusing a change from one since changed with the version now, to read again and redo; `update_panel` still takes `revision`. See `design/projection.md` and `design/mcp.md`.

- For other clients and servers: **a wall's and a panel's version is now the projection revision at which it last changed, and live panels have data ops.** `tomori-protocol`'s `Panel::revision` is now `Panel::version`, still sent as `revision`, and `Panel::with_revision` is `with_version`; `Wall` gains `version`, sent as `revision`, and `with_version` and `same_but_version`; `Panel` gains `data_seq`, `with_data_seq`, `same_structure` and `same_data`, and `Chart` `same_structure` and `same_data`. `PatchOp::Place` gains `version`, and `PatchOp` gains `ChartAppend` and `ChartReplace`, `chart_append` and `chart_replace` on the wire, which `ProjectionPatch::apply` applies only where newer than the data held, with `PatchError::Data` for one that does not fit; `ProjectionPatch::between_live` makes patches with them, while `between` makes them as before, a panel whose version or data changed sent whole. `Hello` gains `live_panels`, `ClientMessage::SetWall` gains `revision`, the wall's version the change was made against, and `ServerMessage::Error` gains `wall`, naming the wall of a change refused for it. `tomori-client`'s `Options` and `Session` gain `live_panels`, `false` by default, which a session that applies patches with `ProjectionPatch::apply` returns `true` to be sent data ops; and a refused change naming a wall is cued as a refused tick is. A version is begun from the time each run, rather than counted from 1 and kept, so versions are large numbers, and a panel's count of its changes kept with a space before is read and no longer written. **Breaking** for crates that build these messages field by field: `Hello` needs `live_panels: false`, `SetWall` `revision: None`, `ServerMessage::Error` `wall: None` and `PatchOp::Place` `version`, and `Panel::revision` and `with_revision` are renamed. See `design/projection.md` and `design/clients.md`.

- For servers, clients and environments built on the library: **Tomori now uses `base-http` 0.4**, with `base-http-server` and `base-http-client` 0.4, `base-scheduler` and `base-scheduler-native` 0.5 and `base-endpoint` 0.3, in place of `base-http` 0.2, `base-scheduler` 0.3 and `base-endpoint` 0.1. This is breaking, since scheduler types such as `base_scheduler::Cancellation` appear in `tomori-environment`'s and `tomori-server`'s interfaces, so a downstream crate moves to the same versions; nothing else in Tomori's interfaces changes, and `/connect`, TLS, the sign-in and status pages, images and the MCP endpoint behave as before. Clients, backends and data sources gain `base-http-client`'s check of an idle pooled connection before reusing it, and send a request of known length with `Content-Length` rather than chunked. The server's connections, and its MCP endpoint's, now disable Nagle's algorithm with `TCP_NODELAY`, as its clients' already did, so small frames such as speech audio and projection patches are no longer held back for an acknowledgement; `Server::run` and `Server::run_with_tools` therefore also require their scheduler to set `TCP_NODELAY`, with `SocketSetOption<TcpNoDelay>`, which the native scheduler does. It is stage 0 of the channels and content proposal (`docs/proposals/channels-and-content.md`); see `design/crates.md`.

- **The headset no longer shakes as you move your head from side to side.** Standing and swaying, the pill, near panels and, less, far walls seemed to bounce between where the head was and where it went, as the headset was making only about 52 of its 72 frames a second and showing the rest again, turned but not moved. Each frame's GPU work now runs while the next frame is prepared, rather than after it, so that a frame has the whole refresh for each. The headset's log now says how many display refreshes it missed each minute.

- **Walls full of panels no longer slow the headset down.** Each panel's face is now laid out once for what it shows, and its text, tables, code, checklists and charts kept ready to draw, rather than worked out again every frame: looking about, moving, tilting or bending a wall, and the Tomori wall travelling cost almost nothing, and only a panel the agent updates, one you scroll, or one whose size changes is laid out again. Pointing at a panel, and the blue frame of the newest, recolour only its frame. On a desktop, 90 panels went from about 6.5 ms of every frame to under 0.1 ms; what you see is the same. For other clients: `tomori-scene`'s `controller_ui::Sheet::canvas` is now an `Option<Arc<Canvas>>`, shared rather than copied, so make one with `Some(Arc::new(canvas))` and read it with `as_deref()`, which is **breaking** for code that builds or reads sheets; `Canvas` gains `reframes` and `frame_mesh`; `tomori-render`'s `ui_panel::Panel` gains `show`, which knows a shared canvas shown already by its pointer, and `scene_graphics::Panels` keeps each face's mesh wherever it is drawn among the others, with `SPARE_CARDS`. `tomori-render`'s `panel_frames` example measures a frame of projected panels. See `design/rendering.md`.

- **Spaces hold ten times as many walls and panels, and each headset is sent what it can draw.** A space can now hold 780 slots on as many as sixty walls you add, and 120 floating panels, where it held 78 slots on six and twelve. The headset is still sent today's 13 walls, 78 slots and 90 panels of it, the first walls and the newest panels, since laying out more each frame would cost it frames, and the desktop window twice that; an older headset in the same space is sent today's, exactly as before. Code panels on a headset are now sent whole, rather than cut to their first 16 lines once a space had many, since a connection between Tomori's own headset or desktop window and server now takes frames of up to 32 MiB rather than 1 MiB. Joining a space, or catching up after a missed change, sends its projection whole, which over a slow link the pill now shows as `Syncing space... 45%` until it has arrived. Speech already queued goes first, and speech after it waits only as long as it takes to send: about 11 ms for 30 MB on one machine. For other clients and servers: `tomori-protocol` gains `MOST_FRAME_BYTES`, `frame_limit`, `Limits::MOST`, `Limits::negotiate` and `frame_limit`, `Frame::try_encode_within` and `encode_control_within`, and `FrameDecoder::with_limit`, `set_limit`, `limit` and `progress`, with `Progress`; a connection takes frames of up to the lesser of what each side states, never above 32 MiB nor below 1 MiB, and a peer that states nothing 1 MiB, as before, so nothing changes with an older peer. `tomori-client`'s `Options::limits` now states 32 MiB frames by default, `Session` gains `limits`, the connection reads at the negotiated limit from the frame after the `Welcome`, and reports `Event::Receiving` while a frame of `PROGRESS_BYTES` or more arrives. `tomori-scene` gains `HEADSET_LIMITS`, `DESKTOP_LIMITS`, `Conversation::set_limits` and `receiving`. The server's `Welcome` states `Limits::MOST`, and it sends each client within what it states. The agent's layouts stay at most six walls each. See `design/projection.md`, `design/server.md` and `design/clients.md`.

- **Headsets and the desktop window are sent only what changed in a space's projection.** Ticking an item, the agent updating a panel, moving a panel or editing a wall now reaches every headset as a patch of a few hundred bytes, rather than every wall and panel again: in a space at every limit, about 400 bytes rather than 598 KB, so speech queued behind it waits far less. What you see is the same: each panel still says how long ago it was projected, a tick you make still shows at once and is undone if the server refuses it, and scrolling stays your own. A headset that misses a patch asks for the projection whole and carries on from there. For other clients: `tomori-client`'s `Options` gains `projection_patches` and `limits`, and `Session` gains `projection_patches`, `false` by default, which a session that applies `ServerMessage::ProjectionPatch` to its projection, asking with `ResyncProjection` where one does not apply, returns `true`; a session that does not is sent the projection whole as before. See `design/clients.md` and `design/projection.md`.

- For other clients and servers: **the projection can now be patched rather than sent whole at every change.** `tomori-protocol` gains `Projection::revision` and `with_revision`, the space's projection revision; `ProjectionPatch`, with `between`, `apply` and `whole`, its `PatchOp`s `wall`, `remove_wall`, `panel`, `place` and `remove_panel`, an op a newer server sends read as `Unknown`, and `PatchError`; `ServerMessage::ProjectionPatch` and `ClientMessage::ResyncProjection`; `Hello`'s `projection_patches` and `limits` and `Welcome`'s `limits`, with `Limits`, `Limits::TODAY`, `meet` and the `PROJECTION_WALLS`, `PROJECTION_SLOTS`, `PROJECTION_FLOATING` and `PROJECTION_PANELS` it names. A client whose `Hello` sets `projection_patches` is sent the projection whole on joining, then each change as a patch from the revision it holds, applying it with `ProjectionPatch::apply` and asking with `ResyncProjection` for it whole where a patch does not apply; every other client, Tomori's own headset and desktop window among them for now, is sent it whole at every change exactly as before. The server's `Welcome` states today's limits, 1 MiB frames, 13 walls, 78 slots, 12 floating and 90 panels, and it keeps what a client states without yet sending more or less. A tick, an agent's update or a wall's edit patches a space at every limit in about 400 bytes rather than 598 KB. **Breaking** for crates that build these messages field by field: `ClientMessage::Hello` and `ServerMessage::Welcome` each gained fields, so a literal naming every field needs `projection_patches: false, limits: None` and `limits: None`; and a `match` on `ServerMessage` needs an arm for `ProjectionPatch`. See `design/projection.md`.

- **`tomori-prometheus`'s tokens can be sent by another client.** `Token::expose` reads the token a `TokenSource` makes, so that another client of an application's, such as one for an events endpoint behind the same gateway, sends the same token in its own `authorization` field, rather than keeping a second secret type and source of its own; a `Token`'s `Debug` still hides it, it still has no `Display`, and the crate still uses it only in its own requests and replaces it in what errors quote. See `design/data-sources.md`.

- **The `--desktop` window's title says how to use the mouse**: `Tomori | mouse: point · left choose · right-drag look · M menu · Esc quit`, so that looking around with the right button is no longer a secret; a knock waiting is named between `Tomori` and the controls. See `design/desktop.md`.

- **The Tomori wall stands beneath the agent again, and the workers on one band through it.** In every environment, the Tomori wall now stands directly beneath the agent, as it did before environments set homes: its row of slots 0.88 m below the swarm's middle, facing the way the agent faces, with a clear gap between them, never behind the swarm as you see it and never lower than 0.52 m above the floor. In the **farmyard**, it now stands beneath the agent by the coop rather than ahead and to the right of where you arrive; the farmyard stays at version 3. Summoned, by the trigger or with `move_agent`, the agent now stands 2.3 m in front of you, a little above your eyes, or 1.4 m above the floor if you sit low, rather than near the floor, and its wall stands beneath it, rather than behind it at a fixed drop below your eyes that put it halfway into the floor for someone seated. The workers stand on one curved band through the agent, either side of it, about its height, at home and summoned, rather than on an arc behind it; nearer than 3.2 m, their columns keep their spacing along the band, so the first eight now span about 82° either side of an agent 2.3 m away. Another project's environment that gives `Environment::with_agent` without `with_stage` now has its Tomori wall beneath its agent; `with_stage` still overrides it. `tomori_protocol::entourage` is the new rule the server and clients share, `Environment::tomori_wall_home` says where an environment's wall stands, and a server stands a summons from an older client a little above the summoner's eyes too.

- **Panels float beside you when you are not looking at a wall you can read.** Ask the agent for, say, a checklist while you are across the space from every wall, or looking away from them, and it now floats beside you, about 1.2 m away and a little below your eyes, 30° to your right, facing you, rather than going onto the nearest wall, however far away. A second floats 30° to your left, then further out on each side, so that several fan out about you rather than stacking, and none covers what is straight ahead; scenery or a wall to your right sends it to your left, and where nowhere beside you is clear, it floats in front of you. They stay where they float, as any floating panel does, and the agent can move them again. Looking at a wall near enough to read, the panel still goes onto it, and the Tomori wall still has the agent's own notices, such as a worker finishing or a pairing. For other servers and tools: `tomori-security`'s `Entry` gains `tag`, given as a token is issued, or, for one issued before, as the store is read, and kept at its next change, with `FIRST_TAG` and `LAST_TAG`, so make an `Entry` only through the store; `tomori-server`'s `presence` gains `fanned_beside`. - **Bend a wall by moving your hand across, tilt it up and down, both more quickly, and its handles sit where they should.** Grip a wall's **knob** and move your hand to the **right** to bend the wall around you, and back to the left to flatten it, as a slider; moving up, down or towards the wall no longer bends it. - A new crate, **`tomori-prometheus`**, lets an environment's server side read Prometheus, the first of Tomori's data sources (#83). It is a blocking client, for a server side's own thread, for the Prometheus HTTP API's instant and range queries, with the endpoint and token given by the application, never by clients: `Client::builder(endpoint)` takes an `https://` endpoint, or `http://` only to a loopback host, a `TokenSource`, such as a closure that runs a command, whose token it keeps for 40 minutes and renews once after a 401, a time-out of 30 seconds for each request, limits of 8 MiB on answers and 16 KiB on queries, the API's prefix, `/api/v1` unless a gateway serves it elsewhere, form-encoded `POST`s or `GET`s, and a `LabelFilter`, which keeps only the labels listed, so that a server side passes on only those it needs. Every query names its time, so that several evaluated at one `Timestamp` agree, and answers are typed vectors, matrices, scalars and strings, with each sample's timestamp and `NaN` and infinities read; samples of native histograms are left out with a warning. ## v0.2.0

- Text on the headset, the desktop window and the spectator is now drawn from fonts rather than 8 × 8 bitmaps, so it is antialiased and stays crisp and steady as you move your head, and Japanese is shown rather than `?`. The hand menu, the wrist transcript, the recording card, the status pill, arrival's labels and workers' signs draw from a glyph atlas of signed distance fields in the new `tomori-text` crate, which bundles subsets of Noto Sans Mono, Noto Sans Symbols 2, Noto Sans Math and Noto Sans JP, under the SIL Open Font License, adding 2.1 MB to the APK. Accented letters, typographic quotes and dashes, Greek and Cyrillic are drawn as themselves, rather than as their base letter or plain equivalent, and a letter followed by a combining accent is drawn as one accented letter. Japanese kana and the 6,355 kanji of JIS X 0208 take two cells each, and lines of Japanese break between characters, never before "。" or after "「". Emoji, Hangul and rarer kanji still show as `?`. Bold is drawn heavier rather than twice, and box drawing joins up without seams. Every row is measured in cells as drawn, so a Japanese title on a worker's sign or a result card wraps or ends with "…" where it reaches the edge, rather than losing its end, and the queue's names on the wrist line up whatever the script; card titles now end with "…" rather than "...". Lines of Japanese no longer touch, and text on distant signs stays steady rather than shimmering as you move. A first screen of new Japanese fills in over a few frames, rather than dropping frames while its glyphs are drawn. Layouts are otherwise unchanged: every hand menu row still fits its 40 cells. `tomori --licenses` now shows the fonts' licences, which the APK carries. Text is not shaped, so scripts such as Arabic are not drawn correctly yet (part of #146).

- The worker you point at now reaches the agent before your words, after your name, as the first stage of shared attention (#81): `Ada: [Pointing at worker "Review commit 2657a52" (opencode session ses_…, running)] how is this one doing?`, rather than on a line after them. What counts is the worker you point at as you press **X**: pointing elsewhere while you talk no longer changes it, and pointing only while you talk no longer counts. The headset still sends only which worker it is, now as the words begin, and the server describes it from what it knows. The queue still shows only your words. For other clients, `utterance_begin` takes an optional `attention`, a list of resources by identity such as `{"kind": "worker", "worker": "ses_1"}`, of which the server takes the first 4 and keeps the context within 1,000 characters; `Pointed` is now `tomori_protocol::Resource`, which is `#[non_exhaustive]` so that kinds can be added, so make a worker with `Resource::worker` and match it with a wildcard arm. An entry the server can't read, or of a kind it doesn't know, is skipped, and the words are kept, where before one malformed entry lost the whole utterance. Square brackets in your words, such as Whisper's "\[Music]" among them, now reach the agent as parentheses, "(Music)", so that only Tomori's description is in square brackets; the queue still shows your words as heard. A client from before this still sends the worker as its words end, and the server still tells the agent, before the words. In `tomori-client`, `Action::EndUtterance` no longer carries what was pointed at; make the utterance with `Utterance::attending` instead, and `Microphone::end_pointing` is replaced by `Microphone::begin_attending`.

- The headset no longer starts in a black world when it asks for the microphone.
