# The Farmyard's Beach

Beside the trail where it passes the river, by the `riverbank` viewpoint, a shingle beach slopes from the trail's edge down into the river, with stones on it that participants take with either hand, carry, throw, stack and lay in the river as a dam. It is all in `beach.rs`, which the server side (`side/shingle.rs`) simulates with `tomori-physics` and the client crate's `stones.rs` mirrors.

## Its ground

The beach is the ground itself, with nothing laid over it. `outline()` cuts the yard's meadow (`props.rs`) and the foothills' faces (`backdrop::ground_triangles`) along it, with `shapes::cut`, and the parts within it, `ground_faces()`, are drawn in `shingle(seed)`, a stony grey-brown, face by face. `height(point)` is how high they are there, the ground as drawn, or `None` beyond the beach. `model()` draws only its `PEBBLES`, `pebbles()`, low pyramids a few centimetres across, sunk a little into the ground, which nothing rests on or walks round.

`ground()` is what participants walk, which the farmyard's `walkable` derives its ground from: the faces from `WALKED`, just beyond the foot of the trail's edge, down the bank to the water's edge and into the shallows, no deeper than `SHALLOWS`, 6 cm, none steeper than 20°. From its lower slope the foothills' outer rings across the yard are up to a metre and a half beyond the headset's far plane, deep in the haze, as from the waterfall rock, which the backdrop's tests allow.

## Its stones

Stones are numbered from 0: the river's first, `RIVER` of them, of four kinds (`Kind::Slab`, `Cobble`, `Long` and `Angular`), half of them `big`; then the building stones, `BUILDING` of them, `building(index)`, five `Kind::Flagstone`s and three `Kind::Block`s, sized by `hewn` and shaped by `dressed`, boxes bevelled and nudged, flat below and level above, for lintels, tables, roofs, pillars and walls. `stone(index)` is any stone's hull, from which it is both drawn and collided, every face of four corners at most, as the physics harness found Rapier needs; `size`, `volume` and `outside` measure it. `homes()` lays them out: the river stones scattered, more towards the water, and the building stones piled and in a row on the flat shingle (`building_homes`, `BUILDING_OUT`, `PILE_ALONG`, `BLOCKS_ALONG`), clear of the trail. `heft(index)` is how much of the hand's speed a stone leaves it with, by its weight, and `fastest(index)` the speed the server side holds a throw to, so that a building stone cannot be flung.

## Its world

`colliders()` are what the stones rest on: `drawn()`, the ground as drawn within reach, the meadow, the foothills' faces down the bank and across the river's bed to its far bank, and the trail's track, one mesh; and the river's stones beside it. `settings()` are the world's, in `SUBSTEPS`, eight, a step, so that a stack on level ground settles where it was put, each contact solved against its own triangle (`Settings::unclustered`), so that a broad stone lying across a bend rests rather than creeping. `bodies()` are the stones as bodies.

`waters()` are the river beside it: first its dam reach, `dam_reach()`, the river beside the beach across its whole channel to the far bank (`DAM_OUT`) and `DAM_ALONG` either side of its middle, where a stone resting stays, as part of a dam; then bands along the river, where one resting a minute goes back to where it began. `stays(point)` says which. The physics proposal's fourth stage, water rising behind a dam, would raise the dam reach's level as its stones close it.

## Changing it

Add a stone by raising `RIVER` or `BUILDING`, which raises `STONES`, within `tomori_physics::BODY_LIMIT`, and the farmyard's version, since an earlier client would draw it as another stone or not at all. Move the beach by `AT`, `NEAR`, `FAR` and `HALF_WIDTH`; the meadow and the foothills are cut along it wherever it is. Run the farmyard's tests: they check each stone's hull, the homes, the budget, that the beach is the ground's own faces with nothing over them but pebbles, that it is walked from the trail to the water's edge within the slope Tomori walks, that the beach settles and then costs nothing, that stones laid in the dam reach stay and one beyond it goes back, and that cairns and structures of its stones (a dolmen, a flagstone on a cairn, a short wall and a tower) stand for ten simulated minutes, while a lintel beyond its pillar falls. Set `TOMORI_FARMYARD_BEACH_PREVIEW` to a PNG path when running the client crate's tests to save a little dolmen of the building stones on the beach, and `TOMORI_FARMYARD_DAM_PREVIEW` to save the view from the beach's slope down to stones laid in the river.
