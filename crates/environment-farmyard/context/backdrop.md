# The Farmyard's Backdrop

Beyond the fence lies a New Zealand evening: pasture rising to bush-clad foothills that ring the yard, a waterfall falling down a cliff in the foothills behind the coop into a pool sunk at its foot, a flat-topped rock standing in the pool before the fall, the river it feeds winding round the yard's right side in a channel, through grey shingle below its banks, river stones along it, native bush, and, beyond the foothills, ranges fading bluer into the dusk, the highest under snow. It is all scenery, but for the trail round the outside of the fence (see `trail.md`), and the chickens stay in the yard.

## How it is drawn

The backdrop is laid out within 50 m of the eyes from anywhere participants stand, the headset's far plane when it was laid out and now the bound that keeps it compact and deep in its haze, as every client draws out to 2 km; so it is in two parts:

- **Within reach** (`backdrop.rs`), in the yard's coordinates, low-poly in the yard's palette and lit as the yard is, hazed with distance: the foothills' ground, one model of rings about `layout::BACKDROP_CENTRE` from 11.5 m to 36 m (`backdrop::RINGS`), whose height is `backdrop::ground`, and which `backdrop::drawn_ground` gives as its triangles draw it, its first ring at the meadow's height, `backdrop::MEADOW`, its corners the edge of the yard's meadow (`backdrop::meadow`), which reaches no further, so that the two meet without a step or a gap and the river's channel beyond is open; and the bush, a model for each `backdrop::Kind` (the bush's canopy, podocarps, cabbage trees and flax), instanced where `backdrop::plants` says, but its tree ferns, which `backdrop::plants` places too and the ferns near the trail's meshes and shader draw, as ponga or mamaku (see `ferns.md`); the river's stones, one model, `backdrop::stone`, instanced where `backdrop::stones` says; and the waterfall rock, `backdrop::rock`.
- **Far beyond that bound**, the ranges (`backdrop::ranges`) are directions, not places: the scenery draws them at the sky's depth, after everything else, where nothing nearer has been drawn, the farthest first, each faded into the sky's own colour that way (`horizon` in the client's `sky.wgsl`), so they stay put as you move, as distant mountains do.

The water (`backdrop::water`) is drawn by the client's `water.wgsl`, which moves it by the time alone: ripples carried downstream, streaks down the fall, and foam and mist at its foot. No texture is needed.

## Its water and banks

The water lies in a channel below the land either side of it, the trail and the yard, as water would stay: the pool at −0.3 m in a hollow at the cliff's foot, and the river leaving it over its lip, just beyond it and just below it (`layout::RIVER[1]`), and running downhill to −0.76 m where it leaves, 0.3 m and more below the trail wherever the trail passes it, and 0.4 m and more beyond the lip. Its surfaces are flat at their levels and reach beneath their banks (`backdrop::SPREAD`), so that the shore is where the ground rises out of them. `backdrop::ground` sinks them (`sunk`): the valley's floor is lowered to the banks' tops near the water, `backdrop::bank` above it, from `LIP`, 0.4 m, to `RISE` more, rolling along the river, widening where the land is higher so that its sides are not cliffs; and the channel is cut from there to the bed, below the water, `backdrop::BANKS` from the river's middle and `RIM` from the pool's. Where the land would lie lower than a lip above the water, it is raised to it, but where the river leaves the pool, as nowhere about the farmyard's water does now. Since the foothills' ground is triangles 1.5 m across, the tests check the ground as drawn, not only `ground`: everywhere the water's surface ends, the drawn ground stands above it, the banks' tops from 0.3 m to a metre above it, and everywhere beyond the water's spread at least 0.25 m above the water nearest it. Flax stands beyond the water's spread, its leaves over the banks and clear of the pool, and `backdrop::stones` sets stones of every size along the river's banks where they rise out of the water, in its shallows, standing out of it, and about the pool's shore, on the ground as drawn.

The waterfall rock (`layout::WATERFALL_ROCK`) stands in the pool, 2.6 m before the fall's foot and 1.5 m to its right as one faces it from the yard, clear of the falling water and its mist, its flat top half a metre above the water, `ROCK_TOP` across, its sides `ROCK_REACH` at the water, its foot on the pool's basin: a teleport target, which the trail declares (see `trail.md`). It stands within 20 m of where participants arrive, as targets and anchors then had to, which is why the cliff stands at 22 and 23.5 m (`layout::CLIFF`), 1.5 m nearer than it first did; the space's bounds are 30 m now, since the upper terrace (see `terrace.md`).

The mountain behind the waterfall rises to the upper terrace at the top of the falls: the cliff's top is the terrace's ground (`backdrop::TERRACE_GROUND`, 5 cm beneath the terrace's floor) across the waterfall's way, from 5 m to its left to 12 m to its right as one faces it from the yard, but for the stream's channel, whose bed at the lip is the cliff's top as it was, `layout::CLIFF_TOP`, so that the waterfall falls exactly as it did; behind the terrace, the upper cliff rises from `layout::UPPER_CLIFF`'s 29.5 to 30.5 m, one ring of its own, to `layout::UPPER_TOP`, 9.5 m, and the knoll above it falls away into the ridge by the last ring. The ring added for the upper cliff keeps the outer rings' cells cut as they were (`cell`), so that the river's channel beyond is drawn exactly as before. The upper pool's basin and the stream from it to the waterfall's lip are cut into the terrace's ground, too small for the rings to draw, so the rings' cells about them, from the lip to the upper cliff's top and two segments either side of the waterfall's way (`backdrop::FINE_RINGS`, `FINE_SEGMENTS`, `fine`), are each cut into rows and columns about 30 cm apart whose corners are on `ground`, and each cell beside them takes in the corners along the edge it shares with them, half fanned from each of its other two corners (`zipped`) rather than all from one, so that the foothills are one closed surface, no corner in the middle of another face's edge, which a test checks edge by edge; the stream's banks rise through its water's level at its edges, so that the water's edges are beneath them. `drawn_ground` gives the height drawn, from the cells' triangles, found once (`foothills_triangles`). An earlier patch drawn over the rings, 4 cm above them, with their corners sunk beneath it, left steps and cracks along its edges and the lip, which the maintainer saw on the headset. The bush stands nowhere on the mountain's top before the upper cliff, nor within reach of the terrace (`open`), so a few plants found places elsewhere. From its top, the foothills' outer rings across the yard lie beyond the far plane, 46 m and more away, deep in the haze, and are cut off; everything else is within it.

Plants are placed deterministically, from `props::scatter`, so every client draws the same bush. `backdrop::clear` says where one may stand: on the foothills' ground, 3 m and more beyond the fence, out of the way in through the gate, out of the water, off the waterfall's cliff, within the 50 m bound, and clear of the sightlines `backdrop::views` keeps open: from the arrival to the waterfall, down to the mist at its foot, and to the river's far bank (`layout::far_bank`), since its water lies below its near bank seen from the yard; from the workbench's curve to the river's far bank; and from the trail's lookout to the pool and the rock, and its riverbank to the river. The pines in `props.rs` keep to the same rule (`backdrop::open`).

## Where its sounds come from

`layout::sounds()` names its places for ambient sound: `waterfall`, the middle of the fall (`layout::WATERFALL`, between `WATERFALL_LIP` and `WATERFALL_FOOT`); `pool`, its middle at its water's level (`layout::POOL`); `upper-falls`, the middle of the falls above the terrace (`layout::UPPER_FALLS`), which the waterfall's sound runs along to the lip and down to its middle, so that the terrace between them hears both; `river`, each point of its middle downstream of the pool (`layout::RIVER`, at the water's level, running downhill), which the river's sound runs along from the pool's lip, the pool itself being still; and `bush`, the middle of each patch in `layout::BUSH`, on the ground. `layout::nearest_river` gives the nearest point of the river to any place.

## The Quest budget

About 19,100 triangles in eleven draws a frame for each eye, all opaque, so nothing is sorted: the foothills (4,350, 38 of them where the beach's outline cuts their faces, and the upper cliff's ring and the fine cells about the upper water among them), the plants (10,862, one draw for each kind with all its instances, but the tree ferns, counted with the ferns, see `ferns.md`), the river's stones (885, 59 of one model in one draw, the terrace's eight among them), the waterfall rock (40), the trail (1,144, see `trail.md`), the upper terrace (414, see `terrace.md`), the water (253, the upper falls, their pool and the stream among it) and the ranges (1,184), 19,132 together, under `backdrop::BUDGET`, 22,000, which the tests check with the exact counts; and the stations' five plaques, small panels of text drawn after the scene. The budget was 20,000 until the trail and the stones added about 2,000 triangles in two draws; sinking the water and adding the rock left it 5 fewer, drawing the tree ferns with the ferns 4,360 fewer, and the upper terrace 1,756 more, in one draw more, its own, and 266 more when its floor's fan became even faces. The yard's meadow, drawn with the yard, is 100 triangles where its disc was 28, cut along the beach's outline (see `beach.md`). The water's and the ranges' shaders are a few arithmetic operations a pixel, over a small part of the view, and nothing is built or uploaded after the scenery is made but the time. On a desktop GPU, at the Quest 3's eye size with 4× multisampling, the backdrop took the scenery from about 0.17 to 0.24 ms an eye.

## Changing it

Move the river, the waterfall or the bush through `layout.rs`; the ground follows them. Run the farmyard's tests: they check that the backdrop stands beyond the fence and within its 50 m bound from the yard and the trail, that the views stay open, that the river runs downhill in its channel below its banks, the trail and the yard, and the waterfall from its cliff into its pool, the rock standing in it, that the flax stands on the banks and the stones by the water, that the named places are on the water or among the bush, that the bush is the same every time, and the triangle counts, which change as plants are added or reshaped, and should be updated here too. Set `TOMORI_FARMYARD_BACKDROP_PREVIEW` to a PNG path when running the client crate's tests to save the views from the arrival and from the workbench, as `-1.png` and `-2.png`, and `TOMORI_FARMYARD_RIVER_ROCK_PREVIEW` to save the river in its channel from the trail's riverbank and the rock from the lookout.
