# Using the Prometheus Source

`tomori-prometheus` reads a Prometheus-compatible endpoint from an environment's server side. Everything it is configured with, the endpoint, the token and the queries, comes from the application, Tomori's own or another project's, and stays in the server: clients and the agent are sent only what the server side chooses to send, with the labels its filter keeps.

## The client

`Client::builder(endpoint)` takes `https://HOST[:PORT][/PATH]`, or `http://` only to a loopback host, such as a test's fake. Before `build()`:

- `token_source(source)` authenticates with bearer tokens. A `Token` is a fixed source, and any `Fn() -> Result<Token, String>` is one, such as a closure that runs a command. The client keeps a token for `token_lifetime` (40 minutes by default), and asks again after it has passed, or once after the endpoint refuses a request with HTTP 401, which it then retries once. Another client of the application's can send the same token: call the source's `token()` and send `Token::expose()` as `Bearer TOKEN` in its `authorization` field, replacing it in its own errors, never logging, printing or storing it; a `Token`'s `Debug` hides it, and it has no `Display`.
- `timeout` (30 seconds, at most a day) bounds each request's wait for a slot and then, separately, its exchange, from when it has a slot until its answer is read, and is sent as Prometheus's evaluation `timeout`. A call refused with HTTP 401 sends a second request, with time-outs of its own, after asking the token source, whose time is not bounded here.
- `concurrency` (4) is how many requests the client and its clones send at once, each over a connection of its own. The address the client keeps has at most twice this many connections, counting those still closing, and briefly more when it moves to another address while the old one's close. Each request in flight may hold an answer of up to `max_response_bytes`, so answers may take about `concurrency` times that much memory at once.
- `max_response_bytes` (8 MiB) and `max_query_bytes` (16 KiB) bound answers and expressions.
- `api_prefix` (`/api/v1`) is where the API is beneath the endpoint, for a gateway that serves it elsewhere.
- `method` sends queries form-encoded in a `POST` (the default) or in the URL of a `GET`.
- `labels(LabelFilter::only([...]))` keeps only the labels listed, removing the rest from every answer; without it, every label is kept. A call may give its own, below.
- `user_agent` replaces `tomori-prometheus/VERSION`.
- `cache(Cache::new())` keeps answers, as described below.

The client sends up to `concurrency` requests at once, from any of its callers' threads, on a thread of its own, over connections that Base's HTTP client pools for the address that last connected: a slow request holds only its own slot and connection, so one shared client serves several pollers. A failure retires only the connection it happened on, a caller giving up while a new connection is being made included; only the address failing to connect makes the request try each of the host's addresses in turn, the one that last connected first, each but the last within its share of the time-out. Clones share the thread, the connections, the slots and the token, and renew a refused token once between them; the token source is asked by one caller at a time, and callers whose token is still good never wait for it. Redirects are not followed. Dropping the last clone stops the thread, waiting for it for at most `DROP_WAIT`, a second, since a lookup stuck in the system's resolver cannot be interrupted.

## Queries

- `query(expression, at)` is an instant query at a `Timestamp`. Evaluate every query of one poll at the same `at`, such as `Timestamp::now().floor(step)`, so that they agree.
- `query_range(expression, Range::new(start, end, step))` is a range query, of at most 11,000 points per series.
- `query_with` and `query_range_with` take a `Call` as well: `Call::new().timeout(duration)` gives that call its own time-out in place of the client's, such as a short one for a quick read and a long one for a slow read of a whole fleet, and `.background()` marks it a background request, which never takes a slot while another request waits for one; background requests together hold at most all but one of the client's slots, so that one is always left for other requests. With a `concurrency` of 1 a background request takes the only slot when nothing else waits, and a request that comes after waits for it, so give a client that sends background requests a concurrency of at least 2. `.labels(LabelFilter::only([...]))` keeps the labels it lists of that call's answer in place of the client's filter, such as a chart's grouping labels, so that one client serves consumers needing different labels: list every label the call needs, since it replaces the client's filter rather than adding to it. It can keep labels the client's filter removes, every label with `LabelFilter::all()`, loosening that protection, since labels can carry URLs, user identifiers or secrets: give a call only the labels it needs and may pass on. A `Call` is `Clone`, not `Copy`.

Answers are `Response`s, the data with Prometheus's warnings. An instant query's `Data` is a `Vector` of `Sample`s (`into_vector()`), a `Matrix` of `Series`, a `Scalar` or a `String`; a range query's is its series. Each `Point` has its own timestamp, to the millisecond, and its value, `NaN` and infinities included. Samples of native histograms are left out, keeping float samples, with a warning saying how many: wrap them in `histogram_count()`, `histogram_sum()` or `histogram_quantile()` to read them.

## Caching

A client built with `cache(Cache::new())` shares the cache among its clones, so build one client for every consumer of a source:

- Identical calls in flight together, the same expression at the same time, or over the same range and step, send one request and share its answer or error; one waiting gives up after its own time-out, twice, as its own request would.
- An answer is kept while fresh: `Cache::fresh(duration)` for every call, none by default, or `Call::fresh(duration)` for one. Make it no longer than the data's resolution, such as half the scrape interval.
- `Call::bucket(duration)` evaluates an instant query at the start of the bucket holding its time, such as `Duration::from_secs(15)`, and keeps its answer for the bucket, so that consumers polling at different cadences ask the same and share one request a bucket.
- A range query's points are kept by expression, step and phase: the next range of the same step, its window moved on, asks only for the steps from the last kept, less `Cache::overlap` (a minute) for late samples, and is answered wholly from the cache while fresh and complete. Give ranges whose start and end are steps from the epoch, with `Range::aligned()`, so that every caller's steps coincide and a query cache in front of the source sees the same requests. A range starting before what is kept, or after a gap, is read whole.
- `Client::remember(expression, range, &series)` gives the cache a range's answer the application kept itself, such as across a restart, as though read but never fresh, so that the next read asks only for the steps after it; `remember_with(expression, range, &series, call)` keeps it under the call's label filter, for answers read with one.
- Answers are kept, and requests in flight shared, under the label filter they were read with: calls with different filters never share an answer, and each filter's answers count towards the cache's bounds.
- A range whose expression uses `@ start()` or `@ end()` is read whole every time and never kept (`promql::anchored`).
- A call waits for an identical request in flight only of its own priority and with a time-out at least as long; otherwise it sends its own.
- `Cache::span_decay` (15 minutes) lets a range's points kept shrink once no range as long has been asked for over two such periods.
- `Response::partial` says the source gave a warning other than PromQL's own `PromQL info:` and `PromQL warning:` annotations, or its `infos`, as a gateway that could not reach a store does: it is returned but never kept, and the next read of its range asks for the whole window.
- `Cache::max_entries` (1,024) and `Cache::max_bytes` (64 MiB) bound it, the least recently used dropped first. `Client::cache_stats()` counts `hits`, `tails`, `misses`, `deduped`, `partial` and `evictions`, with the `entries` and `bytes` held, for a server side's own logs.

## Building queries

`promql::quote` makes a string literal of a value read from elsewhere, such as a pod's name, so that it cannot change the query's meaning; `promql::escape_regex` escapes it for a regular expression matcher, inside `quote`. `promql::batch` joins expressions with `or`, each on a line of its own so that a `# comment` cannot swallow the next, into as few queries as fit a bound, such as `max_query_bytes`. Join only instant vectors, since `or` refuses a scalar (wrap one in `vector()`), and give each expression's series a label of its own, with `label_replace`, since `or` keeps only the left of two series with the same labels.

## Describing a metric to the agent

When the space's agent inspects something a server side presents from Prometheus, such as a chart, through Tomori's `inspect` tool, describe it with `Metric`: `Metric::range(datasource, query, range)` or `Metric::instant(datasource, query, at)`, with `with_labels`, `with_latest`, `with_unit` and `with_link`, and answer with `to_json()`. `datasource` is a name of the application's choosing, such as `production`: `Metric` has no field for the endpoint or the token, so neither can reach the agent through it. Give it only the labels the client's label filter keeps; a link with user information, or that is not `http://` or `https://`, is refused. Times are written in UTC as RFC 3339, with `Timestamp::rfc3339`, and `NaN` and infinities as Prometheus writes them.

## Errors

Every failure is an `Error`: `Invalid` configuration or arguments, `Transport`, `Timeout`, the source not answering within the time-out, `Queued`, no slot coming free within it because the client's own requests held them all, so that nothing was sent, `Status` (with an excerpt of the answer and any `retry-after`), `Auth`, `Parse`, `Limit`, or `Query`, Prometheus's own refusal with its HTTP status, any `retry-after` and its `errorType`; `status()` and `retry_after()` read them from either. An error's own words never name the token or the endpoint; text it quotes from the transport or the source has the token replaced with `[redacted]` and the endpoint's host and path, wherever each stands whole, with `<endpoint>` (a host shorter than three characters, and a path shorter than eight or made only of `api`, `v1`, `v2` and `v3` segments, such as `/api/v1`, are left alone), but may hold anything else the source wrote, unfiltered by the label filter. A server side's error is shown to everyone in its space, visitors included, so say what failed in your own words rather than passing an `Error`'s text on. The client never retries a 429, a 5xx or a request on a connection the source has just closed: poll at your own cadence, waiting at least the `retry-after` the source gave.
