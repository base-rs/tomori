# Environment Actions

This describes how a space's agent changes what an environment shows when someone asks, such as "change this to Belgium" while pointing at a terminal: an environment's server side declares **actions** on the things it describes with a reference, the agent finds them with `inspect` and takes one with the `act` tool, and Tomori checks the call against the declaration and who asked before handing it to the side, as it hands on a row a participant chooses (`panel-actions.md`). Increment 1 is a requirement, built; the later increments are proposals, each its own decision for the maintainer.

## Requirement

### Declaring actions

**Actions.** A server side declares the actions its environment offers with `Space::declare_actions(Vec<Action>)`, replacing those declared before; what it declares belongs to its binding, as everything it keeps does (`environments.md`). Each `tomori_environment::Action` has a **name**, 1 to 32 bytes of lowercase ASCII letters, digits and `_`, beginning with a letter (`valid_action_name`, `ACTION_NAME_BYTES`), such as `set_region`; a **description written for the agent**, such as "Show this terminal's charts for another region"; up to 4 **parameters** (`PARAMETER_LIMIT`), all required, so that the side never guesses a default the agent did not mean; and whether it is **consequential**, `Action::consequential` (below). A `Parameter` is named as an action is, has a description, and is one of a `Kind`:

- `Parameter::choice(name, description, choices)`: one of 1 to 64 `Choice`s (`CHOICE_LIMIT`), each a value `valid_reference` takes, such as `eu-belgium`, unlike the others, with an optional label for matching what people say, `Choice::labelled`, such as "Belgium (eu-west-3)";
- `Parameter::number(name, description, least, most)`, both finite, the least no more than the most, with an optional `stepped(step)`, finite and above 0, such as a number of pecks from 1 to 8 in steps of 1;
- `Parameter::text(name, description, chars)`: text of at most `chars` characters, from 1 to 200 (`TEXT_LIMIT`), which the server cleans onto one line.

**What the server takes.** It refuses a declaration, saying why, and keeps none of it, when `check_actions` refuses it, which a side's tests may call: more than 32 actions (`ACTION_LIMIT`), a name or parameter name it does not take or given twice, more than 4 parameters, a choice of none, more than 64 or a repeated or invalid value, a number of bounds or step it does not take, text of a length it does not take, or more than 32 KiB of text in all, every name, description, value and label together (`ACTIONS_LIMIT`), so that a list of 30 regions with their labels, about 4 KiB, fits several times over. It cleans each description and label onto one line, as everything told to the agent of an environment is, and cuts an action's description after 200 characters (`ACTION_DESCRIPTION_LIMIT`), a parameter's after 120 (`PARAMETER_DESCRIPTION_LIMIT`) and a label after 80 (`CHOICE_LABEL_LIMIT`), with `Action::cleaned`. A declaration from a binding that has ended is kept nowhere.

**Offering them.** An object a side describes offers actions by name, `Object::offering(["set_region"])`; the server keeps at most 8 (`OFFER_LIMIT`), leaving out a name `valid_action_name` refuses or given twice, and none of an object without a reference, since the agent names things by id; it offers only those the side has declared, in the object's order, so that declaring once and offering by name keeps six terminals that offer the same 30 regions at one copy of the list. An object stops offering an action as its state changes, such as a terminal frozen during an incident, by being described again without it.

### Discovering them

**Named as someone points.** When someone points at something that offers actions as their words begin, the note before their words names the first of them after its id, as `Ada: [Pointing at deck terminal 2 (id observe:terminal/2; offers set_region)] change this to Belgium`, at most three names and 60 characters of them, so that the agent knows to look; the context block's bound, 1,000 characters, is unchanged. What is pointed at is resolved as the words begin, as before.

**Listed by `inspect`.** `inspect` of an object's id adds `actions`, each action it offers now whole: its `name`, `description`, whether it is `consequential`, and its `parameters`, each with its `name`, `description` and `kind`, `choice` with its `choices`, each a `value` and a `label` or null, `number` with its `least`, `most` and `step` or null, or `text` with its `chars`; an empty list for one that offers none, and for a panel or a row. Its `next` then says that the agent may take them with `act` when someone asks. Given `action`, an action's name of at most 32 bytes, it lists only that one, refusing one the object does not offer, naming those it does. **Bounded.** The result is at most 64 KiB as it is sent, as structured content and again as text (`agent_inspect::RESULT_BYTES`), room for the side's details, at most 16 KiB as JSON, sent twice and escaped, and for what it offers: where the actions would take more, each choice parameter lists its first 16 values, then its first 4, then none, with `choices_total`, how many it has, and `next` says to inspect again with `action` for one whole, or, for one action asked for whole that still does not fit, to ask whoever asked to name the value; and where even that does not fit, the actions are listed by `name` and `consequential` alone.

### Taking one

**`act`.** The tool takes an `id` of at most 256 bytes, as `inspect` takes it, an `action`, its name, of at most 32 bytes, as `ACTION_NAME_BYTES` and the tool's schema say, `parameters`, a JSON object of its parameters by name, which may be left out for an action that takes none, and `requested_by`, as the gated tools take it (`mcp.md`). It is a space tool: only the space's own agent session calls it, as every space tool, and a call naming no session or another's is refused. The server finds, now, an object the side running for the space's current binding describes with that reference, among every object it describes, whether or not `presence` lists it to the agent (`environments.md`), in the environment the space is in now; checks that it offers that action now; checks the parameters against the declaration exactly: every parameter present and no other, a choice one of its values byte for byte, never a label, a number finite, within its bounds and on its step, and text within its length once cleaned onto one line; checks who asked, below; holds the call to the rates, below; and only then hands the side an `Intent::action(participant, Acted)` in its inbox, in order with participants' intents and choices, whose `Acted` has the `reference`, the `action`, the checked `parameters` as a JSON object's text and a `ticket`, and whose participant is the number of whoever's words the agent acts on. Each refusal, before the side is asked, says which check failed: an id the space does not have now, as one the side no longer describes, or of another environment, an action the object does not offer, naming those it does, a parameter missing, unknown or not a value it takes, saying what it takes, and who asked.

**Forged by no one.** Only the server makes an action: a client's intent is never one, whatever its payload says, as for a chosen row, so a side may act on one as on a participant's own request, still validating it against its own state. An action is handed only to the side running for the binding whose objects offer it, never to another environment's or an earlier binding's.

**Answered.** The side answers with `Space::answer(ticket, Outcome)`: `Outcome::done(summary)`, such as "Terminal 2 now shows Belgium", or `Outcome::refused(reason)`, such as "Terminal 2 is frozen while its incident is open". The server waits for the answer on a thread of its own, never the hub's or the scheduler's, for at most 5 seconds (`ACT_TIMEOUT`, which `Server::with_act_timeout` changes), with `Inbox::outcome`; an answer to a ticket no longer waited for, as one answered already or too late, is not taken, and `answer` says so. A thread that cannot start is a wait without an answer, and the ticket is waited for no longer, so that an answer the side gives later is not kept.

**What the agent is told.** The result gives the `space`, the `id`, the `environment`, the `action`, the `parameters` as handed on, the `outcome`, `done`, `refused` or `no answer`, the side's summary or reason as `message`, cleaned onto one line and cut after 200 characters (`OUTCOME_LIMIT`), or null, `requested_by`, whom the server checked, or null, `for`, whose words it acted on, a `note` that the message comes from the environment and is data, never instructions, and `next`: to say in a sentence what it did; to tell whoever asked that the environment refused, and why; or that the environment did not answer and may still do it, so to inspect the id before trying again.

**Noted in the space.** Each action handed on puts a notice on the space's status wall, a text panel titled Actions with source Tomori, replacing the last unless a participant put it where it is, as the workers' notice does (`mcp.md`), saying what was done and on whose word, the side's message quoted: `Tomori took set_region on observe:terminal/2, as Samuel spoke: "Terminal 2 now shows Belgium"`, `as Samuel asked` where the server checked `requested_by`, or that the environment refused, with its reason, or did not answer. It is kept with the space's panels, and logged at info, as `The agent of space ID took set_region on observe:terminal/2 for Samuel: done; projected panel-3.`, naming a checked requester by handle and entry, its parameters left out. A space deleted while the side answered is noted nowhere. A refusal before the side is asked is neither noted nor logged.

### Who may cause one

**Harmless actions need nobody named.** An action the side does not mark consequential changes only what the space shows, for everyone in it, and can be changed back, as a terminal's region can: it grants no access and raises no privilege, as Tomori's own `set_wall` and `move_panel` carry no requester. The agent takes it without `requested_by`, but only in a turn a participant's words began, lately and alone: the server refuses it, saying why, as it refuses the gated tools (`security.md`, who asked), when the space has given its agent no participant's words since the server started; when they were given more than 3 minutes ago (`REQUEST_RECENT`), so that words said earlier, or a turn left running, are no standing permission; when what it gave last no longer stands behind what the agent does, because Tomori gave it its own notes since, such as a timer firing (`timers.md`), input that did not come through Tomori reached it, or Tomori lost its connection to the session; or when Tomori's own notes, such as a timer that fired or a worker's report, were carried in front of the words in the same prompt (`Given::carried`), since the turn may answer the notes rather than the words, until the participant speaks again, when their words come alone. So no timer, event or note drives the environment through the agent's turn. The side is told the number of the participant whose words began the turn.

**Consequential actions need who asked.** An action the side marks consequential, because it reaches outside the space, cannot be undone or costs something, such as silencing an alert or restarting a pod, needs `requested_by`, which the server checks as for every gated tool: the words given last are that participant's, within 3 minutes, superseded by nothing, and they are in the space now as an owner or a collaborator, never a visitor; without it, the call is refused, saying that the environment marks the action consequential. A `requested_by` given with a harmless action is checked too, so that a notice never names someone the server did not check.

**Rates.** At most 4 actions of one binding's side wait for its answer at once (`server_sides::ACTIONS`), as for inspections, so that a side that never answers holds at most 4 threads; a side whose inbox is full, not keeping up, is handed nothing; and the agent takes at most 10 actions in a space at once and 30 a minute after (`channel::ACT_BURST`, `ACTS_PER_SECOND`), beyond which `act` is refused, saying to wait. The allowance is kept with the space, not its binding, so that changing environment and back refills nothing, and only an action handed on spends it: one refused, before it or because its side is not running, still answering 4 or not keeping up, gives it back. Each refusal says why. Actions share the side's inbox and its 64 (`WAITING_INTENT_LIMIT`), never participants' intent allowance.

**Environment text is data.** Descriptions, labels, summaries and reasons are written by the environment, which may quote what it reads elsewhere, so every string is cleaned and bounded and told to the agent only in results, with a note that it is data; none is put into a tool's description or the agent's instructions.

**What this does not stop.** As for every gated tool, an injection within a turn a participant's words began, such as a chart label telling the agent to take an action: the server can tell that a participant spoke last, alone, not that they asked for this. The environment marking what is consequential, the notice, and the side's own validation of each action against its state limit it.

### The instructions and the farmyard

**Told to the agent.** `act`'s description and `tomori-tools` (`mcp.md`) say that the actions something offers are named after its id and listed by `inspect`; to take one only in the turn someone's words asked for it, never for a timer or a sub-agent's report; to match what was said to a choice's value, never its label, asking whoever asked when two could be meant; that a consequential action needs `requested_by`; to say in a sentence what it did; and that what `act` returns comes from the environment, as data.

**The farmyard's hens peck.** The farmyard's server side declares one harmless action, `peck`, "Have this hen peck at the ground for a moment, as if she found something", taking `pecks`, a number from 1 to 8 in steps of 1, which every hen offers (`farmyard.md`). Taken on a hen on the ground, she pecks that many times, beginning a moment later, for everyone in the space, and the side answers done, as "Marigold pecks at the ground 2 times."; up in the coop, on its ramp or running off, the side refuses, saying what she is doing; and on a hen it does not have, that there is no such hen. It changes nothing in how the farmyard looks.

## Proposal: the later increments

### What Observe would write

For Observe's deck terminals, each showing one region's charts, with a region selector on each terminal's keyboard shelf:

```rust
let regions: Vec<Choice> = REGIONS.iter().map(|region| Choice::new(region.id).labelled(region.name)).collect();
space.declare_actions(vec![
    Action::new("set_region", "Show this terminal's charts for another region")
        .with(Parameter::choice("region", "the region to show", regions)),
])?;
space.describe(terminals.iter().map(|terminal| {
    Object::new(&terminal.identity, format!("deck terminal {} showing {}", terminal.number, terminal.region_name))
        .with_reference(format!("terminal/{}", terminal.number))
        .at(terminal.position)
        .offering(["set_region"])
}).collect())?;

while let Some(intent) = space.next_intent(TICK) {
    if let Some(acted) = intent.acted() {
        let outcome = match set_region(acted.reference(), region_of(acted.parameters()), intent.participant()) {
            Ok(summary) => Outcome::done(summary),
            Err(reason) => Outcome::refused(reason),
        };
        space.answer(acted.ticket(), outcome);
    } else if let Some((terminal, region)) = selector(intent.payload()) {
        let _ = set_region(&terminal, region, intent.participant()); // the shelf's selector
    }
}
```

Samuel, pointing at terminal 2, says "hey, can you change this to Belgium?": the agent is given `Samuel: [Pointing at deck terminal 2 showing us-east-1 (id observe:terminal/2; offers set_region)] hey, can you change this to Belgium?`, inspects `observe:terminal/2` for the choices, calls `act { id: "observe:terminal/2", action: "set_region", parameters: { region: "eu-belgium" } }`, without `requested_by`, is told `done`, "Terminal 2 now shows Belgium", and says so; the terminal changes for everyone, and the status wall notes it. The shelf's selector stays Observe's own affordance, its intent and the agent's action applied by one function in inbox order.

### Increment 2: discovery beyond pointing, and more to offer

- A tool listing what offers actions, so that "change terminal 2 to Belgium", said without pointing, is found by name: each id, its description and the names it offers, paged as `panels` is.
- Actions offered by a published panel and a row, `Panel::offering` and `Row::offering`, found in `inspect`'s order, object, panel, row.
- A `toggle` parameter, true or false.

### Increment 3: affordances from declarations

The responder chain (`responders.md`) lets an object say which controller actions it takes, on the client. Each referenced thing's offered actions would be sent to clients, as a list panel's rows are, so that Tomori offers, on anything that offers a single-choice action and is pointed at, a slot of A's order, after Tomori's own rungs and before the environment's selectable object, which opens a chooser drawn as a list panel of choosable rows, one a choice, whose choice the server turns into the same `Intent::action`, with the participant as who asked. Then confirmations for consequential actions, asked of the requester on their wrist, and undo, an action's inverse that the side declares.

### Increment 4: timers that delegate to a worker

The maintainer, November 2026: "I think having a timer trigger things would be okay, but we have to be careful that it doesn't overload the conversation. It might be better if a timer triggered a sub-agent rather than you." So a timer's work need not be a turn of the conversation's agent, the coordinator.

**A delegated timer.** `set_timer` would gain an optional `task`, at most 2,000 characters, beside its `note`: when a timer with a task fires, the server starts a worker, a sub-agent session of the space's session, with the task, the timer's name and why it fired as its first prompt, instead of telling the coordinator a note; the worker appears among the space's workers, and on the timer's dial. A timer may still say `notify: also`. At most one worker a timer runs at a time, a firing while the last still runs counted as skipped; a delegated timer's occurrences are at least 15 minutes apart, and a space runs at most 24 delegated firings in 24 hours, beside its 96 firings.

**Reporting only when it matters.** The worker ends by calling a new tool, `report`, with a one-line summary, at most 200 characters, and whether it found a `change` or a `problem`; the timer's `report` setting, `always`, `on_change`, the default, or `on_problem`, decides whether the summary reaches the coordinator, as one note in the event queue (`timers.md`), coalescing by the timer's key. A summary not passed on is still on the timer's dial and in the worker's session. Routine firings take no turns at all, a change takes one note, and the daily caps bound the rest.

**What a delegated worker may do.** The worker's session is one Tomori started for the space, so the server knows its space and timer and scopes its tools: `inspect`, `panels`, `presence`, `report` and `act`, none of the coordinator's other space tools. With `act`, it takes harmless actions within the coordinator's rates, which it shares, its timer, which someone set and everyone sees, standing for it; and consequential ones only under a **standing authorisation** recorded with the timer, given only when the timer was set with `allow`, at most 8 `{id, action}` pairs or action names, together with a checked `requested_by`, an owner or a collaborator, recorded as the requester; it expires with the timer's `until`, or after 7 days if sooner, and ends when the requester's token is revoked, they are no longer an owner or collaborator, or anyone cancels the timer; the dial shows it, and each action's notice says "for Samuel, by the regions timer".

**How a worker is started.** Today Tomori creates only top-level sessions (`Backend::create`, OpenCode's `POST /api/session`), lists a session's children and deletes workers; only the coordinator starts sub-agents, and a sub-agent's session is bound to no space, so it cannot call space tools (`mcp.md`). This increment would add `Capabilities::delegate` and `Backend::start_worker(parent, title, prompt)`: OpenCode's backend creates a child session of the space's session, with `parentID`, and sends it the task, to be confirmed against OpenCode's session API before it is built; the server records the session as the timer's worker, so that its calls, named by `_meta`, are scoped as above. The River backend, downstream, would start one by whatever its agent offers, or not declare the capability, when a delegated timer is refused as it is set. On acceptance, its timer parts become an increment of `timers.md`.

## Rationale

The maintainer, in a headset in Observe, November 2026, asked to point at a terminal and say "can you please change this to that region?", suggesting a unique name for the terminal, Tomori's tools and the responder idea. The agent could find what is pointed at by id and inspect it, but nothing let it change anything an environment shows. The maintainer accepted declared actions on referenced things, and asked for increment 1 to be built, with an example in the farmyard.

**Alternatives.** Declared actions on referenced things reuse the ids pointing and `inspect` already use, so "this" resolves with no new mechanism; a typed declaration lets Tomori check every call before the side sees it, and bound it; environment text stays in results, as data; and the side gets one server-made intent in the inbox it already reads, as for a chosen row. An environment's own MCP tools, through Tomori or beside it, would be more flexible, but would change the agent's tool list per space, while OpenCode registers tools per directory, put environment-written descriptions into the tool list the model reads as instructions, take arbitrary JSON Schemas Tomori cannot bound, and lose pointing ids and the space's requester checks. The agent driving a client's interface, pointing and selecting on someone's behalf, needs their device to be there, depends on layout and timing, and races with them. Natural-language requests to the environment could be checked by nothing, every environment would parse language, and text an injection can shape would reach the environment unchecked. Raw intents written by the agent have no discovery and no checks. Each of these was rejected.

**Inbox, not a hook.** `inspect` asks a hook on its own thread because reading must not wait for the side; an action changes the side's state, and the side's own loop is where it already applies participants' intents and choices, so delivering it there keeps one writer and one order, at the cost of the side having to answer, bounded by a time-out. A participant turning a selector and the agent setting the same terminal are applied one after the other, never interleaved.

**Harmless by default.** The first draft required `requested_by` for every action unless the environment opted out. The maintainer, November 2026, inverted it: "those actions are pretty harmless; there's no elevation of access or security, so we shouldn't raise the bar on them." So an action that changes only what the space shows needs no requester, and the environment, which alone knows what an action does, marks the ones that reach outside the space, cannot be undone or cost something, which need a checked owner or collaborator. Refusing harmless actions in a turn Tomori's notes began keeps timers and events from driving the environment through the coordinator; the maintainer asked that such work go to a worker instead, increment 4.

**Pointing as the words begin.** The maintainer judged the current pointing, resolved as the words begin, "good enough for the first iteration"; the agent matches "that region" against the declared choices from what was said.

**Choices by value.** A choice is given by its value, never its label, so that what the side is handed is exactly what it declared, and a label, written for people, can repeat or change; the agent matches what was said to a value through the labels.

**Delegated timers.** A periodic check run as a coordinator's turn every half hour would interrupt the conversation and fill its context; a worker does it beside the conversation and reports a line only when it matters, which the event queue already paces. Standing authorisation is tied to one participant's checked words, named actions, an expiry and the dial, so that a timer acts on what was asked without an injection in its task granting it more.

## Scope

Increment 1: `tomori-environment`'s `action.rs` (`Action`, `Parameter`, `Kind`, `Choice`, `check_actions`, `valid_action_name`, `Acted`, `Outcome` and the limits) and `lib.rs` (`Object::offering`, `Intent::action` and `acted`, `Space::declare_actions` and `answer`, `Inbox::act` and `outcome`, `Outlet::declare_actions`); `tomori-server`'s `channel.rs` (offers and actions kept, `Acts`), `server_sides.rs` (`ServerSides::act`), `space.rs` (offers named in the note), `hub.rs` (the outlet's declaration), `hub/provenance.rs` (`standing`, `HubState::given`), `hub/agent_inspect.rs` (`actions`), the new `hub/agent_actions.rs`, `hub/tools.rs` (`tomori-tools`), `mcp.rs` (`act`) and `server.rs` (`with_act_timeout`); the farmyard's `flock.rs` (`Flock::peck`) and `side.rs`; with the text of `environments.md`, `mcp.md`, `security.md` and `farmyard.md` that describes them. The protocol, the clients, the spectator and the renderer are untouched.

## Validation

For increment 1:

- Tests of `tomori-environment` check that actions, parameters, choices, what was acted and outcomes are what they were given; that `check_actions` takes a declaration at every limit and refuses one past each, badly named or repeated, saying why; that cleaning gives each text the most it may keep; that a client's intent is never an action; that actions wait in the inbox in order with intents, are dropped beyond it with no ticket, and are answered by ticket, before the server waits or while it does, an answer to one no longer waited for not taken; that an object offers what it names; and that an outlet made before actions keeps none.
- Tests of `tomori-server`'s channel check that offers are kept only by valid names, once, at most 8, and none without a reference, that only declared actions are offered, in the object's order, that a declaration is checked and cleaned, and that actions are held to their rate; of its server sides, that an action is handed only to its binding's side, at most 4 waiting, and nothing to a full inbox; of the space, that what is pointed at names the first few actions it offers within their bound; and of `agent_actions.rs`, that an action is told with each parameter and the values it takes, and that parameters are checked exactly.
- Hub tests (`hub_tests/actions.rs`), with a side that answers as each reference says, check through the MCP endpoint that nothing is taken before anyone spoke; that the note names what is pointed at offers; that `inspect` lists the actions; that a harmless action is taken without `requested_by` on a participant's words, reaching the side with their number and the parameters checked, and is answered done, refused with its reason cleaned, or not in time, each told to the agent and noted on the status wall; that a client's intent is never an action; that ids not here, of another environment, not ids, actions not offered, and parameters wrong, missing or not taken, too long ids and actions, missing arguments, a `requested_by` that is not text, and calls of no session or another's are refused without reaching the side; that a consequential action is refused without `requested_by`, or naming a visitor, and taken for an owner, as is a harmless one naming them; that an object no longer described is refused; that a declaration the server does not take, or from a binding that has ended, keeps nothing; that no action is taken in a turn a timer's note began, until a participant speaks again; that a harmless action is refused on words more than 3 minutes old, but not on words just under, on words a timer's note was carried with, until the next words, alone, after input that did not come through Tomori and after a lost connection, and that a consequential one is taken for a collaborator in the space and refused for one admitted elsewhere; that actions are held to 4 waiting and a full inbox, a side whose binding has ended handed nothing, none of which spends the allowance, and to their rate, kept with the space, which changing environment and back does not refill; - The farmyard's tests check that a hen on the ground pecks a bounded number of times and one up in the coop, on its ramp or running off cannot; that its side declares `peck`, which every hen offers, answers it done, announcing the change, or refused for a hen it does not have, and stops when its declaration is refused; and `tomori-server-app`'s `tests/farmyard_actions.rs`, through Tomori's own server and its MCP endpoint, that the note names each hen's id and that she offers to peck, that `inspect` lists `peck` with its parameter, that `act` has a hen on the ground peck, on Ada's words, answered by the farmyard and noted on the status wall, and that a parameter off its step is refused.
- Region coverage of everything added or changed is complete (`testing.md`).

## Open questions

- What a later stage of pointing would need, so that "change *this* to *that*" names both things: word-level timestamps from speech recognition aligned with a time-stamped history of each participant's pointing. The client already sends audio frames' timestamps; the streaming recogniser would need to report each token's time; the server would keep a few seconds of attention per participant and resolve "this" and "that" by time. `spaces.md` already anticipates a time window on `Resource` (#81).
- Whether `tomori_environment::Action` should be renamed, as an operation or a command, to keep it apart from the client's controller `Actions` in `tomori-scene`.
- Whether increment 3's chooser should be Tomori's, or whether environments should keep drawing their own selectors.
- For increment 4: whether only owners may set delegated timers; whether 7 days is the right longest standing authorisation; whether a worker should be able to project panels; and whether starting a child session through OpenCode's API works as expected.
