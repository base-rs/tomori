# Rendering Order

This describes how the scene's surfaces are ordered when they are drawn, so that what is nearer the eye is seen in front.

## Requirement

**Nearer is in front.** In each eye, whatever is nearer the eye is seen in front of whatever is farther, whatever order they are drawn in. This holds between the scene's opaque bodies, the swarm, workers, the forms of arrival's objects and the hand menu's tiles, the ray, the landing ring, and the controllers and hands, and the translucent surfaces with text on them: projection walls and panels, the outline of the slot a panel carried would take, the label on the ray, the hint beside a panel carried, the GitHub inbox and its cards, the status pill, the recording indicator, the `LIVE` badge, the right wrist's transcript, the hand menu's and arrival's labels, and workers' signs. The hand menu, cards, the recording indicator, the hint, the pill and the controllers cover a panel behind them; a panel covers any of them behind it. Text is drawn with the depth test like everything else: nothing is drawn as an overlay, always on top, except the teleport's blink, which darkens the whole view.

**How.** Opaque bodies are drawn first and write depth. Translucent surfaces are then drawn with the depth test, without writing depth, each blended over what is already behind it. Once a surface is drawn, its backing, the quad behind its text, is drawn again into depth alone, where it is at least 60% opaque (`occlusion::OCCLUDING`): whatever is behind it is then hidden even if it is drawn later, and whatever is in front of it still covers it. A surface fainter than that writes no depth, so that nothing vanishes behind what can barely be seen: a wall's board, a curved wall's slot boards, the outline of a slot, and a label or sign fading in or out. Arrival's labels, the hand menu's in a space, are drawn last, farthest first among themselves, so that nothing drawn after them needs their depth: they write none, sparing the headset a draw for each label in each eye. A surface fainter than 60% is blended over what is drawn before it, so it is drawn after what stands behind it, as below.

**Order between surfaces about as far away.** A backing's depth is written a little behind it, `occlusion::TIE`, about 2% of its distance for each metre of it: 4 mm at arm's length, 2 cm at 1 m and 11 cm at 2.3 m. Between surfaces closer in depth than that, the order they are drawn in decides, which the frame step chooses: the space's walls and floating panels, each wall with its panels, farthest first, each wall's panels after the wall and the outline of a slot over the panel in it; the label on the ray and then the hint beside a panel carried; the inbox and cards; then the pill, the recording indicator, the badge and the transcript; and arrival's labels, in a space the hand menu's, last. Workers' signs are drawn before all of these, ordered among themselves by where they overlap.

**One composition layer.** The headset draws everything into the one OpenXR projection layer it submits, both eyes' images with the scene's depth, and submits no quad layers: the compositor would draw layers in their order, not by depth, so a quad layer drawn over the projection layer would cover a hand in front of it. Text is kept sharp within that layer, by the glyph atlas and multisampling (see `text.md` and `desktop.md`). Moving panels into quad layers for sharpness would revise this requirement, and would need the hand menu and every other near surface in later layers, with depth submitted through `XR_KHR_composition_layer_depth` for the runtime to hide them by depth.

## Rationale

On a Quest 3 in November 2026, at `9738a1c`, the maintainer raised the hand menu in front of a projection panel and it looked behind it, and found the status pill's text drawn over a panel in front of the pill. Panels, labels and signs were translucent and wrote no depth, so between two of them the order they were drawn in decided, not their distance: the hand menu's labels were drawn before the panels, and the pill after the space's walls and panels. Writing each backing's depth once it is drawn makes distance decide, wherever a surface is, without sorting every surface of every kind against every other for each eye each frame; drawing the hand menu's labels last keeps them blending over the panels behind them. Glyphs and their blending are unchanged, so text is as sharp as before. The tie keeps the frame step's choices where surfaces are about as far away: a panel carried over a wall's slot stands as far away as the panel in it, nearly in its plane, and the hint below a panel carried faces the eyes, so that seen from above its top leans back under the panel's lower edge by about 5 cm at 2.3 m. The 60% threshold makes every panel's face, the label, the hint, the pill and workers' signs hide what is behind them, and leaves the walls' boards and the outline of a slot see-through.

## Scope

`tomori-render`'s `Renderer::render`, which the headset, the desktop window and the spectator share, with the pipelines of `ui_panel.rs`, `arrival_graphics.rs`, `workspace_graphics.rs` and `occlusion.rs`, and the headset's frame submission in `crates/client-quest/src/xr.rs`. An environment's scenery draws its own translucent surfaces, such as Sky's clouds, before the panels, and is not covered here.

## Validation

- `tomori-render`'s `what_is_nearer_covers_a_panel_behind_it_and_a_panel_covers_what_is_behind_it` draws, on lavapipe, in both eyes, the hand menu with a panel floating behind it and then in front of it, and the status pill with a panel behind it and then in front of it, and checks that where the two overlap the image shows the nearer one, in nearly every pixel; set `TOMORI_DEPTH_ORDER_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to see them. `occlusion.rs`'s test checks which backings hide what is behind them. The projection and carrying previews (`TOMORI_PROJECTION_PREVIEW`, `TOMORI_CARRY_HINT_PREVIEW`) still show the outline of a slot over the panel in it, a panel carried over a slot in front of the wall, and the hint whole below it.
- Inspect the pipelines: every translucent pipeline has the depth test on and depth writes off, every occluder writes depth with no colour, and only the blink compares `Always`. `crates/client-quest/src/xr.rs` submits one projection layer.
- On the headset: raise the left palm in front of a panel and confirm the menu is in front in both eyes; stand so that a floating panel is between you and the pill, and confirm the pill's text is hidden behind it; carry a panel over a wall's slot and confirm it, its hint and the slot's outline show as before.
