# Rendering Order

This describes how the scene's surfaces are ordered when they are drawn, so that what is nearer the eye is seen in front, and how what is drawn on them is drawn: text as text, and shapes as geometry.

## Requirement

**Nearer is in front.** In each eye, whatever is nearer the eye is seen in front of whatever is farther, whatever order they are drawn in. This holds between the scene's opaque bodies, the swarm, workers, the forms of arrival's objects and the hand menu's tiles, the ray, the landing ring, and the controllers and hands, and the translucent surfaces with text on them: projection walls and panels, the outline of the slot a panel carried would take, the label on the ray, the hint beside a panel carried, the GitHub inbox and its cards, the status pill, the recording indicator, the `LIVE` badge, the right wrist's transcript, the hand menu's and arrival's labels, and workers' signs. The hand menu, cards, the recording indicator, the hint, the pill and the controllers cover a panel behind them; a panel covers any of them behind it. Text is drawn with the depth test like everything else: nothing is drawn as an overlay, always on top, except the teleport's blink, which darkens the whole view.

**How.** Opaque bodies are drawn first and write depth. Translucent surfaces are then drawn with the depth test, without writing depth, each blended over what is already behind it. Once a surface is drawn, its backing, the quad behind its text, is drawn again into depth alone, where it is at least 60% opaque (`occlusion::OCCLUDING`): whatever is behind it is then hidden even if it is drawn later, and whatever is in front of it still covers it. Depth is written fragment by fragment, not quad by quad: a fragment of a backing less than 60% opaque, as its colour's alpha and its coverage of the atlas make it, is discarded, so that a rounded corner's outside, a glyph's margin, the outside of any shape from the shared primitives and an antialiased edge write no depth, though drawn in the same quad as what is opaque, and a translucent surface drawn after them behind them still shows there. Every pipeline that writes a translucent surface's depth, the panels' and the signs', does so. A surface fainter than that writes no depth, so that nothing vanishes behind what can barely be seen: a wall's board, a curved wall's slot boards, the outline of a slot, and a label or sign fading in or out. Arrival's labels, the hand menu's in a space, are drawn last, farthest first among themselves, so that nothing drawn after them needs their depth: they write none, sparing the headset a draw for each label in each eye. A surface fainter than 60% is blended over what is drawn before it, so it is drawn after what stands behind it, as below.

**Order between surfaces about as far away.** A backing's depth is written a little behind it, `occlusion::TIE`, about 2% of its distance for each metre of it: 4 mm at arm's length, 2 cm at 1 m and 11 cm at 2.3 m. Between surfaces closer in depth than that, the order they are drawn in decides, which the frame step chooses: the space's walls and floating panels, each wall with its panels, farthest first, each wall's panels after the wall and the outline of a slot over the panel in it; the label on the ray and then the hint beside a panel carried; the inbox and cards; then the pill, the recording indicator, the badge and the transcript; and arrival's labels, in a space the hand menu's, last. Workers' signs are ordered among themselves by where they overlap, as seen from each eye, and drawn among the space's walls and panels back to front from that eye: each list keeps its own order, and between the two the one whose next surface is farther from the eye, by where its middle is, goes first, so that a translucent wall behind a sign is drawn before the sign and a sign fading in front of a wall blends over it, and a sign behind a wall is drawn before the wall.

**Text is drawn as text; shapes as geometry, from shared primitives.** Every line, rule, box, frame, bar, tick, dot or other indicator that a panel, card, menu, hint, label or sign draws is drawn as a shape, from the primitives in `tomori_scene::shapes`, rather than as glyphs: a rule (`Shape::Rule`), a box, filled, rounded or a frame (`Shape::Rect`), a filled area that meets its neighbours exactly (`Shape::Fill`), a bar with its track (`Shape::Bar`), a dot (`Shape::Disc`), a ring (`Shape::Ring`) and a checkbox (`Shape::Check`). A shape is placed in font pixels, the units text is laid out in, so that layout mixes the two; layout that lays text out in cells marks a cell to be drawn as a shape (`Mark`), keeping a character that stands in for it, which the console prints, and a run of a line's cells along a row or down a column is drawn as one line. Shapes are drawn in the same mesh as their panel's text, by the same pipeline and shader, so that they add no draw call, are tested against depth as text is, and are drawn alike in both eyes. Each is drawn from distance fields in the glyph atlas, a square, a disc, a ring, a frame and a wedge, so that its edges are antialiased as a glyph's are, it is as bright all along, with no seam where its pieces meet, and a line thinner than a pixel, far away, keeps its ink as it moves rather than shimmering. Text keeps symbols that are text: an arrow or a bullet inline in a sentence, a tick beside a word, an icon that draws cleanly as one character, and box drawing in content, such as a code block's. The characters that may still draw a shape as text are listed, with why, in `crates/scene/tests/shapes_in_text.rs`, which fails for any other.

**One composition layer.** The headset draws everything into the one OpenXR projection layer it submits, both eyes' images with the scene's depth, and submits no quad layers: the compositor would draw layers in their order, not by depth, so a quad layer drawn over the projection layer would cover a hand in front of it. Text is kept sharp within that layer, by the glyph atlas and multisampling (see `text.md` and `desktop.md`). Moving panels into quad layers for sharpness would revise this requirement, and would need the hand menu and every other near surface in later layers, with depth submitted through `XR_KHR_composition_layer_depth` for the runtime to hide them by depth.

## Rationale

On a Quest 3 in November 2026, at `9738a1c`, the maintainer raised the hand menu in front of a projection panel and it looked behind it, and found the status pill's text drawn over a panel in front of the pill. Panels, labels and signs were translucent and wrote no depth, so between two of them the order they were drawn in decided, not their distance: the hand menu's labels were drawn before the panels, and the pill after the space's walls and panels. Writing each backing's depth once it is drawn makes distance decide, wherever a surface is, without sorting every surface of every kind against every other for each eye each frame; drawing the hand menu's labels last keeps them blending over the panels behind them. Glyphs and their blending are unchanged, so text is as sharp as before. The tie keeps the frame step's choices where surfaces are about as far away: a panel carried over a wall's slot stands as far away as the panel in it, nearly in its plane, and the hint below a panel carried faces the eyes, so that seen from above its top leans back under the panel's lower edge by about 5 cm at 2.3 m. The 60% threshold makes every panel's face, the label, the hint, the pill and workers' signs hide what is behind them, and leaves the walls' boards and the outline of a slot see-through.

In November 2026, on a Quest 3, the maintainer reported that "the worker signs' transparency is wrong": "where a sign has a rounded corner (the transparent part of its quad), it still writes depth, knocking out whatever is drawn after it behind it", so that with a wall behind a sign they could "see the fence (the background) through the wall, in a halo around the sign's perimeter". The 60% threshold had decided per surface whether a backing wrote depth, and a sign's plate then wrote it over the whole of each of its quads, its transparent rounded corners and margins with them, while the wall's translucent board, drawn after every sign, failed the depth test there. Deciding per fragment, by the same threshold, keeps what was opaque enough to hide what is behind it doing so, exactly, and leaves what is transparent see-through, for every shape and glyph rather than only signs. Drawing signs among the walls and panels by distance from each eye, rather than every sign first, also blends a translucent wall and a sign in the right order either way about; merging the two lists, each already in order, costs nothing per surface, where sorting every surface of every kind against every other would.

In November 2026 the maintainer, seeing seams in a table rule drawn from glyphs, agreed that shapes should be drawn as geometry throughout: a table's header rule and the lines between its columns were a translucent glyph a cell, each grown to join its neighbours, so that every join was brighter, which #247 fixed by drawing each run as one quad, and the maintainer asked that every line, box, bar, tick mark or indicator be drawn the same way, from primitives every panel, menu and card shares, so that they stay crisp and consistent as panels curve, scale, scroll and wrap. Rules, quote bars, the lines and rule of tables, checkboxes, status dots, scroll and time bars, speakers' bars, frames, and result cards' rule and signs' status symbols are now shapes. Thin lines are drawn across from one column of a wedge, a band that widens along its length, and frames from a square frame, so that the field is continuous across them: drawn from a square's two edges, a line at a distance lost and gained up to four times its light as it moved, as the samples the shader averages across a pixel read past where the two met.

## Scope

`tomori-render`'s `Renderer::render`, which the headset, the desktop window and the spectator share, with the pipelines of `ui_panel.rs`, `arrival_graphics.rs`, `workspace_graphics.rs` and `occlusion.rs`, and the headset's frame submission in `crates/client-quest/src/xr.rs`; and the shapes every surface draws besides its text: `shapes` and `canvas` in `tomori-scene`, the shape tiles of `tomori-text`'s atlas, and the panels and signs that draw them in `tomori-render`. An environment's scenery draws its own translucent surfaces, such as Sky's clouds, before the panels, and is not covered here.

## Validation

- `tomori-render`'s `what_is_nearer_covers_a_panel_behind_it_and_a_panel_covers_what_is_behind_it` draws, on lavapipe, in both eyes, the hand menu with a panel floating behind it and then in front of it, and the status pill with a panel behind it and then in front of it, and checks that where the two overlap the image shows the nearer one, in nearly every pixel; set `TOMORI_DEPTH_ORDER_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to see them. `occlusion.rs`'s test checks which backings hide what is behind them. `workspace_graphics/depth_writes.rs` draws, on lavapipe, a sign's plate, a glyph's quad and a rounded box from the shared shapes in front of a magenta background, each before a translucent wall drawn after it between them, and checks that wherever its quads reach but it does not cover, the wall shows over the background, where every quad written whole showed the bare background in a halo, and that where it is opaque it still hides the wall; and a sign before a translucent wall in front of a magenta background, through the renderer, its rounded corner showing the wall as beside it, its plate hiding the wall, and fading, blending over the wall rather than under it. Set `TOMORI_DEPTH_WRITES_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to see the first, with quads written whole beside each, and `TOMORI_SIGN_CORNER_PREVIEW` the second. The projection and carrying previews (`TOMORI_PROJECTION_PREVIEW`, `TOMORI_CARRY_HINT_PREVIEW`) still show the outline of a slot over the panel in it, a panel carried over a slot in front of the wall, and the hint whole below it.
- Inspect the pipelines: every translucent pipeline has the depth test on and depth writes off, every occluder writes depth with no colour, discarding fragments less than `occlusion::OCCLUDING` opaque, and only the blink compares `Always`. `crates/client-quest/src/xr.rs` submits one projection layer.
- `tomori-render`'s `ui_panel::tests::shapes` draws, on lavapipe, a rule, a quote's bar down rows that wrap, a speaker's bar, a bar of time used and a scroll bar, and checks that each is as bright all along, within a tenth, with no gap; that checkboxes are hollow square frames as bright on every side, crisp across their edges, and filled when ticked with a dark tick; that a thin rule and a frame far away keep their ink within a tenth and three twentieths as they move by eighths of a pixel; and that rules on panels in three neighbouring slots of the curved Tomori wall lie along each slot with no seam. Set `TOMORI_SHAPES_PREVIEW`, `TOMORI_CHECKBOX_PREVIEW` and `TOMORI_CURVED_RULE_PREVIEW` to PNG paths to see them; the table's (`TOMORI_TABLE_RULE_PREVIEW`) is checked as before. `tomori-scene`'s `shapes` tests check each primitive's pieces against the atlas's fields, and `crates/scene/tests/shapes_in_text.rs` that no other shape is drawn as text.
- On the headset: confirm that rules, tables' lines, quote bars, checkboxes, the pill's dot and the scroll bar look as they did or crisper, and are steady from across the room.
- On the headset: raise the left palm in front of a panel and confirm the menu is in front in both eyes; stand so that a floating panel is between you and the pill, and confirm the pill's text is hidden behind it; carry a panel over a wall's slot and confirm it, its hint and the slot's outline show as before.
