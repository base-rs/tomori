# The Farmyard

This describes the farmyard environment, `farmyard`, in crates of its own, `tomori-environment-farmyard` and `tomori-environment-farmyard-client`: an evening yard with a chicken coop, which participants walk about by teleporting. It follows the common and client convention of `environments.md`, is built as another project would build an environment, from Tomori's published crates and their extension points, and is the proof that a downstream environment crate can customise everything about its environment without changing Tomori.

## Requirement

**Offered by Tomori's own programs.** Tomori's own server program, `tomori-server` in `tomori-server-app`, offers the farmyard, labelled Farmyard, after Sky and the grid, at the identity in its common crate, `tomori_environment_farmyard::ID`, `VERSION` (4: raised from 1 to 2 when it gained an arrival of its own, so that an earlier client, which would draw it uncarried, shows the grid, to 3 when its wall moved onto its notice board, so that an earlier client, which draws no board, shows the grid rather than a wall standing where its board should be, and to 4 when the agent's home moved to its workbench, so that an earlier client, which draws no bench, shows the grid rather than the agent and its workers floating in an empty corner) and `LABEL`; and Tomori's own app, the headset, desktop window and console, and its spectator present it, so an owner can create a space in it, or change a space to it, from the Space section of the hand menu (see `environments.md`). Each registers it explicitly, as it registers Sky: the server with `Builder::environment`, the app with `tomori::Builder::register(tomori_environment_farmyard_client::register)` and the spectator by calling it on its sceneries. The core's builders start empty, as for every environment, so a build of another project's offers or presents the farmyard only if it registers it the same way.

**Its own crates.** Everything about the farmyard is in its own crates: `tomori-environment-farmyard`, its common crate, what its server and its clients share, building no renderer or GPU stack: its identity, its layout, its coop and yard models, the workbench among them, built in code as `tomori-assets` models with that crate's GPU-free core, the chicken's asset, its animator graph and its `ChickenController`, the chickens' behaviours, the flock, the encoding of its state and intents, and its server side, `FarmyardSide`; and `tomori-environment-farmyard-client`, its client half: its scenery, drawn with `tomori-assets`' renderer, the hens skinned on the GPU, its teleport profile, and its client side, which plays the chickens' clucks. It has no server crate: what `environments.md` puts in one is what must never reach a client, such as credentials, data sources and the options that configure them, and the farmyard has none. Its server side is the flock's simulation, which every client replays with the same functions (`Behaviour::place`, the controller and the animator), the validation of its intents, and their limits, none of which is secret, and it builds only on `tomori-environment`'s interface, not on `tomori-server`; so it is kept beside what it shares, and a server crate of its own would hold no more than its registration. Should the farmyard gain something server-only, it moves into `tomori-environment-farmyard-server`. Tomori's own crates provide only the extension points it builds on: `tomori-assets`' models, renderer and animator (see `assets.md` and `animation.md`), `tomori-render`'s `Scenery` and `Sceneries`, `tomori-scene`'s locomotion profiles and client sides, and the builders' registration. Nothing in Tomori's core crates knows about chickens or coops, as the dependency rule in `bake/tests/dependencies.rs` checks, naming the farmyard's crates among those it must find; where the farmyard needs a hook that Tomori lacks, the hook is added in general, for any environment, and its pull request says so.

**The yard.** In the yard's own coordinates, the gate on the +Z side and the coop towards −Z, which Tomori carries into a space's so that where participants arrive is home (see `environments.md`): a fence of weathered posts and rails around a yard 10 m wide and 12.6 m deep, with a gate on its +Z side between two taller posts, one with a lantern; packed earth inside, worn along a path to the coop, and meadow beyond, with pines around and the backdrop beyond them (below). In the yard: the coop at the back left; a water trough on the right; a round feeder with grain scattered about it in the middle; hay bales stacked in the back right corner, with loose straw; and the workbench in the front left corner, below. `layout.rs` holds where each is, which the scenery, the teleport profile and the chickens share. **The notice board.** A notice board stands against the right of the fence, short of the trough, square to the fence and facing into the yard: a wooden back on two dark posts, framed in darker wood, with a weathered plank across its top and a little lean-to roof over it, sloping down towards the yard, drawn with the yard (`props.rs`). The farmyard's own projection wall, which Tomori's own server registers with it as `tomori_server_app::farmyard_noticeboard`, two standard slots side by side and one high, about 1.3 by 0.5 m, stands on its back, just in front of it, a little below eye height, the wood framing it (`layout::NOTICEBOARD`, `NOTICEBOARD_FACING`, `NOTICEBOARD_GRID`, `NOTICEBOARD_BACK` and `NOTICEBOARD_POSTS`, see `projection.md`), so that what the agent projects onto it for whoever asks from that side of the yard, and what participants put on it, reads as notices on the farm's board. It is locked until a space's owner unlocks it, as every environment's own wall is; unlocked, it may be moved for the space, and the board stays where it is. Nobody lands on it and nothing walks through it: its footprint is one of the yard's obstacles and solids, and the earth participants land on stops short of it. It is about 4.7 m from where participants arrive, to their right, and read from the earth in front of it.

**Arriving, the agent and the status wall.** The farmyard sets two of the homes `environments.md` describes, which Tomori's own server registers with it, as `layout.rs` gives them: participants arrive inside the gate, 2.4 m in from the yard's middle line and 0.6 m to its right (`layout::ARRIVAL`), facing the coop's front corner beside its ramp (`layout::ARRIVAL_FACING`); the agent's home is at its workbench, above the middle of the bench's curve, at 1.5 m, about 3.5 m from the eyes where participants arrive, facing them and out of the corner (`layout::AGENT_HOME`), about 90° to their left as they arrive. **Its workers hang about the agent's home in a ring**, its `home` cluster anchor (`tomori_server_app::farmyard_home`), at the agent's home, standing in `Formation::Ring` over 300° of it (`layout::HOME_SWEEP`), centred on the way the home faces, out of the corner towards the arrival, so that none stands straight behind the agent, hidden by the swarm as seen from the arrival: on the ring drawn on the earth there, 1.4 m about the swarm, the sides first as seen from the arrival, six to a row, a row above as more come, twelve at full size, then compact, ten to a row in three rows, none above 2.5 m, those behind the agent over the workbench (below); the ring stays there, a little dimmer, while the agent is away (see `environments.md`). It gives the ring no room, so that a client that knows no ring stands them on today's arc about the arrival, which fits the yard too. The layout's tests check, with `tomori_environment::sightlines::fits`, that one to eighteen stand on the ring clear of the workbench, its rack and the yard's other solids, and seen from the arrival. **Its notice board is its status wall** (`Environment::with_status_wall(StatusWall::own(0))`, see `projection.md`): a farm's notices go on its board, and no second board stands beneath the agent. The board keeps its lock, locked by default, and its own edits, while Tomori's notices and the space's other status panels share its two slots with the board's own panels; it stands beyond reading distance from the arrival, facing into the yard, so a notice there is read by walking up to it, which suits notices, since what matters is said aloud. Its version is unchanged by which wall holds the status panels: that is the projection's, which every client draws as sent. The layout's tests check, with `tomori_environment::sightlines`, that the agent is seen from the arrival, clear of the yard's solids (`layout::solids`), and that the space's own wall would read beneath the agent, were it shown.

**The workbench, the agent's home.** In the yard's front left corner, where the fence by the gate meets its left side, on the coop's side of the yard, stands a wooden workbench curving through 100° about the middle of its curve, its concave side facing out of the corner towards where participants arrive, so that the agent's home, above that middle, stands at the heart of a workshop, the bench and its tools behind it, and its workers about it: the abstraction of an agent at its bench, its sub-agents gathered round, made visible. The bench's top, 0.9 m up, is 1.65 m to 2.3 m from that middle, on four pairs of dark legs, with a shelf low down, a vice at its front and, on its top, no higher than 0.97 m (`WORKBENCH_CLUTTER`), a plank, a box of nails, a mallet and shavings; behind it, a curved rack of weathered boards between two posts, capped, 1.9 m tall and 2.34 m to 2.42 m from the middle, with a saw, a hammer, a wrench, three chisels and a square hung on pegs, and a lantern on the post at the gate's end, whose warm light is the yard's third lamp. Its rack stands 8 cm inside the fence on the left, where the curve passes square to it, and at the gate's end. It is drawn with the yard (`workbench.rs`), low-poly in the yard's palette, under a thousand triangles (943, its lantern's glass included), and laid out by `layout::WORKBENCH`, `workbench_facing`, `on_workbench` and the constants beside them, from which its solids and footprints come too: its top and what lies on it as five solids as high as its clutter, and its rack as twelve, one for each of its boards, as tall as its posts, so that they follow its curve closely enough for the workers before it to stand clear; nobody lands on it, the chickens walk round it, and the agent, walls and panels keep clear of it. Its workers stand in the ring the home anchor gives, 1.4 m about the agent: at full size, of each row's six, the two behind the agent as seen from the arrival hover over the bench, either side of its middle, which the agent stands before, above what lies on it and in front of its rack; compact, of each row's ten, the four behind it hover over the bench and by its ends; and the rest stand in the yard about the agent, all clear of the fence and the lantern; the ring drawn on the floor runs just inside the bench's front edge, so that the bench's curve is the back of the ring. The arrival is unchanged, so that walls and panels kept in a farmyard space stay where they were in the yard. See the crate's `context/workbench.md`.

**The coop.** A red hen house with white trim, raised on four legs, its floor 0.6 m up, under a pitched roof that overhangs every side, with its little door facing home at the top of a cleated ramp, the door swung open beside it, a lantern on a bracket by the door, and a window in its side towards the middle of the yard, through which participants peek in at the straw, two nest boxes along the back and a perch, in the shade of a dark lining. It is a model of its own, in its own space, built in code (`coop.rs`) and placed on the layout's footprint; a coop modelled in Blender replaces it through the same loader as every asset, `Coop::Asset`, with the built one standing in if it cannot be loaded (see the crate's `context/coop.md`).

**The look.** Low-poly, built in code, like Sky's shrine: every face flat, its colour varied a little face by face. A moonlit evening: a deep blue sky with a dusky band at the horizon and the last warmth of sunset low in the west, stars overhead and a moon rising in the east; the moon's cool light and the sky's and ground's ambient light on everything, and the lanterns' warm light falling off within a few metres, their glass glowing; haze over the far meadow. The scenery grows out of a space through the reveal sphere, as every scenery does.

**The backdrop.** Beyond the fence, a New Zealand evening, in the same low-poly style, palette and light: pasture rising to bush-clad foothills that ring the yard, from 11.5 m to 37.5 m from its middle, low enough that the ranges show above them; a waterfall falling 5.5 m down a cliff in the foothills behind the coop, to its right as participants arrive, into a pool, which feeds a river winding downhill through grey shingle towards the yard and round its right side, at least 7.5 m beyond the fence, and away into the foothills behind a spur; native bush, as instances of five models, the bush's canopy on the foothills, podocarps (rimu and kahikatea) standing above it, tree ferns (ponga and mamaku), each a parasol of arching fronds over a skirt of dead ones, and flax (harakeke) by the water, and cabbage trees (tī kōuka) on the pasture, placed deterministically, so that every client draws the same bush; and, beyond the far plane, three ranges ringing the horizon, bluer and fading further into the sky's colour with distance, the farthest highest, its peaks under snow, the highest behind the waterfall and under the moon. Everything within reach stands within the 50 m far plane from anywhere participants land, and the ranges are drawn at the sky's depth, so they never move with the eyes and nothing nearer is hidden by them. The water moves by the time alone, ripples carried downstream, streaks down the fall, which curls over its lip, and foam and mist at its foot, in a shader of the farmyard's own. Nothing beyond the fence is walkable, nothing of it stands within 2.5 m of the fence, no plant within 3 m nor in the way in through the gate, and nothing stands in the views from the arrival to the waterfall, its pool and the river, nor from the workbench's curve to the river. The layout names where its sounds come from, for ambient sound to attach to: `layout::sounds()`, the `waterfall` at the middle of its fall, the `pool`, the `river` at each of its points and each `bush` patch, with `WATERFALL`, `POOL`, `RIVER`, `BUSH` and `nearest_river`. It stays modest on the Quest: under 20,000 triangles (`backdrop::BUDGET`; 19,483 today: the foothills 2,880, the plants 15,222, the ranges 1,184 and the water 197), in eight draws an eye, all opaque, instanced and uploaded once, with only a uniform written each frame; on a desktop GPU it added about a third to the scenery's cost an eye (0.17 to 0.24 ms at the Quest 3's eye size with 4× multisampling), which the headset trial is to confirm. Its version is unchanged, since an earlier client draws the yard as it did, without the backdrop. See the crate's `context/backdrop.md`.

**Walking the yard.** The farmyard declares a teleport profile, beside its scenery, as `locomotion.md` describes: participants land on the yard's packed earth, clear of the coop, its ramp, the trough, the feeder, the hay, the notice board and the workbench, on six rectangles at the ground's height, which include where participants arrive, the spot beside the coop's window to peek in from and the ground beneath the agent's home, within the workbench's curve, and turn in snaps of 30°. It is the first of Tomori's own environments to allow travel.

**The chickens.** A flock of three to five hens wanders the yard, the chickens Hinoki Maple Williams modelled and animated, each the same model, one wearing a traffic cone. They walk, a step with each foot, as far as their feet carry them; peck at the ground; look about; now and then one goes up the ramp, in at the door and to a nest or the perch to roost, and comes out again a while later; and they keep clear of everything in the yard and of each other's paths. A participant who points at one confuses it: it turns to them, tilts its head with a questioning cluck and holds it while they point, and straightens it when they look away. One pointed at for 4 seconds, or by two participants at once, is startled: it squawks, spins round and sprints a metre and a quarter away, the clearest way it can, at most once a minute. A chicken on the ramp, in the coop or running cannot be pointed at. Now and then a chicken clucks of its own accord. Each has a name, and the agent is told, when a participant points at one as they talk, who she is, whether she wears the cone, what she is doing and where, as `Ada: [Pointing at Pip, wearing a traffic cone, a hen in the farmyard, pecking at the ground by the feeder (id farmyard:hen/pip)] what's she eating?`. Each hen's id is her name, `hen/` and her name in lower case, which no two of a flock share and which stays hers for as long as the space is in the farmyard; and the agent may inspect it (see `environments.md`) for her name, what she is doing, the nearest landmark, where she is to the centimetre and which way she faces, whether she wears the cone, her pace, whether she roosts in the first nest, the second or on the perch, where her home is, how many point at her, and how long ago she was last startled. The farmyard's server side answers from the flock it owns, which it shares with its inspector behind a lock it holds only while it moves the flock on, so an answer is the flock as it is then. The coop, the feeder and the rest of the yard are scenery that cannot be pointed at, so they have no ids.

**Model, view and controller.** The chickens follow `animation.md`'s split:

- **The model** is the chicken's asset, `chicken.glb`, and what each chicken does, which the farmyard's server side owns. A chicken's state is a `Behaviour`: when it began, on the farmyard's clock, where the chicken was and which way it faced, and what it does: stand, peck until a moment, look to a side until a moment, walk to a point, be confused by someone at a place, recover, sprint a way, or roost. Where that puts the chicken at any later moment is computed by one function, `Behaviour::place`, from the walk's and sprint's root motion, on the server to plan and on every client to draw, so that the server never streams where a chicken is.
- **The view** is an instance of the chicken's model, its cone shown or hidden, drawn with the yard and the coop by the farmyard's scenery, every chicken in one draw for each of its two meshes.
- **The controller**, `ChickenController`, is the farmyard's own, and deliberately specific: it sets the chicken's animator's parameters, `walking`, `confused` and `startle`, at the moments each behaviour says, including when a walk turns and stops, places the instance where the behaviour puts it, leaning as it pecks, and hears the animator's events, the cluck as it tilts its head and the squawk as it sprints. The animator's graph is the farmyard's own too: standing, the walk looping at the chicken's own pace, the tilt, a held tilt that sways, straightening, and the sprint, cross-fading between them.

**The server side.** The farmyard's server side, `FarmyardSide`, in its common crate, which Tomori's own server registers with the farmyard through `Environment::with_server_side`, runs the flock of each space bound to the farmyard, from the space's identifier, so that a space's flock has the same chickens, names and paces each time it starts. Its clock is the time since it started, in milliseconds. Every 50 ms, or as an intent arrives, it moves the flock on: each chicken whose behaviour has ended begins its next at the moment the last ended, so that it stops and starts where its feet are down. It shares the flock's state, with its clock, for whoever joins, and announces each change as an event with the state it leaves; and it describes every chicken to the agent once a second, where she stands then (`Object::at`), so that the agent can go to her when asked. The chicken is loaded from the program's assets, once, as clients load it, so that both agree on how far it goes; a server that cannot load it shows the side's error in the space.

**Intents.** A participant's client asks the side to `notice` a chicken, from where they stand, as they begin pointing at it, and says they look `away` as they stop. The side validates each against the flock: a chicken it has, on the ground and not running; a place that is a number, taken as at most 50 m away; at most once in 6 seconds from each participant for each chicken, and once in 1.5 seconds from each for any, so that sweeping the ray across the flock does not set them all clucking; and looking away only from a chicken they point at. A chicken's reaction begins 150 ms after the server takes it, so that every client has it before it shows. A chicken nobody has said they looked away from recovers after 8 seconds of its own accord.

**The clock.** Each message says the farmyard's clock as it was sent, so a client knows it to within the time a message takes to arrive: a state sets it, and an event that arrives later than the clock says moves it on. Clucks of the chickens' own accord are drawn from the space, the chicken and each 30 seconds of the clock, the same for everyone; a client hears only the clucks and reactions of the last second, so that joining, or a clock that jumps, clucks nothing.

**The client side.** The farmyard's client side is a `ClientSide`, registered with its scenery: it keeps a `ChickenController` for each chicken the server side shares, moves them on by the clock, picks the chicken the ray meets first, by a sphere about its body, named by her name, asks the side to notice it and looks away again, sends the clucks and squawks its controllers hear as ambient sounds from each chicken's head, and gives its scenery the chickens to draw.

**Its ambience.** The farmyard sounds an evening in the foothills beneath the conversation, an ambience its client crate declares and registers with its scenery (`ambience.rs`; see `environments.md` and `spatial-audio.md`), each sound a slot, its file `crates/environment-farmyard-client/assets/sounds/SLOT.ogg`: two beds, `wind`, at −8 dB, and `bush`, distant bush at dusk, at −10 dB; two loops, the `river`, from the `pool` along each `river` point of `layout::sounds()`, heard within 35 m, at +5 dB, and the `waterfall`, from the middle of its fall, heard within 60 m, at +8 dB, both heard from the arrival; and four calls of native birds from the canopy, 6 m above every `bush` patch's middle, within 5 m of it across the ground: a tūī, `tui-1` to `tui-3`, every 25 to 70 s, at +14 to +18 dB; a bellbird, `bellbird-1` to `bellbird-3`, every 30 to 90 s, at +13 to +17 dB; a kererū's wingbeats, `kereru-1` and `kereru-2`, every 90 to 240 s, at +12 to +17 dB; and a ruru, `ruru-1` and `ruru-2`, every 120 to 300 s, rarely, as one owl would call, though the farmyard is always evening, at +13 to +17 dB; each at a pitch a few per cent either side of its own. **Its levels**, standing at the arrival or at the workbench: the beds together are a quiet bed, about −39 LUFS at the default ambience level, low; the river and the waterfall are heard softly, 4 to 6 dB beneath the beds, louder towards the fence nearest each, yet never above the beds; and the birds, usually 1 to 3 dB above the beds, never more than 12 dB above them from where they call nearest, nor more than 8 dB beneath them from furthest. Distance takes about 17 dB from the river, 19 dB from the waterfall and 21 dB from a bird in the canopy where participants stand, which their gains make up for. That is four of the ambience's eight voices for the beds and loops, and four for the birds. The folder's `readme.md` says what each slot should sound like, how long it is, its format and loudness, and its `LICENSE.md` records each sound's author, source, licence and changes, which the client crate adds to a client's licences, as it does the chicken's. Its build finds every `SLOT.ogg` there, by its digest, at most 1 MiB each and 6 MiB together, refusing the build otherwise, embedded everywhere but on Android, where the APK carries the folder as `assets/farmyard/sounds/`; a slot without a file is silent, so that a sound is added by dropping its file in. Every slot has its sound: the tūī, bellbird and kererū from the Department of Conservation (NZ), and the ruru by eklemp, from iNaturalist, under CC BY 4.0, which makes the client crate `MIT AND CC-BY-4.0`, as the chicken does its common crate, and the wind, bush, river and waterfall from Freesound under CC0 1.0; 2.5 MiB together. Its version is unchanged: nothing of it is exchanged.

**The chicken's asset.** `crates/environment-farmyard/assets/models/chicken.glb`, named in code by its digest, embedded everywhere but on Android, where the APK carries it as `assets/farmyard/chicken.glb`, is exported from its author's three `.blend`s, kept in `assets/source`, by the export script there, with Blender in background mode and `--disable-autoexec`; the export is deterministic. Its feet are its last leg bones, and each foot's sole is the lowest point of the mesh that moves with that foot; the walk's root motion counts as planted the feet within 2% of the chicken's height of the lowest, which carries it about 8 cm a loop of a second and a half, rocking back a little as each foot comes down, and the sprint's counts every foot, as it scrambles, which carries it about 1.24 m. The chicken stands on its feet, 0.4 m tall at the top of its walk, cone and all. It is by Hinoki Maple Williams, and is licensed, with its `.blend` sources, under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0), as `assets/models/LICENSE.md` says, which credits her, links the licence and says that the export changed nothing but the format. That notice is the credit the licence asks for wherever the chicken goes: the farmyard's client crate adds it to a client's sceneries as it registers, `tomori --licenses` shows it, and the APK carries it as `assets/farmyard/LICENSE.md`. The crate as a whole is `MIT AND CC-BY-4.0`.

**What is the farmyard's and what is Tomori's.** The farmyard needed these general hooks, which any environment can use: the environment state channel (`environments.md`); `tomori-assets`' loading, model instances, skinned drawing and animator (`assets.md`, `animation.md`); a client side, `ClientSide`, registered with `Sceneries::set_client_side`, driven by the frame step, picking what the ray meets as `Resource::object` and drawing through `Scenery::animate`; ambient sounds, `Feedback::ambient`, played by the mixer at a place in the scene (`feedback.md`, `spatial-audio.md`); an ambience, `Sceneries::set_ambience`, played by the player on its ambience bus (`environments.md`, `spatial-audio.md`); licence notices, `Sceneries::add_license`, which `tomori --licenses` shows; and Tomori's own APK's `assets` folder, `android/assets`, read by an `ApkAssets` source. Everything about chickens, flocks, coops and clucks, their state's encoding and their intents, is in the farmyard's crates, and nothing in Tomori's core crates names them, but for the link to its models in Tomori's own APK's `android/assets`, which the app's packaging carries as it registers the farmyard; a downstream environment could do the same without changing Tomori.

## Rationale

In November 2026 the maintainer asked for a farmyard environment to give the chickens that their daughter modelled, animated and agreed to make public, an official home, and to use it to exercise and grow the environment framework: a scenery built procedurally in low-poly code to match Sky's shrine, with a fenced yard of grass and packed earth, a coop with a ramp and a door that is a separate model their daughter can later replace with her own, a little hay, a water trough and a feed area, and a cosy, consistent palette, a moonlit or warm evening. The maintainer then set the principle that the point of a dedicated environment is the ability to customise everything about it, so everything of the farmyard's is in its own crates, built on general extension points, and Tomori's own programs register it as another project's would. Once the core crates started empty (#200, #201), the farmyard was reworked onto the convention they set: its identity moved from `tomori-protocol` into its common crate, and its registration from the core's default builders into Tomori's own programs. The coop's door faces home, so that arriving participants see it and the chickens' comings and goings; its window faces the yard's middle, so that peeking in needs no climbing. Lamps light through walls, as nothing casts shadows yet, so the inside of the coop is lined dark to stay in the shade.

The chickens were first an animated scene in Sky, baked at build time; the maintainer asked for them to move to a farmyard of their own, their behaviour owned by the server and their motion by a general, deterministic animator, so that everyone in a space sees the same chickens doing the same thing, and so that the farmyard exercises everything an environment needs. The server sends what a chicken does and since when, rather than where it is each frame, because the animator and root motion compute the rest the same everywhere, and because a chicken's behaviour changes a few times a minute while it moves every frame. Startling is rare, held for 4 seconds or by two at once and once a minute, so that pointing at a chicken to talk about it mostly gets a puzzled look, and the limits on notices keep a sweep of the ray from being a flood. Clucks of their own accord are drawn from the clock rather than sent, as they change nothing. The walk's stride differs from the first bake's, 8.3 rather than 6.8 cm a loop, because each foot's sole, rather than its bone, is what is planted now; it is to be checked on the headset for sliding.

In November 2026, trying the farmyard on a Quest 3, the maintainer found its wall, three by two, could not be moved, and it felt broken; they asked for environment walls to be locked by default and visibly part of the place, the farmyard's becoming a wall two slots wide and one high on a notice board with posts and a little roof, in the farmyard's simple style (see `projection.md`). It moved from the middle of the yard's right side, where its posts would have stood in the earth participants land on, to the fence, as a farm's notice board stands, facing into the yard rather than turned towards the arrival, as a board on a fence is.

In November 2026 the maintainer decided that the Tomori wall, the agent and participants' arrival each have a fixed home the environment sets, rather than the wall following the agent, and asked for the farmyard to be the worked example: a sensible agent spot by the coop, not inside it, a Tomori wall home near the arrival, and an arrival point (see `environments.md`). Participants now arrive a little way into the yard, turned towards the coop, so that the agent beside it, its workers about it and the Tomori wall to their right are all in view, and the gate is behind them.

In November 2026, trying the final build of #262, the maintainer expected the Tomori wall underneath the agent, as before environments set its home, and the farmyard's wall stood ahead and to the right of the arrival; the farmyard now gives no home of its own for the wall, which stands beneath the agent by the coop, as Tomori's default puts it. Its version is unchanged, since the server stands the Tomori wall for every client, which draws it wherever it is sent, and nothing the farmyard's own crates draw moved.

In November 2026, approving the proposal that the agent follows the speaker (`docs/proposals/agent-follows-the-speaker.md`), the maintainer asked that the farmyard's notice board become its status wall, rather than the Tomori wall beneath the agent: a place that already has a notice board should not grow a second one beside it, while the space's notices still go with the space to the next environment's status wall. The board is not readable from the arrival; what matters is said aloud, the newest panel is framed in blue and a pairing waiting is also on the owners' pill and hand menu, and whether a distant status wall should give a quiet cue when a notice arrives is left open.

In November 2026, speaking from the headset in the farmyard, sitting in its front left corner, the maintainer asked for the agent's home to be "a curved workbench, like a wooden workbench with some tools or a tool rack, oriented in the corner, with your home sort of oriented out, and then the sub-agents hanging around that workbench", to "nail home the abstraction and the concept". The bench takes that corner, the one nearest where they sat, and its curve is centred on the agent's home and faces where participants arrive, so that the ring's places behind the agent, as seen from the arrival, stand over the bench, but for the one straight behind it, which the ring's 300° sweep leaves out, as the swarm would hide it: the bench's curve is the back of the ring, rather than the ring drawn as an arc before it, which would leave the workers no longer about the agent. The ring is a fixed 1.4 m, so the bench stands just beyond it, and its rack beyond the reach of the signs above it; the rack's solids follow its boards, as one solid for the whole curve would take in the agent and its workers. The arrival stays where it was, about 90° to the bench's right as one arrives, rather than turning towards it, since walls and panels a space keeps in the farmyard are kept in the space's coordinates, about the arrival, and would move in the yard were it turned; whether to turn it is for the headset trial. No named anchor stands at the bench beside `home`, such as `workbench`, since panels or workers gathered there would stand among the home's workers.

In November 2026, from the headset, the maintainer asked for ambient sounds, "New Zealand birds, wind, water, that kind of stuff", which they would find samples for, careful that they never overload conversations (see `spatial-audio.md`). The farmyard's slots follow its backdrop: its bush, its river and its waterfall, and the birds of such bush at dusk, the tūī and bellbird singing, the kererū flying over and the ruru calling as night falls; the sounds live in the client crate, since no server needs them, and the build finds them, so that a sound is added by dropping in a file, without code, its digest still checked as it loads. Its sounds' licences are each sound's own, recorded beside them. The first sounds were found from CC0 and CC BY sources only, since a CC BY-NC sound cannot be shared with Tomori and a CC BY-SA one would bind its changes; a review found that, by distance alone, the birds and the waterfall would be 20 to 25 dB beneath the wind and bush, so the placed sounds were raised and the beds lowered until the birds were a little above the beds where participants stand, which a test checks, to be confirmed by ear in the headset.

In November 2026, from the headset, the maintainer asked for an opportunity to make the space their own: "I would love to put some mountains in the background, especially New Zealand-style bush", keeping the straightforward geometry, "but maybe a river or some waterfalls". The backdrop keeps the farmyard's low-poly look and evening, and is drawn cheaply for the Quest: the foothills and bush are models within the far plane, instanced, and the ranges, which must look far beyond it, are directions drawn at the sky's depth rather than distant geometry, so they need no larger far plane and do not shift as one walks the yard. The water moves in a shader of its own rather than through a texture, which the yard's renderer has no way to scroll. The pines stay, as a farm's shelterbelt would, but none stands in the water or before the waterfall. Ambient sound is a separate piece, which attaches to the places the layout names.

## Scope

`crates/environment-farmyard`: its identity in `lib.rs`, `layout.rs`, `shapes.rs`, `props.rs`, `workbench.rs`, `backdrop.rs`, `coop.rs`, `chicken.rs`, `behaviour.rs`, `controller.rs`, `cluck.rs`, `flock.rs`, `side.rs` and `assets/`; `crates/environment-farmyard-client`: `scenery.rs`, `sky.wgsl`, `water.wgsl`, `client.rs`, `ambience.rs`, its sounds in `assets/sounds`, found by `build.rs`, and its registration in `lib.rs`; its registration in `tomori-server-app`'s `builder`, with its server side, `tomori-client-quest`'s `app` and `tomori-client-spectator`'s `app`; and the APK's `android/assets/farmyard` folder, of links to its models and its sounds.
