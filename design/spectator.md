# Spectator View

## Requirement

Spectator output shows the scene without a head-locked HUD, including status captions and recording indicators, and plays no sound cues (see `feedback.md`), and, unless asked, no environment's ambience or ambient sounds, so that its audience hears the conversation (see `spatial-audio.md`). This applies to windowed, PipeWire and recorded output; the headset retains its own HUD. Connection and follow status may still be logged.

**One frame step.** The spectator assembles each frame with `tomori-scene`'s frame step, the one the headset and the desktop window run, in its watcher mode, `Step::watch`, and draws the frame it returns with `tomori_render::scene_graphics::draw`, as they draw theirs, rather than deciding for itself what a frame holds. A watcher sees from a camera the spectator gives it, the followed participant's head or the neutral view below, and senses nothing else: no rays, triggers, grips, sticks, palms, controllers or hands, so that nothing is pointed at, hovered, chosen or carried, and it holds no rights over walls, whatever its role. For the same space state, seen from the same place, a watcher's frame holds everything a participant's does, but for the elements that are a participant's own, `tomori_scene::scene::PERSONAL`: the attractor and whether it attracts, arrival's objects, with the lobby, the hand menu and the rays among them, the status pill, the recording indicator, the wrist's transcript, the presence a participant reports, and the teleport landing, its blink and the pads. Every element of `Frame` is named by `Frame::elements`, so an element added to the frame is drawn by the spectator unless it is named personal too. It draws the others in the space as a participant does (`Frame::people`, see `people.md`), but for the swarm of the participant it follows, whose head it sees from inside and whose controllers and hands it draws as theirs, which it leaves out (`Step::follow`); their rays it draws, as everyone's. The spectator keeps only its own concerns: its connection, its window and video output, whom to follow, its frame pacing, the camera, the followed participant's controllers and hands, drawn as theirs, and hearing from the frame's listener and agent.

For the virtual camera, the PipeWire spectator renders at 1920×1080 at up to 30 fps, matching the virtual camera's 1920×1080 output rather than rendering at 4K and scaling down.

The spectator animates its swarm, through the frame step, from the joined space's agent state as the server says it, as the headset renders it, or else, from an older server, its observed activity (see `agent-state.md`), rather than displaying a static neutral arrangement, and shows it speaking while the agent speech it plays for its audience does, as the headset does (see `speech.md`). Worker transitions use the animated swarm's positions. In a space whose backend names an avatar registered with the sceneries it is given, it shows that avatar instead, made, animated and drawn by the same rule as the headset, heard from it and with workers flying from it; Tomori's own spectator registers none, and shows the swarm (see `avatars.md`). This does not replace the shared-scene requirement in [clients.md](clients.md).

The spectator plays stereo and hears as the participant it follows does, from their head as their presence gives it in the space's shared scene, with each other participant at their head as their presence gives it, without seats, or from its neutral view with nobody to follow (see `spatial-audio.md`), so that its audience hears the agent where they see the swarm and each person where they are, wherever they have travelled.

The spectator follows the participant's head from their presence, which carries where they travelled in an environment that allows it (see `locomotion.md`), so it sees from where they are; it draws none of their teleport preview, nor teleport pads, since it applies no profile. It never follows someone who chose that others see their name only (see `people.md`), waiting for them, or following the next, until they let others see them.

The spectator shows the environment of the space it watches, as everyone in it does, growing in out of the space's object as it does for a participant who joins, and follows it when an owner changes it; outside a space, before it joins one, and in a space whose server names no environment, it shows the grid, as a participant does, and `TOMORI_ENVIRONMENT`, which only the server-less desktop window reads, chooses nothing for it (see `environments.md`). It stands the swarm at the agent's home, or where it went, gliding there as the headset's does, appearing at a new home as the headset's does, draws the environment's scenery carried into the space as the headset does, and stands the workers' arrangement at its workers anchor, or else the agent's home, and places workers with the placement registered with the sceneries it is given, or the default arc, by the same rule as the headset, so that both draw the coordinator and workers in the same places, and draws the space's timers as dials at the agent's home by the same rule too, counting down by its own clock (see `timers.md`). With nobody to follow, it sees and hears from its spawn point (see `environments.md`), at standing eye height, `tomori_scene::arrival::HOME`'s, above the point, facing its way, as a room arriving there would: the first the space's environment tags `spectator`, or else its first untagged, chosen by the spectator itself from the space's state by the rule the server uses, without occupancy, since nobody sees the spectator standing anywhere; it asks the server for none, so that nobody arrives away from where it watches. Where the environment declares no point that suits it, it watches from where a participant joins without spawn points, home at standing eye height, facing −Z, with no rig moved, as a headset starts, which is where the environment has them arrive, carried to home. Its client side's head is there too. One thing stays at the swarm's default place, an accepted limit: the place its audience hears the agent from when the swarm has no agents to take the middle of, which it always has.

The spectator runs the client side registered for the environment it draws (see `environments.md`), as its frame step runs it for a participant, once the environment has grown in, so that its audience sees what the environment shows beyond its scenery, such as the farmyard's chickens, sticks, stones and fire, or a downstream environment's globe: made for the joined space while the spectator draws the environment it is registered for, at the version the space names, and dropped when the spectator leaves the space or the environment, on the grid and in another environment; told each frame what the environment's server side sent since the last, its states and events, the others' hands from their presence, the followed participant's among them, and, as its head, the spectator's camera, the followed participant's head or the neutral view, in the environment's own coordinates, so that what a client side ties to a head follows the camera; and what it shows is handed to its scenery each frame, as on the headset. A spectator only watches: its client side is told nothing a participant does, pointing at nothing, so that nothing of it is hovered or pointed at, and no select, back, stick, scroll or grip, and no participant number, as a visitor is not; what it asks its server side is dropped and never sent, whatever role the spectator connected with, an owner's token included, so that a spectator never acts in the space; its sounds are handed to the spectator's feedback, as a participant's are, and played only with the ambience on, below, and its passes are not felt. A spectator built on `tomori-client-spectator` gets this from the sceneries it is given, the client sides registered with them by `Sceneries::set_client_side` as for the headset, such as a downstream project's spectator mode that registers its environment's client crate, with nothing more to register. It runs once a frame on the render thread, as on the headset, but is never asked what a ray or hand picks or reaches, since nobody points from a spectator, so a client side costs the spectator no more than it costs a participant, one update a rendered frame, at most 30 a second for the virtual camera; its cost is the client side's own, which an environment bounds as it does for the headset, and needs no measurement of its own in the spectator.

The spectator draws what the joined space projects, its walls, the panels on them and the floating panels, charts, code, images and volumes among them, and the GitHub environment's cards a server sends it, laid out by the frame step, paced as a participant's is (see `rendering.md`), and drawn by the same panel buffers, so that its audience sees the panels everyone in the space sees, whatever role it connected with. It fetches the images its image panels show from the server as a participant does, a visitor included, since a visitor is sent what its space shows (see `security.md`), each once and two at a time, and until one arrives its panel shows where it will be. As it points at nothing and holds no rights over walls, it draws none of a participant's own marks: no ray labels, readouts or linked cursors, no hints, no wall handles or padlocks, and every panel at the top, unscrolled. See `projection.md`.

The spectator reports no engagement, so the server keeps it at the live edge of agent speech: its audience hears replies as they are spoken, never a catch-up of what was said before it joined. See `speech.md`.

The spectator plays the voices it is told to: every participant's and the agent's speech, `all`, by default, as before; the agent's speech alone, live or played again, `agent`; or none, `none`. `tomori-spectator --voices` and the `tomori` spectator modes' `--voices` choose, as does `SpectatorOptions::voices` for an application that runs the spectator with options of its own, and V in its window cycles through them, `all`, `agent`, `none`, logging the choice. It shows nothing for it on screen, neither an overlay nor a change to its window's title: the spectator shows no interface, its borderless window has no title to see, and window capture in OBS finds the window by its title, which stays the same. A participant's voice it does not play is dropped as each frame arrives, by the source in its header, before its samples are decoded or reach the player, so that it costs nothing to mix, nor, unless the spectator ducks under everyone, below, ducks the agent's speech or the ambience; what had reached the player already plays out. The agent's speech, with `none`, is muted as a participant mutes it, still consumed in real time and measured, so that the swarm still speaks while it plays, at its level, as it does for the audience of `all`. Its sound cues stay off whichever is chosen, and so do the environment's ambience and its ambient sounds unless the ambience is on, below, so that `none` is silent by default. There is no environment variable for it: the `tomori` spectator modes, which downstream builds' `main` runs, take it on the command line as `tomori-spectator` does.

**Ducking under everyone, optional.** What ducks in the spectator, the agent's speech and the ambience (see `spatial-audio.md`), ducks under the voices it plays by default, `played`, exactly as before: a participant's voice it does not play is dropped as above and ducks nothing. `--duck-under everyone`, in `tomori-spectator` and the `tomori` spectator modes, or `SpectatorOptions::duck_under`, for an application that runs the spectator with options of its own, ducks them under everyone's speech, whether or not `--voices` plays it, as a participant's client does; D in its window switches between the two, logging the choice and showing nothing on screen, as V and A do. With `everyone`, a participant's voice it does not play is measured and never played: decoded and delivered to the player apart from the voices it plays, consumed in real time, its level in each mix counted towards ducking as a played voice's is, and never rendered into what the spectator plays, never given a level a speaking indicator could show, never counted among the voices playing, and never stored or logged; a test proves nothing of it reaches the output. It is measured only while something heard ducks under it, the ambience on or the agent's speech playing and not muted, as with `--voices agent`; otherwise, as with `--ambience off` and no agent speech playing, or `--voices none` and the ambience off, such a voice is dropped as it arrives, before it is decoded, as with `played`, so that the cost of the old choice is kept whenever nothing would duck. At most four voices are measured at once; a fifth speaker's frames are dropped until one of them runs dry, so that a crowd cannot grow the work the audio callback does. A voice measured that `--voices` comes to play is played from its next frame; a voice played that comes to be measured is silent at once, what had reached the player of it included. Whatever is chosen, the voices played and the cost of playing them are unchanged, and a participant's client, which plays every voice, measures none apart.

**Ambience, off by default.** The spectator plays the environment's ambience, its beds, loops and calls, and its client side's ambient sounds, such as the farmyard's clucks, only when asked: `--ambience on`, in `tomori-spectator` and the `tomori` spectator modes, which downstream builds' `main` runs, or `SpectatorOptions::ambience`, for an application that runs the spectator with options of its own; `--ambience off`, the default, plays none, as before. It applies to every output, the window, the PipeWire source and its preview, since each plays through the same speaker. A in its window turns it on and off, logging the choice and showing nothing on screen, as V does. On, it plays them as the participant it follows would hear them, through `tomori-client`'s feedback and player, as the headset and desktop window do (see `spatial-audio.md`): from the frame's listener, the followed participant's head or, with nobody to follow, the neutral view's camera, in the space's coordinates, once the environment's scenery has grown in, on its ambience bus at the default ambience level, low, 10 dB beneath the sounds as mastered, ducked under the voices it plays, or under everyone's with `--duck-under everyone`, above, and with ambient sounds never beginning over speech, a voice measured included; only the ambience is added: its sound cues stay off, and the voices it plays are those `--voices` chooses. Off, nothing of it reaches the player: no ambience is set on it and no ambient sound is queued, so that the spectator is as silent as it was before the option existed. Turned off, the ambience playing stops and the ambient sounds waiting are dropped; turned on, the environment's ambience starts where it is. The spectator never opens a microphone, so what it plays never comes back to it, but a participant whose microphone is near its speakers may send the ambience back as their voice, as with the agent's speech: `--voices agent` drops that voice.

**Recording, optional.** The spectator records what it shows and plays to a video file with sound only when asked: `--record FILE`, in `tomori-spectator` and `tomori --spectator`, or `SpectatorOptions::record`, for an application that runs the spectator with options of its own; neither PipeWire output records, and both command lines refuse `--record` with them, saying so, as they refuse its options without `--record`. The container is the file's extension, `.mp4` or `.mov`, in any case; any other is refused, naming the two, before anything starts, and so is a file that exists, unless `--record-overwrite` says to replace it, and a folder that does not. What is recorded is exactly what the spectator draws and plays, so that it shows no HUD, caption, status or indicator, and hears no cue: nothing is drawn or mixed for the recording that the spectator does not draw or play.

*The video* is drawn offscreen, once, at a fixed size, whatever the window's, with the camera's aspect taken from it: 1920×1080 by default, or `--record-size WxH`, even and from 64 pixels a side up to 3840×2160 in all; and at a constant rate, 30 frames a second by default, or `--record-fps N`, from 1 to 120. Each frame is timestamped by the recording's clock, which starts when the first frame is drawn, not when the encoder starts, so that the file does not begin with the seconds the window and the GPU took to start, and the file has one frame for every 1/*rate* of it: frame *n* shows the newest frame drawn at or before *n*/*rate*. A frame that is slow, or none drawn in a slot, repeats the frame before, rather than the file losing time or speeding up afterwards; a frame drawn when a newer one is drawn before its slot is dropped. The report and the log count the frames repeated and dropped. The window, unless `--no-window` says to record without one, is the recording's preview, as it is the PipeWire source's: the recording whole, as large as it fits and centred between black bars, and it keeps working as the spectator's window, closing it ends the recording, and its keys choose voices, ambience and ducking as before. The recording is drawn and read back from the GPU on the render thread's timer, even while the window is hidden, and written by a thread of its own, so that the render loop never waits for the encoder: as many frames wait as 128 MiB holds, at most sixteen and at least four, and a frame beyond them, or drawn while every readback buffer is still in flight, is dropped and counted, never waited for.

*The sound* is the mix the spectator's player renders, copied, as the speaker is given it, by a tap on the player (`tomori-client`'s `Player::tap`), so that it is the mix that `--voices`, `--ambience` and `--duck-under` choose and the player's ducking makes, including a choice made in the window while recording, and the tap changes nothing of what is rendered or played. It is stereo, 48 kHz, written in AAC, in step with the video, within a frame of it: the writer puts each block on the recording's clock, from when it starts, with silence before the first block and wherever the player rendered none, resampling a device that does not run at 48 kHz, and ends the sound at the length of the video. The spectator goes on playing to its speaker, unless `--record-playback off`, when it renders its player itself, at 48 kHz, on a thread paced by the clock, and plays nowhere; if its speaker cannot be opened it records the same way, saying so. The tap copies each block from the audio callback, allocating one block as the player does for each frame it receives, and never waits: a block the writer is too far behind to take is dropped, counted, and replaced by silence.

*The encoder* is an ffmpeg child process, found and tried at startup, so that a missing ffmpeg, or one without an encoder, or without AAC, is refused before the window opens, saying how to install it, with Homebrew on macOS. On macOS it encodes with VideoToolbox's hardware encoder, `h264_videotoolbox`, or `hevc_videotoolbox` with `--record-codec hevc`; elsewhere with NVIDIA's NVENC if ffmpeg has it and a trial frame encodes, and otherwise `libx264`, or `libx265`. The video goes to it as raw RGBA frames on its standard input and the sound as raw 16-bit stereo samples on descriptor 3, with no named pipe or file between them, converted to BT.709 limited-range 4:2:0 and tagged so, with the file's index moved to its front. It runs in a process group of its own, so that Ctrl+C in a terminal reaches the spectator alone, which finishes the file, and it ends with its inputs, so that a spectator that is killed outright still leaves a finished file.

*Finishing.* The recording ends when the window closes, when SIGINT, SIGTERM or SIGHUP arrives, when the connection to the server, up once, is lost, or when the encoder stops, whichever comes first, and the first reason is the one reported. The spectator stops drawing, hands on the frames still being read back, writes the video's slots and the sound up to the recording's length, closes the encoder's inputs, and waits for it to write the file's index before it reports the file, its path, its duration, the frames in it, how many repeated and dropped, its size and why it stopped, on its standard output; it fails, saying what ffmpeg said, if the file could not be finished, and ends an encoder that does not finish within two minutes, saying the file may not play. A second Ctrl+C while the file is finished ends the spectator at once, and the encoder, in its group, finishes the file by itself.

The spectator's window, `tomori-spectator`'s and `tomori --spectator`'s, is a normal window by default, decorated with the platform's title bar, so that it can be moved, resized and made full screen with the platform's own controls on macOS and Linux. It opens at 1920×1080 physical pixels, so that desktop scaling does not change what OBS captures, and its content fills it at any size and aspect it is given, rendered at the window's own size in physical pixels with the camera's aspect taken from it each frame, including after a change of display scale or a move to a display with another, which the window reports as a new size. Full screen on macOS, its picture keeps its size while the menu bar and title bar slide down over it, rather than flickering through a resize in every frame of the slide (see `desktop.md`). `--borderless`, in either, and `SpectatorOptions::borderless`, for an application that runs the spectator with options of its own, show it as before, without decorations and at least 1080p, for a clean capture into OBS or a shared screen. The PipeWire source's preview window, `--pipewire-preview` and `tomori --spectator-pipewire-preview`, is decorated by default too and borderless with `--borderless`; it shows the fixed 1920×1080 output whole, as large as it fits and centred, with black bars beside or above and below it rather than stretched. The PipeWire source without a window has no window to be borderless: both command lines refuse `--borderless` with it, saying so, rather than ignoring it, and `run_pipewire` takes no notice of the option. The window does not remember its size or place between runs, and has no key of its own for full screen: the platform's own controls make it full screen.

## Rationale

The spectator view is intended for an audience and recording, where a headset HUD obstructs the scene and a static swarm misrepresents the session. The user identified both problems in November 2026 and requested a matching 1080p render and virtual camera for huddles.

In November 2026 a downstream project's spectator mode showed Tomori's walls, panels and floor in its environment but none of what the environment's client side draws, its globe, racks and deck, and the farmyard's chickens, sticks, stones and fire were missing from Tomori's own spectator too, since the spectator ran no client side. The maintainer asked that the spectator run and draw client sides as a participant's scene does, from its camera, with the same messages, while unable to act as a participant. Intents are dropped by the spectator itself rather than left to its role, since a spectator may connect with an owner's token; pointing and hovering are off, since nobody points from a spectator; and its sounds stay off, so that its audience hears the conversation, as for cues and the ambience.

In November 2026 the same downstream spectator drew its environment's consoles and deck but none of the panels on its walls, its charts and terminals' screens, which the headset showed: the spectator had never laid out or drawn the space's projection, and handed the renderer no panels, leaving only the walls' bounds for its timers' dials. The maintainer asked that it draw them as the headset does.

In November 2026 the maintainer asked that the spectator start at a spectator-tagged spawn point when it has nobody to follow, replacing the neutral view at home that #470 made its joining position wherever an environment declares one (see `environments.md`).

In November 2026 the maintainer, demonstrating Tomori in a room with the headset on and the spectator on a laptop and its speakers, heard his own voice come back out of the laptop, since the spectator plays every participant, and asked for a way to play the agent alone. Everyone's voice stays the default, so that a spectator shared in a call or recorded is heard as before; the agent's speech alone is for a room where the participants can be heard already; and none is for a recording that takes its sound elsewhere.

Both of those, the client sides in #470 and the walls and panels in #479, were the same failure: the spectator assembled its frames along a path of its own, sharing the renderer but not the logic that decides what goes into a frame, so each thing the frame step learned to draw was silently missing from the spectator until someone noticed. In November 2026 the maintainer asked, in #482, that the spectator run the frame step itself, in a watcher mode without input or a participant's own overlays, with a test that a scene feature the spectator does not draw fails rather than ships. The spectator stopped starting on the scenery `TOMORI_ENVIRONMENT` names, as it had before joining a space or where a server named no environment, since a participant shows the grid there and the spectator shows what they see. A frame's elements are named exhaustively, and what a watcher leaves out is a declared list, which a test pins, so that leaving something out is a decision a reviewer sees. Running the frame step cost the spectator no measurable time: its frame assembly took about 13 µs before and 14 µs after, in a release build on an Apple M4 Pro, and its whole frame at 1080p was within run-to-run noise of 0.6 ms on the CPU and 1.5 ms with the GPU, both ways.

The spectator played no ambience from the start, so that an audience in a call or a recording hears the conversation and nothing beneath it. In November 2026 the maintainer, running the spectator on his own Mac in a Farmyard space with `--voices agent`, asked to hear the environment's ambience as a participant would. It is behind an option, off by default, so that recording and demonstration keep the silence they had, and asking for it is a decision made for a spectator's own listener; it adds only the ambience, not the cues, which mark an interface its audience does not see.

In November 2026 the maintainer, presenting with the spectator's speakers in the room and `--voices agent --ambience on`, so that his own voice did not come back out of them doubled over his live voice, found that the ambience ducked under the agent but not under him, since the voices the spectator drops never reached the mixer that ducking measures. He asked that ducking follow his speech without his voice being played, as an option rather than the new default, so that a spectator that does not ask keeps exactly its behaviour and cost. Measuring a voice not played in the same mixer, rather than adding a second measure beside it, keeps one definition of speaking for every duck; delivering it apart from the voices played, rather than marking frames, leaves the protocol and the player's input as they were; measuring only while something heard ducks keeps the cost of decoding a voice to the times it can change what is heard; and the cap bounds the mixer's work however many speak.

In November 2026 the maintainer found that the spectator's window, borderless for capture, could be neither moved nor resized, and asked that it be a normal, decorated window by default, with the borderless window kept behind `--borderless` for clean capture, the preview window following the same rule, and the headless PipeWire source unchanged.

In November 2026 the maintainer, preparing a demo video of the Observe interface for upper management on macOS, asked for the spectator to save a video file instead of only presenting it, so that a demo can be recorded at a fixed resolution and frame rate with its sound, without screen capture. Screen capture records the window as it is sized, with its title bar, the cursor and whatever else the desktop shows, at the display's scale and the screen recorder's frame rate, and the sound has to be routed to it by hand. The recording is drawn once, offscreen, as the PipeWire source is, so that its size and rate are the ones asked for; the sound is taken from the player's mix, so that it is what the spectator plays and no more; and a constant frame rate with the frame before repeated rather than time lost keeps the file in step with its sound however long a frame took, which an editor needs. ffmpeg encodes it, because Homebrew and every Linux distribution's packages have it, with the hardware encoder on a Mac, which leaves the CPU to the renderer, and because it writes the containers and their indexes correctly. The tap is on the player rather than on a device, so that the same mix is recorded with or without a speaker, and the same code serves macOS and Linux.

Currently the headset's particle positions and controller attraction are not transmitted. The spectator runs the same activity-driven simulation locally, so it is not an exact copy of the headset's scene. This is a limitation to resolve as the shared scene model develops, not a requirement for the simulations to remain independent.

## Scope

Spectator rendering and its shared view, including the environment client sides it runs (`tomori-scene`'s watcher mode, `scene/watching.rs`, and `tomori-client-spectator`'s feed and view), its window (`tomori-client-spectator`'s `spectator_window.rs` and command line, and `tomori-client-quest`'s spectator modes), the voices it plays (`tomori-client`'s `Hearing`, which the spectator's options, command lines and window choose), whose speech its ducking follows (`tomori-client`'s `DuckUnder`, which they choose too, and the voices measured and not played in `Client::measure`, `Player::measure` and `Mixer::measure`) and whether it plays the ambience (`tomori-client`'s `Feedback::hear_ambience`, which they choose too), and its recording (`tomori-client-spectator`'s `recording` module: its options, frame clock, ffmpeg command, audio tap and writers, and `tomori-client`'s `Player::tap`, which they copy the mix from), not headset rendering or in-world scene content.

## Validation

- Inspect the spectator render path to confirm it does not draw the caption or recording indicator; confirm that the headset continues to draw its HUD.
- View a connected spectator in a window or OBS and confirm that the scene is unobstructed while the headset HUD remains visible: by default in a decorated window that can be moved, resized to another aspect, made full screen and moved to a display with another scale, its content filling it undistorted each time; and with `--borderless`, without decorations, as captured into OBS.
- Test, in `tomori-client-spectator`, that the preview draws the output whole between black bars in a window of another aspect, that the window is decorated without a minimum size by default and borderless at least 1080p with `--borderless`, which the window and `--pipewire-preview` take and `--pipewire` refuses; and in `tomori-client-quest`, that `--borderless` applies to `--spectator` and `--spectator-pipewire-preview` alone and is handed on in `SpectatorOptions`.
- Test, in `tomori-scene`'s `scene/watching/tests.rs`, that for a space with walls an owner may change, panels, cards, workers, a timer, an environment's client side and another participant, a watcher seeing from a participant's head draws every element a participant's frame step draws for a visitor there, frame by frame, but those `PERSONAL` names, which it leaves out, though the participant shows their pill and reports their presence and an owner sees their walls' handles; that it hands the environment's ambience and its client side's sounds to its feedback as a participant's step does, which plays none of them muted as the spectator's is, and feels none of its passes; and that every element of a frame is either shared, which a frame cannot be without, or named personal. Inspect that `tomori-client-spectator` draws the frame step's frame with `scene_graphics::draw` and assembles nothing of it itself.
- Test that observed activity drives a moving spectator swarm, and inspect that worker transitions use its current positions.
- Check the PipeWire and virtual camera formats and verify that the camera receives frames at 1920×1080.
- Test that the spectator's conversation never reports engagement.
- Test, in `tomori-scene`'s `scene/watching/tests.rs`, that a watcher's client side is made once for the joined space once the environment has grown in, none outside a space or on the grid, and anew after leaving the environment; that it is given the server side's state and events, the others' hands and the camera as its head in the environment's own coordinates, and no pointing, controls or participant number; and that what it asks is never sent, as a visitor and as an owner. Test, in `tomori-client-spectator`, that the feed runs the client side registered with its sceneries, as an owner too, seeing, hearing and giving its client side a head from where a participant joins, home at eye height facing −Z, when nobody is followed, and, in a space whose environment declares spawn points, from eye height above its own, the one tagged `spectator` or else the first untagged, facing its way, never asking the server for one, drops what it asks and hands what it shows to its scenery in an offscreen render; and, in `tomori-environment-farmyard-client`'s `tests/spectator.rs`, that the farmyard's client side, run by a watcher's frame step, shows its flock.
