# Spectator View

## Requirement

Spectator output shows the scene without a head-locked HUD, including status captions and recording indicators, and plays no sound cues (see `feedback.md`), nor an environment's ambience or ambient sounds, so that its audience hears the conversation (see `spatial-audio.md`). This applies to windowed and PipeWire output; the headset retains its own HUD. Connection and follow status may still be logged.

**One frame step.** The spectator assembles each frame with `tomori-scene`'s frame step, the one the headset and the desktop window run, in its watcher mode, `Step::watch`, and draws the frame it returns with `tomori_render::scene_graphics::draw`, as they draw theirs, rather than deciding for itself what a frame holds. A watcher sees from a camera the spectator gives it, the followed participant's head or the neutral view below, and senses nothing else: no rays, triggers, grips, sticks, palms, controllers or hands, so that nothing is pointed at, hovered, chosen or carried, and it holds no rights over walls, whatever its role. For the same space state, seen from the same place, a watcher's frame holds everything a participant's does, but for the elements that are a participant's own, `tomori_scene::scene::PERSONAL`: the attractor and whether it attracts, arrival's objects, with the lobby, the hand menu and the rays among them, the status pill, the recording indicator, the wrist's transcript, the presence a participant reports, and the teleport landing, its blink and the pads. Every element of `Frame` is named by `Frame::elements`, so an element added to the frame is drawn by the spectator unless it is named personal too. The spectator keeps only its own concerns: its connection, its window and video output, whom to follow, its frame pacing, the camera, the followed participant's controllers and hands, drawn as theirs, and hearing from the frame's listener and agent.

For the virtual camera, the PipeWire spectator renders at 1920×1080 at up to 30 fps, matching the virtual camera's 1920×1080 output rather than rendering at 4K and scaling down.

The spectator animates its swarm, through the frame step, from the joined space's agent state as the server says it, as the headset renders it, or else, from an older server, its observed activity (see `agent-state.md`), rather than displaying a static neutral arrangement, and shows it speaking while the agent speech it plays for its audience does, as the headset does (see `speech.md`). Worker transitions use the animated swarm's positions. In a space whose backend names an avatar registered with the sceneries it is given, it shows that avatar instead, made, animated and drawn by the same rule as the headset, heard from it and with workers flying from it; Tomori's own spectator registers none, and shows the swarm (see `avatars.md`). This does not replace the shared-scene requirement in [clients.md](clients.md).

The spectator plays stereo and hears as the participant it follows does, from their head as their presence gives it in the space's shared scene, with each other participant at their head as their presence gives it, without seats, or from its neutral view with nobody to follow (see `spatial-audio.md`), so that its audience hears the agent where they see the swarm and each person where they are, wherever they have travelled.

The spectator follows the participant's head from their presence, which carries where they travelled in an environment that allows it (see `locomotion.md`), so it sees from where they are; it draws none of their teleport preview, nor teleport pads, since it applies no profile.

The spectator shows the environment of the space it watches, as everyone in it does, growing in out of the space's object as it does for a participant who joins, and follows it when an owner changes it; outside a space, before it joins one, and in a space whose server names no environment, it shows the grid, as a participant does, and `TOMORI_ENVIRONMENT`, which only the server-less desktop window reads, chooses nothing for it (see `environments.md`). It stands the swarm at the agent's home, or where it went, gliding there as the headset's does, appearing at a new home as the headset's does, draws the environment's scenery carried into the space as the headset does, and stands the workers' arrangement at its workers anchor, or else the agent's home, and places workers with the placement registered with the sceneries it is given, or the default arc, by the same rule as the headset, so that both draw the coordinator and workers in the same places, and draws the space's timers as dials at the agent's home by the same rule too, counting down by its own clock (see `timers.md`). With nobody to follow, it sees and hears from its spawn point (see `environments.md`), at standing eye height, `tomori_scene::arrival::HOME`'s, above the point, facing its way, as a room arriving there would: the first the space's environment tags `spectator`, or else its first untagged, chosen by the spectator itself from the space's state by the rule the server uses, without occupancy, since nobody sees the spectator standing anywhere; it asks the server for none, so that nobody arrives away from where it watches. Where the environment declares no point that suits it, it watches from where a participant joins without spawn points, home at standing eye height, facing −Z, with no rig moved, as a headset starts, which is where the environment has them arrive, carried to home. Its client side's head is there too. One thing stays at the swarm's default place, an accepted limit: the place its audience hears the agent from when the swarm has no agents to take the middle of, which it always has.

The spectator runs the client side registered for the environment it draws (see `environments.md`), as its frame step runs it for a participant, once the environment has grown in, so that its audience sees what the environment shows beyond its scenery, such as the farmyard's chickens, sticks, stones and fire, or a downstream environment's globe: made for the joined space while the spectator draws the environment it is registered for, at the version the space names, and dropped when the spectator leaves the space or the environment, on the grid and in another environment; told each frame what the environment's server side sent since the last, its states and events, the others' hands from their presence, the followed participant's among them, and, as its head, the spectator's camera, the followed participant's head or the neutral view, in the environment's own coordinates, so that what a client side ties to a head follows the camera; and what it shows is handed to its scenery each frame, as on the headset. A spectator only watches: its client side is told nothing a participant does, pointing at nothing, so that nothing of it is hovered or pointed at, and no select, back, stick, scroll or grip, and no participant number, as a visitor is not; what it asks its server side is dropped and never sent, whatever role the spectator connected with, an owner's token included, so that a spectator never acts in the space; its sounds are not played, as the spectator plays no ambient sounds, nor the environment's ambience, and its passes are not felt. A spectator built on `tomori-client-spectator` gets this from the sceneries it is given, the client sides registered with them by `Sceneries::set_client_side` as for the headset, such as a downstream project's spectator mode that registers its environment's client crate, with nothing more to register. It runs once a frame on the render thread, as on the headset, but is never asked what a ray or hand picks or reaches, since nobody points from a spectator, so a client side costs the spectator no more than it costs a participant, one update a rendered frame, at most 30 a second for the virtual camera; its cost is the client side's own, which an environment bounds as it does for the headset, and needs no measurement of its own in the spectator.

The spectator draws what the joined space projects, its walls, the panels on them and the floating panels, charts, code, images and volumes among them, and the GitHub environment's cards a server sends it, laid out by the frame step, paced as a participant's is (see `rendering.md`), and drawn by the same panel buffers, so that its audience sees the panels everyone in the space sees, whatever role it connected with. As it points at nothing and holds no rights over walls, it draws none of a participant's own marks: no ray labels, readouts or linked cursors, no hints, no wall handles or padlocks, and every panel at the top, unscrolled. See `projection.md`.

The spectator reports no engagement, so the server keeps it at the live edge of agent speech: its audience hears replies as they are spoken, never a catch-up of what was said before it joined. See `speech.md`.

The spectator plays the voices it is told to: every participant's and the agent's speech, `all`, by default, as before; the agent's speech alone, live or played again, `agent`; or none, `none`. `tomori-spectator --voices` and the `tomori` spectator modes' `--voices` choose, as does `SpectatorOptions::voices` for an application that runs the spectator with options of its own, and V in its window cycles through them, `all`, `agent`, `none`, logging the choice. It shows nothing for it on screen, neither an overlay nor a change to its window's title: the spectator shows no interface, its borderless window has no title to see, and window capture in OBS finds the window by its title, which stays the same. A participant's voice it does not play is dropped as each frame arrives, by the source in its header, before its samples are decoded or reach the player, so that it costs nothing to mix, nor ducks the agent's speech; what had reached the player already plays out. The agent's speech, with `none`, is muted as a participant mutes it, still consumed in real time and measured, so that the swarm still speaks while it plays, at its level, as it does for the audience of `all`. Its sound cues, the environment's ambience and its ambient sounds stay off whichever is chosen, so that `none` is silent. There is no environment variable for it: the `tomori` spectator modes, which downstream builds' `main` runs, take it on the command line as `tomori-spectator` does.

The spectator's window, `tomori-spectator`'s and `tomori --spectator`'s, is a normal window by default, decorated with the platform's title bar, so that it can be moved, resized and made full screen with the platform's own controls on macOS and Linux. It opens at 1920×1080 physical pixels, so that desktop scaling does not change what OBS captures, and its content fills it at any size and aspect it is given, rendered at the window's own size in physical pixels with the camera's aspect taken from it each frame, including after a change of display scale or a move to a display with another, which the window reports as a new size. `--borderless`, in either, and `SpectatorOptions::borderless`, for an application that runs the spectator with options of its own, show it as before, without decorations and at least 1080p, for a clean capture into OBS or a shared screen. The PipeWire source's preview window, `--pipewire-preview` and `tomori --spectator-pipewire-preview`, is decorated by default too and borderless with `--borderless`; it shows the fixed 1920×1080 output whole, as large as it fits and centred, with black bars beside or above and below it rather than stretched. The PipeWire source without a window has no window to be borderless: both command lines refuse `--borderless` with it, saying so, rather than ignoring it, and `run_pipewire` takes no notice of the option. The window does not remember its size or place between runs, and has no key of its own for full screen: the platform's own controls make it full screen.

## Rationale

The spectator view is intended for an audience and recording, where a headset HUD obstructs the scene and a static swarm misrepresents the session. The user identified both problems in November 2026 and requested a matching 1080p render and virtual camera for huddles.

In November 2026 a downstream project's spectator mode showed Tomori's walls, panels and floor in its environment but none of what the environment's client side draws, its globe, racks and deck, and the farmyard's chickens, sticks, stones and fire were missing from Tomori's own spectator too, since the spectator ran no client side. The maintainer asked that the spectator run and draw client sides as a participant's scene does, from its camera, with the same messages, while unable to act as a participant. Intents are dropped by the spectator itself rather than left to its role, since a spectator may connect with an owner's token; pointing and hovering are off, since nobody points from a spectator; and its sounds stay off, so that its audience hears the conversation, as for cues and the ambience.

In November 2026 the same downstream spectator drew its environment's consoles and deck but none of the panels on its walls, its charts and terminals' screens, which the headset showed: the spectator had never laid out or drawn the space's projection, and handed the renderer no panels, leaving only the walls' bounds for its timers' dials. The maintainer asked that it draw them as the headset does.

In November 2026 the maintainer asked that the spectator start at a spectator-tagged spawn point when it has nobody to follow, replacing the neutral view at home that #470 made its joining position wherever an environment declares one (see `environments.md`).

In November 2026 the maintainer, demonstrating Tomori in a room with the headset on and the spectator on a laptop and its speakers, heard his own voice come back out of the laptop, since the spectator plays every participant, and asked for a way to play the agent alone. Everyone's voice stays the default, so that a spectator shared in a call or recorded is heard as before; the agent's speech alone is for a room where the participants can be heard already; and none is for a recording that takes its sound elsewhere.

Both of those, the client sides in #470 and the walls and panels in #479, were the same failure: the spectator assembled its frames along a path of its own, sharing the renderer but not the logic that decides what goes into a frame, so each thing the frame step learned to draw was silently missing from the spectator until someone noticed. In November 2026 the maintainer asked, in #482, that the spectator run the frame step itself, in a watcher mode without input or a participant's own overlays, with a test that a scene feature the spectator does not draw fails rather than ships. The spectator stopped starting on the scenery `TOMORI_ENVIRONMENT` names, as it had before joining a space or where a server named no environment, since a participant shows the grid there and the spectator shows what they see. A frame's elements are named exhaustively, and what a watcher leaves out is a declared list, which a test pins, so that leaving something out is a decision a reviewer sees. Running the frame step cost the spectator no measurable time: its frame assembly took about 13 µs before and 14 µs after, in a release build on an Apple M4 Pro, and its whole frame at 1080p was within run-to-run noise of 0.6 ms on the CPU and 1.5 ms with the GPU, both ways.

In November 2026 the maintainer found that the spectator's window, borderless for capture, could be neither moved nor resized, and asked that it be a normal, decorated window by default, with the borderless window kept behind `--borderless` for clean capture, the preview window following the same rule, and the headless PipeWire source unchanged.

Currently the headset's particle positions and controller attraction are not transmitted. The spectator runs the same activity-driven simulation locally, so it is not an exact copy of the headset's scene. This is a limitation to resolve as the shared scene model develops, not a requirement for the simulations to remain independent.

## Scope

Spectator rendering and its shared view, including the environment client sides it runs (`tomori-scene`'s watcher mode, `scene/watching.rs`, and `tomori-client-spectator`'s feed and view), its window (`tomori-client-spectator`'s `spectator_window.rs` and command line, and `tomori-client-quest`'s spectator modes), and the voices it plays (`tomori-client`'s `Hearing`, which the spectator's options, command lines and window choose), not headset rendering or in-world scene content.

## Validation

- Inspect the spectator render path to confirm it does not draw the caption or recording indicator; confirm that the headset continues to draw its HUD.
- View a connected spectator in a window or OBS and confirm that the scene is unobstructed while the headset HUD remains visible: by default in a decorated window that can be moved, resized to another aspect, made full screen and moved to a display with another scale, its content filling it undistorted each time; and with `--borderless`, without decorations, as captured into OBS.
- Test, in `tomori-client-spectator`, that the preview draws the output whole between black bars in a window of another aspect, that the window is decorated without a minimum size by default and borderless at least 1080p with `--borderless`, which the window and `--pipewire-preview` take and `--pipewire` refuses; and in `tomori-client-quest`, that `--borderless` applies to `--spectator` and `--spectator-pipewire-preview` alone and is handed on in `SpectatorOptions`.
- Test, in `tomori-scene`'s `scene/watching/tests.rs`, that for a space with walls an owner may change, panels, cards, workers, a timer, an environment's client side and another participant, a watcher seeing from a participant's head draws every element a participant's frame step draws for a visitor there, frame by frame, but those `PERSONAL` names, which it leaves out, though the participant shows their pill and reports their presence and an owner sees their walls' handles; that it plays no ambience nor its client side's sounds and feels none of its passes; and that every element of a frame is either shared, which a frame cannot be without, or named personal. Inspect that `tomori-client-spectator` draws the frame step's frame with `scene_graphics::draw` and assembles nothing of it itself.
- Test that observed activity drives a moving spectator swarm, and inspect that worker transitions use its current positions.
- Check the PipeWire and virtual camera formats and verify that the camera receives frames at 1920×1080.
- Test that the spectator's conversation never reports engagement.
- Test, in `tomori-scene`'s `scene/watching/tests.rs`, that a watcher's client side is made once for the joined space once the environment has grown in, none outside a space or on the grid, and anew after leaving the environment; that it is given the server side's state and events, the others' hands and the camera as its head in the environment's own coordinates, and no pointing, controls or participant number; and that what it asks is never sent, as a visitor and as an owner. Test, in `tomori-client-spectator`, that the feed runs the client side registered with its sceneries, as an owner too, seeing, hearing and giving its client side a head from where a participant joins, home at eye height facing −Z, when nobody is followed, and, in a space whose environment declares spawn points, from eye height above its own, the one tagged `spectator` or else the first untagged, facing its way, never asking the server for one, drops what it asks and hands what it shows to its scenery in an offscreen render; and, in `tomori-environment-farmyard-client`'s `tests/spectator.rs`, that the farmyard's client side, run by a watcher's frame step, shows its flock.
- Test, in `tomori-client-spectator`'s feed, that a visitor's spectator's frame holds the space's wall and the panels published on it, with the images they show, and draws them over the wall without them in an offscreen render.
- Test, in `tomori-client`, that each choice of voices plays exactly the participants' voices and the agent's speech, live or played again, that agent speech not played is muted and still reported playing, that it holds from a connection's first frame and changes while connected, and, in `tomori-protocol`, that a frame's source is read from its header alone; in `tomori-client-spectator`, that `--voices` names each, that every voice is the default, and that the feed tells its connection the option and each choice the window's V cycles to; and in `tomori-client-quest`, that the spectator modes alone take `--voices` and hand it on in `SpectatorOptions`.
- Test that, after the followed participant travels and turns, the spectator hears from their head and places another participant at that participant's head from presence.
