# Panel Backs

This describes the **back of a panel**: its physical back, which shows how the panel is configured, as that view's slice of a workbook. The first version is a requirement, built; what comes later is listed at the end, each its own decision for the maintainer.

## Requirement

**The back is the panel's physical back.** There is no state of being flipped, shared or private: everyone in a space sees the same object, so a participant who walks behind a floating panel, or turns one round in the hand with the right stick, sees its back, as anyone else standing there would. Where a client once drew a panel seen from behind as its front mirrored, it draws its back: the front's face is not drawn from behind, and the back's text faces out of the back and reads left to right, never mirrored.

**The view's slice of a workbook.** The back is laid out in the sections a view of a visualisation workbook has (#539, section 10), so that today's panels and later a workbook's share one layout, and a workbook fills it without a change of the protocol. Tomori writes what it knows of every panel (`tomori_protocol::back::describe`), the same for every client and for the agent, each line a key and a value:

- **Inputs:** what it depends on. Today, each chip of its context, its label and its value as the chip writes it, with where that comes from, `its own`, `inherited from its wall` or `following the space` (`panel-context.md`), and a fixed chart's **Window**, where no chip says it. Later, the workbook's inputs and the shared channels it follows.
- **Outputs:** what it publishes. Today, **Linked**, the panels whose linked time cursor follows its own (`projection.md`), as `time cursor with Errors, Saturation`, a live chart's the live charts on its wall and a fixed window's every chart whose window overlaps it, at most 8 named and `+N more`; and, for an instance the server made (`panel-context.md`), **Copy of**, the title of the panel it is a copy of, or that panel's id where it is no longer shown, which only the server writes. Listed for now, they are to become its cables. Later, the workbook's outputs and channels.
- **Definition:** what makes it. Today, **Shows**, what kind of panel it is, as `Chart of 3 series`, `List of 12 rows`, `Table of 4 rows`, `Face`, `Code, rust, 40 lines`, `Image, 640 × 480`, `Diagram of 5 nodes` or `Text`; a live chart's **Points**, as `every 5 s`; **Source**, its environment's name and the source its side names after it, as `Observe · Prometheus`, or `Tomori's agent` for the agent's panels; and the query or expression its side says it runs, as a code block tagged with its language, as `promql`. Later, the view's definition and the names it uses.
- **Status:** how fresh it is. **Updated**, how long ago what it shows last changed, its live data included, as `12 s ago`, `3 min ago`, `2 h ago` or `4 d ago`; **Refresh**, as its side says, as `every 15 s`, or `live` for a live chart whose side says nothing (refresh policies, #509, are to say more); and the errors its side says its last refresh met, drawn in a warning colour.

Tomori's lines come first in each section, its environment's after them; a section of another name, as an environment or a newer peer sends, follows the four under its own name; a section with nothing in it is left out.

**Generic and bounded.** What an environment adds is `tomori_protocol::back::Back`, re-exported by `tomori-environment` and given with `Panel::with_back(Back)`: named sections, each of lines of a key, a value and whether it warns, and an optional code block of a language and its text. The server cleans and bounds it (`Back::cleaned`): at most 6 sections, each named by 1 to 24 ASCII lowercase letters, digits and `-`, the first of each name kept; at most 8 lines with a key and a value in each, each key on one line of 24 characters and value of 240; a code block's language of 1 to 24 ASCII letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `+`, left out otherwise, and its text, its controls but new lines left out and tabs as four spaces, of at most 16 lines and 1,024 characters, each cut with `…`. It leaves out a side's `Copy of` among its outputs, which only the server says, writes the environment's name before the side's `Source` line in its definition, or adds `Source` with the name alone first there, so that every panel an environment publishes says whose it is, and counts the back among the bytes its side publishes. A back is meant to say how a panel is configured, which seldom changes: it is part of the panel's version, as its text is, so a side changes it only when its configuration does, and never writes its data's age into it, which Tomori says itself.

**On the wire.** A projection's `Panel` gains `back`, the `Back` the server keeps, with the origin of an instance, sent only where it says something, and `updated`, how long ago what the panel shows last changed, in seconds, when the server sent it, as `age` is how long ago it was projected. `back` is part of the panel's version and of what its patches compare (`Panel::same_structure`, the patches' `same_content`); `updated` is part of neither, so that it never changes a version, and a client counts it on from when it was sent and from each change of the panel's version or data seq after. An older client ignores both. Where a projection does not fit its frame, the backs go first, the least recently placed first, before any code panel is sent short or chart as its summary (`stage/frames.rs`), since a back is seen only from behind, and a panel's bound counts its back.

**Drawn.** A client draws a panel's back in place of its face where the panel stands free, floating, carried or let go and not yet confirmed, and its viewer is on the far side of its plane from where its face looks:

- **In its sections.** Its title, bold, `Back · configuration` below it and a rule, then each section headed by its name, `Inputs`, `Outputs`, `Definition` and `Status`, or another's with its first letter capital and its dashes spaces, in a colour of its own; its lines' keys dim in one column, as wide as the longest and at most 10 cells, their values wrapped under themselves, a warning's in its colour; and its code block, its language dim above it, its text monospaced and wrapped on a tinted ground, each later row of a line marked as continued, so that a client may highlight it by its language later.
- **In its place, turned.** The back stands where the face would, turned half about the panel's vertical, so that its text faces out of the back, at the same size as the face, as the panel stands now, its text as large as a panel's, or a step smaller, down to two thirds, so that what it says fits; what still does not fit ends with `…`.
- **Opaque.** Its plate is the face's colour, wholly opaque, drawn first in each eye and writing depth, its text over it, as every opaque face is (`rendering.md`), so that nothing behind it shows through, and framed as the face is framed, brightly while pointed at or carried.
- **A panel in a wall's slot** shows its face alone: its wall's board stands behind it, and hides it from behind, so walking behind a wall shows the wall's back, and a panel on a wall shows its back once it is taken off and turned, or left floating turned. A way to see the back of a panel on a wall with no walkway behind it is later (below).
- **Far away, its title.** Where a row of its text would subtend less than the angle a panel's face in a slot is drawn as its title below (`rendering.md`, 0.09°), its back is drawn as its title alone, as the face's far face is, and whole again only above a fifth more, so that a viewer at the threshold does not switch it back and forth.
- **Kept.** A back is drawn once and kept while it says the same, at the same size and frame: its age, as it shows, changes it once a second within the first minute and once a minute after, and only while it is seen from behind. Backs no longer seen from behind are forgotten.
- **Pointing at it** from behind names the panel, as the ray on any panel does, and the grip takes it, but none of its face's items, chips or chart readouts, nor a readout linked to it, since none of those is drawn.

**The agent** reads a panel's back, as participants see it, whenever it reads that panel whole with `panels`, as `back`: its sections, each its `name`, its `lines`, each a `key`, a `value` and whether it warns, and its `code`, its `language` and `text` (`mcp.md`). The context's values are written as the agent is told them elsewhere.

**Turning it round.** Holding a panel, the right stick turns it continuously while held sideways, a quarter turn a second all the way, a short flick still turning it a 10° step, as `projection.md` says; the desktop window's `[` and `]` are the stick held all the way, and a tap a flick. Held with both hands, it turns as the line between the hands turns.

## Rationale

The scripted visualisations proposal (#539) asked for the back of a panel, like the rack of the Reason audio app, to see how a panel is configured: its source, query, time window, refresh and links. It proposed a flip: a chip turning a panel about, privately by default with a way to flip it for everyone. Deciding on it in November 2026, the maintainer refined it: there is no flip, and the back of a panel is its physical back. Looking at a panel from behind had shown its front mirrored, which says nothing; the back is where its configuration belongs, and since everyone sees the same object, whether a flip is private or shared stops mattering. Walking behind a panel, or turning it round in the hand, shows it. Turning a panel round needed the stick to turn it continuously, since eighteen flicks for half a turn was too many, so holding the stick turns it, faster the further it is pushed; a flick still turns a 10° step, so that the snaps that were there still work and square is still exactly reachable.

Designing the visualisation workbook language (#539, section 10), the maintainer then decided that the back of a panel is that view's slice of a workbook, its inputs, outputs, definition and status, so that today's panels and a workbook's share one layout; and that the protocol is generic, named sections of lines and a code block tagged with its language, so that the workbook phase fills it without a change of the protocol, a workbook snippet or a query highlighted by its language later, and a "show workbook" action added to it later.

Tomori writes most of the back itself, from what it knows of every panel, its kind, context bindings, chips, environment, refresh and age, so that every panel has a useful back without its environment's help, and the agent and every client say the same. An environment adds only what it alone knows, such as Observe's PromQL and the source and scope it reads, bounded as everything an environment publishes is. The data's age is counted by Tomori from when the panel last changed, rather than written by the environment, so that a live chart's back never changes its version at each point.

A panel on a wall is backed by its wall's board, and that wall's back faces whoever walks behind it, so the first version draws backs only for panels standing free; a way to see the back of a panel on a wall with no walkway behind it is later.

## Scope

`tomori-protocol`'s `back` module (`Back`, `BackSection`, `BackLine`, `BackCode`, the section names, their bounds, `valid_name`, `valid_language`, `describe`, `linked` and `ago`) and `Panel::back` and `Panel::updated`; `tomori-environment`'s `Panel::with_back` and `back_spec`; `tomori-server`'s `stage/published.rs` (`Publishing::of_environment`, keeping and comparing a back), `Stage::back` and `view_within` in `stage.rs`, `stage/frames.rs` (a back in a panel's bound, and sent first to fit a frame), `hub/sides.rs` (the environment's name), and `hub/agent_panels.rs` and the `panels` tool's description in `mcp.rs`; `tomori-scene`'s `projection::back` (`Updates`, `Backs`, `face`, `turned`, `seen_from_behind`), and in `projection.rs` `Projector::backs_of`, `seen_behind`, the sheets drawn turned, and pointing from behind; `manipulation.rs`, the stick's continuous turn.

## Validation

- `tomori-protocol`'s `back` tests check that a back is cleaned and bounded, each part cut or left out where empty, a warning kept, a language left out where unsound, a code block keeping its first lines and their indents, at most 6 sections of sound and distinct names, none of nothing; that a back is part of a panel's version and its update age is not, both on the wire only where a panel has them and read from an older server as none; that a live chart's back is its inputs, each chip with where its value comes from, its outputs, at most 8 linked panels and `+N more` and its origin, its definition, its kind, its points' spacing and its source, and its status, its age, refresh and error as a warning, then a section of another name, its environment's lines after Tomori's and its code block in its definition; that a back says what kind every panel is, the agent's `Tomori's agent`, and that a live chart refreshes `live` where its side says nothing, its spacing in its unit; that a fixed window says its window among its inputs unless a chip says it; that a chart is linked to the live charts on its wall, or the fixed windows overlapping its own, and nothing else; and that sections are found and made by name. Its doctests show the wire format, `ago`, and the names and languages taken.
- `tomori-environment`'s doctest checks `Panel::with_back`.
- `tomori-server`'s `stage/published` tests check that a side's back is cleaned, its source named for its environment, its `Copy of` left out, counted among its bytes; that a panel whose side gave none, or names no source, is named for its environment alone, first in its definition, and the agent's says nothing more; that a panel is sent with how long ago it was updated; that the same back again changes nothing and another query is an update of a new version, updated just now; that a live chart's next point, applied, leaves its update age to the client; and that a projection that does not fit its frame sends its panels' backs first, the least recently placed first, before any chart, and a panel's bound counts its back. `stage/context` tests check that a copy's back names the panel it is a copy of among its outputs, by its title, and by its id once that panel is withdrawn. `hub_tests.rs` checks, through the hub, that a side's back reaches clients named for its environment, its code block's language kept, with how long ago the panel was updated, a panel without one named for its environment alone, and that the agent reads a panel's back whole with `panels`, its inputs, outputs, definition and status in order, each with its lines, and its code block.
- `tomori-scene`'s `projection::back` tests check that when a panel last changed is followed from what the server says and each change of its version or data seq after, and forgotten once it is not shown; that a back is its title, heading and sections, as large as its face, opaque and framed, each section headed in its colour, keys in one column cut to their room, values wrapped under themselves, a warning in its colour, a code block's language above its text on its tint, and a section of another name headed by it; that one longer than its face ends with `…`, and code without a language shows its text; that a back is drawn once while it says the same and again as its age, size, frame or title changes, and forgotten once not seen; that a back far away is its title alone, and whole again only a fifth nearer; that its context is written as its chips write it; and that a panel is seen from behind beyond its plane and its back turned about its vertical. `projection::tests::backs` checks that a floating panel seen from behind shows its back, in its place, turned to face the viewer, as large as its face, kept while still and drawn again a second on and as the server changes it, and its face from in front; that a panel in a wall's slot shows its face from behind its wall, and its back once carried and turned half round with the stick; that one far away shows its title alone from behind; and that pointing at a panel from behind names it but none of its items, nor reads its chart. `manipulation.rs` and the projection's and frame step's tests check the stick's turn (`projection.md`).
- `tomori-render`'s `scene_graphics::tests::panel_back` draws, on lavapipe, a live latency chart floating behind home, from in front and from behind, and checks that from behind its back says how it is configured, its inputs, outputs, definition and status headed, its PromQL among it, opaque within its frame, its title's ink at its left, as text reads, not its right, as a mirrored face's would be, and none of the chart's colour; set `TOMORI_PANEL_BACK_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to see both side by side.
- Region coverage of everything added is complete (`testing.md`).
- On the headset: in Observe's space, pull a terminal's chart off its wall, hold the right stick to turn it round, and confirm that it turns smoothly, faster the further the stick is pushed, ticking every 10°, that a flick still turns it a step, and that its back reads in its four sections, saying its inputs, source, PromQL, window, cadence and how fresh it is; leave it floating turned, walk round it, and confirm that its front shows from in front and its back from behind, and that another participant sees the same; confirm that a chart far away shows its title from behind.

## Later

Each the maintainer's call:

- **Workbooks:** a workbook's views filling the same sections, their inputs and shared channels, outputs, definitions and the names they use, and a "show workbook" action on the back that opens the workbook a view belongs to.
- **Highlighting:** a code block highlighted by its language, PromQL and the workbook language among them.
- **Take me behind it:** a control on a panel on a wall with no walkway behind it that shows its back, such as moving the participant behind it where the scenery allows, or turning it out of its slot for a moment.
- **Cables:** outputs and inputs drawn as cables between panels' backs rather than listed, with jacks for the channels a panel follows and sets (#539).
- **Actions on the back:** refresh now, freeze, export, and asking the agent about it.
- **Refresh policies** (#509): a panel's policy, its next refresh and its budget in its status.
- **Provenance:** the last refresh's reads, their timings, series counts and any partial answer.
