# Clients

This describes the layout of Tomori clients. The headset and desktop application is `crates/client-quest`; it connects to the new server, and no longer uses `bridge/`.

## Requirement

Each kind of hardware or output has its own client implementation in a separate workspace crate in `crates/client-<target>`, named `tomori-client-<target>` as `crates.md` requires, such as `crates/client-quest` (`tomori-client-quest`) for Meta Quest headsets, `crates/client-desktop` (`tomori-client-desktop`) for the desktop window, console and discovery, and `crates/client-spectator` (`tomori-client-spectator`, whose executable is `tomori-spectator`) for the Linux spectator/OBS output. The `tomori` executable runs the desktop and spectator modes through those crates. Clients share a platform-neutral core:

- `crates/client` (`tomori-client`): the server connection, reconnection, outgoing audio, per-source jitter buffers, the playback mixer and the player that feeds it received audio for a device callback. Its `connection::Connection` is a client's whole connection: it carries out a `connection::Session`, the client's conversation with the server, choosing the server, keeping each server's certificate and token for the device, browsing for servers, recording from the microphone and playing through the speaker, behind the `capture::Capture` and `speaker::Playback` traits that each client's devices implement, and cueing what happens through the feedback model. It contains no rendering, windowing or device code, builds for every client target, and depends on no other client crate.
- `crates/scene` (`tomori-scene`): the shared scene model derived from space state and participant presence, with the frame step that the headset and the desktop window share, and the conversation, also free of rendering, windowing and device code, so that clients with different renderers present the same scene. It builds for every client target, and its test `tests/dependencies.rs` fails if it comes to depend on such a crate.
- The conversation is the connection's session: it implements `tomori_client::connection::Session`, so the dependency points from the scene to the client and never back. The session asks for `Action`s, which the connection takes one at a time; it is told the client's `Event`s and the connection's own `Report`s in order, whether to browse is asked of it, as are the environments it presents, which each connection lists in its `Hello` (see `environments.md`), and once a frame it tells the feedback model what waits for the participant. Where each voice is placed, at each participant's head from presence, and the agent at the avatar shown, is the scene's, given to the connection through `Connection::listen`.
- The connection carries out every `Action` in every state, and never panics on one: an action that needs a connection it does not have fails with a `Report`, or, where nothing failed that the participant should see, such as a token from pairing that arrives as the participant leaves the server, is ignored and logged. A token from pairing or a knock is kept with the certificate the connection presented, which the session does not choose.
- `tomori-client`'s interface can grow without breaking: its public enums that may take new variants, such as `Action`, `Report`, `Event` and `ConnectionState`, and its public structs that may take new fields, such as `Options`, `server_choice::Choice` and `connection::Utterance`, are `#[non_exhaustive]`, made with constructors, and hold what may grow in named fields rather than in several positional ones. Its public interface is the same on every target.
- Engagement, whether the participant is wearing the device and attending, is derived in the core from platform-neutral signals (whether the application has input, and whether a sensor reports the device worn) by one state machine free of rendering and platform code. Each client feeds it its platform's signals, such as the OpenXR session state and the user presence extension on Quest, and the protocol carries only the resulting device-neutral fact; see `spaces.md`.
- Text below layout, the bundled fonts, which characters they cover and the signed-distance-field glyph atlas, lives in `crates/text` (`tomori-text`), free of GPU code, so that `tomori-scene` lays text out with it and `tomori-render` draws it; see `text.md`.
- Rendering code shared by clients that use the same engine lives in its own crate, not in one client that others depend on: `crates/render` (`tomori-render`) holds the wgpu renderers and shaders that the headset, the desktop window and the spectator share.
- How the agent is shown is an avatar, the swarm by default: what it does each frame, its voice and where workers fly from are `tomori-scene`'s `Avatar`, and its drawing a `tomori-render` `Scenery`, so that every client shows the same avatar by the same rule (see `avatars.md`).

**The projection, patched.** The conversation holds its space's projection as the server last sent it whole, with each `ProjectionPatch` since applied by `ProjectionPatch::apply`, the rule the server's patches are made by, and says so in every `Hello`, through `Session::projection_patches`, which a session that does not apply them leaves `false`, so that it is sent the projection whole at every change, as before patches; and that it applies their data ops, `chart_append` and `chart_replace`, to live panels, keeping each wall's and panel's version as patches carry them, `place` ops included, through `Session::live_panels`, which a session leaves `false` unless it applies patches with that same rule, so that it is sent a live panel whole at each change of its data. It applies a patch only from the revision it holds, of its joined space and environment; one it cannot apply it leaves unapplied, keeping what it shows, and asks once with `ResyncProjection` for the projection whole. A panel a patch does not send again is shown as old as it now is, counted on from when it was last sent; a tick shown at once is undone by whatever the server sends next, a patch or the projection whole, as the projection sent whole undid it. A tick says the panel's version it holds, and a change to a wall made by its handles the wall's version as the handle was taken hold of, so that the server refuses one made against what has changed since; a refusal naming the panel or the wall is cued as not done, not shown as a problem, and the projection sent again shows it as it is (see `projection.md`). How far each panel is scrolled, and which way a sign or panel turns to face the participant, stay the client's own, never sent. Drawing is unchanged by patches: each panel's face is laid out once for what it shows and kept until a patch, or the projection whole, changes that panel, and its mesh uploaded only then (see `rendering.md`), so a patch costs the frame no more than the projection whole did. See `projection.md`.

**What each client states it draws.** Each connection states in its `Hello` the limits its session gives, `Session::limits`: by default, as for a session built on `tomori-client` that says nothing, frames of up to 32 MiB, which the connection reads, and today's projection, 13 walls, 78 slots, 12 floating and 90 panels. The conversation states `HEADSET_LIMITS`, the same, unless `Conversation::set_limits` says otherwise, and the desktop window `DESKTOP_LIMITS`, twice today's walls owners add, slots, floating panels and panels, 19, 156, 24 and 180, with the same frames. These caps were chosen while the frame step laid out every panel's face each frame, which cost about 25 µs for each line of its text on a desktop machine, measured in a release build: 0.7 ms for a panel of 30 full lines, 67 ms for ninety of them, and 3 ms for ninety of one line. Today's caps were then already more than a headset's frame of 14 ms held when every panel was full, so the headset keeps them, taking longer frames only so that its code panels are sent whole; the desktop window, about three times as fast as a headset and whose slower frame is not felt as one in a headset is, takes twice them. Faces are now kept from one frame to the next, laid out again only where they change (see `rendering.md`), so either may take more, once what more panels cost a headset's frame, in drawing both eyes as well as in laying out what changes, is measured on the headset. The server sends each client within what it states, so that one space shows the headset and the desktop window each what it draws (see `projection.md`). The connection reads frames of up to the lesser of what it states and what the server's `Welcome` states, 1 MiB from an older server, from the frame after the `Welcome` on.

**Progress.** While a frame of 256 KiB or more arrives, as the projection whole on joining a space or after asking for it again, the connection reports `Event::Receiving`, the bytes received of the length its header declares, as each whole percent more arrives and once more when all of it has, just before what it carries. The conversation shows it on the status pill, where the connection's state shows, as `Syncing space... 45%`, until all of it has arrived or the connection is lost, when the pill says how the connection is instead.

The protocol stays device-neutral. A client reports optional capabilities, such as hand tracking or controllers, through its messages; the server never branches on which hardware a participant uses.

The root manifest only defines the workspace and its build profiles; it has no package.

## Rationale

Tomori should support several kinds of hardware and outputs, each with its own platform constraints, without duplicating connection, audio or scene logic, and without one client's platform dependencies leaking into another. Agreed in the October 2026 design discussion.

The connection lives in `tomori-client` and the conversation in `tomori-scene`, at the maintainer's decision on #132 in November 2026. The `Session` trait between them lets another project drive Tomori's connection, with its pinning, pairing, reconnection, microphone and speaker, from a session of its own, and lets the connection be tested against a scripted session without the scene. It was drawn before the first release to crates.io, so that `tomori-client`'s interface does not change straight after it; marking what may grow `#[non_exhaustive]`, after the review of #174, is for the same reason.

Frames of up to 32 MiB were the maintainer's decision in November 2026, rather than splitting the projection over frames: 1 MiB is too small for a modern system, chunked synchronisation would complicate the transport without much advantage since the memory still has to be allocated, and most use is local, a headset to a laptop or PC, with progress shown while an initial sync or a resync arrives over a slow link (see `projection.md`). The caps each client states are bounded by what it can draw, which the frame step's cost, measured above, decides, rather than by what fits in a frame.

A visionOS client is a possible later target. Bevy and OpenXR are unavailable there, so it would render natively and use the core through a generated Swift binding. It is not planned, and the core is not required to provide that binding yet.

## Scope

Workspace layout and dependencies of client crates, the client core, the shared scene model, and the protocol's treatment of device differences.

## Validation

- Inspect the workspace: each client is a separate crate in `crates/client-<target>`, and the core and scene crates depend on no rendering, windowing or platform audio crate. `tomori-client` depends on no other client crate; Cargo refuses the cycle that a dependency on `tomori-scene` would make.
- `tomori-client`'s connection tests drive it through `Session` alone, with a scripted session, a scripted server and a real one, and `tomori-scene`'s tests check that the conversation applies each `Report`. They carry out each action without a connection, while connecting, connected without TLS and in the frame the connection closes, and leave the server in the frame that pairing completes.
- `tomori-scene`'s `patched/tests.rs` checks that a patch applies from the revision held, one after a gap is not applied and asks once for the projection whole, which it then patches from, one naming what it does not hold asks too, and one with nothing held asks; that a panel not sent again is shown as old as it now is; and that a tick shown at once is undone by a patch of nothing, replaced by the panel ticked, and by the projection whole. Its conversation tests check that the conversation says it applies patches, patches its joined space's projection, ignores another space's, and asks once for it whole after a gap. `tomori-client`'s connection tests check that `Hello` says whether the session applies patches, and states today's limits.
- `tomori-client`'s connection tests check that `Hello` states the session's limits, by default frames of 32 MiB and today's projection, and that a long frame from a server stating the most is read, its arrival reported as each whole percent more arrives, rising, and once more when all of it has, before the message, while one from a server stating nothing is refused at 1 MiB as before. The conversation's tests check that the pill says `Syncing space... 45%` while a long frame arrives, nothing once all of it has, and how the connection is once it is lost part way through; and that the headset states today's projection and the desktop window twice it, both with frames of 32 MiB. The draw cost above was measured with a throwaway release-build test of the frame step over 0 to 900 floating panels of 1, 5 and 30 lines, not kept. The conversation's tests check that it says it applies data ops, that a tick says the version held and a wall's change the version taken hold of, and that a refusal naming a wall is no problem shown.
- Check that the core builds for every client target, including `aarch64-linux-android`.
- Inspect the server for logic that depends on a client's hardware rather than its reported capabilities.

Whether the spectator client mixes audio itself, instead of the server mixing the spectator feed as `live-audio.md` currently requires, is a pending follow-up.
