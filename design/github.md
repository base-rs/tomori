# The GitHub Environment (Prototype)

This describes the GitHub environment's first prototype: one repository's open issues and pull requests as cards that participants lay out in a space. It is the first experiment of `docs/proposals/github-and-code-environments.md`, "inbox cards, layout only" (issue #148). It is built before the environment framework (#75, #76, #78), on narrow, clearly named protocol additions that are meant to move onto that framework, and it may be withdrawn or reshaped with it.

## Requirement

**Offered only when configured.** A server offers the GitHub environment, identifier `github`, labelled GitHub, only when it is started with `--github-repository OWNER/NAME`, after the environments it offers otherwise (see `environments.md`). Its token is the value of `GITHUB_TOKEN`, or else what `gh auth token` prints; a server given the flag without a token does not start, and says where it looked. A server built through the builder offers it with `Server::with_github` (see `builders.md`).

**The token stays on the server.** The token is used only for the `authorization` field of the server's requests to GitHub. It is never sent to a client, put in a space's state or kept spaces, told to the agent or logged, and a failure names where the token was looked for but never what was found. The server only reads: one GraphQL query, and nothing that changes GitHub.

**Cards.** The server reads the repository's open issues and open pull requests, at most 100 of each, keeps at most 200, most recently updated first, and shares them by every space in the environment. A card is the issue's or pull request's identity, such as `github://ioquatix/tomori/issues/97` or `…/pull/164`, its number, kind (issue, pull request or draft), title, author, labels, the combined status of a pull request's latest commit's checks (passing, failing, pending or none), and its age. Titles, authors and labels are cut to one line.

**Read while watched, within the rate limit.** The server reads the cards only while a participant watches them in a space that shows them: at once when none have been read, then no more than once every 60 seconds. When fewer than 100 requests remain of the token's rate limit, it waits for the limit to reset. After a failure it waits 30 seconds, doubling with each failure in a row up to 15 minutes, or longer if GitHub says to with `retry-after` or the limit's reset. Each read runs on a thread of its own and times out after 30 seconds, so that it never holds up the server. A failed read keeps the cards read last and says why on the inbox.

**Watching.** A space's state says whether it shows cards (`SpaceState::cards`). A client sends `WatchCards` only then, once for each space and connection, and the server sends it `Cards` at once, and again whenever the cards or their placements change, coalesced so that a burst sends one. Watching ends with leaving the space, and a space changed to another environment sends no more. Only participants watch and place cards; a visitor is refused, since a repository may be private. Older clients ignore the flag and are sent nothing; older servers never set it, and are sent nothing new.

**Placements are the space's.** A participant puts a card somewhere in the space's coordinates, or back in the inbox, with `PlaceCard`. The server accepts a placement only of a card on the board, finite and within 20 m of the space's origin on each axis, keeps at most 400 per space, forgetting first those of cards that have left the board, and sends the change to everyone watching. Placements are kept with the space across restarts, and never sent to GitHub. A card that closes on GitHub leaves the board; its placement is kept, and used again if it reopens.

**On the headset and desktop window.** Cards start in an inbox, a tray front left of home at about waist height, tilted to face the participant, in columns of 12, each card covering the one above but for its first two rows: its number, kind, checks and the start of its title. A card's frame shows its checks' colour; a card pointed at or carried is framed brightly. In a space, the right ray points at the nearest card it meets, or the nearer of a card and a worker, highlighting it with a light hover tick (see `feedback.md`). Squeezing the right grip while pointing at a card picks it up; it follows the ray at the distance it was picked up from, facing the participant, and letting go puts it there, or back in the inbox when let go over it. While the hand menu or the right wrist's transcript takes the ray, a card carried is put down where it is, and the grip does what those say (see `wrist-transcript.md`). A card put down shows where it was put until the server's cards agree, or for 3 seconds.

**Pointing tells the agent.** While X is held, the card pointed at or carried is remembered as workers are (see `interaction.md`), and sent with the words as `Pointed::Card`, by identity only. The server describes it from its own board when the participant stops speaking, as a line after their words, such as `[Pointing at the card for issue ioquatix/tomori#97 "Physics for cards" (by ioquatix, opened 3 days ago, labels enhancement), https://github.com/ioquatix/tomori/issues/97]`; a card the board lacks adds nothing. The status line and recording indicator name the card, such as "#97 Physics for cards".

**The agent's layer.** The environment's layer of a space's instructions says that the cards are the repository's open issues and pull requests, that layout changes nothing on GitHub, what the pointing line means, that the agent reads more with its own tools, such as `gh issue view`, that it does not have the server's token, and that it changes nothing on GitHub unless asked in words.

**Not in this prototype.** Write-back and triage boxes (#149), physics (#97), snapping to piles or surfaces, a done tray for closed cards, the spectator's view of cards, live movement of a card while another participant carries it, and showing cards to visitors.

## Rationale

The maintainer asked, in November 2026, for one of the proposal's more adventurous ideas as a prototype to try on the Quest 3, to start finding out what the GitHub environment should look like. The proposal recommends the inbox with layout only first, and keeping GitHub access on the server. A separate message keeps the space's snapshots small: they are sent many times a second while a reply streams, and 200 cards would multiply them. Watching mirrors the interest of #76, so that GitHub is read only while someone looks, and the token's rate limit, which its owner uses for other things, is spared. Placements in the space's coordinates and the identity-only pointing follow #78 and #81. The grip picks cards up, since the trigger attracts the swarm in a space and the right grip is free while the transcript is not showing. Visitors see no cards until the proposal's question about private repositories is decided.

## Scope

`crates/protocol` (`cards.rs`, `ClientMessage::WatchCards` and `PlaceCard`, `ServerMessage::Cards`, `Pointed::Card`, `SpaceState::cards`); the server's `github.rs`, `cards.rs`, the hub's watching, placing and pointing, the outbox's cards lane, kept spaces' placements and `--github-repository`; `tomori-scene`'s `board.rs`, the conversation's cards and pointing, and the frame step; `tomori-render`'s card panels.

## Validation

- `crates/server/src/github_tests.rs` checks the query and its parsing against a fake GitHub service: the token only in `authorization`, the cards' order, kinds, checks, labels and ages, the 200-card limit, refusals, rate limits and `retry-after`, a body cut short or too large, an unreachable or unresolvable host, HTTPS against the trusted roots, and the timeout. `crates/server/tests/github_token.rs` finds the token with a command in a process of its own.
- `crates/server/src/cards.rs` checks the refresh schedule, backing off, the rate reserve, the board through failures, placements and their limits, and the agent's description of a card.
- `crates/server/tests/github.rs`, against a fake GitHub, checks that nothing is read until someone watches, that watchers are sent the cards and see placements made by others, that visitors are refused, that placements are kept across a restart, that pointing at a card tells the agent and pointing at an unknown one adds nothing, that other environments show no cards, that a failed read is shown and not retried at once, and that the token appears in no message a client received, no prompt or instruction the agent was given, the record of spaces or any log line.
- `crates/scene/src/board.rs`, the conversation's and the frame step's tests check the inbox's layout, card faces, pointing, picking up, carrying, putting down, returning to the inbox, a card leaving while carried, watching once per space and connection, and pointing at a card while talking. `tomori-render`'s tests draw the inbox and cards on the shared test device; set `TOMORI_CARDS_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to see them.
- On the headset: start a server with `--github-repository ioquatix/tomori`, change a space to GitHub, and confirm the cards are legible in the inbox, that a card is easy to pick up and put down, that another participant sees it move, that it is where it was after a restart, and that asking "what's this one about?" while pointing at one is answered about that issue.
