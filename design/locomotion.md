# Locomotion

This describes how a participant travels in a space beyond the room they stand in, and how an environment allows it. It is the first part of the locomotion in #78.

## Requirement

**Environments declare it; Tomori owns the devices.** An environment declares a locomotion profile, and Tomori's clients turn the controllers, the keyboard and the mouse into travel for it. There are three:

- **None**, the default: room scale, as before. Nothing in this document changes anything in a space whose environment declares none, or anywhere outside a space. Sky and the Minimal grid declare none; the farmyard declares teleport about its yard (see `farmyard.md`).
- **Teleport**: point and release onto walkable surfaces that the environment declares, with a preview, turn in snaps of 30°, and step back a short way.
- **Fly**: teleport, turn and step back, and rise as well, up to a ceiling the environment declares; landing on a surface brings the participant back down to it.

There is no smooth artificial walking or turning in any profile; the only continuous artificial movement is rising, where an environment declares fly.

**Declared with the scenery, for now.** Until environments have server sides (#75), a headset build declares an environment's profile beside its scenery, by the environment's identifier, through `tomori::Builder::locomotion` or `tomori-render`'s `Sceneries::set_locomotion`, as `builders.md` describes. Both are provisional: once an environment's server side exists (#180), the profile is to move there, and these may be deprecated. A client applies the profile only inside a space bound to that environment, and only when it draws the environment's scenery: a client that shows the grid for an environment it lacks applies none, since the surfaces would not be seen. Once the server side exists, it is to declare the profile, so that everyone in the space has the same one.

**Walkable surfaces.** A walkable surface is a horizontal rectangle, aligned with the environment's axes, a disc, a ring sector or a polygon, at a height, declared in the environment's own coordinates, as its scenery is drawn; a client carries them into the space's coordinates with the environment's arrival (see `environments.md`), moving and turning each, so that a rectangle or a polygon may stand turned in the space, and raising or lowering a fly profile's ceiling with them. The environment declares only where a participant may land, already clear of edges and obstacles, each surface at the height of the top of what it draws there, so that a participant stands on what they see, neither sunk into it nor above it, and the ring where they would land is drawn on it rather than hidden beneath it. A teleport lands where the pointing ray first meets a declared surface from above, if that is at least 0.25 m along the ray and at most 15 m from the ray's origin horizontally; the ray must point at least about 1° below the horizontal; and a surface hides any beneath it from a ray that passes through it first, even where it is too near to land on. Nothing else blocks the ray in this first pass, since environments declare no colliders yet. A ray that meets no surface by these rules has nowhere to land.

**Curved and outlined surfaces.** A surface that is neither a rectangle nor a disc is declared as what it is, rather than faked with rectangles and discs that overlap: a ring sector, `Surface::sector`, for a curved deck, or a polygon, `Surface::polygon`, for anything else, such as a crescent, an L or a deck with a notch. Both are landed on where a ray first meets them from above, hide what is beneath them, are stepped back along, hold teleport pads and are carried into the space exactly as a rectangle and a disc are. Neither keeps a participant in from its edge, as no surface does: the environment declares only where a participant may land, already clear of edges, and a point on the edge itself is on the surface.

- **A ring sector** is the part of a ring about a centre, at its height, between an inner and an outer radius, from one direction round to another. It is exact, as a disc is, not a polygon that follows it: a point is on it when its distance from the centre is between the radii and its direction between the angles. The angles are in radians about the vertical from −Z, as a pad faces and the rig turns, so that 0 is ahead of the environment's origin, along −Z, and a quarter turn is along −X, and the sector runs from the first anticlockwise, seen from above, to the second: a second less than the first is a turn further round, and a full turn or more is the whole ring, which is how a surface with a round hole is declared, while one whose end comes back round to its start, as the start itself or a whole turn less, sweeps nothing and holds nothing, rather than the line along its start. The radii are taken without their signs and in order, as a disc's is taken without its sign, and an inner radius of nothing makes a wedge of a disc, whose point is on it. A sector with a radius or an angle that is not a number holds nothing, as a rectangle or a disc declared so does. To draw one, `Surface::sector_triangles` cuts its sweep into from 1 to 1024 equal steps, whose edges are chords of its arcs, so that the floor drawn falls short of its outer arc and reaches into its hollow by at most the radius times one less the cosine of half a step, about 1.2 cm for 32 steps over half a ring of 10 m, while what it holds stays exact.
- **A polygon** is a flat outline at one height, given as its corners in order, either way round, each the X and Z of a place in the environment's horizontal plane. Any simple outline is taken, convex or not: from 3 to 64 corners, each finite and within 1 km of the environment's origin on each axis, as an arrival is bounded (see `environments.md`), whose edges do not cross, which encloses at least a square centimetre, and none of whose corners comes within 1 mm of an edge it does not end, so that no corner is repeated, the first is not given again to close it, no edge folds back along another and the outline nowhere touches itself, where rounding would decide what is inside. Corners in a straight line are allowed. It has no holes: a deck with a hole is declared as outlines that meet around it, or, where the hole is round, as a ring. A part of an outline too narrow to aim at is still walkable, and harmless.
- **A bad outline is refused, saying why.** `Surface::polygon` checks the outline once, as the surface is declared, and answers an `OutlineError` for one that fails, naming the rule and the corners or edges that break it, rather than a surface that silently holds nothing, so that a build finds the mistake as it starts and not in a headset; nothing is checked again as a frame runs. The height is not checked, as a rectangle's is not: a surface at a height that is not a number is never met.
- **Tomori cuts a polygon into triangles itself**, once, as it is declared, by clipping ears; an environment never declares triangles, and one that cannot be cut up is refused. The triangles are for whoever draws the surface, such as an environment that draws its deck's floor from the outline it declares, through `Surface::outline` and `Outline::triangles`, in the coordinates it was declared in, which are its scenery's. Landing does not use them: whether a point is on a polygon is decided against the outline's own edges, by how it winds about the point, with a point on an edge or a corner on it, so that there is no seam between triangles for a point to fall through, and a rectangle declared as four corners holds what `Surface::rectangle` holds, its edge included, and, once carried, where the polygon turns about its outline's origin and the rectangle about its centre, the same up to rounding, a tenth of a millimetre near 1 km away.
- **What it costs a frame.** Testing a point against a polygon is one comparison with the box around it, kept from when it was declared, and then, within the box, one test of each edge, at most 64, in double precision, in which a point on an edge is found on it; a sector costs a square root and an arc tangent. Carrying a polygon into a space moves and turns where it stands and shares its outline, so that carrying a profile, which a client does each frame, copies no corners. How many surfaces a profile has is the environment's own business, as it was.

**An interface that can grow.** `tomori-scene`'s `Locomotion` and `Surface`, and each of their variants with fields, are `#[non_exhaustive]`, made with their constructors, `Locomotion::teleport` and `Locomotion::fly`, `Surface::rectangle`, `Surface::disc`, `Surface::sector` and `Surface::polygon`, a polygon's `Outline` has private fields and `OutlineError` and its variants with fields are `#[non_exhaustive]` too, and teleport pads are a `Pad` with private fields, made with `Pad::at` and given to a profile with `Locomotion::with_pads`, and teleport targets a `Target` with private fields, made with `Target::object` and given with `Locomotion::with_targets`, so that colliders (#97), a fly speed, rotated surfaces or a form declared by the server can be added without breaking a build that declares them, as `clients.md` asks of `tomori-client` and `spaces.md` of `Resource`.

**The rig.** Everyone in a space is in one shared space, the space's scene coordinates, and each headset tracks its participant relative to their own play space, their room. Travel is a per-participant rig, a translation and a turn about the vertical, local to each client, that takes the participant's room into the shared space. Each client applies its participant's rig to the head, eyes, controllers, hands and pointing ray before the scene, pointing, the panels held by the hands, rendering, presence and the listener see them, so that all of them agree on where the participant is. A teleport moves the rig so that the head stands above the landing at the same physical height, with the room's floor at the surface's height; a turn turns the rig 30° about the head, which stays where it is; a backstep moves the rig 0.6 m straight back from where the head faces, horizontally, keeping the floor's height; flying raises the rig at 0.6 m/s, never above the ceiling. Travel belongs to one space in one environment: the rig goes back home, where it starts and where the participant stood before, which is where the environment has participants arrive, since home is always the space's origin, facing −Z, and the environment is carried into it (see `environments.md`), on leaving the space, on joining another, when the space's environment changes, and whenever the profile is none, before that frame's poses are placed with it, or, for a change the frame makes itself, such as leaving from the hand menu, from the next frame. On leaving, the rig going home moves nothing the participant sees: the environment, while it shrinks away, and the agent are drawn where they stood in the participant's room, carried home with them, until the environment has gone (see `arrival.md`). Leaving places the room nowhere new: back in the lobby, the participant stands where they stand in their room, whose origin is where the headset stood as the app started or as they last recentred, as on first launch, so that whatever travel did is undone and only steps taken in the room remain, which the system's recentre places again. Recentring on leaving is not done, since it would move the room under the participant and lay every later space out about wherever they happened to stand rather than their play space's middle. A teleport, turn, backstep or rise applies from the next frame, so that every pose in a frame is placed with the same rig. Recentring is unchanged: it places the room again as before and keeps the rig. The status pill stands in the participant's room (see `interaction.md`), on the headset and in the desktop window alike, so travelling carries it along.

**Controls.** On the headset, in a space whose profile is teleport or fly, the left stick, which does nothing else in a space (see `interaction.md`):

- **forward**, while the right controller points, aims a teleport, and returning the stick to the middle lands there. While it aims, the ray ends at the landing, highlighted, with a ring on the surface, its bars standing 2.5 cm above the landing and reaching 1 cm into the surface, so that it sits on what is drawn there rather than floating, and a surface drawn up to 2 cm above or 1 cm below where it is declared, such as a path's stones on the earth, neither hides it nor shows a gap beneath it, in every environment; or the ray is plain and short where there is nowhere to land, and returning the stick then does nothing. Losing focus, the right controller's ray or the space, or the hand menu or the right wrist's transcript showing, cancels the aim without travelling, and so does a return in the frame the ray is lost, or the left controller's stick being lost, which reads as returned.
- **left or right** turns 30° that way, once per push.
- **back** steps back as the stick returns to the middle: 0.6 m straight back from where the head faces, horizontally, once per push. Where the profile is teleport, it steps only along the surface the participant stands on, at the floor's height, as far as it goes without a gap, checked every 5 cm, and nowhere if that is less than 0.2 m, so that it never steps off an edge, down from a ledge or across a gap; where the profile is fly, the participant may hover, so it steps the whole way. Where the profile is fly, holding it back for 0.3 s rises instead, from then on, and returning it then steps nowhere.

Each push of the stick is one gesture, owned by the direction it first passes its threshold in, more forward than sideways or more sideways than forward or back: forward at 0.7 from the middle, sideways at 0.6, back at 0.7. The gesture lasts until the stick returns within 0.2 of the middle, and nothing else starts meanwhile: easing off while aiming, as from 0.9 to 0.55, keeps aiming rather than landing, and rolling the thumb sideways or back while aiming neither lands nor turns; a push that turned aims nothing, and turns no more, however it rolls; a push back, eased off or rolled sideways or forward, still steps back on its return, and turns and aims nothing; rising continues while the stick is still held back past 0.2, and rolling it elsewhere stops rising without turning or aiming. A push forward without the ray, or one that is cancelled, or that began while travel was inactive, as before the scenery grew in, does nothing until the stick returns to the middle. While a teleport is aimed, the ray serves only it: no worker, card or panel is pointed at, and the right grip picks up nothing. While a card is carried (see `github.md`), the left stick does nothing, since aiming would put the card down. While a projected panel is carried with the right grip (see `projection.md`), the left stick travels as ever: the panel stays in the hand, following the ray at the distance it was picked up from, through an aim, a teleport, a turn and a backstep, since the rig carries the ray as it carries the participant, so that a panel is carried across the space and let go, or put on a wall, there. Letting go of the grip while a teleport is aimed lets the panel go where it is, as anywhere else, and lands nothing until the stick returns. Nothing else changes meaning, and travel never happens while the hand menu or the right wrist's transcript shows, nor before the space's scenery has grown in on entering it, or on a change of environment, so that its surfaces are seen. Hand tracking alone does not travel by the stick in this first pass, since it has no stick to aim with, nor choose a pad, which takes the trigger or A; it steps onto one as anyone does, since that needs only the head.

**The desktop window** does the same with the left stick's keys, the arrow keys (see `desktop.md`): hold **↑**, point the mouse at a surface, and release **↑** to land at its ring; **←** and **→** turn; and **↓**, released, steps back, or, held, rises where the profile is fly. A released key returns its stick to the middle. The window draws the ring but no ray, as its cursor shows where it points. A teleport pad or target the mouse points at is highlighted and labelled, and the left button, the trigger, or **Enter**, A, takes the participant to the pad's partner, or onto the target; teleporting onto one with the arrow keys steps onto it.

**Teleport pads.** A profile of teleport or fly may declare pairs of teleport pads, each pad of a pair taking a participant to the other, both ways, so that an environment of several levels, such as a floor with racks below it and a deck above, needs no flying to go between them. A pad is a horizontal disc at a height, declared, as surfaces are, in the environment's own coordinates and carried into the space's with them, with `Pad::at` and its centre alone; it may say its radius, 0.5 m unless it does, which way a participant arriving on it faces, horizontally, and its name, which the label on the pad paired with it shows, kept to its first 24 characters with control characters dropped, a name left with none being no name. Each pad has exactly one partner, its pair's other. A profile uses the first 8 pairs whose pads are both finite, no wider than 2 m across from their centres, within 20 m of home on each axis, as the environment's anchors are bounded (see `environments.md`), and each standing on a declared surface, within 1 cm of its height and over it, so that whoever arrives stands on a floor; a pair with either pad otherwise is ignored, and the pads of a profile of none are, as nothing travels there. Turning travel off on the headset turns pads off with it.

- **Stepping on.** A participant stands on a pad while their head, as the rig places it, is over its disc and the room's floor is within 5 cm of the pad's height, so that someone hovering above a pad, or walking under it, is not on it, while one who sits or stands is. Standing on it for 0.4 s, counting no more than 0.125 s of any one frame, it takes them to its partner, so that walking across one does not, nor does one long frame take someone who has only just stepped on; arriving on it by teleporting counts as stepping on it. A pad found under the participant before they could step onto it, as on joining a space, or once travel has been inactive, as while the hand menu or the right wrist's transcript shows or before the scenery has grown in, is disarmed, as is the pad they arrive on: it takes nobody until their head has gone more than 15 cm beyond its edge, so that it never sends them straight back, nor anyone who did not step onto it.
- **Pointing and choosing.** The ray points at a pad where it meets the pad's disc, or the column 0.6 m high above it, as high as the chevrons drawn above it reach and no higher, so that nothing invisible takes the ray, within 15 m of the ray's origin horizontally, as a teleport's reach, unless a declared surface crosses the ray first, from above or below, as a deck hides a pad beneath it and, seen from beneath, one on top of it; a ray from within a pad's column meets only its disc. It points at a pad only while travel is active, no teleport is aimed and nothing is carried, and only where nothing else it may point at or take hold of is nearer: a card, a worker, an environment's object, a projected panel or a wall. A pad the ray meets nearer than a panel stops the grip pulling off that panel, as a card nearer does; the panel's frame is still lit, as one behind a card's is, which is accepted. Pointed at, the pad is highlighted, the ray ends on it, it ticks as any new target does, and a label beside it says where it goes, the partner's name, or Teleport, with ↑ or ↓ where the partner stands at least 0.5 m higher or lower, and how to go, `Trigger  go`, or `Click  go` in the desktop window. The pad is Tomori's own, chosen as arrival's objects and the hand menu's rows are, with the trigger, a pull begun on it choosing it once pressed as far as choosing an arrival object takes, and with **A**: either takes the participant to its partner without stepping onto it. A is the last of its meanings, after everything that decides something, a request, a queued entry's cancellation, the list and a checklist item, so that a pad is chosen with A only where A would do nothing else: a request waiting, or not yet read, is decided or refused by A, never answered by teleporting. A pull on a pad never attracts the swarm.
- **Arriving.** A pad takes the participant as a teleport does: from the next frame, with the head above the partner's centre at the same physical height and the room's floor at its height, through the blink, clicking as landing does; facing the way the partner faces, turned about the head, where it says, and otherwise the way they faced. Nothing else moves them that frame, and a teleport being aimed is dropped, until the stick returns to the middle.
- **Drawn by Tomori.** Every client that draws the space's environment and applies its profile draws its pads itself, once the scenery has grown in, in the scene with the ring where a teleport would land: a ring of bars flat on the floor about a dim plate, and above it two chevrons pointing up or down where its partner stands a storey higher or lower, or a small diamond where it is level, all in one colour, the cyan of Tomori's highlight, dimmer until pointed at, when the ring brightens to the hover highlight's colour and a second ring stands about it. An environment declares only where they are.

**Teleport targets.** A profile of teleport or fly may also declare teleport targets, `Target::object`: objects of the environment's own, such as a rock in a pool, which take a participant who points at one and chooses it to that object, one way, rather than to a partner, so that two targets, one at each end, make a way there and back. A target is the upright cylinder the ray points at it within, about the middle of its foot, its radius and height, declared, as surfaces and pads are, in the environment's own coordinates and carried into the space's with them, its axis staying upright; and where a participant arrives, the middle of its top unless it says elsewhere with `arriving`, which way they face there, horizontally, and its name, kept as a pad's is. Whoever arrives on a target's top stands on it: its top is a floor for whoever it takes there, at its height, but it is not a surface, so that a teleport aimed with the stick never lands on it, a backstep there steps nowhere, off it, and it is reached only by pointing at it. A profile uses the first 8 targets that are finite, no wider than 2 m from their axis, no taller than 4 m, they and their arrival within 20 m of home on each axis, as pads are, and arriving on their own top or on a declared surface, within 1 cm of its height and over it.

- **Pointing and choosing.** The ray points at a target where it first meets its cylinder from outside it, its side between its foot and its top or its top from above, within 15 m of the ray's origin horizontally, unless a declared surface crosses the ray first, as a pad is, and not from over it, where whoever points stands on it and it would take them nowhere; of a pad and a target the ray meets, the nearer is pointed at. It is pointed at, chosen with the trigger or A, and gives way to anything else nearer exactly as a pad does. Pointed at, the ray ends on it, it ticks as any new target does, the label beside it gives its name, or Teleport, and how to go, `Trigger  go`, and the ring where a teleport would land stands where it takes them, on its top or wherever it says; the environment draws the object, and Tomori draws nothing else of it.
- **Arriving.** A target takes the participant as a pad does: from the next frame, the head above where it arrives at the same physical height and the room's floor at its height, through the blink, clicking once; facing the way it says, turned about the head, or else the way they faced. A pad they arrive on is disarmed until they step off it, as on joining, so that a target's arrival never fires one.

**Feedback.** The ray finding a landing while a teleport is aimed ticks as hover does, once until it has nowhere to land again, and landing clicks as choosing does, felt on the right controller (see `feedback.md`); pointing at a pad ticks the same way, and a pad's taking a participant clicks, once, whether by stepping, the trigger or A. Turning, stepping back and rising have no cue: the view moving is their signal.

**A blink on landing.** A teleport does not cut from one place to the next: the frame in which it lands is drawn dark, from where the participant was, and the view comes back from dark over 0.2 s from where they landed, on the headset and in the desktop window alike, panels included. A backstep blinks the same way; a push back that steps nowhere does not. Turns and rising do not blink. The spectator, whose audience is not the one travelling, does not blink.

**Turning travel off.** In a space whose environment allows travel, the hand menu's Space section offers **Travel: on**, under "On this headset", beside the status pill, and choosing it turns travel off on this headset, **Travel: off, room scale**, and on again. Off, the left stick travels nowhere and the rig goes home, as for a profile of none, so the participant stays room scale; nothing else changes. It is kept on the headset, as the status pill is, for whichever space they are in.

**Presence carries where each person travelled.** Presence carries the head, controllers and hands in the shared space (see `spaces.md`), so a client sends them with its rig applied, without a change to the protocol. To the server and every other client, a participant is only those positions: whatever shows presence shows each person where they travelled, today the spectator, which follows a participant's head and draws their controllers and hands (see `spectator.md`), and each voice, which comes from its head (see `spatial-audio.md`); participants' own clients draw no other participants yet. The rig itself is not sent, since nothing needs it: it may be revisited if something such as drawing a participant's play-area boundary does. The preview, the ray and the ring, is drawn only for the participant aiming. A teleport pad moves only the rig, so the server, the protocol and every other client need nothing for pads: others see the participant arrive where their presence puts them. Pads come from the build's own compiled code, as surfaces do, bounded as above, and never trap anyone: the participant can always teleport, step back or rise anywhere else the profile allows, turn travel off, or leave, and the rig goes home on leaving and joining.

**Voices are where people are.** Spatial audio places the listener and every source in the shared space: the listener at their head where they travelled, and each other participant at their head from presence, where they travelled, so that two people who travelled to meet hear each other where they stand. Two people can be in the same place, as when both stand at the centre of their own play space at home; that is allowed, and they can move apart (see `spatial-audio.md`).

**Known limits.** The spectator draws the sceneries it is given (see `builders.md`): `tomori`'s spectator modes draw the build's, but `tomori-spectator` draws only Tomori's own, so it shows another build's environment, the kind that declares travel, as the grid, from where the participant travelled. Comfort, the blink, the snap angle, the stick's thresholds and the ring's size are still to be confirmed on the headset.

## Rationale

Issue #78 and the environment framework proposal (`context/environments.md` on the `environment-framework-proposal` branch, section 5) set locomotion profiles, declared by the environment, with `none` the default, `teleport` onto declared walkable surfaces with a preview and 30° snap turns, and `fly` for vertical travel; no smooth artificial motion by default; travel as a per-participant rig of translation and yaw; presence carrying it, so that others and the spectator see each person where they travelled; and recentring unchanged. Teleport and snap turns avoid the discomfort of smooth artificial movement. The maintainer's prototype Fantail Observe proved the rig, teleports that keep physical eye height, snap turns about the head, a ceiling for flying and landing on the nearest supported floor, which this follows.

The issue and the proposal leave the controls and the details of each profile open; the first pass, in November 2026, took the details below from Fantail, where it proved them, or chose them where Tomori differs, and its pull request lists the choices for the maintainer's review. The left stick carries all of travel, rather than Fantail's right stick for turning and left stick click for flying, because Tomori's left stick is free in a space while the right stick moves the right wrist's focus and pages result cards and the left stick click mutes spoken replies; the maintainer confirmed this in November 2026, reviewing the first pass, so that turning never moves the wrist's focus. Fly includes teleport, as in Fantail, since rising without a way down is no use. Profiles are declared beside the scenery until the server side that the proposal gives them exists. Presence already carries poses in the space's scene, so sending them with the rig applied carries travel without a protocol change. The reach of 15 m is measured horizontally, unlike Fantail's along the ray, so that a participant who has flown high can still land straight below. A release in the frame the ray is lost cancels, as in Fantail, since a controller that stops being tracked reads as a released stick; a card carried blocks travel, since the board puts down a card whenever the ray is taken from it.

The review of the first pass found that a stick eased from 0.9 to 0.55 landed, and that rolling the thumb from forward to (0.8, 0.5) landed and turned in one frame, because aiming ended as soon as the stick was no longer pushed forward. A thumb resting near the threshold, or drifting sideways while aiming, landed the participant somewhere they did not choose, which is the discomfort teleport is meant to avoid. Landing only as the stick returns to the middle gives the aim hysteresis, as #78 asks for pinch, and giving each push to the direction that engaged first keeps a turn and a landing from sharing one gesture. Forward needs a firmer push than sideways, since aiming takes the ray from everything else, while a turn is over at once. The blink and the setting to turn travel off came from the same review's comfort findings: an instant cut is jarring, and #78's rationale for teleport is comfort, so anyone who still finds travel uncomfortable can stay room scale. Rising has no vignette, and the snap angle no setting, yet.

The maintainer, trying travel on a Quest 3 in November 2026, asked for pulling the left stick back to step back a short way, with the gestures and blink of the rest of travel, and for the left stick to keep travelling while a panel is carried, so that a panel can be taken across the space. A backstep of 0.6 m is about a pace, enough to see a wall that is too close; stepping only along the surface stood on, and as far as it goes, means a backstep near an edge still steps where it can, rather than refusing, while never stepping off an edge, down a ledge or over a gap the participant did not choose, and 0.2 m is the least that is worth a blink. In fly, back already rose, so a tap steps back and a hold of 0.3 s rises, which keeps both on one direction and leaves teleport's back unambiguous. A carried panel follows the ray, which the rig carries, so letting the stick travel with it keeps the panel in the hand with no new state; a card still blocks travel, since the board puts a card down whenever the ray is taken from it.

The maintainer decided, reviewing the first pass in November 2026, that the builder's declaration stays, documented as provisional, rather than waiting for the environment's server side (#180). The first pass kept voices on seats around the listener, carried by the listener's rig, and left carrying the others' rigs in presence to #183; the maintainer then decided, in November 2026, that travel stays local to each client and presence carries only shared-space positions, with every voice at its head and no seats, since that already places everyone where they are (see `spatial-audio.md`).


In November 2026 the maintainer decided that the Tomori wall, the agent and participants' arrival each have a fixed home the environment sets, rather than the wall following the agent, and that the arrival is the environment's: a position and facing, such as the street below a balcony, from which travel stays free. Home stays the space's origin, facing −Z, so the rig, presence and everything that goes home are unchanged; the environment's content, its walkable surfaces among it, is carried into the space instead (see `environments.md`).

In November 2026 the maintainer, leaving the farmyard for the lobby on a Quest 3 after moving about it, found themselves away from the lobby's origin, and asked whether that was expected. A headset test that travels by stick and by pad and leaves found the rig home, nothing carried and the lobby drawn exactly as on first launch, so what remained was where they stood in their room: the room's origin is where the headset stood as the app started, or at the last recentre, and stays there through every space, so steps taken in the farmyard carry back to the lobby. That is the intended rule, now stated above, with the system's recentre to place the room again.

In November 2026 the maintainer, in a Quest 3 in a downstream environment of three levels, a main floor, racks 4.5 m below and a deck 5.4 m above, found himself stuck among the racks, since the only way up was to hold the stick back and rise at 0.6 m/s, then aim and land, and asked for a teleport pad: a platform that, stepped on, takes you to the one paired with it, both ways, with a default geometry, and that, pointed at with the controller, is highlighted and taken with the button without stepping on it. Pads are declared with the profile and carried like its surfaces, since they move the rig as a teleport does and belong to the same environment's coordinates; a pad that must stand on a declared surface, and within the anchors' 20 m, cannot put anyone where they could not otherwise stand or take them out of the space's bounds. The pair is the smallest that serves a floor and a level below or above, and three or more pads in a ring or group are left open. Standing on a pad is measured from the room's floor, which a teleport sets exactly to the surface's height, and the head over the disc, rather than from the head's height, so that sitting or standing alike counts and someone flying over or rising past a pad does not; a dwell of 0.4 s keeps walking across one from firing it, and disarming the pad arrived on, and any pad found under the participant before they could step onto it, keeps it from sending them straight back or taking someone who joined, or closed the hand menu, standing on it. A pad is chosen with the trigger and A, as arrival's objects and the hand menu's rows are, which is Tomori's own way to choose what is pointed at; a pull on one is the pad's own rung of the trigger, after everything else of Tomori's and before the environment's, so that it never attracts the swarm, and A's meaning is the last of Tomori's, after the request's decision, the queue's cancellation and the checklist item, since on the desktop Enter, with the mouse nearly always over the view, decides a request and a prompt must never be answered by teleporting; pads need A only where it would otherwise do nothing. The column a pad is pointed at by is no taller than what is drawn, so that an invisible volume takes nothing from the swarm's trigger, as the renderer's test checks. The default look takes the landing ring's bars and the hover highlight's colour, so that a pad reads as Tomori's own and as somewhere to go; one colour for every pair, since the label names where each goes. Arriving faces the way the partner says, or else the way the participant faced, so that an environment that knows what a participant should see on arriving can say it, and one that does not leaves them as they were.

In November 2026 the maintainer, in a headset, building a downstream environment whose balcony is a crescent, which had to be faked with discs and rectangles that overlap, wondered whether walkable surfaces should be triangular meshes, so that a surface need not be a rectangle. He agreed instead that an environment declares a flat polygon's outline at one height and Tomori triangulates it: a loose set of triangles does not say which of its edges are the surface's edge, which anything that must know the edge needs, such as keeping a participant in from it, a collider along it or a line drawn around it, and cannot be checked for gaps and overlaps as one outline can; and that a ring sector might be a second built-in shape, since curved decks will be common. Any simple outline is taken, rather than only a convex one, since a crescent, an L and a notch are the shapes that asked for it, and telling whether an outline is simple is paid for once, as it is declared. The sector is a shape of its own, exact as the disc is, rather than a helper that makes a polygon: 64 corners follow half a ring of 10 m only to within about 1.3 cm, spend the corners an environment may want for the rest of an outline, and cannot close into a whole ring, which the sector gives as the one surface with a hole. Tomori keeps no margin in from a surface's edge, for a rectangle or a disc, so a polygon and a sector have none either, and a rectangle declared as four corners is that rectangle, up to rounding once carried; the outline is what would let one come to every shape alike. The triangles Tomori cuts are not what landing tests, since the outline's own edges decide it without a seam for a point to fall through; they are kept for whoever draws the surface, and cutting them proves the outline can be drawn. `Surface::polygon` refuses a bad outline with an error, where a rectangle or a disc declared with nonsense simply holds nothing, because an outline can be wrong in ways its author does not see, such as two edges that cross, and a deck nobody can land on, found in a headset, does not say why. `Surface` is no longer `Copy`, since a polygon shares its outline rather than every surface holding 64 corners, which breaks a build that copied one, noted in the releases.

In November 2026 the maintainer, on the farmyard's new trail in a Quest 3, found the ring where a teleport would land hidden under the path, and suggested casting onto the drawn geometry, declaring the surfaces on the path's top, or a taller ring. The trail was declared at the yard's earth while its track was drawn 3 cm above it, and the ring's bars reached only 0.5 to 1.5 cm above the landing. The surfaces are now declared at what is drawn, which also puts the participant's feet there, rather than casting onto the scenery, which Tomori does not have in a form to cast against and which would make a surface's height depend on what a client draws. The ring is taller as well, sunk into the surface rather than raised off it, so that a small mismatch, such as stones a little proud of the earth, never hides it and it never floats.

In November 2026 the maintainer, on the farmyard's trail in a Quest 3, asked for a rock by the waterfall that they could teleport to by pointing directly at it, so as to enjoy being in that space. Pads are portals, chosen where they stand and taking a participant to a partner, and drawn by Tomori as floor discs; what was asked for is a destination one points at, drawn by the environment as part of the place. So a target is a third kind of travel beside surfaces and pads, one way, rather than a pad of another shape: pointing at a pad goes to its partner, which would make pointing at the rock take one away from it. Its top is a floor only for whoever it takes there, not a surface, because a surface is where an aimed teleport lands, and the rock was to be reached by pointing at it; it also keeps a backstep from stepping off it into the water. The upright cylinder is what a pad's column is, carried unchanged by the arrival's turn, and fits a boulder; an object of another shape, such as the farmyard's log seat, is pointed at within the cylinder about it. A target pointed at shows the ring where a teleport would land, where it takes the participant, so that the hint is Tomori's in every environment while the object is the environment's. Targets are bounded to 20 m of home as pads and anchors are, so that nobody is taken beyond the space's bounds, where the agent and walls cannot follow; the farmyard's waterfall came 1.5 m nearer to keep its rock within them.

## Open questions

- How profiles move to the environment's server side (#75, #180), so that everyone in a space has the same one, and how a client that cannot draw the environment then behaves.
- The snap angle, the thresholds and the blink's length, once tried on a Quest; whether turns should blink too, whether rising needs a vignette, and whether the snap angle should be a setting.
- Whether hand tracking gets a teleport gesture, and whether surfaces gain colliders that block the ray (#97).
- Whether a landing should be kept a margin in from a surface's edge, for every shape alike, rather than the environment declaring surfaces already clear of edges, and whether a pad should then stand wholly within its surface; whether a polygon needs holes or more than 64 corners; whether Tomori should draw a surface's outline, for debugging or to show where one may land; and whether the server side's declaration (#180) carries outlines as corners.
- Whether a target should be highlighted on its own object, by the environment, as well as by the ring where it takes the participant; whether a target needs shapes other than an upright cylinder; and whether its top should hold a small walkable surface of its own, so that one may teleport about on a large one.
- Whether a group of three or more pads, or a pad with several destinations to choose from, is needed; whether an environment may hide the default look and draw its own pads; whether the agent should take someone to a pad's partner, as it summons; whether a pad's taking someone should sound a whoosh of its own rather than choosing's click; and the dwell, the step-off margin and the look, once tried on a Quest.

## Scope

`crates/scene`'s `locomotion.rs` (the profiles, surfaces, teleport pads' and targets' declaration and carriage, landing, backsteps, the rig, the stick's gestures, a pad's transport and the blink), `locomotion/outline.rs` (a polygon's outline: its checks, its triangles and whether a point is on it) and `pads.rs` (stepping onto pads, pointing at pads and targets, their labels, a target's ring and what is drawn of pads), the frame step's use of them in `scene.rs`, its trigger rung for pads in `scene/precedence.rs`, A's for them in `conversation.rs`, `scene/headset.rs` and `scene/desktop.rs`, the conversation's profile for its space and the setting that turns it off in `conversation.rs` and `hand_menu.rs`, and the voices at the heads in the shared space in `spatial_audio.rs`; `crates/render`'s `Sceneries`, and the ring, the pads and the blink drawn by its renderer; `crates/client-quest`'s builder and the clients that hand the profiles to the conversation and the listener to spatial audio (`lib.rs`, `xr.rs`, `crates/client-desktop`'s `desktop_participant.rs`, and `crates/client-spectator`).
