# Panel Context

This makes explicit what an environment's walls show: the context their panels depend on, such as a region and a time window, as state on walls and panels that the agent reads and sets, that everyone sees, and that an environment supplies content for. Pinning a panel pulled off its wall, freezing a chart, and a wall locked to one time window are all special cases of it. The maintainer approved phase 1. Its first step, the model, its wire form and the server's state, with each side told how its panels are bound, is a requirement below. The linked time cursor, which needs none of the model, is a requirement of `projection.md`. The rest of phase 1, and the later phases, are a proposal, step by step.

## Requirement

**The linked time cursor** (`projection.md`) links charts by their structured time axes (`TimeAxis`): live charts only on one wall, by how long before now, since each wall may have a time window of its own; and fixed windows, absolute instants in UTC, by the instant. It never reads labels and never matches a time of day. `TimeAxis::Fixed` is the window that fixed time uses below.

**Parameters.** A side declares the parameters its panels depend on with `Space::set_context(Vec<ContextParameter>)`, each with an id, a label, the value its own selection gives it now and, optionally, the values it may take, replacing what it declared before; it calls it again as its selection changes. The server refuses, saying why: more than 3 (`CONTEXT_PARAMETERS`), an id `valid_context_id` refuses, which is a panel id's form but never `time`, or another parameter's, and more than 16 choices (`CONTEXT_CHOICES`). It cleans each label onto one line and cuts it after 16 characters (`CONTEXT_LABEL_CHARS`), an empty label taking its id, and each value and choice after 32 (`CONTEXT_VALUE_CHARS`). Declarations are held to the side's call rate, which its publishing shares; a change of them is a change of what the space projects, paced as the side's changes are. They belong to the side's run, kept with the space's stage for that environment but never written, and are forgotten as the run ends.

**Dependence.** A published panel names the parameters it depends on, `Panel::depends_on`, at most 3 and `time`, each once, each a parameter id or `time`; the server refuses another, saying why. A chart depends on time already. A panel that depends on nothing is shown as published whatever its wall's context.

**Settings and inheritance.** Each wall of an environment's room but the Tomori wall, its own walls and walls owners added, and each panel its environment published, may hold a setting for each parameter, at most 4, the 3 and time: a value (`Setting::Fixed`), and for time only live over the last 1 minute to 1 week (`Setting::Live { minutes }`) or a window (`Setting::Window { from_ms, to_ms }`), two absolute instants in milliseconds since the Unix epoch in UTC, ending after it begins, spanning at most 31 days, never a time of day, from the first instant of the year 0 to the last of 9999, UTC (`CHART_EARLIEST_MS` to `CHART_INSTANT_MS`), as a chart's fixed window is held to. A value is cleaned, not empty, and one of the parameter's choices where it declares any; only a parameter its side declares now, or time, takes one; a panel takes one only for what it depends on. A parameter without a setting follows. **Settings of parameters a side no longer declares are kept.** Whether to declare a parameter is the side's own choice, so the server never drops a setting for it: chips show only what the side declares now, time always, and a setting of a parameter it does not declare is shown on no chip and its side is told no binding for it, since it does not use it; once it declares the parameter again, the setting shows and binds as before. A value fixed that is no longer among a parameter's choices is kept too, and still shown and told as fixed, so that what a chip shows is always what the side is told; only a new setting must be one of the choices. A run that has not declared yet, as after a restart, shows and binds only time until it declares. The agent's tools list every stored setting of walls and panels, those not shown marked as such ("not shown: the environment doesn't declare it now"), so that nothing set is ever hidden from it. A wall removed takes its settings with it, so that a wall added later with its identity begins following. What the state file holds is read back only as the server would set it, time's within its bounds and a parameter's a value of an id it takes, cleaned, at most 4 a wall or panel, the rest dropped, so that no file written by another version or by hand holds a wall to a setting the server would refuse. A panel's effective context is its own setting, else its wall's, else following: the side's value now for a parameter, and for time what its chart's `TimeAxis` shows, live over as many minutes as its points span, rounded up, or its window. Walls' settings are kept with the space's room across restarts; a panel's are kept with it, and with it as it rests, so that it comes back so set. Changing them is the stage's (`Stage::set_wall_context`, `Stage::set_panel_context`), which the agent's `set_context` reaches.

**Chips on the wire.** A projection's `Wall` gains `context`, a `ContextChip` for each setting of its own of a parameter its side declares now, or time, labelled as its side declares the parameter; none for a wall that only follows. A published `Panel` gains `context`, a chip for each parameter it depends on that its side declares, in their declared order, and one for time where it depends on time and a setting or its chart says what it shows, each saying its value, `ContextValue::Text`, `Live { minutes }` or `Window { from_ms, to_ms }`, and its `ContextSetting`, following, fixed by its own setting or inherited from its wall's. Both are part of their versions, absent where empty, ignored by an older client, and read as nothing from an older server; a setting or value a client does not know reads as following or unknown. A panel's bound for fitting a frame counts its chips.

**Chips drawn.** A client draws a wall's and a panel's chips, read-only but for a copy's time chip, which freezes it (below), at most 3 (`projection::chips::SHOWN`), those fixed first, then those inherited, then those following, each in the order sent, and `+N` after them for those left out, beyond the 3 or not fitting at all. Each is a small rounded backing behind its label and value, as `Region EU-WEST1`, or its value alone where they do not all fit, or cut to its share of the row, ending with `…`, with a cell of padding either side and a cell between. Its look says where its value comes from, apart at a glance: fixed by its own setting, bold white text on a teal backing, outlined brightly; inherited from its wall, light text on a fainter, greyer backing, without an outline; following, dim text with only a faint outline. A live window reads `last 15 min`, or `last 2 h` in whole hours; a window `02:20–02:50 UTC` within today, `11-12 02:20–02:50 UTC` within another day this year, `2025-11-12 02:20–02:50 UTC` another year, and each end with its date, as `11-11 23:40–11-12 00:10 UTC`, across midnight, always in UTC, today by the device's clock as the face is drawn. **Which panels:** only text, list and chart panels draw chips, as only those are what an environment publishes and so all the server gives a context; a code, image or volume panel, the agent's, draws none and names none, whatever it is sent. A panel's chips stand at the start of the row below its title, before its metadata, or a chart's status and unit, after a dim ` · `, where both fit whole; or else on a row of their own below it, in place of the rule, a thin rule drawn below them, so that neither crowds the other, which is decided by what its metadata always says, never by what a list adds to it as it scrolls. A flat wall's chips stand along its top edge after its label, a curved wall's after its label below its slots, no wider than its row of slots, and a wall of a group, its group's label and its own chips on its group's labelled wall, its chips alone at its top left on the others, no wider than its own board. They are drawn in the face or board drawn once and kept, and laid out again only as the chips change, never each frame, and a wall's curved label is kept by its chips too. Pointing at a panel's chip as words begin names it, `{"kind": "panel", "panel": "panel-3", "chip": "region"}` (`Resource::panel_chip`), found among the chips its face's layout laid out once with it, drawn or not, without laying them out again as the ray moves, and the server describes it from its own copy, after the rest of the panel, as `; pointing at its chip: "Region: EU-WEST1", inherited from its wall`, a window as instants in UTC; a parameter longer than any is dropped and the panel described alone; an older server ignores `chip`. **A wall's chips are not named by pointing yet**, since nothing of a wall is pointed at as words begin; the agent reads every wall's settings with `contexts`. The desktop window runs the same frame step and draws the same faces, so it shows and names chips as the headset does.

**The side is told.** Whenever a panel's effective binding changes, its own setting, its wall's, or the wall it is on, the server hands its side an `Intent` it makes itself, never from a client's, `Intent::context()`, a `Context` of the side's panel id, a `Binding` for each parameter it depends on, `Follows` or `Fixed(value)`, and its `Time`, `Follows`, `Live { minutes }` or `Window { from_ms, to_ms }`, at the same moments it tells moves (`panel-actions.md`), `Intent::participant` being whose message changed it, as a participant moving the panel onto another wall, or 0, the number the server gives nobody, for the agent, the layout or the side's own declarations. A side's selection changing tells nothing, since a following panel follows it; a parameter it stops or starts declaring is told, as the panel's bindings change. A side bound anew is told each panel bound to anything but following everything. The side then shows in the panel what its context says, held to its window where its time is fixed (below).

**Instances.** One panel id shows one context, so a panel shown in another context is an instance of it, a copy the server makes, as the agent's `copy_panel` asks, under an id the server mints, `~` and digits (`tomori_environment::is_instance`), which `valid_panel_id` refuses, so that a side never names a panel of its own so. The copy is kept as any panel its environment published, with `of`, the environment's id for the panel it is of, a copy of an instance being another instance of the same panel, and its own settings: the panel's own, those the agent gave over them. A copy is made only where its context differs from the panel's: the agent gives at least one setting, and one at least differs from what the panel shows by its own settings or else its wall's, or it is refused, saying the copy would show the same. It shows what the panel showed until its side publishes it, but without the panel's reference or its rows' keys, fields and references, which name the panel's rows in its side's terms, and until then a choice of one of its rows is refused, saying `That copy is not ready yet: its environment has not shown it in its context.` (`stage::published::COPYING`), never handed on with the panel's key as if it were the copy's; once its side publishes it, even just as it shows, its rows are its own. Its side is told it, whatever it is bound to, even where that follows everything, as an `Intent::context()` whose `Context::of` names the panel and whose `Context::panel` is the instance's id, as nobody's, and publishes under that id what the panel shows in that context, kept updated, which the server takes only for an instance it made, shown or resting, refusing any other `~` id, saying `only the server makes instances`; an update keeps it an instance. It is moved, thrown away and retracted as any panel, the side told of its moves and its dismissal by its id. It rests as any panel as its side's run ends, with `of` and its settings, and a side started again is told of each instance resting, bound as it was shown, by its own settings and else those of the wall it rests on, so that it publishes it again, when it comes back where it rested, an instance still. At most 8 instances an environment in a space, shown or resting (`stage::context::INSTANCES`), counted within the 32 panels it may show; a copy of a panel that depends on nothing, of a setting of a parameter it does not depend on or a setting `checked` refuses, of one not its environment's, onto the Tomori wall, and past either bound is refused, saying why. **Source withdrawn:** where its side retracts the panel an instance is of, each instance shown says so, its title ending ` (source withdrawn)` (`stage::published::WITHDRAWN`), is told to its side no more, takes no more updates and no choices, and is taken away as the hub sweeps 300 seconds later (`stage::published::WITHDRAWN_SECS`), as dismissed, so that its side is told it was and what it publishes to it after is ignored; one resting is forgotten at once, and one withdrawn does not rest as its side's run ends. A side may still publish to an instance a participant dismissed, which is ignored, not refused. An instance whose time is fixed is held to its window (below).

**Fixed time, one rule.** A published panel that depends on time, a chart or one naming `time`, whose effective time is a window, by its own setting or else its wall's, as an instance copied for a window or any panel on a wall fixed to one, shows only content its side declared for exactly that window: a chart whose `TimeAxis::Fixed` is that window, from and to, kept however few points it has, or any other panel published `Panel::for_window(from_ms, to_ms)`; the server refuses a window that does not end after it begins or reaches beyond the years 0 to 9999, and a chart published for a window whose axis is not that window, fixed, as a live one. Anything else its side publishes to it, live data or another window's, is ignored, so that it keeps what it shows and never scrolls (`stage/held.rs`). Until its side answers, it says so honestly instead of what it showed live: a chart, over the window, with no values and the status `Waiting for this window's data, 2026-11-12 02:20–02:50 UTC.`, which a client draws as `No data: …`, its body saying the same; any other panel that body, without rows, and a choice of one of its rows is refused, saying `That panel is waiting for its window's data.` A side that never answers, as an older one that ignores how its panels are bound, leaves no panel waiting for ever: after 45 seconds of waiting for the same window (`stage::held::WAIT_SECS`), as the hub sweeps, it says instead, in a warning tone, `No data for this window, 2026-11-12 02:20–02:50 UTC.`, until its side publishes content for it or its window changes, when it waits again. An environment without history, or one asked for a window past what it keeps, publishes for that window a chart over it with no values and a status saying why, as `no history kept`, or a panel for it saying so, never live data; a chart without values is drawn as no data already. A chart held to its window has its axis labelled by the server from the window alone, never by the side's labels: its first, middle and last points' clock times in UTC, `02:20`, `02:35`, `02:50 UTC`, and, for a window not within today by the server's clock, the date before the first, as `11-12 02:20`, and, across days, before any on another day than the one before, as `11-11 23:20`. A live time setting, or none, holds nothing. The side learns the window from `Context::time()`, `Time::Window`.

## Proposal

### The model

- **Parameters.** An environment's server side declares, as its state changes, at most 3 named parameters its panels depend on, each with the value it has now (the environment's own selection, such as the region its bridge has selected) and, optionally, the values it may take: `Space::set_context(vec![Parameter::new("region", "Region", "US-EAST1").with_choices(...)])`. Each parameter has an id like a panel's, a label of at most 16 characters, and values of at most 32 characters, cleaned onto one line; it has at most 16 choices. Tomori adds a parameter of its own, `time`, which it understands: *live*, the last N minutes, ending now, or *fixed* to an absolute range, two instants in milliseconds since the Unix epoch in UTC, as a chart's `TimeAxis::Fixed` says. It is never a time of day or a local time. A zone appears only in what is shown, such as labels and chips, which say `UTC`. Tomori treats every other parameter's values as opaque text. Tomori has no shared selection of its own: the side says what its selection is.
- **Dependence.** A panel says which parameters it depends on: `Panel::depends_on(["region"])`. A chart panel depends on `time` as well.
- **Settings.** A wall and a panel each hold, for each parameter, a setting: *follows*, the environment's current value (for `time`, live over the window the side publishes), or *fixed* to a value. A panel's effective context is its own setting, else the setting of the wall it is on, else *follows*. Settings are kept with the space, across restarts, as walls and where panels rest are.
- **Instances.** One panel id shows one context. A panel whose effective context differs from the one its side publishes under that id becomes an instance of it, under an id the server mints, such as `~7`, which `valid_panel_id` refuses so that no side can mint or forge one. The side is told each instance's effective context as an intent the server makes itself, as it makes a choice or a move (`panel-actions.md`): `Intent::context()`, naming the panel it is an instance of, the instance's id, each parameter's value and the time window. The side publishes the instance's content under that id, and is told again whenever the instance's effective context changes, and when its side starts again. At most 8 instances an environment in a space, counted within its 32 panels.
- **Fixed time, one rule.** While a panel's effective time is fixed, Tomori keeps what it shows and takes only content the side declares for that window. That content is a chart whose `TimeAxis::Fixed` is exactly the window, or a panel published with `Panel::for_window(from_ms, to_ms)`. A frozen chart already holds its window's content, so it never changes and costs nothing. A wall fixed to a window asks its panels' sides for content for that window, which an environment that keeps history, such as Observe, fetches.
- **No data for a window.** An environment without history, or one asked for a window past its retention, publishes a panel saying it has no data for that window, declared for it. A chart's no-data state does this already. It never shows live data under a fixed window, and Tomori never takes live data for one. Until the side answers, the panel says it is waiting for that window's data.
- **Labels are display.** A chart whose time is fixed has its axis labels written by Tomori as clock times in UTC from its `TimeAxis::Fixed`, the last ending ` UTC`, so that the window never scrolls what was seen away. Labels are only shown; linking, inheritance and the rule above use the structured window alone.

### What the existing behaviours become

| Behaviour | In the model |
| --- | --- |
| An environment's wall following its selection, today implicit | the wall's `region` follows |
| Pulling a panel off its wall | an instance, every parameter it depends on that followed fixed to what it showed; the wall shows a fresh following panel at once, in the slot it left |
| The pinned badge | the panel's chip for a parameter fixed |
| Freeze and unfreeze | the panel's `time` fixed to the window it shows, and back to live |
| A wall locked to a time window | the wall's `time` fixed; panels on it take it for whatever they do not fix themselves |

Only a participant's own move pins a panel; the agent and the layout move it as they do today. A side is never told a participant's pin as anything it could make itself, and nothing a side publishes fixes a parameter.

### The agent controls it

Every tool is declared with strict arguments (`mcp.md`). Built (the requirement above, **The agent's tools**): `contexts`, reading, and `set_context`, setting a wall's or a panel's parameter. Proposed with instances:

- **`copy_panel`** `{panel, wall, slot}` and a context, making an instance with it, such as the same chart for every region onto one wall.
- **`create_layout`**'s walls each taking a context, so that "show me the incident's two critical windows on two walls" is one call.
- Freezing as a panel's time fixed to the window it shows, `set_context` with a `shown` time.

"Line up the 02:30 spike across all regions" is then a wall's time fixed to 02:20–02:40 with `set_context`, and `copy_panel` of the latency chart for each region onto it.

### Everyone sees it

- A wall with any setting of its own shows a slim strip of chips along its top edge, read-only, such as `Region · US-EAST1`, `Time · 02:20–02:50 UTC` or `Time · live 30 min`. A wall that only follows shows none.
- A panel's own settings show as chips in its chrome below its title: `📌 Region · US-EAST1`, `❄ Time · 02:20–02:50 UTC`. That is the pinned badge.
- At most 4 chips on a wall or a panel: its parameters and time. Pointing at one says what it means on the ray's label. Chips are drawn with the face, laid out again only as the context changes.
- Direct control stays small: pulling a panel off pins it, the time chip of a chart toggles freeze with A, Enter or the trigger, and later a chip may offer the environment's declared choices.

### Tangible time tokens (a concept, not to build now)

Walls have sockets; a time window is a token, "last 30 min" or "02:20–02:50", that is plugged into a wall, pulled out, duplicated and frozen, one token for each critical window of an incident, plugged into different walls. A token is a context value. Plugging it in fixes the wall's parameter to it, and pulling it out lets the wall follow again. Duplicating copies the value. Freezing a live token makes it the absolute window it shows now. The chip strip is the token seen from afar. Nothing in the model changes for tokens: they are another way to set the same state.

### Phases

1. **The model, the agent's tools and visibility**, in steps:
   1. Parameters and dependence, and wall and panel settings with their inheritance, kept across restarts, absolute UTC throughout, with chips on the wire and each side told how its panels are bound: built, as the requirement above says.
   2. **Instances:** the server minting an instance for each effective context that differs from its panel's, its bounds, the side told each instance's context (`Intent::context()`) and republishing its content under the instance's id, again when its side starts again, and a side never able to mint or forge one; with the agent's `copy_panel` and `create_layout`'s contexts: built, as the requirement above says. Every later step stands on this one.
   3. Content declared for a window and the rule for fixed time, with no data said for an environment without history: built, as the requirement above says.
   4. The agent's reading, `contexts`, and `set_context` on a wall or a panel: built, as the requirement above says, and `copy_panel` and `create_layout`'s contexts with instances.
   5. Read-only chips on walls and panels, drawn and named by pointing: built, as the requirement above says.

   The linked time cursor came first, needing none of the model beyond a chart's time axis.
2. **Gestures:** pulling off pins and the freeze toggle on a copy's time chip, with the agent's `pin_panel` and `set_context`'s `shown`: built, as the requirement above says; and choosing a chip's value.
3. **Tangible tokens.**

## Rationale

In November 2026 the maintainer, in the headset, saw a spike on a downstream operations console's Charts wall, which shows charts for the region its bridge has selected. He wanted to pull the chart off, keep it, switch regions and look for correlated spikes elsewhere, but a chart pulled off kept following the selection. The environment's System wall already pins a panel dragged off it, on the move intent (`panel-actions.md`), and he asked for that as a general mechanism, with a badge saying what a panel is pinned to, a freeze that keeps exactly what is shown with absolute times, and a wall locked to one window so that charts from different regions line up. He then saw that walls could carry a context their panels inherit, made explicit rather than implicit, and asked for pin, freeze and the time-locked wall to be its special cases. He also asked that charts sharing a time share the pointer's time, and that the agent, rather than he, mostly coordinate the arrangement, while everyone can always see what a wall or panel shows. A tangible metaphor of time tokens plugged into walls was asked for as a concept only.

The model holds up because it adds only parameter declaration and the inheritance rule to what pin and freeze need anyway. It turns the time-locked wall and the agent's layouts into settings rather than mechanisms of their own, and gives tokens a meaning in advance. Instances are unavoidable, since one id shows one context. Tomori mints their ids so that a side cannot fake a participant's pin. Time is the one parameter Tomori interprets, because it rewrites axes, links cursors and enforces a freeze; every other parameter is the environment's.

## Scope

The first step covers `tomori-environment`'s `context.rs` (`ContextParameter`, `Binding`, `Time`, `Context` and their limits), `Space::set_context`, `Outlet::set_context`, `Panel::depends_on` and `Intent::context`; `tomori-protocol`'s `context.rs` and `Wall::context` and `Panel::context`; and `tomori-server`'s `stage/context.rs`, `stage/held.rs` (the rule for fixed time), `stage/pin.rs` (pin and freeze), `tomori-scene`'s `conversation.rs` and frame step (pressing a chip), `stage/published.rs` (dependence, a panel's settings resting with it), `stage.rs` (chips in the view, their bound, a room's and a panel's settings) and `hub.rs` (`channel_set_context`, telling contexts). The agent's tools cover `tomori-server`'s `hub/agent_context.rs` and `mcp.rs` (`contexts`, `set_context`). Drawing the chips covers `tomori-scene`'s `projection/chips.rs`, the faces of text and chart panels, a wall's board, curved label and boards of a group, `projection::faces`'s chip met by the ray and the frame step's naming of it; `tomori-protocol`'s `Resource::Panel`'s `chip`; and `tomori-server`'s description of a chip pointed at (`stage.rs`, `space.rs`). When built, the rest of phase 1 would cover: `tomori-environment`'s `Parameter`, `Space::set_context`, `Panel::depends_on` and `for_window`, and `Intent::context`; `tomori-protocol`'s context of a `Wall` and a `Panel`; `tomori-server`'s stage (settings, instances, the rule for fixed time, absolute labels), hub (telling the side), `mcp.rs` and the agent's tools; and `tomori-scene`'s chips. The linked time cursor's scope is in `projection.md`.

## Open questions

- Whether a parameter's choices should be offered by a chip's chooser in phase 2, or only by the agent.
- Whether a wall's `time` setting should apply to panels that do not depend on time, as a list of events in that window.
- Whether instances should outlive their side's absence, shown frozen, or rest until it runs again.
- Whether a live window shorter or longer than the side publishes, such as `live 60 min` on a 15-minute chart, should be asked of the side, as a fixed window is.
