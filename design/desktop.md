# Desktop Participation

This describes taking part in a server's spaces from a desktop window, without a headset or an OpenXR runtime.

## Requirement

**Taking part from a window.** `tomori --desktop` opens a desktop window that connects to a Tomori server and does what the headset does: arrival (`arrival.md`), visiting and knocking, pairing and admission (`security.md`), joining a space, its panel and controls (`interaction.md`), the hand menu (`hand-menu.md`), push-to-talk and spoken replies (`speech.md`, `live-audio.md`). It takes `--server`, `--name` and `--token` as the other participant modes do, and without `--server` lists the servers it discovers. The window without `--desktop` remains the server-less demonstration, the only place where **E** switches the scenery, since it has no space; with a server, the window shows the space's environment, and an owner changes it for everyone from the hand menu's Space section (see `environments.md`).

**The same behaviour, from the keyboard and mouse.** The window runs the frame step it shares with the headset, with the keyboard and mouse standing in for its controls, not a separate interface: each control in `interaction.md` has a key with the same meaning and the same safeguards, such as holding B for a second to leave a space, and the rules that cancel held controls on losing focus apply when the window loses focus. The participant stands at arrival's home at standing height and turns their head by dragging with the right mouse button. In a space whose environment allows travel, the arrow keys, the left stick's, travel as it does (see `locomotion.md`): hold **↑**, point the mouse at a surface and release **↑** to land at its ring, **←** and **→** turn 30°, and **↓**, released, steps back, or, held, rises where the environment flies; a teleport pad the mouse points at is highlighted and labelled `Click  go`, and the left button, or **Enter**, takes the participant to the pad paired with it. As on the headset, the window draws from the participant's room, which travel takes into the space, so the status pill comes along. The mouse points as the right controller's ray does, and its left button is the trigger. Holding **G**, the right grip, carries the projected panel pointed at, and while it is carried **Page Up** and **Page Down**, the right stick's forward and back, push it further or pull it closer, **[** and **]** turn it, **R**, the right stick's click, squares it up, and **Backspace**, B, throws it away for everyone, as on the headset; **Backspace** on its own never dismisses a panel, and the hint beside a panel carried names these keys (see `projection.md`). The mouse's wheel, three rows a notch, scrolls the panel the mouse points at that has more than it shows, for this participant alone, as the right stick does pointing at one, and the label on the ray says so (see `projection.md`). **Enter**, A, ticks or unticks the checklist item the mouse points at, for everyone, as A does on the headset, and the label says `Enter  tick`; the left button stays the trigger, which ticks nothing (see `projection.md`). With the mouse on a selectable object of the environment's client side, and nothing before it in A's order wanting Enter, Enter selects it, as A does, from when it is pressed until it is let go, in the same stream as the left button, one select at a time, and one struck and let go between two frames still presses and is let go in the next (see `environments.md`). With nothing carried and the mouse on nothing of Tomori's, Page Up, Page Down and the brackets are the right stick's push as ever, which in a space whose environment has a client side is the environment's, and the arrow keys, in an environment without travel, the left stick's, its too, in the order `interaction.md` gives each control (see `environments.md`). The hand menu opens and closes with a key, held in front of the participant as a raised left palm would show it, and is chosen in with the mouse. The window's title lists the mouse's controls in short from its first frame, as `Tomori | mouse: point · left choose · right-drag look · M menu · Esc quit`, within about 80 characters, and keeps them there, since a title bar costs nothing; `--help` and the startup log give every control. A waiting knock, which a headset cues on the wrist, is also named in the window's title, after its name and before the controls, which stay. A desktop has no controllers to vibrate, so its feedback is heard only (see `feedback.md`). It plays stereo, heard from its participant's head as it turns (see `spatial-audio.md`). A participant's presence reports their head only; a desktop has no controllers or hands to report.

**Pushing what an environment shows.** Pointing the mouse at an object of an environment's that takes the right stick (see `environments.md`), **Page Up**, **Page Down**, **[** and **]**, the right stick's, push it as the stick does, each key held a push, from when it is pressed until it is released, told to its client side with the object's identity. The mouse's wheel, with those keys released, is a push too, forward for a turn away from the participant, that goes as far as the wheel would scroll a panel, three rows a notch and a touchpad's 40 pixels a notch: what it is turned is kept, at most two notches, and pushed at the stick's rate, 15 rows a second all the way, the last frame only as far as is left, so that a fraction of a notch is a small push, and the push stays open a tenth of a second once it has pushed all it was turned, so that a slow scroll of a touchpad is one push. A panel scrolls with the stick as the square of how far past a dead zone of 0.2 it is pushed, while the axis an environment's object is told is the stick's, as it is, so the wheel's push goes as far as a panel scrolls for a client side that integrates its axis at 15 rows a second, `axis × dt × 15`; pointing at a panel, or at anything that does not take the stick, the wheel does as before.

**Its own audio streams.** On Linux, the participant modes (`--desktop`, `--xr`, `--console`) name their audio streams **Tomori**, and the spectator, `tomori-spectator` or the `tomori` spectator modes, **Tomori spectator**, unless the user sets `PIPEWIRE_ALSA`. The audio server remembers routes by application name, so routing the spectator to a capture sink (`docs/slack-huddle.md`) must not send a participant's speaker there too. A spectator can instead be given its output with `--audio-output NAME`, which applies to that process only; it is refused in the participant modes, since the same PipeWire setting would also choose their microphone.

**Engagement.** A focused window is engaged, a visible but unfocused window is distracted, and a hidden window is away, as `spaces.md` derives from the client's focus; a desktop has no sensor for being worn.

**Anti-aliasing.** Desktop windows, including the server-less window and the spectator's window, and the headset draw their scene with 4× multisample anti-aliasing where the adapter supports it for the target's format and for depth, and with a single sample otherwise. On the headset the intent extra `tomori.msaa` chooses: `4` or `on` (the default), or `1` or `off`, so that multisampling can be compared on the device or turned off if it costs frames; other values keep the default with a warning. Only 1 and 4 are offered, the counts wgpu allows without adapter-specific format features.

**Keeping samples on the GPU.** Each eye's scene pass resolves its samples into the eye's target as the pass ends, with the pass's resolve target, never with a separate copy or pass. The multisampled colour and the depth are cleared when the pass begins and discarded when it ends, and are transient attachments, so a tiling GPU such as the Quest 3's Adreno 740 keeps them in tile memory, writing only the resolved image out, and can leave them without memory of their own where the driver allocates lazily. Without lazy allocation, 4× costs about 158 MiB more at the Quest 3's 1680×1760 per eye: about 45 MiB each for every eye's multisampled colour and depth, in place of 11 MiB of single-sampled depth. They are per eye and not per swapchain image, so the swapchain's three images cost nothing more. Passes drawn after the resolve, such as the voice caption, draw single-sampled into the resolved target.

## Rationale

The user asked for a friend without a headset to try Tomori from the desktop client, admitted by knocking, in November 2026; until then only the terminal console and an OpenXR runtime could connect from a desktop, and the window could not. Reusing the headset's frame loop keeps one behaviour across devices, as `clients.md` requires. The user asked for anti-aliasing on the desktop display on first seeing the window, and then on the Quest 3, whose edges were harsh. Multisampling is applied only to the scene pass: offscreen passes, such as the cloud integration and the panel's text, keep their own targets.

## Scope

`crates/client-desktop`: the `--desktop` mode (`desktop_participant.rs`, its logic in `crates/scene`'s `scene/desktop.rs` and the shared frame step in its `scene.rs`), the server-less window (`desktop.rs`), the window's events and surface as its contents see them (`window.rs`) and the winit and surface calls themselves (`desktop_window.rs`), and the desktop's microphone and speaker (`desktop_audio.rs`, `cpal_audio.rs`); `crates/client-spectator`'s window (`spectator_window.rs`) and command line (`cli.rs`); and, in `crates/client-quest`, the `tomori` command line (`cli.rs`), the headset's sample count (`xr.rs`, `vulkan.rs` and the `tomori.msaa` extra in `lib.rs`), and, in `crates/render`, the renderer's sample count (`graphics.rs` and every pipeline drawn in its scene pass, including Sky's, in `crates/environment-sky-client`).

## Validation

- Unit tests check that the spectator and participant modes name their audio differently, and leave a user's `PIPEWIRE_ALSA` alone; with a participant and a spectator running, `pactl list sink-inputs` shows each under its own name.
- Unit tests check that Enter, held across frames, struck and let go within one, and beside the left button, selects the selectable object the mouse points at, once for each press, and is let go with the key or on losing focus.
- Unit tests check that the participant window's title lists the mouse's controls from the start, with a knock waiting between its name and them and after it is decided, and that each control it names is one the window's controls bind.
- Unit tests cover the participant's head: it starts at arrival's home facing arrival, the window's centre points where it looks, looking is clamped short of straight up and down, and the hand menu's palm is in view and faces the eye.
- The Vulkan test `multisampling_smooths_edges_in_both_environments` renders the scene pass's pipelines with one and four samples in each environment, without validation errors, and checks that four samples blend edges; set `TOMORI_MULTISAMPLE_PREVIEW=PATH` to save the comparison.
- The Vulkan test `headset_layers_multisample_every_pass` draws as the headset does, into the two layers of one image, with the swarm, controllers and a hand, the workspace and its labels, arrival, both environments and the caption after the resolve, with one and four samples, without validation errors; set `TOMORI_HEADSET_MULTISAMPLE_PREVIEW=PATH` to save it. A unit test covers reading `tomori.msaa`.
- Manually, on the Quest 3, the log says `Drawing 4x multisampled` and edges are smooth; started with `-e tomori.msaa off`, it says `1x` and edges are as before. Compare frame timing between the two.
- Manually, run `tomori --desktop --server HOST:PORT` against a server with tokens: as a visitor, join a space, knock, and be admitted from another device; then talk with Space held, and confirm that the panel, the hand menu, and leaving with a held Backspace behave as on the headset.
- The frame step's tests check that, with the mouse pointing at an environment's object that takes the right stick, Page Up, Page Down and the brackets push it, begun as each is pressed and ended as it is released, and that a notch of the wheel away from the participant pushes it forward; and that the wheel's push goes as far as it would scroll a panel, a touchpad's slow scroll one push in proportion, and a fast spin keeps at most two notches and ends within half a second (see `environments.md`).
