# Desktop Participation

This describes taking part in a server's spaces from a desktop window, without a headset or an OpenXR runtime.

## Requirement

**Taking part from a window.** `tomori --desktop` opens a desktop window that connects to a Tomori server and does what the headset does: arrival (`arrival.md`), visiting and knocking, pairing and admission (`security.md`), joining a space, its panel and controls (`interaction.md`), the hand menu (`hand-menu.md`), push-to-talk and spoken replies (`speech.md`, `live-audio.md`). It takes `--server`, `--name` and `--token` as the other participant modes do, and without `--server` lists the servers it discovers. The window without `--desktop` remains the server-less demonstration.

**The same behaviour, from the keyboard and mouse.** The window is the headset's frame loop with the keyboard and mouse standing in for its controls, not a separate interface: each control in `interaction.md` has a key with the same meaning and the same safeguards, such as holding B for a second to leave a space, and the rules that cancel held controls on losing focus apply when the window loses focus. The participant stands at arrival's home at standing height and turns their head by dragging with the right mouse button. The mouse points as the right controller's ray does, and its left button is the trigger. The hand menu opens and closes with a key, held in front of the participant as a raised left palm would show it, and is chosen in with the mouse. A waiting knock, which a headset cues on the wrist, is also named in the window's title. A desktop has no controllers to vibrate, so its feedback is heard only (see `feedback.md`). It plays stereo, heard from its participant's head as it turns (see `spatial-audio.md`). A participant's presence reports their head only; a desktop has no controllers or hands to report.

**Its own audio streams.** On Linux, the participant modes (`--desktop`, `--xr`, `--console`) name their audio streams **Tomori**, and the spectator modes **Tomori spectator**, unless the user sets `PIPEWIRE_ALSA`. The audio server remembers routes by application name, so routing the spectator to a capture sink (`docs/slack-huddle.md`) must not send a participant's speaker there too. A spectator can instead be given its output with `--audio-output NAME`, which applies to that process only; it is refused in the participant modes, since the same PipeWire setting would also choose their microphone.

**Engagement.** A focused window is engaged, a visible but unfocused window is distracted, and a hidden window is away, as `spaces.md` derives from the client's focus; a desktop has no sensor for being worn.

**Anti-aliasing.** Desktop windows, including the server-less window and the spectator's window, and the headset draw their scene with 4× multisample anti-aliasing where the adapter supports it for the target's format and for depth, and with a single sample otherwise. On the headset the intent extra `tomori.msaa` chooses: `4` or `on` (the default), or `1` or `off`, so that multisampling can be compared on the device or turned off if it costs frames; other values keep the default with a warning. Only 1 and 4 are offered, the counts wgpu allows without adapter-specific format features.

**Keeping samples on the GPU.** Each eye's scene pass resolves its samples into the eye's target as the pass ends, with the pass's resolve target, never with a separate copy or pass. The multisampled colour and the depth are cleared when the pass begins and discarded when it ends, and are transient attachments, so a tiling GPU such as the Quest 3's Adreno 740 keeps them in tile memory, writing only the resolved image out, and can leave them without memory of their own where the driver allocates lazily. Without lazy allocation, 4× costs about 158 MiB more at the Quest 3's 1680×1760 per eye: about 45 MiB each for every eye's multisampled colour and depth, in place of 11 MiB of single-sampled depth. They are per eye and not per swapchain image, so the swapchain's three images cost nothing more. Passes drawn after the resolve, such as the voice caption, draw single-sampled into the resolved target.

## Rationale

The user asked for a friend without a headset to try Tomori from the desktop client, admitted by knocking, in November 2026; until then only the terminal console and an OpenXR runtime could connect from a desktop, and the window could not. Reusing the headset's frame loop keeps one behaviour across devices, as `clients.md` requires. The user asked for anti-aliasing on the desktop display on first seeing the window, and then on the Quest 3, whose edges were harsh. Multisampling is applied only to the scene pass: offscreen passes, such as the cloud integration and the panel's text, keep their own targets.

## Scope

`crates/client-quest`: the `--desktop` mode (`desktop_participant.rs`), the shared desktop window setup (`desktop.rs`), the headset's sample count (`xr.rs`, `vulkan.rs` and the `tomori.msaa` extra in `lib.rs`), and the renderer's sample count (`graphics.rs` and every pipeline drawn in its scene pass, including `crates/environment-sky`).

## Validation

- Unit tests check that the spectator and participant modes name their audio differently, and leave a user's `PIPEWIRE_ALSA` alone; with a participant and a spectator running, `pactl list sink-inputs` shows each under its own name.
- Unit tests cover the participant's head: it starts at arrival's home facing arrival, the window's centre points where it looks, looking is clamped short of straight up and down, and the hand menu's palm is in view and faces the eye.
- The Vulkan test `multisampling_smooths_edges_in_both_environments` renders the scene pass's pipelines with one and four samples in each environment, without validation errors, and checks that four samples blend edges; set `TOMORI_MULTISAMPLE_PREVIEW=PATH` to save the comparison.
- The Vulkan test `headset_layers_multisample_every_pass` draws as the headset does, into the two layers of one image, with the swarm, controllers and a hand, the workspace and its labels, arrival, both environments and the caption after the resolve, with one and four samples, without validation errors; set `TOMORI_HEADSET_MULTISAMPLE_PREVIEW=PATH` to save it. A unit test covers reading `tomori.msaa`.
- Manually, on the Quest 3, the log says `Drawing 4x multisampled` and edges are smooth; started with `-e tomori.msaa off`, it says `1x` and edges are as before. Compare frame timing between the two.
- Manually, run `tomori --desktop --server HOST:PORT` against a server with tokens: as a visitor, join a space, knock, and be admitted from another device; then talk with Space held, and confirm that the panel, the hand menu, and leaving with a held Backspace behave as on the headset.
