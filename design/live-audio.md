# Live Audio

This describes the Rust server and the headset client, which uses it. The current Node bridge differs; changes to it are not reviewed against this document.

## Requirement

Participants in a space can talk to each other. Each participant sends one microphone stream, and each audio frame carries a gate:

- `off`: nothing is sent.
- `live`: heard by the other participants, visitors watching the space (see `security.md`) and the spectator feed.
- `agent` (push-to-talk): heard by the other participants, visitors and the spectator feed, and also transcribed for the agent.

Visitors send no audio; the server neither relays nor transcribes audio from a visitor's connection.

Push-to-talk audio is not a separate upload path. Only `agent` audio is transcribed; open-microphone conversation between participants never reaches the agent.

Live audio is explicitly toggled and off by default. The Quest left grip toggles it, and a `LIVE` badge on the left controller (see `hand-menu.md`) and the panel's status line show when the microphone is live. Holding X temporarily switches to the agent gate; releasing X returns to the previous live setting. Losing headset focus, taking the headset off or the headset sleeping (the participant becoming away, see `spaces.md`), disconnecting, leaving the space, or a microphone failure stops live capture and abandons a push-to-talk recording; live capture does not resume without another explicit toggle. Platforms without a capture implementation that can stop independently of the render thread refuse live capture rather than leave a microphone open after disconnection.

Captured audio belongs to the connection it was captured for. A client binds each capture to its connection and never sends that capture's audio on a later connection, even after the microphone is rearmed for it; reconnecting does not reopen the microphone. Once a connection has ended, no further audio captured for it is sent. On Quest, live capture checks the connection again after the device opens and does not start it if the connection has ended; a disconnection racing the start itself stops the stream before anything is read.

Push-to-talk ends when the client processes the release, within about a frame of it. On Quest, every sample the device has delivered by then is sent before the utterance ends: a read in progress and what the stream already holds, including a final partial frame. Audio still in the device's own pipeline at that moment, typically a few milliseconds, and up to one read after the release may be included in the utterance. Push-to-talk always captures with the recognition input preset, even when live audio was on. If the audio cannot be read intact, including when the device reports an overrun, the utterance fails visibly rather than being transcribed with a gap. On desktop, the capture library delivers what it has captured until its stream is closed at that boundary.

Each client also plays its own sound cues (see `feedback.md`), which are local: never sent, never a source, never counted as someone speaking and never muted with agent speech.

Each client mixes every incoming participant stream together with the space's agent speech stream, and agent speech played again for its participant from `REPLAY_SOURCE` (see `speech.md`), into one stereo output, each source placed where it is in the client's scene, and agent speech ducked while a person speaks, as `spatial-audio.md` describes. Agent speech played again is mixed like live agent speech: placed, muted and ducked with it, and silenced during push-to-talk; the two are one voice, so neither plays over the other's end (see `speech.md`).

What a participant records never plays back to them, through any gate. The server never relays a participant's audio to that participant, and a client also discards any audio attributed to its own participant number, in case a server does. The agent's speech is not the participant's own audio: its reply to a question they asked plays to them like everyone else.

A client that shows who is speaking takes it from the audio its mixer actually plays, measured before local volume, so muting agent speech locally silences it without hiding that the agent is speaking. The server mixes the same sources once for the spectator feed.

Playback and mixing are real time: audio that arrives too late to play is dropped from playback rather than delayed or replayed. This does not apply to transcription: every `agent` frame reaches recognition, in order, and a gap in `agent` audio fails the utterance with a visible error, as `speech.md` requires.

The agent speech stream is real time too. Synthesis runs ahead of playback, but the server sends each segment's audio at the rate it plays, a fifth of a second ahead, to whoever in the space is listening live at that moment, so that a hitch shorter than that anywhere between the server's pacer and a listener's speaker, in the pacer's thread, the network or the client receiving it, is not heard. A client keeps up to half a second of agent speech before discarding the oldest, room for what is sent ahead and for its speaker falling behind, and a quarter of a second of each participant's; it starts each as soon as 60 ms has arrived. The lead stays below the quarter of a second that clients built before agent speech had a limit of its own keep of it, so that they never discard what is sent ahead. A participant who joins or returns having missed part of the latest reply hears it from its start, paced the same way, and is not sent live frames until they have caught up, as `speech.md` describes; a participant whose client does not report engagement and joins mid-segment hears it from that point. On each connection, participant audio never displaces agent speech: agent frames are queued separately and dropped only when they could no longer play in time, which receivers see as a gap in the agent stream's sequence numbers.

A gap in agent speech says where it came from, in the logs, at most once every 30 seconds with how many more since: the server, when its pacer sent a frame of a segment after it was due to play, and whether it was waiting for synthesis, as when the server is too busy to synthesize a segment's next sentence in time, or was held up, as when its thread or the hub's lock was; a client, when a frame of a segment arrived later after the segment's first than its stamp says, held up by the network or the device; and the headset, when its speaker ran out of audio to play, with how much its device holds, said as it opens.

Participant playback should not feed back into their microphone stream:

- While a client records push-to-talk, it silences its own playback, fading out over a few milliseconds, so that agent speech and other participants playing through its speakers are not transcribed. Silenced audio is still consumed in real time, so when recording ends playback resumes live rather than replaying what was missed, except that agent speech that was playing as recording began stays silent for the rest of its message; a reply that starts while they record is not sent to them until they stop, and then plays from its start, as `speech.md` describes. Silencing is local: the agent speech stream and what other participants hear are unchanged. Sound cues are silenced too, except the short tick as recording starts, which plays as X is pressed while the microphone is still opening, and is not played when the live microphone is already open; the tick must never be transcribed. The client silences playback before opening a microphone for push-to-talk, or before switching an already-live microphone to the agent gate. Audio the output device has already buffered, typically a few tens of milliseconds, can still sound as recording starts; that is accepted for push-to-talk.
- An open (`live`) microphone does not silence playback, so participants can hear each other. The Quest captures live audio with Android's voice-communication input preset in an audio session of its own, asks for the platform's echo canceller on it, and plays as voice communication in the same session while the live microphone is on (see `spatial-audio.md`), which may provide echo cancellation on some devices but does not guarantee it. Until cancellation is measured on the device, with the echo test in `spatial-audio.md`, or a software canceller is integrated, users should use headphones or lower speaker volume if they hear feedback. Push-to-talk still silences local playback; it is to duck other sound instead only once echo cancellation is verified.

## Rationale

The existing audience microphone and push-to-talk recording are separate paths that only reach OBS and the transcription bridge. A shared workspace needs people to hear each other, including when someone addresses the agent. Agreed in the October 2026 design discussion.

A participant hearing their own recording back is disorienting and, through their microphone, feeds back; the user required in November 2026 that what a user records must not play back to that user. Silencing playback during push-to-talk was chosen because the Quest's `VoiceRecognition` capture preset and desktop capture have no echo cancellation, and without it a spoken reply playing while the participant records is transcribed and queued as their input, looping the agent's words back to it.

In November 2026, deciding the audio and haptics proposal (`docs/proposals/audio-and-haptics.md`), the user asked for echo to be measured first, keeping the feedback warning until measurements justify dropping it, and for push-to-talk to duck rather than silence other sound once echo cancellation is verified, but not before.

In November 2026 the user asked to restore the live toggle and explicitly chose to allow it with best-effort echo cancellation and a feedback warning rather than block it until cancellation is verified. This replaces the earlier requirement to block live audio until echo cancellation is available: the Quest requests the communication preset, while the actual availability and effectiveness of cancellation remain unverified and must not be presented as guaranteed.

In November 2026, on the headset, the maintainer heard the agent's speech drop out for 100 to 200 ms about half the times they took hold of a wall's handle or grab bar with the right grip while it spoke. Taking hold of a wall sends nothing to the server and plays no sound, only a haptic tick; the speaker's callback runs at real-time priority and never waits for the frame loop, the connection thread or a lock; and on the desktop a frame that takes hold of a wall costs what any other does, about 10 ms of the scene's step at the headset's optimization, with its walls, handles and hint. The headset's log showed no stall of more than six frames and no speaker underrun in the minutes it kept, and none of a grab, so the cause was not found. What was found is that agent speech had only a tenth of a second to spare: any hitch longer than that on the way, of the server's pacer, the network or the client, was heard, and one of 200 to 300 ms as a gap of 100 to 200 ms. The lead was doubled, which clients already deployed accept, and each part of the way now says when it causes a gap, so that the next one is placed. The server shared its machine with builds that loaded it far beyond its cores, and synthesis, which streams a segment a sentence at a time, can then fall behind its playback, which no lead hides: the pacer starts its clock again from the next sentence, and clients play on from it after a gap, replaying and discarding nothing, which its log now says.

## Scope

Client audio capture and playback, the microphone gate, server routing and the spectator mixer.

## Validation

- Test that `agent` frames are both forwarded and transcribed, `live` frames are only forwarded, and `off` produces no frames.
- Test that a client's output contains other participants and agent speech but not its own microphone, and that muted agent speech plays silently while still reporting a level.
- Test end to end that a participant who records, through every gate, while the agent answers them receives the agent's reply and no frame of their own; and that a client given audio attributed to its own participant number does not deliver it.
- Test that silenced playback fades out, consumes audio in real time while silenced and resumes live, and inspect that playback is silenced before an agent utterance begins and until it ends, whether or not live capture was already open.
- Test that frames arriving too late for playback are dropped from playback and mixing, while the same `agent` frames still reach recognition.
- Test that agent speech sent a fifth of a second ahead plays, with the headset's defaults, through 180 ms in which nothing arrives without a silent sample or a frame discarded, and that a participant's voice still keeps a quarter of a second; and, in the scene, that taking hold of a wall and carrying it while the agent speaks asks nothing of the connection, plays no sound, and leaves every sample of speech playing in order.
- Test that the server's pacer says, at most once every 30 seconds with the count since and how many waited for synthesis, when it sends a frame of a segment after it was due to play, and not for a segment's first frame, after a breath, or ahead of time; that a client says when agent speech arrives after it was due to play, measured from the frames before it, afresh after a breath, and not for participants' audio; and that the speaker thread asks an open output how often it ran out of audio and says so as the count rises, counting on from a count that went back.
- On the headset, after a gap in agent speech, read `adb logcat -s tomori` and the server's log for which of the three said it.
- Test that a missing `agent` frame fails the utterance visibly instead of producing a partial transcript.
- Test that agent speech is sent at its playback rate rather than as fast as it is synthesized, that a participant joining mid-segment without reporting engagement receives the rest of it, that one who reports engagement receives the reply from its start and no live frame of it, and that a backlog of participant audio on a connection does not displace agent frames.
- On a headset and a desktop, confirm that agent speech and other participants playing through the speakers are not transcribed from the local microphone while push-to-talk is held, and that the tick as recording starts does not reach the transcript.
- Test that silencing silences sound cues except the recording tick, and that cues are not sources.
- Test, with a scripted capture device, that a disconnect before or while it opens never starts it, that a disconnect during a read delivers nothing further, that a release during a read delivers that read and the device's remaining buffer including a final partial frame, that a failed final read or an overrun fails push-to-talk while an overrun only glitches live audio, and that push-to-talk during live audio captures with the recognition preset. Test that audio bound to an ended connection is not sent on a later, rearmed connection.
- Test live-to-agent-to-live switching and that focus loss, becoming away, disconnect, leaving the space and capture failure stop live audio, and that returning does not reopen it. On Quest, test for speaker-to-microphone feedback with two participants before relying on echo cancellation.

Latency targets for participant-to-participant audio are not yet agreed.
