# Hand Menu

This describes the headset's general menu, held on the participant's hand. It is the place for people and security now, and for settings and other controls as they move off the heads-up panel.

## Requirement

**Showing it.** Turning the left hand, or the left controller, so that the palm faces the participant's head shows the hand menu, anchored just above the wrist and facing the head; turning it away hides it. It works with hand tracking and with controllers, in arrival and inside a space. Showing and hiding have hysteresis, in angle and in time, so that the menu neither flickers at the boundary nor appears when the hand passes through the pose: it appears once the palm has faced the head, within about 35°, for about a fifth of a second, and hides once it has turned more than about 55° away. It moves with the hand while shown. It is in the world, attached to the hand, never to the view, in keeping with `arrival.md`. It, and everything else held by the hands, faces the middle of the head, above the neck and level with the eyes, rather than the eyes themselves, which swing about the neck as the head turns: turning the head with the hand still neither moves nor turns it.

**Choosing in it.** Items in the hand menu are pointed at with the right hand's ray and chosen with the trigger, as arrival objects are (see `arrival.md`), with the same hover feedback. While the menu is shown, the right trigger chooses in the menu rather than in the world behind it. Labels are anchored to their items.

**Pinning it.** Y in a space pins the menu open at its Space section, standing above the left controller and facing the head whatever its angle, until Y or B is pressed or the participant leaves the space, for anyone who finds the gesture awkward. While it is pinned, the right wrist's transcript does not show, as when the menu shows by pose.

**How it is arranged.** The hand menu is a top-level menu of sections; choosing a section opens it in place, and the participant can step back to the top level. Each section holds what applies to the participant at that moment, and nothing else. Inside a space, the top level and the Space section start with a **header**, which is only read: the space's title, wrapped over up to two rows, then what the agent is doing, its mode, the speech setting when brief, and how many are here, so that a glance at the left palm says where the participant is and what the agent is doing. Inside a space, the sections are **Space**, **Agent** and **People & security**; outside one, People & security alone.

**Space** holds the space's title in full, with its backend and the directory's last part; a row for each mode the agent offers, the current one marked, with the description of the one pointed at beneath it, choosing one switching the agent to it directly; the space's speech setting, brief or full, for everyone in it, as `speech.md` describes, which choosing switches; on this headset, muting spoken replies, as the left stick click does, and turning the status pill on or off (see `interaction.md`); and leaving the space, which first asks "Leave this space?" with *Leave* and *Stay*, so that a stray trigger pull does not leave. A visitor sees the modes and the speech setting, and cannot switch them.

**People & security** is in two parts:

- **Requests**, for admitting people: for a visitor, knocking on the space they are watching, and whether the knock is pending, declined or expired; for a participant, the space's pending knocks, each admitted or declined; and pairing, with its code while the device is pairing.
- **Roles & security**: their display name and role (see `security.md`); inside a space, the participants there, each with a swatch of their colour (see `spaces.md`), "You" marked and "away" for one who is away, the key to the transcript's colours (see `wrist-transcript.md`), and how many visitors are watching; and security notices, such as that the server's identity has changed or that the device is no longer admitted.

Inside a space, the second section is **Agent**: the instructions Tomori gives the space's agent (see `spaces.md`), with a row for each layer, Tomori's, the environment's and the space's, showing its start, or that it is empty. Choosing a layer opens its text in place, wrapped and cut to fit with a mark where it is cut, and stepping back returns to the section; the console shows every layer whole. The section shows why while the agent has not taken them, and offers owners a way to clear the space's own; it says so when the agent takes none. Typing on the headset is not practical yet, so the space's layer is given or changed from the console, which shows the layers in full.

**On the left controller: your voice.** The left hand is the participant's voice and the space, so what they say is shown where they say it from:

- **The recording indicator.** While X is held to talk, a small card rises out of the left controller, above X and Y and a little forward, facing the head with its rows level, and follows the controller through a short smoothing filter, of about 60 ms, so that hand tremor does not shake it. It grows in over about a tenth of a second. It shows `REC` with the time so far, against the server's limit when the server gives one (see `speech.md`), with a bar of the time used; the last two rows of the words heard so far, or, when none have arrived for 3 seconds, that the words are transcribed on release; and "Release X to send". While answering a question it shows `ANSWER` and the question, and otherwise the worker pointed at, which the words will be about (see `interaction.md`). In the last 30 seconds before the limit the time and bar turn amber and it says how long is left; in the last 10 seconds they turn red. After X is released it says "Sending…", then for about one and a half seconds where the words went, such as "Queued, 2nd" or "Answered". Words that were not sent are shown as "Not sent:" with why, with a red border, until X is pressed again or B dismisses the problem. Without a tracked left controller it follows the left wrist from hand tracking, and with neither, it stands low in the view, so that a recording in progress is never invisible. The timer is no longer shown above the panel.
- **The `LIVE` badge.** While the live microphone is on (see `live-audio.md`), a small `LIVE` badge with a pulsing dot stands just above the left controller, except while the recording indicator shows.

The desktop window, which has no controllers, shows the indicator low in the view.

**Cues.** Something waiting for the participant, such as a pending knock, highlights the section that holds it in the top-level menu, flashing until it is dealt with, and shows a small cue on the left wrist, such as a glow, while the menu is closed, so that they know to look. Both clear once nothing is waiting.

**The general menu.** New settings and controls are added to the hand menu as sections, rather than to the heads-up panel, and existing ones move to it over time. The menu's contents come from the client's state and the server's messages; it adds no protocol of its own beyond the actions it offers, which are ordinary client messages.

**Without hands or controllers.** Clients without tracked hands or controllers, such as the desktop window and the console, offer the same items another way, such as a key that opens them in a panel: the desktop window opens the menu with M and pins it with Tab, and the console shows People & security with `m`, the header and Space section with `s`, and the Agent section with `i`.

## Rationale

The user found that the hand metaphor fits Tomori: in some headset apps, the participant turns their hand to look at their palm or wrist to reach more of the interface and its settings. Knocking, seeing who is connected and handling security need an interface in the world, and a menu that is always at hand but out of sight until wanted keeps the view clear. The user chose it as the general menu, not only for security: a top-level menu whose sections other controls will join, with People & security first, its requests for admission kept apart from roles and security, and its section flashing when someone knocks. Stated by the user in the November 2026 authentication discussion.

In November 2026 the user also asked for the left-hand menu to gain space-level information and controls, such as the chat title and the current mode; the controller-anchored interface proposal (PR #89) added the header and the Space section beside the Agent section of issue #72, the participants' swatches, and Y pinning the menu, and the user accepted its recommendations.

In November 2026 the user asked for the indicator that says "Release X to send" to pop up from the left controller, where X is, while recording, rather than floating in the middle of the space; the controller-anchored interface proposal (PR #89) placed it there with the timer, the words heard and what became of them, and the user accepted its recommendations. Its size, a font pixel of 0.75 mm, and its offset from the controller are to be tuned on the headset.

In November 2026 the user found a panel held by the hand turning against the head: turning the head left moved it right. It faced the eyes, which swing several centimetres about the neck, and was raised above the wrist along an up that followed them; facing the middle of the head instead keeps whatever the hands hold still.

## Scope

The headset and desktop client (`crates/client-quest`): showing the hand menu from hand and controller poses, its items, pointing in it, and the wrist cue; the recording indicator and `LIVE` badge on the left controller; and the equivalent for the desktop window and console.

## Validation

- With hand tracking and with controllers, turn the left palm towards the head: the menu appears above the wrist after a brief hold and follows the hand; turning it away hides it. Test the visibility rule as a function of hand pose and time: it appears only after the hold within the inner angle, stays shown until the outer angle, and does not appear for a hand passing through the pose.
- Test that the menu's items and the recording indicator neither move nor turn as the head turns about the neck with the hand still. Confirm that the menu stays attached to the hand when the head turns, and that while it is shown the right trigger chooses an item in it and nothing behind it.
- Test the menu's arrangement and items against client states: the top level lists People & security, which opens in place and steps back; under Requests, a visitor sees knocking and its state, a participant sees pending knocks with admit and decline, and the pairing code appears while pairing; under Roles & security, everyone sees their name and role, everyone in a space sees its participants and visitor count, and security notices appear only while they apply.
- Test the Agent section against client states: it is offered only inside a space, shows each layer or that it is empty, opens a layer's text wrapped to fit and steps back to the section, shows a refusal, offers clearing the space's layer only to owners, and says when the agent takes none. On the headset, confirm that it shows the layers in effect and that only owners can change the space's layer.
- Test the recording indicator's content against the conversation: the time against the limit with its bar, or the time alone without one; amber with the time left in the last 30 seconds and red in the last 10; the newest words in view, or that they come on release once none have arrived for 3 seconds; answering a question; "Sending…", then where the words were queued or that they answered, for about one and a half seconds; and a failure until X is pressed again or B dismisses it. Test that every state fits the card, that it stands above and in front of the left controller and faces the head with level rows, that it falls back to the wrist and then the view, and that smoothing bounds tremor. On the headset, confirm that it rises from the controller while X is held and is easy to read, and that the `LIVE` badge shows while the live microphone is on.
- Test the header and the Space section against client states: the title wrapped over two rows with the activity, mode, speech and count; a row per mode with the current one marked and the pointed one's description; choosing a mode, the speech setting, muting and the pill; a visitor shown the mode and speech without switching them; leaving only after *Leave*, and *Stay* stepping back; Y pinning the menu at the Space section until Y or B; and a swatch per participant in People & security, with "You" and "away" marked.
- Confirm that a pending knock flashes People & security in the top-level menu and shows the wrist cue with the menu closed, and that both clear once the knock is resolved.
- On the desktop and console, confirm that the same items are reachable without hands or controllers.
