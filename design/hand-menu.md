# Hand Menu

This describes the headset's general menu, held on the participant's hand. It is the place for people and security now, and for settings and other controls as they move off the heads-up panel.

## Requirement

**Showing it.** Turning the left hand, or the left controller, palm up shows the hand menu, anchored just above the wrist and facing the head; turning it back hides it. It is the same gesture, shared in one implementation, as the right wrist's transcript (see `wrist-transcript.md`), mirrored to the left hand. Any pose that puts the palm up counts, wherever the hand is and wherever the head looks: it appears once the palm has faced within 45° of straight up, in the world, for about a fifth of a second, leaning towards the body, away from it, forwards or back, and hides once it has turned more than 55° from up, or the hand is no longer tracked, so that it neither flickers at the edge nor appears when the hand passes through the pose. In the neutral grip the palm faces inwards, 90° from up, so holding the controller as usual does not show it. With a controller, the left palm faces along the grip pose's +X axis, since OpenXR's grip +X points away from the left palm, and the right palm along −X. It works with hand tracking and with controllers, in arrival and inside a space. Whichever of the menu and the right wrist's transcript shows first wins, and the other does not show until it hides. It moves with the hand while shown. It is in the world, attached to the hand, never to the view, in keeping with `arrival.md`. It, and everything else held by the hands, faces the middle of the head, above the neck and level with the eyes, rather than the eyes themselves, which swing about the neck as the head turns: turning the head with the hand still neither moves nor turns it.

**Choosing in it.** Items in the hand menu are pointed at with the right hand's ray and chosen with the trigger, as arrival objects are (see `arrival.md`), with the same hover feedback. While the menu is shown, the right trigger chooses in the menu rather than in the world behind it. Labels are anchored to their items. The menu is a flat panel, facing the head from the middle of its rows' markers, and each row is pointed at by its rectangle in that panel: a row tall, centred on its marker, and the panel's whole width, from two markers' widths left of the marker to the panel's right edge, so that a long row is chosen along all of it. The ray is met in the panel's own plane and orientation, and the point where it meets the plane is tested against the rows' rectangles, so that a ray aimed at a row meets that row however far the menu tilts back and from whatever angle it is aimed. What is pointed at may change what the rows say, but never how many there are nor where they are, so that a ray held on a row stays on it, and the trigger chooses it. Neighbouring rows share their edge, so a point is in one row or the other, never both nor neither; past the panel's ends, or on a row that is only read, the ray meets nothing. Each row's marker and label, highlighted or not, are drawn within the same rectangle, in the same plane, from one layout (`hand_menu::row_rect` and `panel` in `crates/scene`), so that what is pointed at is what is drawn. Any other panel whose rows are pointed at uses the same implementation.

**Pinning it.** Y in a space pins the menu open at its Space section, standing above the left controller and facing the head whatever its angle, until Y or B is pressed or the participant leaves the space, for anyone who finds the gesture awkward. While it is pinned, the right wrist's transcript does not show, as when the menu shows by pose.

**How it is arranged.** The hand menu is a top-level menu of sections; choosing a section opens it in place, and the participant can step back to the top level. Each section holds what applies to the participant at that moment, and nothing else. Inside a space, the top level and the Space section start with a **header**, which is only read: the space's title, wrapped over up to two rows, then what the agent is doing, its mode, the speech setting when brief, and how many are here, so that a glance at the left palm says where the participant is and what the agent is doing. Inside a space, the sections are **Space**, **Agent**, **People & security** and **Sound**; outside one, People & security and Sound.

**Space** holds the space's title in full, with its backend and the directory's last part; for an owner, the avatar the space's backend or environment names, when this build lacks it and shows the swarm instead, on a row of its own as "Unknown avatar: river", only its identifier cut, a diagnostic that collaborators and visitors are not shown, since they see the default swarm and can do nothing about it (see `avatars.md`); who renamed it, if anyone did rather than its session's title being followed (see `spaces.md`), as "Renamed by Ada", or "Renamed by Tomori" for the agent; for an owner on a server that renames spaces, **Rename this space**, after which the next press of X says the space's new title rather than talking to the agent, the row saying "Hold X, say the new title" until then, and choosing it again, or B, changing nothing after all, as Change your name does, and, while someone has renamed it, **Use its agent session's title**, which lets its title follow its session's again; a title not changed, because no speech was heard, transcribing failed, the words were lost or the server refused it, is shown as the problem, "Title not changed:" with why, and the next press of X says the title again, until one is taken, B cancels or the owner leaves the space; a row for each mode the agent offers, the current one marked, choosing one switching the agent to it directly, and beneath them the description of the one pointed at, or else of the current one, in as many rows as the longest description takes, whichever is shown; the space's speech setting, brief or full, for everyone in it, as `speech.md` describes, which choosing switches; the space's environment, for everyone in it, with a row for each environment the server offers this client, of those it can present (see `environments.md`), the current one marked, choosing another changing the space to it, for owners only, as `environments.md` describes, while anyone else reads the environment's name alone; for an owner, in a space the server projects in, its projection walls, for everyone in it, with *Add a wall here* and, where an added wall stands, *Remove the nearest wall* (see `projection.md`); under *Tomori, for everyone*, on a server that says how the agent moves, how it moves, **Moves: follows the speaker**, **Moves: only when asked** or **Moves: stays home**, which an owner steps through and anyone else reads, and, while it follows, how long it waits, quiet, before going home, **Goes home after 90 s quiet**, stepping through 30 s, 1, 1½, 2, 5 and 10 minutes and **Goes home: never**; for an owner or collaborator, where it may move and unless it is already pinned within 3 m of them, **Call Tomori here**, which brings it beside them, or before them where it moves only when asked, and pins it there, for everyone; and while it is away or pinned, for an owner or collaborator on a server that keeps summons or says how it moves, **Send Tomori home**, which sends it back to the home its environment gives it and ends its pin, for everyone (see `environments.md`); on this headset, muting spoken replies, as the left stick click does, turning the status pill on or off (see `interaction.md`), and, in a space whose environment allows travel, turning travel off, staying room scale, or on again (see `locomotion.md`); leaving the space, which first asks "Leave this space?" with *Leave* and *Stay*, so that a stray trigger pull does not leave; and, last, for an owner on a server that deletes spaces, **Delete this space** (see `spaces.md`). It first asks "Delete this space?", with the space's title, saying that everyone here returns to the lobby and whether its agent session is kept, with *Delete it, for everyone*, then, where the space's session can be deleted, *Also delete its agent session*, off each time it is asked, which choosing turns on and off, and *Keep this space* last. Turning the session's deletion on changes what the rows say but not how many there are; the row that deletes is well above the last, which keeps the space, since the last is where the ray was as the rows changed. With the session's deletion on, *Delete it, for everyone* asks again, "Delete its agent session too?", saying that its conversation, and its workers' sessions, are lost for good and cannot be brought back, with *Delete the space and its session* and, last, *Back, keeping the session*, which returns to the first question with the session kept. Deleting closes the menu, and lets go of it if pinned, as leaving does, and the lobby then says what became of the space and its session. A visitor sees the modes and the speech setting, and cannot switch them; a collaborator or visitor is offered no deleting. On the console, `delete` says what deleting the space does, `delete space` deletes it, keeping its session, and `delete space and session` deletes its session too.

**Its text.** Every row of the menu fits its width, 40 cells: as many of the font's cells as fit in its panel, a CJK character taking two, which reaches 42 cm to the right of the rows' markers (`hand_menu::PANEL`), with each row's background sized to its text. Longer text, such as the title, a layer, a description, an error, a notice or the names of those here, wraps at words, breaking a word longer than a row, onto at most the rows it is given. In a row with a name, mode, environment or label in it, only that part is cut, so that the rest of the row is kept: "Turn {name} away" keeps "away", and a participant's role, "away" and "(now)" stay. Either ends with "…" where text is cut. Every kind of line break, such as CRLF, a lone carriage return or a form feed, breaks a line or separates words as a line feed does. The title on the top level is marked where it is cut; the Space section goes on with the rest of it. Its labels are drawn, as the transcript's are, with the same rules and characters (see `ui_text` in `crates/scene` and `text.md`): accented letters, typographic punctuation and Japanese as themselves, from the glyph atlas, and `?` only for what no bundled font has, such as emoji. Its labels draw their glyphs and their background in the menu's plane, so that their text is solid at any wrist angle.

**In front of what is behind it.** The menu is drawn by distance, as `rendering.md` requires: held in front of a projection panel, a card or the pill, its tiles and labels cover them, in both eyes, and a panel between it and the eyes covers it.

**People & security** is in two parts:

- **Requests**, for admitting people: for a visitor, knocking on the space they are watching, and whether the knock is pending, declined or expired; for a participant, the space's pending knocks, each admitted or declined, its visitor marked as one, as "Let Ada (visitor) in", since nothing vouches for a visitor's name (see `security.md`); pairing, with its code while the device is pairing, or "Pairing is closed" when the server refused it because pairing is closed (see `arrival.md`); and, for an owner on a server that says whether pairing is open, **Pair a new device**, which opens pairing on the server for 2 minutes so that a new device can ask to pair, and while it is open, by anyone, says how long is left and offers closing it, as "Pairing open, 1:42 left: close it", counting down each second, or "Pairing open: close it" once a pairing begun within it holds it past its time (see `security.md`). Choosing it while it is closed opens it, and while it is open closes it; the notice says "Opening pairing for a new device..." and then that pairing is open and to choose the new device's pairing object, or "Closing pairing..." and then that it is closed. Counting down changes what the row says, never how many rows there are. Nobody else is offered it, and a server that does not say offers it to nobody.
- **Roles & security**: their display name, as the server knows them, and role (see `security.md`); inside a space where they take part, on a server that renames, **Change your name**, after which the next press of X says the new name rather than talking to the agent, the row saying "Hold X, say your name" until then, and choosing it again, or B, changing nothing after all; a name that was not changed, because no speech was heard, transcribing failed, the words were lost or the server refused it, such as a name the rules refuse, is shown as the problem, "Name not changed:" with why, and the next press of X says the name again, until one is taken, B cancels or the participant leaves the space; inside a space, the participants there, each with a swatch of their colour (see `spaces.md`), "You" marked and "away" for one who is away, the key to the transcript's colours (see `wrist-transcript.md`), and how many visitors are watching; and security notices, such as that the server's identity has changed or that the device is no longer admitted.

Inside a space, the second section is **Agent**: the instructions Tomori gives the space's agent (see `spaces.md`), with a row for each layer, Tomori's, the environment's and the space's, showing its start, or that it is empty. Choosing a layer opens its text in place, wrapped and cut to fit with a mark where it is cut, and stepping back returns to the section; the console shows every layer whole. The section shows why while the agent has not taken them, and offers owners a way to clear the space's own; it says so when the agent takes none. Typing on the headset is not practical yet, so the space's layer is given or changed from the console, which shows the layers in full.

**On the left controller: your voice.** The left hand is the participant's voice and the space, so what they say is shown where they say it from:

- **The recording indicator.** While X is held to talk, a small card rises out of the left controller, above X and Y and a little forward, facing the head with its rows level, and follows the controller through a short smoothing filter, of about 60 ms, so that hand tremor does not shake it. It grows in over about a tenth of a second. It shows `REC` with the time so far, against the server's limit when the server gives one (see `speech.md`), with a bar of the time used; the last two rows of the words heard so far, or, when none have arrived for 3 seconds, that the words are transcribed on release; and "Release X to send". While answering a question it shows `ANSWER` and the question, and otherwise the worker pointed at as X was pressed, which the words are about (see `interaction.md`). While saying a new name it shows `NAME`, "Say your new name", the words heard and "Release X to rename", then "Renaming…", and then for about one and a half seconds "You are" and the name the server took, or "Name not changed:" with why and "X tries again, B cancels", with a red border, until X is pressed again or B cancels. While saying the space's new title it shows the same, with `TITLE`, "Say this space's new title", "Renamed:" and the title the server took, or "Title not changed:" with why. In the last 30 seconds before the limit the time and bar turn amber and it says how long is left; in the last 10 seconds they turn red. After X is released it says "Sending…", or "Answering…" for an answer, until the server says it has the words; then "Transcribing…", "Transcribing, part 2 of 3" when they are transcribed in several parts, or "Waiting to transcribe" while other words are transcribed first, another participant's or their own earlier ones (see `speech.md`), in the same text and colour; then for about one and a half seconds where the words went, such as "Queued, 2nd" or "Answered". From a server that does not say, it says "Sending…" until where the words went, as before. Words that were not sent are shown as "Not sent:" with why, such as "Not sent: no speech heard" for a recording with no speech in it, as when X was pressed by accident (see `speech.md`), with a red border, for about five seconds, longer than where words went so that why can be read, or until X is pressed again or B dismisses it first. Words that were lost are also the participant's problem, which the next words' outcome replaces (see `interaction.md`); a recording with no speech in it lost nothing, and is shown only on the card. Without a tracked left controller it follows the left wrist from hand tracking, and with neither, it stands low in the view, so that a recording in progress is never invisible. The timer is no longer shown above the panel.
- **The `LIVE` badge.** While the live microphone is on (see `live-audio.md`), a small `LIVE` badge with a pulsing dot stands just above the left controller, except while the recording indicator shows.

The desktop window, which has no controllers, shows the indicator low in the view.

**Cues.** Something waiting for the participant, such as a pending knock, highlights the section that holds it in the top-level menu, flashing until it is dealt with, and shows a small glow on the left wrist, steady and breathing slowly as the right wrist's does (see `wrist-transcript.md`), while the menu is closed, so that they know to look. Both clear once nothing is waiting. A new knock is also heard as two soft knocks and felt as two taps on the left controller (see `feedback.md`).

**Sound.** The top level's last section, **Sound**, after People & security and in or out of a space, holds this device's Feedback setting under "On this headset", as the Space section holds its other settings: **Sounds** and **Vibration**, each full, reduced or off, which choosing steps to the next level (see `feedback.md`).

**The general menu.** New settings and controls are added to the hand menu as sections, rather than to the heads-up panel, and existing ones move to it over time. The menu's contents come from the client's state and the server's messages; it adds no protocol of its own beyond the actions it offers, which are ordinary client messages.

**Without hands or controllers.** Clients without tracked hands or controllers, such as the desktop window and the console, offer the same items another way, such as a key that opens them in a panel: the desktop window opens the menu with M and pins it with Tab, and the console shows People & security with `m`, the header and Space section with `s`, and the Agent section with `i`, an owner changes the space's environment with `environment ID`, an owner opens pairing for a new device, or closes it, with `o`, as Pair a new device does, and anyone changes their own display name with `name NAME`, on a server that renames; an owner renames the space with `title TITLE`, or lets it follow its agent session's title again with `title` alone, on a server that renames spaces. The desktop window's menu has the same rows.

## Rationale

The user found that the hand metaphor fits Tomori: in some headset apps, the participant turns their hand to look at their palm or wrist to reach more of the interface and its settings. Knocking, seeing who is connected and handling security need an interface in the world, and a menu that is always at hand but out of sight until wanted keeps the view clear. The user chose it as the general menu, not only for security: a top-level menu whose sections other controls will join, with People & security first, its requests for admission kept apart from roles and security, and its section flashing when someone knocks. Stated by the user in the November 2026 authentication discussion.

In November 2026 the user also asked for the left-hand menu to gain space-level information and controls, such as the chat title and the current mode; the controller-anchored interface proposal (PR #89) added the header and the Space section beside the Agent section of issue #72, the participants' swatches, and Y pinning the menu, and the user accepted its recommendations.

In November 2026 the user asked for the indicator that says "Release X to send" to pop up from the left controller, where X is, while recording, rather than floating in the middle of the space; the controller-anchored interface proposal (PR #89) placed it there with the timer, the words heard and what became of them, and the user accepted its recommendations. Its size, a font pixel of 0.75 mm, and its offset from the controller are to be tuned on the headset.

In November 2026, after the right wrist's transcript was changed to show for any palm-up pose, the user found on the headset that the left menu did not behave the same: "the transcript is great, the menu is not", and asked for the two to behave the same, ideally shared. The menu still needed the palm to face the head within 35°, so it took a precise pose held near the face, where the transcript took any palm turned up. Both now share one palm-up gesture, with the same angles and hold, and keep only their own reasons not to show.

Later in November 2026, speaking from the headset, the user found the card saying "Sending…" for a while after releasing X, and could not tell whether the words were still on their way or the server was working on them. The card now says "Sending…" only until the server has them, and then what the server is doing with them. Its colour and timing are unchanged, and the change has no cue of its own, as `feedback.md` gives none for it.

In November 2026, speaking from the headset, the maintainer found the card, or the pill, still saying "Not sent: no speech heard" about six minutes after an accidental press of X, though several messages had been sent since. A failure stayed on the card until X was pressed again, and as the participant's problem on the pill and the right wrist until B dismissed it, so that it read as current long after later words had gone through. The maintainer asked for it to be a transient outcome on the card, not something needing attention for minutes. A failure now fades from the card after a few seconds, as other outcomes do; a recording with no speech in it, which lost nothing, is no problem at all; and the next words' outcome replaces words that were lost wherever they are shown.

In November 2026 the user found a panel held by the hand turning against the head: turning the head left moved it right. It faced the eyes, which swing several centimetres about the neck, and was raised above the wrist along an up that followed them; facing the middle of the head instead keeps whatever the hands hold still.

In November 2026, fixing the menu's text (#169), the rows were found to be pointed at only along their first 20 cm, while they had come to fill the 42 cm panel, so the right half of a long row could not be chosen; and, unifying the palm-up gesture (#140), their targets were found to be boxes aligned with the world's axes, so that, with the menu held low at the chest and tilted back, a steep ray could meet the row above the one aimed at. Each row is now its rectangle in the panel's own plane, laid out once for drawing and pointing, so that the two cannot drift apart again. Drawing every row in that one plane, rather than each label turned to face the head on its own, is what makes the rectangles drawn and pointed at the same.

In November 2026 the maintainer, speaking from their headset, closed pairing, also on a server with no owner yet, except during a short window an owner or the operator deliberately opens (see `security.md`), and asked for it to be opened from the hand menu, in People & security, as "Pair a new device", showing its countdown and closing on demand. It sits under Requests, with pairing and knocks, since it is about admitting a device, and is one row that opens and closes, so that the countdown is where it was opened.

In November 2026 the maintainer asked for owners to delete a space, and optionally its agent session, from inside the space through the Space section, with a clear confirmation like leaving's and a second, explicit one for the session, which cannot be undone; not from the server or space browser, so that the space is seen before it is deleted (see `spaces.md`).

In November 2026, speaking from the headset, the maintainer found it confusing to tell spaces apart, and asked for owners to be able to rename the space they are in from the hand menu, by saying the title as they say their own name, since typing on the headset is not practical yet (see `spaces.md`). Renaming is in the Space section, with the title it changes, and the row that lets the title follow the session again appears only while someone has renamed it.

In November 2026, reviewing that change (#171), pointing at a mode other than the current one in the Space section made its highlight flicker, so choosing it worked about half the time. The description of the mode pointed at was added as rows beneath it, and the menu, which rises from above the wrist, grew by them, raising every row. In the next frame the ray met another row, the description went away, and the menu dropped back. The description now has rows of its own beneath the modes, kept whatever is pointed at, so that pointing changes what the rows say and never where they are. When no mode is pointed at, they show the current mode's description, rather than standing empty.

In November 2026 the maintainer decided that the agent has a fixed home the environment sets, where it starts and goes back to when asked, and that a participant summons it with the trigger to look at something together; Send Tomori home is the hand menu's way to ask, beside asking the agent aloud, shown only while it is away so that the section does not grow otherwise (see `environments.md`). Later in November 2026 the maintainer approved that the agent follows whoever speaks to it and that the trigger no longer summons it; **Call Tomori here**, beside Send Tomori home, brings it without speech, as when someone's speech is off or the agent is busy with someone else's words, and works with any backend since the server does it without asking the agent; how it moves is an owner's choice, as the environment is, stepped through in place as the speech setting is.

## Scope

The headset and desktop client (`crates/scene`, drawn by `crates/client-quest`): showing the hand menu from hand and controller poses, its items, pointing in it, and the wrist cue; the recording indicator and `LIVE` badge on the left controller; and the equivalent for the desktop window and console.
