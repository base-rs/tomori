# Hand Menu

This describes the headset's general menu, held on the participant's hand. It is the place for people and security now, and for settings and other controls as they move off the heads-up panel.

## Requirement

**Showing it.** Turning the left hand, or the left controller, palm up shows the hand menu, anchored just above the wrist and facing the head; turning it back hides it. It is the same gesture, shared in one implementation, as the right wrist's transcript (see `wrist-transcript.md`), mirrored to the left hand. Any pose that puts the palm up counts, wherever the hand is and wherever the head looks: it appears once the palm has faced within 45° of straight up, in the world, for about a fifth of a second, leaning towards the body, away from it, forwards or back, and hides once it has turned more than 55° from up, or the hand is no longer tracked, so that it neither flickers at the edge nor appears when the hand passes through the pose. In the neutral grip the palm faces inwards, 90° from up, so holding the controller as usual does not show it. With a controller, the left palm faces along the grip pose's +X axis, since OpenXR's grip +X points away from the left palm, and the right palm along −X. It works with hand tracking and with controllers, in arrival and inside a space. Whichever of the menu and the right wrist's transcript shows first wins, and the other does not show until it hides. It moves with the hand while shown. It is in the world, attached to the hand, never to the view, in keeping with `arrival.md`. It, and everything else held by the hands, faces the middle of the head, above the neck and level with the eyes, rather than the eyes themselves, which swing about the neck as the head turns: turning the head with the hand still neither moves nor turns it.

**Choosing in it.** Items in the hand menu are pointed at with the right hand's ray and chosen with the trigger, as arrival objects are (see `arrival.md`), with the same hover feedback. While the menu is shown, the right trigger chooses in the menu rather than in the world behind it. Labels are anchored to their items. The menu is a flat panel, facing the head from the middle of its rows' markers, and each row is pointed at by its rectangle in that panel: a row tall, centred on its marker, and the panel's whole width, from two markers' widths left of the marker to the panel's right edge, so that a long row is chosen along all of it. The ray is met in the panel's own plane and orientation, and the point where it meets the plane is tested against the rows' rectangles, so that a ray aimed at a row meets that row however far the menu tilts back and from whatever angle it is aimed. What is pointed at may change what the rows say, but never how many there are nor where they are, so that a ray held on a row stays on it, and the trigger chooses it. Neighbouring rows share their edge, so a point is in one row or the other, never both nor neither; past the panel's ends, or on a row that is only read, the ray meets nothing. Each row's marker and label, highlighted or not, are drawn within the same rectangle, in the same plane, from one layout (`hand_menu::row_rect` and `panel` in `crates/scene`), so that what is pointed at is what is drawn. Any other panel whose rows are pointed at uses the same implementation.

**Pinning it.** Y in a space pins the menu open at its Space section, standing above the left controller and facing the head whatever its angle, until Y or B is pressed or the participant leaves the space, for anyone who finds the gesture awkward. While it is pinned, the right wrist's transcript does not show, as when the menu shows by pose.

**How it is arranged.** The hand menu is a top-level menu of sections; choosing a section opens it in place, and the participant can step back to the top level. Each section holds what applies to the participant at that moment, and nothing else. Inside a space, the top level and the Space section start with a **header**, which is only read: the space's title, wrapped over up to two rows, then what the agent is doing, its mode, the speech setting when brief, and how many are here, so that a glance at the left palm says where the participant is and what the agent is doing. Inside a space, the sections are **Space**, **Agent**, **People & security** and **Sound**; outside one, People & security and Sound.

**Space** holds the space's title in full, with its backend and the directory's last part; a row for each mode the agent offers, the current one marked, choosing one switching the agent to it directly, and beneath them the description of the one pointed at, or else of the current one, in as many rows as the longest description takes, whichever is shown; the space's speech setting, brief or full, for everyone in it, as `speech.md` describes, which choosing switches; the space's environment, for everyone in it, with a row for each environment the server offers, the current one marked, choosing another changing the space to it, for owners only, as `environments.md` describes, while anyone else reads the environment's name alone; on this headset, muting spoken replies, as the left stick click does, and turning the status pill on or off (see `interaction.md`); and leaving the space, which first asks "Leave this space?" with *Leave* and *Stay*, so that a stray trigger pull does not leave. A visitor sees the modes and the speech setting, and cannot switch them.

**Its text.** Every row of the menu fits its width, 40 characters: as many of the font's cells as fit in its panel, which reaches 42 cm to the right of the rows' markers (`hand_menu::PANEL`), with each row's background sized to its text. Longer text, such as the title, a layer, a description, an error, a notice or the names of those here, wraps at words, breaking a word longer than a row, onto at most the rows it is given. In a row with a name, mode, environment or label in it, only that part is cut, so that the rest of the row is kept: "Turn {name} away" keeps "away", and a participant's role, "away" and "(now)" stay. Either ends with "…" where text is cut. Every kind of line break, such as CRLF, a lone carriage return or a form feed, breaks a line or separates words as a line feed does. The title on the top level is marked where it is cut; the Space section goes on with the rest of it. Its labels are drawn, as the transcript's are, with the same rules and characters (see `ui_text` in `crates/scene`): accented letters, typographic quotes, dashes and ellipses as themselves or their nearest equivalent the font has, and `?` only for what it cannot show, such as Japanese (#103) or emoji, until the glyph atlas of #146. Its labels draw their glyphs and their background in the menu's plane, so that their text is solid at any wrist angle.

**People & security** is in two parts:

- **Requests**, for admitting people: for a visitor, knocking on the space they are watching, and whether the knock is pending, declined or expired; for a participant, the space's pending knocks, each admitted or declined; and pairing, with its code while the device is pairing.
- **Roles & security**: their display name, as the server knows them, and role (see `security.md`); inside a space where they take part, on a server that renames, **Change your name**, after which the next press of X says the new name rather than talking to the agent, the row saying "Hold X, say your name" until then, and choosing it again changing nothing after all; inside a space, the participants there, each with a swatch of their colour (see `spaces.md`), "You" marked and "away" for one who is away, the key to the transcript's colours (see `wrist-transcript.md`), and how many visitors are watching; and security notices, such as that the server's identity has changed or that the device is no longer admitted.

Inside a space, the second section is **Agent**: the instructions Tomori gives the space's agent (see `spaces.md`), with a row for each layer, Tomori's, the environment's and the space's, showing its start, or that it is empty. Choosing a layer opens its text in place, wrapped and cut to fit with a mark where it is cut, and stepping back returns to the section; the console shows every layer whole. The section shows why while the agent has not taken them, and offers owners a way to clear the space's own; it says so when the agent takes none. Typing on the headset is not practical yet, so the space's layer is given or changed from the console, which shows the layers in full.

**On the left controller: your voice.** The left hand is the participant's voice and the space, so what they say is shown where they say it from:

- **The recording indicator.** While X is held to talk, a small card rises out of the left controller, above X and Y and a little forward, facing the head with its rows level, and follows the controller through a short smoothing filter, of about 60 ms, so that hand tremor does not shake it. It grows in over about a tenth of a second. It shows `REC` with the time so far, against the server's limit when the server gives one (see `speech.md`), with a bar of the time used; the last two rows of the words heard so far, or, when none have arrived for 3 seconds, that the words are transcribed on release; and "Release X to send". While answering a question it shows `ANSWER` and the question, and otherwise the worker pointed at, which the words will be about (see `interaction.md`). While saying a new name it shows `NAME`, "Say your new name", the words heard and "Release X to rename", then "Renaming…", and then for about one and a half seconds "You are" and the name the server took. In the last 30 seconds before the limit the time and bar turn amber and it says how long is left; in the last 10 seconds they turn red. After X is released it says "Sending…", or "Answering…" for an answer, until the server says it has the words; then "Transcribing…", "Transcribing, part 2 of 3" when they are transcribed in several parts, or "Waiting to transcribe" while other words are transcribed first, another participant's or their own earlier ones (see `speech.md`), in the same text and colour; then for about one and a half seconds where the words went, such as "Queued, 2nd" or "Answered". From a server that does not say, it says "Sending…" until where the words went, as before. Words that were not sent are shown as "Not sent:" with why, such as "Not sent: no speech heard" for a recording with no speech in it, as when X was pressed by accident (see `speech.md`), with a red border, until X is pressed again or B dismisses the problem. Without a tracked left controller it follows the left wrist from hand tracking, and with neither, it stands low in the view, so that a recording in progress is never invisible. The timer is no longer shown above the panel.
- **The `LIVE` badge.** While the live microphone is on (see `live-audio.md`), a small `LIVE` badge with a pulsing dot stands just above the left controller, except while the recording indicator shows.

The desktop window, which has no controllers, shows the indicator low in the view.

**Cues.** Something waiting for the participant, such as a pending knock, highlights the section that holds it in the top-level menu, flashing until it is dealt with, and shows a small cue on the left wrist, such as a glow, while the menu is closed, so that they know to look. Both clear once nothing is waiting. A new knock is also heard as two soft knocks and felt as two taps on the left controller (see `feedback.md`).

**Sound.** The top level's last section, **Sound**, after People & security and in or out of a space, holds this device's Feedback setting under "On this headset", as the Space section holds its other settings: **Sounds** and **Vibration**, each full, reduced or off, which choosing steps to the next level (see `feedback.md`).

**The general menu.** New settings and controls are added to the hand menu as sections, rather than to the heads-up panel, and existing ones move to it over time. The menu's contents come from the client's state and the server's messages; it adds no protocol of its own beyond the actions it offers, which are ordinary client messages.

**Without hands or controllers.** Clients without tracked hands or controllers, such as the desktop window and the console, offer the same items another way, such as a key that opens them in a panel: the desktop window opens the menu with M and pins it with Tab, and the console shows People & security with `m`, the header and Space section with `s`, and the Agent section with `i`, an owner changes the space's environment with `environment ID`, and anyone changes their own display name with `name NAME`, on a server that renames.

## Rationale

The user found that the hand metaphor fits Tomori: in some headset apps, the participant turns their hand to look at their palm or wrist to reach more of the interface and its settings. Knocking, seeing who is connected and handling security need an interface in the world, and a menu that is always at hand but out of sight until wanted keeps the view clear. The user chose it as the general menu, not only for security: a top-level menu whose sections other controls will join, with People & security first, its requests for admission kept apart from roles and security, and its section flashing when someone knocks. Stated by the user in the November 2026 authentication discussion.

In November 2026 the user also asked for the left-hand menu to gain space-level information and controls, such as the chat title and the current mode; the controller-anchored interface proposal (PR #89) added the header and the Space section beside the Agent section of issue #72, the participants' swatches, and Y pinning the menu, and the user accepted its recommendations.

In November 2026 the user asked for the indicator that says "Release X to send" to pop up from the left controller, where X is, while recording, rather than floating in the middle of the space; the controller-anchored interface proposal (PR #89) placed it there with the timer, the words heard and what became of them, and the user accepted its recommendations. Its size, a font pixel of 0.75 mm, and its offset from the controller are to be tuned on the headset.

In November 2026, after the right wrist's transcript was changed to show for any palm-up pose, the user found on the headset that the left menu did not behave the same: "the transcript is great, the menu is not", and asked for the two to behave the same, ideally shared. The menu still needed the palm to face the head within 35°, so it took a precise pose held near the face, where the transcript took any palm turned up. Both now share one palm-up gesture, with the same angles and hold, and keep only their own reasons not to show.

Later in November 2026, speaking from the headset, the user found the card saying "Sending…" for a while after releasing X, and could not tell whether the words were still on their way or the server was working on them. The card now says "Sending…" only until the server has them, and then what the server is doing with them. Its colour and timing are unchanged, and the change has no cue of its own, as `feedback.md` gives none for it.

In November 2026 the user found a panel held by the hand turning against the head: turning the head left moved it right. It faced the eyes, which swing several centimetres about the neck, and was raised above the wrist along an up that followed them; facing the middle of the head instead keeps whatever the hands hold still.

In November 2026, fixing the menu's text (#169), the rows were found to be pointed at only along their first 20 cm, while they had come to fill the 42 cm panel, so the right half of a long row could not be chosen; and, unifying the palm-up gesture (#140), their targets were found to be boxes aligned with the world's axes, so that, with the menu held low at the chest and tilted back, a steep ray could meet the row above the one aimed at. Each row is now its rectangle in the panel's own plane, laid out once for drawing and pointing, so that the two cannot drift apart again. Drawing every row in that one plane, rather than each label turned to face the head on its own, is what makes the rectangles drawn and pointed at the same.

In November 2026, reviewing that change (#171), pointing at a mode other than the current one in the Space section made its highlight flicker, so choosing it worked about half the time. The description of the mode pointed at was added as rows beneath it, and the menu, which rises from above the wrist, grew by them, raising every row. In the next frame the ray met another row, the description went away, and the menu dropped back. The description now has rows of its own beneath the modes, kept whatever is pointed at, so that pointing changes what the rows say and never where they are. When no mode is pointed at, they show the current mode's description, rather than standing empty.

## Scope

The headset and desktop client (`crates/scene`, drawn by `crates/client-quest`): showing the hand menu from hand and controller poses, its items, pointing in it, and the wrist cue; the recording indicator and `LIVE` badge on the left controller; and the equivalent for the desktop window and console.

## Validation

- With hand tracking and with controllers, turn the left palm up: the menu appears above the wrist after a brief hold and follows the hand; turning it back hides it. Test the visibility rule as a function of pose and time, for both hands alike: a neutral grip, palm inwards, does not show it; palm straight up shows it after the hold, as do 40° towards the body, away from it, forwards and back; 50° from up does not show it from hidden but keeps it shown; 70° hides it; turning the head does not change whether it shows; and it does not show for a hand passing through the pose. Test the left palm from hand joints and from the grip's +X axis, facing inwards in a neutral grip.
- Test that the menu's items and the recording indicator neither move nor turn as the head turns about the neck with the hand still. Confirm that the menu stays attached to the hand when the head turns, and that while it is shown the right trigger chooses an item in it and nothing behind it.
- Test the menu's arrangement and items against client states: the top level lists People & security, which opens in place and steps back; under Requests, a visitor sees knocking and its state, a participant sees pending knocks with admit and decline, and the pairing code appears while pairing; under Roles & security, everyone sees their name and role, everyone in a space sees its participants and visitor count, and security notices appear only while they apply.
- Test that Change your name is offered only inside a space, to a participant, on a server whose `Welcome` gives a name; that choosing it makes the next press of X say the name, marked as naming, answering no question and about no worker, and choosing it again does not; that the indicator says each step and the name taken; and that the name shown is the one the server gives, from `Welcome` and `Name`, and starts afresh on another server. On the headset, change the name from the menu and confirm that the participants' list and the agent's next message use it.
- Test the Agent section against client states: it is offered only inside a space, shows each layer or that it is empty, opens a layer's text wrapped to fit and steps back to the section, shows a refusal, offers clearing the space's layer only to owners, and says when the agent takes none. On the headset, confirm that it shows the layers in effect and that only owners can change the space's layer.
- Test the recording indicator's content against the conversation: the time against the limit with its bar, or the time alone without one; amber with the time left in the last 30 seconds and red in the last 10; the newest words in view, or that they come on release once none have arrived for 3 seconds; answering a question; "Sending…" until the server has the words, then "Transcribing…", the part being transcribed, or "Waiting to transcribe", as the server says, and "Sending…" throughout from a server that does not say; then where the words were queued or that they answered, for about one and a half seconds; and a failure until X is pressed again or B dismisses it. Test that every state fits the card, that it stands above and in front of the left controller and faces the head with level rows, that it falls back to the wrist and then the view, and that smoothing bounds tremor. On the headset, confirm that it rises from the controller while X is held and is easy to read, and that the `LIVE` badge shows while the live microphone is on.
- Test the header and the Space section against client states: the title wrapped over two rows with the activity, mode, speech and count; a row per mode with the current one marked and the pointed one's description; choosing a mode, the speech setting, muting and the pill; an owner offered the server's environments with the current one marked, and anyone else, or anyone on a server that does not change environments, shown only the environment's name; a visitor shown the mode and speech without switching them; leaving only after *Leave*, and *Stay* stepping back; Y pinning the menu at the Space section until Y or B; and a swatch per participant in People & security, with "You" and "away" marked.
- Test that every row of every section fits the menu's width for long titles, names, modes, environments, layers, errors and notices, marked where cut, keeping the rest of a row whose name is cut; that the widest rows, as drawn, fill the panel without passing its edge; that a tilted label's quads lie in its plane, and render solid on lavapipe; and that labels draw accented letters as the transcript does. On the headset, confirm that the menu's text is solid at any wrist angle, with its background behind every row, and that rows 40 characters wide, about 40 cm, read comfortably above the wrist.
- Test, at tilts of 0°, 30° and 60° back and with rays from the head and from a hand ahead of, below, above and beside the menu, and steeply from beneath it, that a ray aimed anywhere at a row meets that row: at its marker, its middle, near its top and bottom edges, on either side of the edge it shares with its neighbours, and at the right edge of a row 40 characters long; that past the panel's ends, or at a row that is only read, it meets nothing; and that each row's marker and label, highlighted or not, are drawn within its rectangle and in the panel's plane. Test that pointing at any row of the top level, the Space section or the Sound section moves no row, and, over a second of frames with a steady ray, that a mode above and below the current one, an environment, the speech setting and the Sound section's levels stay highlighted and are chosen by the trigger. On the headset, confirm that the far end of a long row highlights and chooses it, and that with the menu held low at the chest the row aimed at is the one highlighted.
- Confirm that a pending knock flashes People & security in the top-level menu and shows the wrist cue with the menu closed, and that both clear once the knock is resolved.
- On the desktop and console, confirm that the same items are reachable without hands or controllers.
