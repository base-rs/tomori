# Projection

This describes agent projection's first slice (issue #82): text panels the agent projects onto walls in a space, which participants pull off, leave floating, put back and dismiss, and point at as they speak. It belongs to the space and the agent stage, not to any environment, and works in every environment. Later slices add the other content formats, manipulation and MCP tools #82 describes.

## Requirement

**Two kinds of wall.** Projection is spatially registered, and environments differ in shape, so there are two kinds of wall:

- **The Tomori wall.** Every space has exactly one. It belongs to the space and the central Tomori interface, not to an environment, and what is on it stays as the space changes environment. It stands beside Tomori: at the environment's `stage` anchor, when its registration gives one (`tomori_server::Environment::with_stage`, the first anchor of #79), and otherwise front right of home, beside the swarm, 2.8 m away at eye height, facing home, which is clear in Sky, the grid and the farmyard. When the space changes environment, it moves to where the new one puts it, with its panels.
- **Environment walls.** These belong to the environment the space is in now: those its registration declares, with `Environment::with_walls`, a general hook that names no particular environment, of which the server uses at most six; and those its owners add. Tomori's own server gives the farmyard one, a noticeboard on the right of the yard (see `farmyard.md`); Sky and the grid declare none.

A wall holds six panels, in three columns of two rows, with its label, `TOMORI` or `WALL`, above them. It is upright, about 2 m by 0.9 m, translucent, with its empty slots faintly marked.

**Adding and removing walls.** An owner adds a wall where they stand, from the hand menu's Space section (see `hand-menu.md`): it stands 1.8 m ahead of their head, level, a little below their eyes, upright and facing them. An owner removes the added wall nearest them from the same section, and the panels on it with it. Only walls an owner added are removed; at most six are added in each environment of a space, each within 20 m of the space's middle. A collaborator or visitor is refused both, and their menu does not offer them.

**What the server keeps.** The server keeps every space's walls and panels with the space, in its record of spaces, in a format of its own with a schema version, so that they survive a restart:

- the panels on the Tomori wall, for the space;
- for each environment the space has been in, its added walls and the panels on its walls or floating in it, keyed by the space and the environment.

Walls and floating panels of an environment are hidden, not deleted, while the space is in another, and shown again, where they were, when it returns. At most eight environments are kept for a space; going to a ninth forgets the one left longest ago. Panels on a wall the environment no longer declares are kept, but not shown.

**Which wall the agent uses.** When a participant asks something, the panels the agent projects in answer go onto the wall nearest that participant, of the Tomori wall and the current environment's walls, from where their head was in the shared space as their utterance began, as their presence last said. Panels the agent projects without anyone asking, such as in a reply after a restart, go onto the Tomori wall.

**Layout.** The server lays walls out, and a client draws each panel in the slot it is given. A wall's slots are numbered from its top left, as seen from in front of it, left to right and then down. A panel put on a wall, by the agent or by hand, takes its most prominent free slot; when none is free, the panel that has been on the wall longest leaves it, dismissed, and the newcomer takes its slot. Nothing else ever moves: a panel pulled off leaves its slot empty until the next one fills it, so that nobody's reading is disturbed. The newest panel on each wall is framed in blue, so that it is found at once.

**Floating panels.** Squeezing the right grip while pointing at a panel pulls it off its wall; it follows the ray at the distance it was picked up from, facing the participant, as a GitHub card does (see `github.md`). Let go within 0.4 m of a wall's plane and 0.25 m of its edges, it snaps back into that wall's layout, the nearest such wall; anywhere else it floats where it was left, upright and facing the participant, within 20 m of the space's middle. A floating panel belongs to the environment it was left in, since where it floats means something only in that environment's geometry; one pulled off the Tomori wall stays with that environment, and is hidden when the space changes environment, unless it is put back on the Tomori wall first. At most twelve float in each environment of a space; leaving another floating takes away the one floating longest. A panel let go is shown where it was let go until the server agrees, or for 3 seconds. While the hand menu, the right wrist's transcript or a card carried takes the ray, a panel carried is let go where it is. When a card and a panel are both on the ray, the grip picks up the nearer, as the ray points at the nearer.

**Dismissing.** Pointing at a panel and pressing **B** dismisses it, for everyone, once there is nothing else for **B** to back out of: the list, a selected queue entry, waiting for a name, the card saying no speech was heard and a problem shown come first (see `interaction.md`). A dismissal cannot be undone; the panel's text stays in the transcript. A visitor's **B** dismisses nothing, and says to hold B to leave, as it always does.

**Shared.** Everything here is the space's, the same for everyone in it, participants and visitors alike. Any participant pulls off, puts back and dismisses any panel, and the server sends the result to everyone. Visitors see the walls and panels, as they see the transcript that holds the same text, but change nothing.

**Text panels.** A panel has a title on one line, a body of Markdown-lite text, and a little metadata: up to four keys and values, and how long ago it was projected. Its face shows the title, bold, the metadata and age on the next row, a rule, and the body on twelve rows, 34 cells wide, styled as the right wrist's transcript styles a message, with the same renderer and the same glyph atlas (see `wrist-transcript.md` and `text.md`), ending with "…" where it is longer. Its font pixel is 1.9 mm, so a panel is about 60 by 36 cm, legible from about 3 m.

**Pointing.** In a space, the ray points at the nearest panel it meets where nothing else it meets, a card, a worker or what the environment shows, is nearer; a panel carried is pointed at all along. It highlights the panel and ticks lightly, as for any new target (see `feedback.md`). A small label on the ray, just in front of where it meets the panel, names the panel, its metadata, how long ago it was projected and where it is. A panel pointed at as **X** is pressed is what the words are about: the client sends only its identity, `{"kind": "panel", "panel": "panel-3"}` (`Resource::panel`), with the utterance's beginning, and the server describes it from its own store, never from what a client says, before the words, as `Ada: [Pointing at panel "Build status" (on the Tomori wall, projected 2 minutes ago; source: CI)] explain this one`. Everything in the description is cleaned onto one line, with double quotes and square brackets made single quotes and parentheses, so that nothing in it can end the block (see `spaces.md`). A panel the server does not show in the space's environment, or an oversized identity, is dropped and the words kept. The recording indicator and status line name the panel by its title, or the start of its body.

**How the agent creates panels.** The agent projects a panel by writing a fenced code block whose language is `panel` in its reply:

````text
```panel
title: Build status
source: CI
---
**3 crates** failed.
```
````

- **The format.** An optional header of `key: value` lines, each key a letter and up to 23 more letters, digits, `-` or `_`, ended by a line of `---`; then the body. Without such a header the whole block is the body, so prose with a rule in it stays prose. `title` is the panel's title; every other key is its metadata, lower case, the first of each kept, at most four.
- **Bounds.** Everything in a block is untrusted. A block larger than 8 KiB is not projected, nor one with neither a title nor a body, nor more than four from one reply. A title is cut to 80 characters, a key to 24 and a value to 80, each on one line as text others choose is cleaned (see `security.md`), without format characters, and a body to 30 lines and 1,200 characters, each line cleaned the same way; each ends with "…" where it is cut. The body is drawn as text, never as HTML.
- **Where it is found.** The server finds a reply's panel blocks when the reply completes, not while it streams, from its Markdown: a fence of three or more backticks or tildes, indented at most three spaces, whose info string starts with `panel`, closed by a fence of the same character at least as long, or else running to the end of the reply, as CommonMark reads it. A block of another language is skipped whole, so that a panel block quoted inside one is not projected. Each reply is projected once, as it completes, or, for one that completed while the backend was reconnecting, as the session is read again or the message restored; the history a space reads when it is bound again after a restart is not projected again.
- **Speech and the transcript.** A panel block is a code block, so speech skips it, as it skips every code block (see `speech.md`), while the transcript keeps the reply as the agent wrote it, the block shown as code.
- **The agent's instructions.** Tomori's own layer of the agent's instructions (see `spaces.md`) says that it may project a text panel when something is better read than heard, such as a list, steps, a summary or figures, in a fenced block of language `panel`, with its header, a line of dashes and a short Markdown body; that Tomori shows it without reading it aloud and keeps it in the transcript, so it says in a sentence what it shows; that it uses at most one or two in a reply, and only when they help; and that a participant may point at one as they speak.

**The protocol.** Projection has messages of its own rather than the environment state channel, which belongs to one environment's binding and is forgotten when the space changes environment, and cannot yet keep state across restarts (#202):

- `ServerMessage::Projection` carries the space's projection as seen in its current environment, complete each time: the Tomori wall first, then the environment's walls and the added ones, and the panels on them or floating. It is sent on joining, after the space's state, and whenever it changes, coalesced so that a burst of changes sends one, only to a client whose `Hello` sets `projection`, so an older client, which would refuse a message it does not know, is never sent it.
- `SpaceState::projection` says the server projects, and takes `AddWall` and `RemoveWall` from owners and `PlacePanel`, onto a wall or floating at a pose, and `DismissPanel` from participants. A client sends them only to such a server, so an older server is never sent them. The server checks each against its own store and refuses one it cannot do, saying why.
- The new types, `Projection`, `Wall`, `WallKind`, `Panel`, `Place` and `Meta`, are non-exhaustive, with constructors, and `WallKind` and `Place` read a newer server's values as `Unknown`, which a client does not draw.

**Not in this slice.** The other content formats, items as #82's general model with revisions, scaling, rotating, pinning, grouping and attaching to anchors, a time-to-live, a control to clear the agent's content, refusals reported to the agent, the MCP tools (with #100), panels in the spectator and the console, and panels a participant creates.

**Environment-specific content, later.** Generic content, text and later charts, may stand on any wall. Content types an environment defines will stand only on that environment's walls, and be hidden with it. Whether moving such content onto the Tomori wall degrades it to a text summary is an open question on #82.

## Rationale

The maintainer asked, in November 2026, for a simple first slice of #82 that shows how projection looks and works, without overfitting for later content types: projection walls with text panels, created from fenced blocks in the agent's replies, which participants rearrange and point at. The maintainer then refined it: projection is spatially registered, and environments differ in shape, so the space has one Tomori wall that travels with Tomori across environments, while each environment's walls and the panels left floating in it are kept for that environment, hidden and restored with it.

Fenced blocks are what agents already write, and keep a reply useful in the transcript as well as in the space; projecting when the reply completes means a block is never projected half written. The nearest wall to whoever asked puts the answer in front of them, as #82's section on presentation suggests, and capturing where they stood as their words begin follows how attention is captured (see `spaces.md`). A calm layout, where nothing moves but the panel that leaves, follows the maintainer's brief that panels must not shuffle while someone reads them; dropping the panel there longest, rather than showing "+N more", keeps a wall from growing a second page nobody can reach, and its text stays in the transcript. Visitors see panels because they see the transcript, which holds the same text; they change nothing, as they change nothing else.

The open questions of #82 were decided for this slice as follows: panels persist until dismissed, with no time-to-live yet; they are kept in a store of the stage's own, in the space's record, with a schema version, rather than on the state channel, which cannot yet keep state across restarts or across a change of environment, and could adopt #202's persistence and visibility once it has them; visitors may see every panel; there is no "clear agent content" control yet, and a dismissal cannot be undone; fenced blocks are projected when the reply completes, and a refused block is left in the transcript and not reported to the agent; and participants rearrange panels but do not create them.

## Scope

`crates/protocol` (`projection.rs`, `Resource::Panel`, `ClientMessage::AddWall`, `RemoveWall`, `PlacePanel` and `DismissPanel`, `ServerMessage::Projection`, `Hello`'s and `SpaceState`'s `projection`, and `Pose::upright_facing`); the server's `stage.rs`, with the panel blocks' parser in `stage/blocks.rs`, the space model's capture of where an asker stood and of a reply's panels, the hub's handling and the outbox's projection lane, the kept spaces' `stage`, `Environment::with_stage` and `with_walls`, and the `TOMORI` layer in `instructions.rs`; `tomori-scene`'s `projection.rs`, the conversation's projection and actions, the hand menu's walls, and the frame step; `tomori-render`'s drawing of the frame's projection; and the farmyard's noticeboard in `tomori-server-app`.

## Validation

- `crates/server/src/stage/blocks.rs` checks the parser: panel blocks lifted out of a reply with their headers; tilde and longer fences, an info string with more after the language, and a block left open; other languages, indented code, a backtick in a backtick fence's info string, and a panel block quoted inside another not projected; closing fences; empty blocks; a header only of key and value lines before a rule; keys cleaned, deduplicated and bounded; and hostile and oversized input: format and control characters, line separators, long titles, values and bodies cut with "…", a block over 8 KiB and too many in one reply.
- `crates/server/src/stage.rs` checks the default stage facing home; the wall nearest whoever asked, or the Tomori wall; that a new panel takes the next free slot and moves nothing until the wall is full, then replaces the panel there longest in its slot; pulling off, snapping back, keeping a slot on the same wall and dismissing, with refusals; the floating limit; environment walls and floating panels hidden with their environment and restored with it, the Tomori wall standing where each environment puts it, and a panel pulled off the Tomori wall staying with its environment; that only added walls are removed, with their panels, and their number bounded; forgetting the environment left longest ago; panels on a wall no longer declared kept but not shown; the description told to the agent, hostile titles inside their quotes; and the versioned format read again.
- `crates/server/src/space.rs` checks that a reply's panels are taken once, with where the speaker of the input delivered stood, and none from a reply completed again or one of tool calls alone; that an entry cancelled, or a space closed, forgets where its speaker stood, and an entry without it projects onto the Tomori wall; that a reply completed during a gap is projected, one restored complete projected once, and history read after a restart not; that a bounded number of replies is remembered; and that a panel attended to is resolved only from the stage, once.
- `crates/server/src/hub_tests.rs` checks that the projection is sent only to clients that read it, visitors included, with the space's state saying it projects; that a reply's panels go onto the wall nearest whoever asked, where they began speaking, and stay in the reply's text; that pulling off, snapping back and dismissing reach everyone, a visitor's attempts are refused, and refusals say why; that only owners add and remove walls; that environment walls and floating panels hide and return with their environment while the Tomori wall moves with its panels and the agent projects onto an environment's wall; that walls and panels survive a restart; and that a panel pointed at as the words begin, or named at their end by an older client, is described from the stage before them, while an unknown or oversized one is dropped. `crates/server/src/access.rs` checks who may send each message, `crates/protocol`'s tests the wire format, `crates/speech`'s tests that a panel block is never spoken, and `tomori-server-app`'s that the farmyard alone declares a wall.
- `crates/scene/src/projection` checks the wall's slots, the panel's face and the ray's label, drawing panels in their slots wall by wall and floating ones where they float, pointing, pulling off, carrying, leaving floating, snapping back, the time a panel let go is shown, something nearer taking the grip, and a wall added ahead of the participant. The conversation's tests check that the joined space's projection is kept, its panels moved and dismissed, B dismissing the panel pointed at once nothing else is open, the panel pointed at as X is pressed attended to, and an owner adding and removing walls. The hand menu's tests check its rows, and the frame step's that the ray names a panel and the grip pulls it off unless a card is nearer. `tomori-render`'s tests draw a wall with four panels, one pointed at and one carried, on lavapipe; set `TOMORI_PROJECTION_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to see them.
- On the headset: ask the agent for a list or steps, and confirm a panel appears on the wall nearest you, is legible, can be pointed at with its label, pulled off, left floating, snapped back and dismissed with B, that another participant sees each change, that pointing at one while asking "explain this one" is answered about it, that an added wall is where you stood, that changing to the farmyard moves the Tomori wall with its panels and shows the noticeboard, that changing back restores Sky's walls and floating panels, and that everything is where it was after a restart.
