# Diagrams in Volumes

This describes how the agent shows how a system is organised in three dimensions: a volume, a panel with depth, showing a diagram of nodes, edges and groups the agent describes and the server lays out, which participants handle as any panel and point into; and how its nodes point at things already in the world. It is stage 1 of `docs/proposals/three-dimensional-diagrams.md` and stage 1 of `docs/proposals/diagrams-in-the-world.md`, whose later stages remain proposals.

## Requirement

**A volume is a panel.** A volume is a `Panel` with a `diagram`, a `tomori_protocol::diagram::Diagram`, as a chart panel is one with a chart: it has a title, metadata, an age, an id, a version and a place, and is projected, laid out on walls, floated, carried, stretched or shrunk with both hands, its depth with it, docked, thrown away, kept with the space across restarts, moved by `move_panel` and listed by `panels` exactly as `projection.md` says of every panel. Its body is the diagram's outline, its nodes in no group, then each group as a heading over its nodes, each a list item with its detail, then its edges, a line each, as `Renderer → Octree: queries`, cleaned and cut as a panel block's body is; a client that draws no volumes shows that. By default it goes where a panel goes, onto the wall whoever asked was looking at if near enough to read, or else floating beside them; `place` takes a panel's forms.

**What the agent describes.** Structure, never geometry. A diagram has a layout, `layered` with a direction, `down`, `up`, `right`, `left` or `away`, `force`, `grid` or `radial`; at most 64 nodes, 128 edges and 12 groups (`NODES`, `EDGES`, `GROUPS`). A node has an id, 1 to 32 ASCII letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `.`, a label of 32 characters, a shape, `box`, `sphere`, `cylinder`, `prism`, `diamond` or `card`, a colour of the chart palette, a size from 1 to 5, a detail of 240 characters, a reference of 256 bytes, which the agent is told and Tomori never follows, and at most one group. An edge has its ends, a label of 24 characters, a direction, `forward`, `back`, `both` or `none`, a style, `solid`, `dashed`, `dotted`, `thick` or `flow`, a colour and a weight from 1 to 3; at most four join one pair of nodes. A group has an id, a label and a kind, `cluster`, `layer` or `container`, and is nested at most three deep. Everything is cleaned onto one line and drawn as text.

**Two ways to write it, one model.**

- **A `diagram` block** in a reply: a fenced block of language `diagram`, of at most 16 KiB, with a panel block's header, `title`, `id`, `place`, `layout` and up to four keys of metadata, ended by `---`, then a body shaped like a Mermaid flowchart (`tomori_protocol::diagram::parse`): a node's shape from its brackets, `[ ]` a box, `(( ))` a sphere, `[( )]` a cylinder, `{{ }}` a prism, `{ }` a diamond, `[[ ]]` a card; attributes as `@{ size: 3, colour: teal, detail: "…", ref: … }`; edges `-->`, `---`, `<-->`, `<--`, `-.->` dashed, `==>` thick and `~~>` a flow, chained and joined with `&`, labelled `-->|label|`, `-- label -->` or `: label`; `subgraph ID [Label] … end`, with `kind: layer` or `kind: container` inside; `classDef` and `class` or `:::`; `%%` comments; and a first line of `flowchart` or `graph` with Mermaid's direction. It counts among a reply's four panels, and at most two of a reply's blocks are volumes. A block with the id of a volume shown updates it in place, as a panel block does.
- **The `show_diagram` tool**, taking `title`, `id`, `place`, `layout`, and `nodes`, `edges` and `groups` as JSON objects, or `source`, a block's body (see `mcp.md`).

Both are read through one `Builder`, lenient by the line and strict by the bound: a line that cannot be read is skipped and named; an edge to a node never declared makes that node a box, which a block warns of only where its id is within two edits of another's, and the tool always; a second declaration of a node keeps the first of each attribute; what is past a bound is left out in the order written and counted; an unknown shape, colour, style, direction, kind or layout is the default, with a warning. A diagram of no node, or over 16 KiB, is not projected. Reading is bounded too: at most 512 edges are read to choose from, the rest counted, and misspellings are looked for only while nodes are still kept, so that 16 KiB of any block reads in a few milliseconds. The tool answers with its warnings at once, the first eight and how many more. A block's warnings, by the block's own lines, reach the agent before the next words spoken in the space, as a participant's tick does, as `Tomori showed the diagram "Renderer" (id renderer, panel-3) with 2 warnings: line 7: …`, cut at eight and counted beyond; and its metadata says `warnings: 2` while it has room for it.

**Laid out on the server, deterministically, and kept.** The server lays a volume out as it is projected or updated (`crate::diagram`), and sends the positions with it, so that clients lay nothing out and everyone sees the same; they are kept with the panel. Every layout runs in `f64` for a fixed number of steps, over nodes in the order written, by adding, multiplying, dividing and square roots alone, which IEEE 754 rounds alike on every platform, with no random number and no hash map's order, a node's start where one is needed hashed from its id, so that the same diagram from the same previous layout is always laid out alike. Positions are in millimetres of the volume at its own scale, `[i16; 3]` about its middle, a node of size 1 being 6 cm across and each size a quarter more; its extent is everything with a margin.

- **Layered**, Sugiyama's method in 3D: cycles broken by reversing what a depth-first walk in the order written finds going back; ranks by longest path, sources pulled down beside what they feed; long edges given waypoints on the ranks they cross, at most four kept; crossings reduced by four barycentre sweeps each way; a group's members on a rank standing together; and a rank of more than five folded into two or three rows in depth rather than spread wide.
- **Force**, stress majorisation in 3D: nodes placed so that their distances follow their distances in the graph, members of a group at most two apart and different groups at least two, for 200 steps, then nodes nearer than their half widths and a gap pushed apart; laid out afresh, turned so that it is widest across, then tallest, then shallowest.
- **Grid**, rows and columns in the order written. **Radial** comes in a later stage: it is laid out by force, with a warning.
- **Groups' boxes** hold their members and the groups within them; edges between one pair of nodes are curved apart by a waypoint each.
- **Stable across small edits.** Laid out again by its id, every node that stood before starts there: in a layered layout each rank keeps the order its nodes had, only what is new moving in the sweeps, and in a force layout each is held where it was by a spring as strong as its other forces together, a node added starting among its neighbours.

Layout is bounded by its steps, and is done under the hub's lock: at every bound, 64 nodes and 128 edges, a layered layout took 0.16 ms and a force layout 3.3 ms on a desktop machine in a release build, in November 2026.

**On the wire.** `Panel::diagram`, every type non-exhaustive, a shape, style, arrow, kind or rank a client does not know read as the default and a layout as `Unknown`, since positions come with it. `Hello::diagrams` says a client draws volumes, and `Limits::volumes` and `Limits::nodes` how many, of how many nodes together; both are absent from older peers, which state none. The server states 32 and 2,048 (`Limits::MOST`), and Tomori's headset 16 and 1,024 (`HEADSET_LIMITS`). A client that does not say `diagrams` is sent every volume without its diagram, as the text panel of its outline, and one that does, those past its limits, the first in order kept; nothing older breaks. A volume counts as one panel against every other bound. `Resource::Panel` gains `node`, by id, and `edge`, by index, which an older server ignores.

**Drawn on the client.** A volume's face is a panel's, its title, metadata and age above a plate the diagram stands in front of, its back 1 cm in front of the face: in a wall's slot, a dark plate, the diagram fitted to the slot below the heading and no deeper than half its height, as a shallow display case; floating, a faint plate, which writes no depth, as large as the diagram with its longest side from 40 cm to 1.2 m. What a volume draws (`tomori_scene::projection::volume::Mesh`) is built once for each diagram it is sent and kept: its nodes as unit solids, a box, a sphere, a cylinder, a hexagonal prism, a diamond and a card as a thin box; its edges as thin hexagonal tubes through their waypoints, from surface to surface, dashed as short tubes, dotted as small diamonds, thick twice as wide, a flow with three beads moving along it at 12 cm a second, and cones for arrowheads; its groups as a tray beneath their members, a floor for a layer and twelve thin bars for a container. Every solid is drawn by the swarm's instanced pipeline, opaque, writing depth, lit from above, one draw for each solid of every volume in view (`tomori_render::solids`), within 100,000 triangles and 16,384 solids a frame, beyond which the volumes last in the frame's order are drawn without their solids, their faces and labels still shown; one volume at every bound is within them alone. Its labels, at most 32 nodes', the largest first, 16 edges' with labels and each group's, are small panels of the shared glyph atlas, turned each frame about the vertical to face the viewer, their font pixel 1.3 mm at the volume's own scale and smaller with it to six tenths of that.

**Pointing.** The ray meets a volume where it meets its face or, nearer, one of its nodes, within a tenth more than its radius, or edges, within 1.5 cm or half a degree of their path, whichever is larger. Words begun pointing at a node or an edge name it with the volume, `Resource::volume_at`, either hand's, and the server describes it from its own copy, as `Ada: [Pointing at node "projection::faces" (id faces) in diagram "How a frame is drawn" (id renderer, floating, projected 4 minutes ago; box, in group "tomori-scene"; edges: from Frame step "panels changed", to canvas and shapes; detail: "…"; ref: "…")] when does this lay a panel out again?`, an edge as `edge "Frame step → projection::faces: panels changed" (edge 3) in diagram …`, each text in quotes and cleaned onto one line; a node or edge it does not have names the volume alone. Two hands on two parts of one volume name both. **The trigger, pressed on a node**, focuses on it for that participant alone, nothing of it sent: the node, the nodes joined to it and the edges between them stay bright, everything else dims to a fifth and its labels hide, and a node's detail stands on a card beside it; pressed on it again, it ends.

**World references.** A line `ID := KIND NAME`, optionally followed by `[Label]` and `@{ mark: …, colour: … }`, binds a short id, a node's kind of id, to a thing in the world (`tomori_protocol::world::Reference`), of one of seven kinds, each named as the agent's tools name it: `object`, an object's id as `inspect` takes it, such as `farmyard:campsite/bonfire`, or its reference alone; `anchor`, a cluster anchor's name; `wall`, a wall's identity or name; `panel`, a panel's id, the agent's id for it or its title; `worker`, its session or title; `person`, a participant's handle, such as `Samuel#11`, or a name only one has; and `agent`, naming nothing. A name runs to its label or attributes, or is in double quotes; one written without a kind is an object's where it holds a `:`, and is otherwise tried as an anchor, a wall, a panel and a person, in that order, with a warning saying which it took. The id is then used as either end of an edge, which is a **world edge**, in every style and direction, its arrow forward where it points at the thing. The tool takes the same as `world`, a list of `{ id, kind, name, label, mark, colour }`. A diagram keeps at most 16 references, 24 world edges and 8 edges to any one (`REFERENCES`, `WORLD_EDGES`, `REFERENCE_EDGES`); an id bound after an edge made it a node, a node's attributes given to a reference, an edge between two references and a reference no edge uses are each warned of, by the block's line. Tomori acts on `:=` lines alone; a node's `ref` is still never followed.

**Resolved on the server, within the space.** As a volume is shown or changed, the server resolves each reference against what the agent's own tools read in its space alone (`crate::diagram::world`): the objects its current environment's side describes with a reference, its anchors, its walls and panels as shown, its workers, the participants in it, never visitors and never anyone in another space, and the agent. Each target is sent as a `world::Target`, its state `here`, with where its middle stands in the space's coordinates, its radius and, for a panel or a wall, its frame; `absent`, a thing found but with no place now, an object described without a position, a panel being carried, a participant who has not reported for 5 s (`presence::STALE`); or `unknown`. An anchor's target stands 1.4 m beneath its point, where the floor is beneath a swarm there (`cluster::RING_BELOW`); an object reaches as far as its side says, `Object::with_extent`, within 5 cm and 3 m, or else 15 cm, and the farmyard gives its bonfire and sticks theirs. `show_diagram` answers with `world`, a line for each reference here or absent, as `fire → the campsite's bonfire, crackling in its ring of stones beside the pool lookout's log seat (farmyard:campsite/bonfire), 3.1 m from Samuel`, and each unknown one among its warnings, as `typo: no object "farmyard:campsite/stick-7" in this space (did you mean farmyard:campsite/stick-2?); nothing is drawn to it`, a name within two edits of one of that kind suggested, or else up to six of them listed, or the tool that lists them, and never whether such a thing is elsewhere; a block's are told before the next words, as its other warnings are. Positions are taken once, as the diagram is shown or changed: following what moves comes in a later stage. A node pointing at targets here is pulled towards its **port**, where the line from the middle of the box about the nodes towards the target leaves the box, as the volume will stand, by `1 / (1 + (d / 2 m)²)` of at most six tenths of the way, averaged over its targets, a layered layout's nodes only across and in depth so that its ranks stay, and nodes parted again after, so that its leaders leave the volume short and uncrossed. `place: near references` stands a volume between whoever asked and the middle of its targets here, weighted by their edges: within 6 m of their eyes, floating 1.3 m from them at their eyes' height, turned 25° off the line to it towards the side with fewer targets, facing them; further, 1.5 m from that middle on their side, facing back; spread more than 120° about them, or none here, beside them; any other panel placed so goes beside them.

**World edges on the wire and drawn.** `Diagram::world` and `Diagram::world_edges`, each world edge's `from` a node and its `to` a target, by index, without waypoints. `Hello::world_edges` says a client draws them; one that does not is sent its diagrams without them, and every client's outline lists each world edge, as `1. Find the fire → The bonfire (farmyard:campsite/bonfire): here`. A client draws a world edge to a target here as a **leader**, a thin tube of the instanced pipeline in the space, from its node's surface out of the volume's face nearest the target, a cubic curve whose last tangent comes down onto the target from above at 30°, its radius 1.5 mm at a metre from the viewer and growing with distance, in the edge's style and colour or else the target's, with its arrowheads, a flow's beads moving towards its arrowhead; and a **mark** on the target, by its kind unless `mark` says otherwise: a ring about an object's middle as wide as it reaches, a ring on the floor about an anchor, a halo above a person, a worker or the agent, and a panel's or wall's frame outlined; `none` draws the line alone. The line ends on its mark, nearest where it comes from, or at a frame's top edge. A volume's leaders are drawn at half strength and their marks at a third, unless it has three or fewer, which are drawn in full; the participant's trigger focus on a node draws its own in full, with a label at each target, its edge's label or the target's above the step's, and dims every other to a fifth. A target absent or unknown is drawn as nothing in this version. The ray meets a leader within 1.5 cm or half a degree of its line or its mark, which words name as `Resource::world_edge_at`, by the world edge's index, and the server describes by both its ends, as `world edge "1. Find the fire → The bonfire: here" (world edge 0) in diagram … (…; from node find_fire to object "farmyard:campsite/bonfire")`, a node pointed at listing what it points at among its edges; the trigger on a leader focuses on its node.

**Tools and instructions.** `panels` lists a volume as of kind `diagram`, with its node and edge counts, its body its outline; `update_panel` refuses it, naming `show_diagram`; a participant's tick on one is refused. Tomori's layer of the agent's instructions says, briefly, to show how something is organised, flows or depends as a diagram block or with `show_diagram`, describing parts and connections only, under about thirty nodes, with an id to revise it, and to say in a sentence what it shows; and, for steps done in the space, to bind what a step needs with `:=` and point at it, and that `place: near references` stands the diagram near it.

## Rationale

In November 2026 the maintainer asked, from the headset, for a way to talk about the organisation of systems in three dimensions, wondering whether a panel was the right container. The proposal (#373) recommended a panel with depth, a declarative description the agent writes and the server lays out, and pointing into it, and the maintainer approved it with four decisions: a volume is a kind of panel, not a sibling; the agent writes both a Mermaid-like block and the tools, which report errors in the same turn; volumes are placed as panels are today; and moving nodes by hand comes in stage 3. A panel kind inherits walls, floating, carrying, versions, patches, limits and persistence, so stage 1 changes none of them. Language models write structure well and coordinates badly, so layout is done once, in code, and deterministically, so that every participant sees the same and a diagram someone has learned does not rearrange itself.

Stage 1 departs from the proposal where it was cheaper and as good to try first: edges are thin tubes of the same instanced pipeline rather than ribbons from the shared stroke, which read alike from every side without facing each eye; labels are small panels turned each frame, as workers' signs are, rather than a pipeline of their own; the layout runs under the hub's lock, bounded by its fixed steps, rather than on a thread of its own with a fallback; the layouts live in the server crate rather than a crate of their own until #147's toolkit needs them; a block's warnings are told before the next words, as a tick is, rather than as an event of their own; and the trigger alone focuses, as A ticks items. Each may change once the volumes are tried on a Quest 3.

Also in November 2026 the maintainer asked, from the headset, how a diagram could connect to the anchors, panels and geometry already in the world, with the example of a marshmallow recipe whose steps point at the fire and a stick, and for springs to make diagrams look natural; they approved the proposal (#389) that answered. A binding written once and used by a short name keeps edges short and a typo in one place, and Mermaid has no `:=`, so nothing written from habit reads as one. The server decides which thing, within the space alone and by the same tables the agent's tools read, so that a diagram tells nobody anything they could not already learn. Stage 1 departs from that proposal where it was cheaper or asked for: people, workers and the agent are targets already, as the maintainer asked that stage 1 bind every kind, with their positions taken once, the proposal's live tracking, stubs for what is absent and the participant's opt-out coming with stage 2; the world springs are applied as one pull after the layout, towards the box about the nodes, rather than added to the stress the force layout majorises, which reads alike and serves the layered layout too; an object's ring stands about its middle rather than on the ground beneath it, which the server does not know, and an anchor's at the floor beneath a swarm at its point; the space's bound of 64 world edges and the client's of 32 leaders a frame wait for routing and clutter in stage 4. The marshmallow example is `crates/server/src/diagram/world/marshmallow.diagram`, resolved against the campsite in its tests, and is what stage 1 is judged by on the headset.

## Scope

`tomori-protocol`'s `diagram` and `world` modules, `Panel::diagram`, `Hello::diagrams` and `world_edges`, `Limits::volumes` and `nodes`, and `Resource::Panel`'s `node`, `edge` and `world_edge`; `tomori-environment`'s `Object::with_extent`; the server's `diagram` layouts and world references, its hub's gathering of what references name, its stage's volumes, its hub's sending of them, `show_diagram`, `place: near references`, and its descriptions of what is pointed at; `tomori-scene`'s `projection::volume` and its leaders, and the projector's and frame step's use of them; `tomori-render`'s `solids`; and the farmyard's extents.

## Validation

- `crates/protocol/src/diagram/tests.rs` and `parse/tests.rs`: every bracket, link, label form, attribute, class and subgraph; lines that cannot be read skipped and named; bounds counted; the wire format, and a newer server's values read as defaults; what a client draws bounded.
- `crates/server/src/diagram/tests.rs`: golden layered positions; the same diagram laid out alike; each direction; folded ranks; waypoints; parallel edges curved apart; a small edit keeping each rank's order and a force layout's shape; groups held by their boxes; force separating and turning.
- `crates/server/src/stage/volume/tests.rs` and `hub_tests/volumes.rs`: a block and `show_diagram` projecting and updating a volume in place, warnings told at once or before the next words, the outline sent to older clients and past a client's limits, and nodes and edges described with either hand.
- `crates/scene/src/projection/volume/tests.rs` and `scene/tests/volumes.rs`: every shape, style and arrow drawn; a volume moved by its model; focus dimming and its card; the ray meeting the nearest node or an edge; a volume's size in a slot and floating; and words begun on a node or an edge naming it.
- `crates/protocol/src/world/tests.rs`, and the world references in `diagram/tests.rs` and `parse/tests.rs`: every kind and mark; a binding's line read, or skipped and named; references apart from nodes; world edges pointing at their references either way; the bounds on references and world edges; the tool's `world`; the wire format; and the outline listing world edges.
- `crates/server/src/diagram/world/tests.rs`, `diagram/tests.rs` and `hub_tests/references.rs`: the marshmallow example resolved against the campsite; each kind by any of its names; a reference without a kind inferred, saying which; an unknown one told with a suggestion, never whether it is elsewhere; an absent one saying why; a person in another space unknown and one whose presence is stale away; the tool's answer, a block's note, the targets sent to a client that draws world edges and the outline to one that does not; a node pulled towards its target within its rank; `place: near references`; a volume's pose wherever it goes; and a world edge pointed at named by both its ends.
- `crates/scene/src/projection/volume/tests.rs` and `scene/tests/volumes.rs`: a leader for each target here, in each style with its arrowheads and each mark; their strength, focused or not, and their labels; a leader's end on its mark; the ray meeting a leader or its mark; and words begun on one naming its world edge.
- `crates/render/src/solids/tests.rs` and `scene_graphics/tests/volumes.rs`: each solid's mesh; a frame within its budget at the headset's limits; and Tomori's own architecture drawn floating and in a slot on lavapipe, which `TOMORI_VOLUME_PREVIEW` saves.

## Open questions

The proposal's questions not yet decided keep its recommendations: whether focus and highlight are right as a participant's own and the agent's for everyone, `diagram:` links in speech, stilling flows' movement, the bounds, and which diagram stage 1 is judged by. Stages 2 to 4 (updates with easing and `update_diagram`, collapsing groups, radial layout, highlights timed to speech; a volume's own size and dragging nodes; glTF models and 3D charts) remain proposals, as do the world's stages 2 to 4 (live tracking of what moves, highlights on targets, stubs for what is absent, the opt-out and the springs run live; callouts and `world:` links in speech; routing, edge-of-view pointers and the clutter rules) and its open questions, kept at its recommendations.
