# Diagrams in Volumes

This describes how the agent shows how a system is organised in three dimensions: a volume, a panel with depth, showing a diagram of nodes, edges and groups the agent describes and the server lays out, which participants handle as any panel and point into; and how its nodes point at things already in the world. It is stage 1 of `docs/proposals/three-dimensional-diagrams.md` and stage 1 of `docs/proposals/diagrams-in-the-world.md`, whose later stages remain proposals.

## Requirement

**A volume is a panel.** A volume is a `Panel` with a `diagram`, a `tomori_protocol::diagram::Diagram`, as a chart panel is one with a chart: it has a title, metadata, an age, an id, a version and a place, and is projected, laid out on walls, floated, carried, stretched or shrunk with both hands, its depth with it, docked, thrown away, kept with the space across restarts, moved by `move_panel` and listed by `panels` exactly as `projection.md` says of every panel. Its body is the diagram's outline, its nodes in no group, then each group as a heading over its nodes, each a list item with its detail, then its edges, a line each, as `Renderer → Octree: queries`, cleaned and cut as a panel block's body is; a client that draws no volumes shows that. By default it goes where a panel goes, onto the wall whoever asked was looking at if near enough to read, or else floating beside them; `place` takes a panel's forms.

**What the agent describes.** Structure, never geometry. A diagram has a layout, `layered` with a direction, `down`, `up`, `right`, `left` or `away`, `force`, `grid` or `radial`; at most 64 nodes, 128 edges and 12 groups (`NODES`, `EDGES`, `GROUPS`). A node has an id, 1 to 32 ASCII letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `.`, a label of 32 characters, a kind, of 16 bytes of an id's characters, a shape, `box`, `sphere`, `cylinder`, `prism`, `diamond` or `card`, a colour of the chart palette, a size from 1 to 5, a detail of 240 characters, a reference of 256 bytes, which the agent is told and Tomori never follows, and at most one group. An edge has its ends, a label of 24 characters, a direction, `forward`, `back`, `both` or `none`, a style, `solid`, `dashed`, `dotted`, `thick` or `flow`, a colour and a weight from 1 to 3; at most four join one pair of nodes. A group has an id, a label and a kind, `cluster`, `layer` or `container`, and is nested at most three deep. A label is on at most three lines (`LABEL_LINES`), each `<br>`, `<br/>` or `<br />`, in any case, a break between two, as in a Mermaid flowchart, its breaks counted among its characters, empty lines left out and those past the third joined to it; a `\n` is not a break, as it is not in Mermaid, and a line break in the tool's JSON is a space. Nothing else is read as HTML: `a < b` and `<b>` are drawn as written. Everything else is cleaned onto one line and drawn as text, and wherever a label is named in text, in the outline or to the agent, its lines are joined by spaces.

**Colour for groups, shape for kinds.** A diagram's visual language is small and consistent: colour says which group a node is in, shape what kind of thing it is, size how important it is, a narrowing edge which way it points, and brightness what the participant attends to; a legend says what each colour and shape means. A node's kind is what it is, its shape following unless it gives its own (`KINDS`): `module`, a box, which a node of no kind is too; `store`, a cylinder; `external`, a sphere; `interface`, a card; `process`, a hexagonal prism; and `decision`, a diamond; or a kind of the agent's own, declared with `classDef`, a box unless it says another shape. A node's colour is its own where it gives one other than grey, the default, and otherwise its group's: each group at the top takes the next of the chart palette's eight colours in the order written (`Colour::SERIES`, told apart with colour blindness, #511), its nested groups a paler shade of it for each level, and a ninth group at the top and its members none, with a warning (`GROUP_COLOURS`); a node in no group, or a group without a colour, is grey. Shape is the cue colour blindness does not lose, so that a diagram never relies on colour alone, and the colour-blind mode (#516) can add more without changing the language.

**Two ways to write it, one model.**

- **A `diagram` block** in a reply: a fenced block of language `diagram`, of at most 16 KiB, with a panel block's header, `title`, `id`, `place`, `layout` and up to four keys of metadata, ended by `---`, then a body shaped like a Mermaid flowchart (`tomori_protocol::diagram::parse`): a node's label from its brackets, and its shape from all but a rectangle's, `[ ]`, Mermaid's plain node, which leaves it to its kind: `(( ))` a sphere, `[( )]` a cylinder, `{{ }}` a prism, `{ }` a diamond, `[[ ]]` a card; attributes as `@{ size: 3, colour: teal, kind: store, detail: "…", ref: … }`, Mermaid's styles, `fill`, `stroke` and the like, read and left to the groups' colours; edges `-->`, `---`, `<-->`, `<--`, `-.->` dashed, `==>` thick and `~~>` a flow, chained and joined with `&`, labelled `-->|label|`, `-- label -->` or `: label`; `subgraph ID [Label] … end`, with `kind: layer` or `kind: container` inside; a class, by `class a,b store` or `a:::store`, which gives the node its `classDef`'s attributes and is its kind where it is one of `KINDS` or its `classDef` gives a shape, as `classDef crate shape: prism`, the first kind given a node kept; a class that only styles, as Mermaid's `classDef hot fill:#f96`, is no kind and named in no legend; any class neither known nor declared is warned of; `%%` comments; and a first line of `flowchart` or `graph` with Mermaid's direction. It counts among a reply's four panels, and at most two of a reply's blocks are volumes. A block with the id of a volume shown updates it in place, as a panel block does.
- **The `show_diagram` tool**, taking `title`, `id`, `place`, `layout`, and `nodes`, `edges` and `groups` as JSON objects, or `source`, a block's body (see `mcp.md`).

Both are read through one `Builder`, lenient by the line and strict by the bound: a line that cannot be read is skipped and named; an edge to a node never declared makes that node a box, which a block warns of only where its id is within two edits of another's, and the tool always; a second declaration of a node keeps the first of each attribute; what is past a bound is left out in the order written and counted; an unknown shape, colour, style, direction, kind or layout is the default, with a warning. A diagram of no node, or over 16 KiB, is not projected. Reading is bounded too: at most 512 edges are read to choose from, the rest counted, and misspellings are looked for only while nodes are still kept, so that 16 KiB of any block reads in a few milliseconds. The tool answers with its warnings at once, the first eight and how many more. A block's warnings, by the block's own lines, reach the agent before the next words spoken in the space, as a participant's tick does, as `Tomori showed the diagram "Renderer" (id renderer, panel-3) with 2 warnings: line 7: …`, cut at eight and counted beyond; and its metadata says `warnings: 2` while it has room for it.

**Laid out on the server, deterministically, and kept.** The server lays a volume out as it is projected or updated (`crate::diagram`), and sends the positions with it, so that clients lay nothing out and everyone sees the same; they are kept with the panel. Every layout runs in `f64` for a fixed number of steps, over nodes in the order written, by adding, multiplying, dividing and square roots alone, which IEEE 754 rounds alike on every platform, with no random number and no hash map's order, a node's start where one is needed hashed from its id, so that the same diagram from the same previous layout is always laid out alike. Positions are in millimetres of the volume at its own scale, `[i16; 3]` about its middle, a node of size 1 being 6 cm across and each size a quarter more; its extent is everything with a margin.

- **Layered**, Sugiyama's method in 3D: cycles broken by reversing what a depth-first walk in the order written finds going back; ranks by longest path, sources pulled down beside what they feed; long edges given waypoints on the ranks they cross, at most four kept; crossings reduced by four barycentre sweeps each way; a group's members on a rank standing together; and a rank of more than five folded into two or three rows in depth rather than spread wide.
- **Force**, stress majorisation in 3D: nodes placed so that their distances follow their distances in the graph, members of a group at most two apart and different groups at least two, for 200 steps, then nodes nearer than their half widths and a gap pushed apart; laid out afresh, turned so that it is widest across, then tallest, then shallowest.
- **Grid**, rows and columns in the order written. **Radial** comes in a later stage: it is laid out by force, with a warning.
- **Groups' boxes** hold their members and the groups within them; edges between one pair of nodes are curved apart by a waypoint each.
- **Stable across small edits.** Laid out again by its id, every node that stood before starts there: in a layered layout each rank keeps the order its nodes had, only what is new moving in the sweeps, and in a force layout each is held where it was by a spring as strong as its other forces together, a node added starting among its neighbours.

Layout is bounded by its steps, and is done under the hub's lock: at every bound, 64 nodes and 128 edges, a layered layout took 0.16 ms and a force layout 3.3 ms on a desktop machine in a release build, in November 2026.

**On the wire.** `Panel::diagram`, every type non-exhaustive, a shape, style, arrow, kind or rank a client does not know read as the default and a layout as `Unknown`, since positions come with it. `Hello::diagrams` says a client draws volumes, and `Limits::volumes` and `Limits::nodes` how many, of how many nodes together; both are absent from older peers, which state none. The server states 32 and 2,048 (`Limits::MOST`), and Tomori's headset 16 and 1,024 (`HEADSET_LIMITS`). A client that does not say `diagrams` is sent every volume without its diagram, as the text panel of its outline, and one that does, those past its limits, the first in order kept; nothing older breaks. A volume counts as one panel against every other bound. `Resource::Panel` gains `node`, by id, and `edge`, by index, which an older server ignores.

**Drawn on the client.** A volume's face is a panel's, its title, metadata and age above a plate the diagram stands in front of, its back 1 cm in front of the face: in a wall's slot, a dark plate, the diagram fitted to the slot below the heading and no deeper than half its height, as a shallow display case; floating, a faint plate, which writes no depth, as large as the diagram with its longest side from 40 cm to 1.2 m, grown by as much as its front is seen larger from 1.3 m, within its most rows. Two rows below the heading are kept clear for the labels above the highest nodes, and the diagram is drawn so that its front, nearer the eyes than its plate, is seen within the area between those rows and its legend from straight in front at 1.3 m floating and 2 m in a slot, so that none of it is seen over its title or its legend. What a volume draws (`tomori_scene::projection::volume::Mesh`) is built once for each diagram it is sent and kept: its nodes as unit solids in their colours, a box, a sphere, a cylinder, a hexagonal prism lying with a hexagon to the front, its points to either side, a diamond and a card as a thin box; its edges as thin hexagonal tubes curved through their waypoints, from the surface of each end's solid as it is drawn to the other's, a millimetre within them, in the colour of the group both ends are in, at three quarters, or else a neutral grey, or their own; an edge that points one way narrowing from 3.5 mm at where it points from to 1.4 mm at where it points, straight as one narrowing tube and curved as eight tubes each narrower than the last, and one that points nowhere or both ways 2.4 mm throughout; one that points one way also has two arrowheads, one with its point on its target's surface and one with its point six tenths of the way along, clear of the labels above its ends, each 34 mm long and 14 mm across, or a fifth of the edge if shorter, in a light tone of the edge's colour, six tenths of the way to white; dashed as short tubes and dotted as small diamonds, each as wide as the edge where it stands, thick twice as wide; a flow with three beads moving along it at 12 cm a second, as are the edges that point to and from the node the participant focuses on; a cluster as nothing but its members' colour, its name in the legend, with a label of its own only for a group without a colour, a layer as the four edges of a floor beneath its members and a container as the twelve of a box about them, bars 2.5 mm thick in its colour, darker, which hide little behind them. Its face holds, at its foot, below the diagram and two rows apart from it, a legend of up to four rows, the legend and those two rows at most a quarter of its body, a slot of twelve rows giving it one, and a slot of fewer than twelve none, its entries kept with its mesh: a dot of each coloured group's colour and its label, then, for each kind its nodes are of but `module`, a small solid of its shape beside its name, drawn with its face, and `+N` for those that do not fit. Every solid is drawn by the swarm's instanced pipeline, opaque, writing depth, lit from above, one draw for each solid of every volume in view (`tomori_render::solids`), within 100,000 triangles and 16,384 solids a frame, beyond which the volumes last in the frame's order are drawn without their solids, their faces and labels still shown; one volume at every bound is within them alone. Its labels, at most 32 nodes', the largest first, 16 edges' with labels and each group's, are small panels of the shared glyph atlas, turned each frame about the vertical to face the viewer, their font pixel 1.3 mm at the volume's own scale and smaller with it to six tenths of that; each line wrapped at its words within 20 columns, and of at most three rows the last cut with `…`, the first row standing where a label of one does and the rest growing upwards, away from what it names. **Labels are drawn in front of the volume's own solids**: each frame, a label, and the card of a node focused on, is moved towards the viewer to where the line from the viewer's head to its middle enters the box of the volume's extent, grown by the label's half diagonal, and shrunk about the viewer's head as it comes, so that it is seen exactly as large and where it was, in front of every node, edge, tray and frame of its volume; it never comes nearer the head than 0.4 m (`NEAREST`) unless it stood nearer, and from within the box, where the line enters at the head, it stands at that distance, so that leaning in to read a diagram its labels neither crowd the eyes nor jump as the head crosses into the box; a label already in front of that box stays where it is. Its volume's labels are drawn farthest first by where they stood before they were brought forward, so that where two overlap the label of the nearer node is drawn over that of the node behind it. The box is the diagram's extent, as the server laid it out, or, while a participant pulls its nodes about, the box about their positions in that frame. Labels are otherwise drawn as every translucent label is, depth tested against the scene and their backings written into depth (`design/rendering.md`), with no bias of their own: as they never leave that grown box, a panel's opaque face, a wall, a panel carried in front, a hand or another volume's solids in front of the volume still hide them, and they show through nothing, but that a label brought forward may stand in front of a panel within its own half diagonal, a few centimetres, of the box's front, which a volume does not know of.

**Pointing.** The ray meets a volume where it meets its face or, nearer, one of its nodes, within a tenth more than its radius, or edges, within 1.5 cm or half a degree of their path, whichever is larger. Words begun pointing at a node or an edge name it with the volume, `Resource::volume_at`, either hand's, and the server describes it from its own copy, as `Ada: [Pointing at node "projection::faces" (id faces) in diagram "How a frame is drawn" (id renderer, floating, projected 4 minutes ago; box, in group "tomori-scene"; 1 in, 2 out; edges: from Frame step "panels changed", to canvas and shapes; detail: "…"; ref: "…")] when does this lay a panel out again?`, an edge as `edge "Frame step → projection::faces: panels changed" (edge 3) in diagram …`, each text in quotes and cleaned onto one line; a node or edge it does not have names the volume alone. Two hands on two parts of one volume name both. **The trigger, pressed on a node**, focuses on it for that participant alone, nothing of it sent: the node, the nodes joined to it and the edges between them stay bright, everything else dims to a fifth and its labels hide, and a node's detail stands on a card beside it; pressed on it again, it ends.


**Pulling nodes about, by hand.** The grip on a node of a volume, its ray meeting the node nearer than anything else or the hand within 12 cm of it (`projection::TOUCH`), takes hold of the node rather than the volume, felt on that hand, which the grip on the volume's face, heading or an edge still carries; the node then follows the hand, held where the ray met it, at that distance along the ray, which the hand's stick reels in or out at up to 1.5 m a second, from 15 cm to 8 m, or, taken by touch, where the hand closed on it. While a node is held its volume is laid out live (`projection::volume::springs`), in its millimetres, in steps of a sixtieth of a second, at most four a frame: Hooke's springs along its edges, each at rest at the length the server laid it out at, so that the server's layout is at rest; a push between two nodes nearer than 85 mm; a weak spring holding each node home; and damping, each node no faster than 1.5 m a second; the node held is where the hand holds it. A node held alone fans its neighbours 15 cm about it, as its holder sees it: those its edges come from on an arc to its left, those they go to on one to its right, those joined both ways or neither above it, each side's in the order they stood, highest first; the rest fall back, away from its holder; it is focused on, its neighbours lit and the rest dimmed with their labels hidden, its card beside it, and beads move along its edges that point. Two nodes held, one in each hand, stand where the hands hold them, unfanned, and the shortest path between them by edges either way, the first of those as short in the order written, is lit with beads moving along it, and the rest dimmed. Where the hand holding it lets it go, as it holds it as the grip opens, within the volume's extent, and 3 cm beyond it, a node is pinned there, the rest settling about it on firmer springs home; let go beyond it, as pulled towards its holder, it springs home, as does everything once nothing is held or pinned, the server's layout drawn again once every node is within half a millimetre of home; a node taken again is unpinned, and the trigger pressed on the volume elsewhere than a node puts every node back. A layout still for half a second sleeps, costing nothing. While its nodes are pulled about, a volume is drawn as they stand, to a fraction of a millimetre: a copy of what it draws, its edges straight and its groups by their colour alone, is made once as a node is first taken, and each frame its nodes' solids and labels are moved, its edges' solids made again along their new paths and its extent made the box about its nodes, before which its labels are brought, while its labels' canvases, cards and legend are kept; a layout asleep is not moved. A hand holds a node by its id: when the agent changes the diagram, its layout is laid out again from where the server now lays it, every home anew, each node of the same id where it stood and held or pinned as it was, and a node held that the diagram no longer has is let go; it is forgotten once the volume is no longer shown. Pulling is the participant's own: nothing of it is sent, every other participant seeing the server's layout, and seeing the holder's ray, where rays are shared, point where it points rather than at the node held, which they do not see moved; sharing the layout and what each hand holds, through the server, is a later stage (`docs/proposals/interactive-diagrams.md`). Words begun while holding a node name it as pointing at it does, the ray ending on it with no label of its own, and the server tells the agent of a node, held or pointed at, with how many of its edges point into it and out of it, one pointing both ways or neither counted as each, as `; process, sphere, in group "tomori-scene"; 1 in, 2 out; edges: …`. A frame's step of a volume at every bound, 64 nodes and 128 edges, with its nodes moved, took 21 µs on an Apple M4 Pro in a release build in November 2026, over 600 frames; a Quest 3's cores score about a third of its single core in Geekbench 6, so about 70 µs there, under one percent of an 11 ms frame at 90 Hz, which a test bounds loosely, at 500 µs.
**World references.** A line `ID := KIND NAME`, optionally followed by `[Label]` and `@{ mark: …, colour: … }`, binds a short id, a node's kind of id, to a thing in the world (`tomori_protocol::world::Reference`), of one of seven kinds, each named as the agent's tools name it: `object`, an object's id as `inspect` takes it, such as `farmyard:campsite/bonfire`, or its reference alone; `anchor`, a cluster anchor's name; `wall`, a wall's identity or name; `panel`, a panel's id, the agent's id for it or its title; `worker`, its session or title; `person`, a participant's handle, such as `Samuel#11`, or a name only one has; and `agent`, naming nothing. A name runs to its label or attributes, or is in double quotes; one written without a kind is an object's where it holds a `:`, and is otherwise tried as an anchor, a wall, a panel and a person, in that order, with a warning saying which it took. The id is then used as either end of an edge, which is a **world edge**, in every style and direction, its arrow forward where it points at the thing. The tool takes the same as `world`, a list of `{ id, kind, name, label, mark, colour }`. A diagram keeps at most 16 references, 24 world edges and 8 edges to any one (`REFERENCES`, `WORLD_EDGES`, `REFERENCE_EDGES`); an id bound after an edge made it a node, a node's attributes given to a reference, an edge between two references and a reference no edge uses are each warned of, by the block's line. Tomori acts on `:=` lines alone; a node's `ref` is still never followed.

**Resolved on the server, within the space.** As a volume is shown or changed, the server resolves each reference against what the agent's own tools read in its space alone (`crate::diagram::world`): the objects its current environment's side describes with a reference, its anchors, its walls and panels as shown, its workers, the participants in it, never visitors and never anyone in another space, and the agent. Each target is sent as a `world::Target`, its state `here`, with where its middle stands in the space's coordinates, its radius and, for a panel or a wall, its frame; `absent`, a thing found but with no place now, an object described without a position, a panel being carried, a participant who has not reported for 5 s (`presence::STALE`); or `unknown`. An anchor's target stands 1.4 m beneath its point, where the floor is beneath a swarm there (`cluster::RING_BELOW`); an object reaches as far as its side says, `Object::with_extent`, within 5 cm and 3 m, or else 15 cm, and the farmyard gives its bonfire and sticks theirs. `show_diagram` answers with `world`, a line for each reference here or absent, as `fire → the campsite's bonfire, crackling in its ring of stones beside the pool lookout's log seat (farmyard:campsite/bonfire), 3.1 m from Samuel`, and each unknown one among its warnings, as `typo: no object "farmyard:campsite/stick-7" in this space (did you mean farmyard:campsite/stick-2?); nothing is drawn to it`, a name within two edits of one of that kind suggested, or else up to six of them listed, or the tool that lists them, and never whether such a thing is elsewhere; a block's are told before the next words, as its other warnings are. Positions are taken once, as the diagram is shown or changed: following what moves comes in a later stage. A node pointing at targets here is pulled towards its **port**, where the line from the middle of the box about the nodes towards the target leaves the box, as the volume will stand, by `1 / (1 + (d / 2 m)²)` of at most six tenths of the way, averaged over its targets, a layered layout's nodes only across and in depth so that its ranks stay, and nodes parted again after, so that its leaders leave the volume short and uncrossed. `place: near references` stands a volume between whoever asked and the middle of its targets here, weighted by their edges: within 6 m of their eyes, floating 1.3 m from them at their eyes' height, turned 25° off the line to it towards the side with fewer targets, facing them; further, 1.5 m from that middle on their side, facing back; spread more than 120° about them, or none here, beside them; any other panel placed so goes beside them.

**World edges on the wire and drawn.** `Diagram::world` and `Diagram::world_edges`, each world edge's `from` a node and its `to` a target, by index, without waypoints. `Hello::world_edges` says a client draws them; one that does not is sent its diagrams without them, and every client's outline lists each world edge, as `1. Find the fire → The bonfire (farmyard:campsite/bonfire): here`. A client draws a world edge to a target here as a **leader**, a thin tube of the instanced pipeline in the space, from its node's surface out of the volume's face nearest the target, a cubic curve whose last tangent comes down onto the target from above at 30°, its radius 1.5 mm at a metre from the viewer and growing with distance, in the edge's style and colour or else the target's, a dashed or dotted one in at most 64 pieces, spaced further apart along a longer one, with its arrowheads, a flow's beads moving towards its arrowhead; and a **mark** on the target, by its kind unless `mark` says otherwise: a ring about an object's middle as wide as it reaches, a ring on the floor about an anchor, a halo above a person, a worker or the agent, and a panel's or wall's frame outlined; `none` draws the line alone. The line ends on its mark, nearest where it comes from, or at a frame's top edge. A volume's leaders are drawn at half strength and their marks at a third, unless it has three or fewer, which are drawn in full; the participant's trigger focus on a node draws its own in full, with a label at each target, its edge's label or the target's above the step's, and dims every other to a fifth. A target absent or unknown is drawn as nothing in this version. The ray meets a leader within 1.5 cm or half a degree of its line or its mark, which words name as `Resource::world_edge_at`, by the world edge's index, and the server describes by both its ends, as `world edge "1. Find the fire → The bonfire: here" (world edge 0) in diagram … (…; from node find_fire to object "farmyard:campsite/bonfire")`, a node pointed at listing what it points at among its edges; the trigger on a leader focuses on its node.

**Tools and instructions.** `panels` lists a volume as of kind `diagram`, with its node and edge counts, its body its outline; `update_panel` refuses it, naming `show_diagram`; a participant's tick on one is refused. Tomori's layer of the agent's instructions says, briefly, to show how something is organised, flows or depends as a diagram block or with `show_diagram`, describing parts and connections only, under about thirty nodes, short labels breaking onto a second line with `<br>`, with an id to revise it, and to say in a sentence what it shows, and `show_diagram`'s schema says a label's `<br>` breaks it; and, for steps done in the space, to bind what a step needs with `:=` and point at it, and that `place: near references` stands the diagram near it.

## Rationale

In November 2026 the maintainer asked, from the headset, for a way to talk about the organisation of systems in three dimensions, wondering whether a panel was the right container. The proposal (#373) recommended a panel with depth, a declarative description the agent writes and the server lays out, and pointing into it, and the maintainer approved it with four decisions: a volume is a kind of panel, not a sibling; the agent writes both a Mermaid-like block and the tools, which report errors in the same turn; volumes are placed as panels are today; and moving nodes by hand comes in stage 3. A panel kind inherits walls, floating, carrying, versions, patches, limits and persistence, so stage 1 changes none of them. Language models write structure well and coordinates badly, so layout is done once, in code, and deterministically, so that every participant sees the same and a diagram someone has learned does not rearrange itself.

Stage 1 departs from the proposal where it was cheaper and as good to try first: edges are thin tubes of the same instanced pipeline rather than ribbons from the shared stroke, which read alike from every side without facing each eye; labels are small panels turned each frame, as workers' signs are, rather than a pipeline of their own; the layout runs under the hub's lock, bounded by its fixed steps, rather than on a thread of its own with a fallback; the layouts live in the server crate rather than a crate of their own until #147's toolkit needs them; a block's warnings are told before the next words, as a tick is, rather than as an event of their own; and the trigger alone focuses, as A ticks items. Each may change once the volumes are tried on a Quest 3.

Later in November 2026, wearing the headset at twice the recommended resolution with 2× multisampling and panels drawn opaque (#530), the maintainer found a diagram's node and edge labels sometimes hidden behind other nodes, edges or groups' boxes, and Mermaid's `<br>` in a label shown as written. Labels had stood among the solids, 18 mm above their nodes, depth tested against them like anything else, so that a dense diagram, or one seen from the side or below, hid many: on lavapipe, of a 30-node diagram with folded ranks and three groups, 5% of its labels' text was hidden from in front and 18% from below to one side, and none once they were brought forward. Of the ways to draw them reliably, a pipeline drawing labels over everything, or with a large depth bias, would have shown them through panels and walls in front of the volume, and one testing against everything but their own volume needs a stencil or a pass of its own; moving each label along the line to the viewer's head, to the front of its volume's box and shrunk as it comes, needs neither, keeps every depth rule of `rendering.md` as it is, and hides a label exactly where something stands in front of the volume. A label so moved is in front of its node rather than beside it, so that each eye sees it a little apart from the node behind it, by a fraction of a degree at arm's length, and a crowded rank's labels still overlap each other, the nearer drawn over; spreading them apart, or placing labels where nothing overlaps, waits for stage 4's clutter rules. Mermaid breaks a label's lines at `<br>` in any of its spellings and not at `\n`, which is why agents write `<br>`; reading only those, and keeping every other tag as text, means a label never interprets HTML, and three lines of a label's 32 characters, wrapped within 20 columns, keep a label compact without hiding any of its words.

Also in November 2026 the maintainer asked, from the headset, how a diagram could connect to the anchors, panels and geometry already in the world, with the example of a marshmallow recipe whose steps point at the fire and a stick, and for springs to make diagrams look natural; they approved the proposal (#389) that answered.
