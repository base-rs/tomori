# People

This describes how the others in a space are drawn, as the first two stages of `docs/proposals/drawing-other-people.md`, which the maintainer approved in November 2026, and the colours of its third: each a small swarm of cubes shaped as a person, in a colour of their own, their tracked hands and controllers, their rays, a cue when someone looks at you and one while they speak, what presence carries for them, and what each participant lets the others see. Labels and pointing at people are its later stages.

## Requirement

**A person is drawn as a swarm and their rays**, built of Tomori's cubes, in the space's scene coordinates, from their presence (`tomori_scene::people`). Nobody is drawn but the others in the joined space whom its state lists, never this participant, and nothing of anyone is drawn outside a space.

**The swarm** (`people::swarm`) is a few hundred small cubes shaped as a person, a head, shoulders, a torso and arms, where the agent's swarm is a shapeless cloud, so that the two are never mistaken for each other. It is stylised for fun, not human, with no face but two eyes. **It glows**: each cube is its own light in the person's colour, nearly unshaded by the soft light from above Tomori's other cubes are drawn with (`graphics::PEOPLE_GLOW`, 0.85), and hazed as everything is. Each is brightest, whitening a little, at the middle of what it is part of as seen from this participant's eyes, and dimmer, to 0.4 of that, and a little smaller, to 0.85, at its outline and behind (`swarm::CORE`, `RIM`), so that a person reads as a volume of light rather than a solid, translucent at its edges without blending or sorting; and each shimmers by up to 15% (`SHIMMER`) at one of three beats with a phase of its own. Cubes are small and of many sizes, packed densely in the head, the shoulders and the upper chest so that the silhouette reads across a space, with air between them only at the edges and below the chest. **Glow without bloom:** Tomori has no bloom pass, so the brightest parts glow by size and brightness alone: a larger, dimmer copy behind them as seen, a hand's gleam, opaque like every other cube. Every colour of the swarm is made from the person's (`Cues::colour`), their own (below); someone the space's state does not name yet is a warm coral (`swarm::COLOUR`), far from the agent's violet.

**Their colour.** Each person has a colour of their own, the same on every client (`tomori_protocol::hue`): a hue in whole degrees, on a continuous wheel, at one saturation and lightness in sRGB's HSL, 1 and 0.765 (`hue::SATURATION`, `LIGHTNESS`), bright and pale enough to glow in the farmyard's and Sky's evening haze and to be read as text on a dark backing, at which hue 13 is the coral everyone was before. **Some hues are no one's:** a person is only ever coral to orange, 16° to 27°, yellow through green to teal, 54° to 175°, or magenta through pink, 280° to 355° (`hue::ARCS`), so that the bands about a failure's red, about 6°, waiting's and a stall's amber, about 40°, and the agent's cyan, blue and violet, about 186° to 268°, which a worker running shares, are each kept about 10° clear. **By default, from their name:** FNV-1a, 32 bits, of their display name, trimmed and in lower case, so that "Ada" and " ada " are one colour, taken modulo the arcs' 210 degrees and laid along them as one wheel with the bands left out (`hue::default`), so that it needs nothing sent: each client works it out from the names the space's state already carries. **Their own, chosen:** a person may choose their colour instead, a hue a person may be (see `hand-menu.md`), which the server keeps with their token beside their name, so that it is theirs on every device and in every space, and the space's state carries as theirs (`Participant::colour`, see `security.md`). **A chosen colour always wins:** every chosen colour is placed first, and each default then moves aside from it, never it from a default, wherever its chooser is in the participant order; two who chose one colour both have it. Choosing their name's colour again, `default`, forgets the one they chose. **Kept apart in a space:** where a default is within 30° (`hue::APART`) of a chosen colour or of a default before it in the space's participant order, it moves to the nearest clear hue at least 30° from each of those, looking a degree further either way in turn, higher first, so that every client given the same participants in the same order agrees; an earlier participant keeps theirs, and nobody's colour changes where nothing is too near (`hue::resolve`). Thirty degrees at this lightness, as yellow and lime or green and teal, are told apart at a glance across a space, where closer hues read as one in a swarm's glow; the arcs hold six or seven people so far apart, and past that each is put as far from the rest as any clear hue is, the nearest to their name's of those. The conversation works a space's colours out once for each state of it (`speakers::Known`), and gives them to the people drawn by their numbers (`People::colour`), so a frame allocates nothing for them; their swarm, their bar and name in the transcript and the queue (see `wrist-transcript.md`), and their swatch in the hand menu (see `hand-menu.md`), are all that one colour. Someone who leaves fades out in the colour they were; someone in the transcript who has left is in their name's colour. Colour is never the only signal: a name is always shown with it.

- **The head** is a rounded, dense cluster about 18 by 20 by 19 cm, a little larger than a head so that it reads across a space: a bright core of 16 cubes inside a shell of 112, the core's middle a still block nearly as large as fits within the shell (`swarm::MIDDLE_BLOCK`), so that the shell's gaps show its glow rather than whatever is behind the head, from 1.9 to 3.1 cm and turned little, packed on its outside so that its outline is round, standing about where their eyes are, a little behind them, facing where they look, nodding and tilting as their head does. **Two small, soft, round eyes** sit flush on its face, in no socket, a little below its middle and set wide, about 8.5 cm apart, for a friendly face rather than a menacing one: each 1.7 by 2 cm, a little taller than wide, three flat boxes facing out of the head, a wide one, a tall one and a square between, as a few pixels draw a circle, just in front of the shell's cubes about it, three of which fill the face behind it so that it never sits before a gap. They are a warm off-white with a little of the person's colour in it (`swarm::EYE_WHITE`), paler and a little brighter than the face, and steady rather than shimmering. **They blink** every 2 to 6 s, each gap chosen anew from dice of the person's own, seeded by their number, so that nobody blinks in step (`BLINKS`): over 0.14 s (`BLINK`) each eye squashes from above and below to an eighth of its height and opens again, kept in the body from frame to frame with nothing allocated, a long frame never blinking twice at once. A little orbit of 16 tiny cubes circles it, a tidy ring evenly spaced, tilted, its front above the eyes and its back below them, turning slowly.
- **The body** hangs from their neck, below and behind their eyes, and turns with their head's yaw alone, never its pitch or roll, so that nodding, looking down or tilting the head bends nothing: following it damped, most of the way in 0.35 s (`FOLLOW`), and never more than 50° (`TWIST`) behind it. A short neck joins the two. The torso, of 148 cubes, most of them near its outside so that its outline reads, is widest at the shoulders, about 38 cm across, with a rounded cap of eight cubes at each, and densest there and in the upper chest; below the chest it **dissolves**, fewer, smaller, dimmer, to 0.45, and looser lower down, ending at the waist, about 70 cm below the eyes: **no legs**. From below the chest 21 cubes fall, more of them lower, each 0.3 to 0.6 m over 3 to 6 s at a phase of its own, growing in from nothing, spreading a little, turning, shrinking and fading as they fall, and beginning again.
- **The arms** reach from each shoulder to each hand by two bones, an upper arm of 30 cm and a forearm of 32 cm to the hand, by the law of cosines, their elbows bending out, down and a little back (`swarm::elbow`): the hand is always reached, the arm straight at full reach and never bending the other way, stretched by at most a fifth to reach farther, and beyond that reaching as far as it can towards the hand. The hand is a tracked hand's wrist, where its joints are drawn from, or its palm where the wrist is not seen, or else its controller's grip; one tracked neither hangs at the side, a little bent, coming up over 0.25 s as it is tracked and falling back over 0.4 s as it is lost. **Each bone is three rods** along it, as the maintainer suggested, mostly with small gaps between them, each turned a few degrees from it and set a little off its line so that it reads as part of the swarm, tapering, thickest at the shoulder and the elbow, about 4.5 cm across, and thinnest at the wrist, about 3 cm, each as long, of its bone, as it rests, so that it stretches as the bone does; an elbow joins the two, and four loose cubes drift about each bone, a few centimetres out from it, so that an arm reads as part of a swarm rather than a stick. Rods read as one limb from shoulder to hand across a space, where a bone of small cubes broke into dots. A hand neither tracks is a small, bright, paler cluster of ten cubes, with three larger, dimmer cubes of its gleam a few centimetres behind it as seen; a tracked hand or a controller replaces it (below), the arm reaching a tracked hand's wrist.
- **Every cube drifts** a few millimetres about where it rests, those below the chest a little more, each at one of three slow beats with a phase of its own, and brightens and dims a little as it does. Where each rests, how large and how bright it is, and when it drifts, is laid out once, the same on every client.

**Speaking.** While someone's voice plays on this device, as its player says by their number (`PlayerControl::voices`, reported once a frame as `Report::Voices`), the orbit about their head quickens, from 0.6 to 3.2 radians a second, and brightens and grows by a third, over 0.15 s, calming over 0.5 s once it stops, so that the breaths between words do not flicker.

**Detail by distance.** Within 5 m of this participant's eyes a person is drawn in full, 399 cubes, less their hands' clusters and with their hands and controllers; beyond it, 173, the head's core, half its shell and the face behind its eyes, two thirds of its orbit, its eyes, the arms' rods, half the hands and a quarter of the rest, each larger to fill in for those left out; and beyond 15 m their head alone, 43 cubes, with its eyes and a little of its orbit. Each changes only half a metre past its edge, so that someone standing there does not flicker.
- **Hands and controllers** (`people::hands`), in the person's colour (`Cues::colour`), in the swarm's batch. **A tracked hand** is its joints and bones, so that pointing, a thumb up or a wave reads: a cube on each of its 25 joints but the palm, as large as the joint's radius says, the fingertips a little smaller and paler, a slender rod along each of its 24 bones, most of the way between its joints, the palm a flat block as wide as the knuckles with a larger, dimmer gleam behind it as seen, 51 cubes, paler and a little brighter than the swarm about it. **A controller** no hand tracks is a small stylised one at its grip, never the runtime's model of this participant's own hardware: a rounded body of three boxes, a face with a button, and a tracking ring of eight rods above it, 13 cubes. Where presence carries both, as with simultaneous hands and controllers, the hand is drawn. Switching between the two **cross-fades** over 0.2 s (`hands::CROSS`), each shrinking or growing where it is, the swarm's own cluster at the arm's end giving way to whichever is drawn; **a hand that loses tracking** dissolves where it was last drawn over 0.15 s (`hands::LOST`), rather than freezing, as the swarm's cluster comes back and the arm falls to rest. Beyond 5 m a tracked hand is one cluster of five cubes at its palm and a controller its body alone; beyond 15 m neither is drawn. All on the timeline below: one update behind, never extrapolated.
- **A ray** is drawn while it is drawn in full on their headset, as presence carries it (below): from its aim as far as it reaches, in this participant's own ray's style, highlighted while it meets something, 0.6 as bright, ending in a small dot where it meets something. Nothing else of their rays is drawn.

**Eye contact.** Someone within 8 m whose head faces this participant's eyes, within 10° of where it looks, warms their eyes over 0.2 s, eased at either end, clearly enough to read across a space: a warm cream (`swarm::EYE_WARM`, 0.7 of the way to it), 12 to 30% brighter and 18% larger (`EYE_GROW`), never gold and with no halo, the eye towards this participant a little warmer; and they cool over 0.4 s once their head faces more than 15° away, so that a glance at the edge, or a head wavering there, does not flicker. Each client works it out for itself from the head poses presence already carries, so nothing more is sent and only the one looked at sees it; a watcher sees it towards the participant it follows.

**Motion.** Everyone is drawn from their whole presence, head, controllers, tracked hands and rays, between its updates, on the timeline `relayed::Relayed` follows (see `physics.md`): each update placed when it arrived, drawn about an update behind, at most `MOST`, 50 ms, and holding still beyond the latest, never extrapolated. An environment's client side is given the others' hands from the same tracks, as the people holding them are drawn that frame, so that what someone holds and the hand holding it are the same pose in the same frame.

- **A jump** between two updates of more than `JUMP`, 1.5 m, as a teleport, fades them out over 0.1 s where they were drawn and in over 0.1 s where they are, never gliding between; what they hold stays with the hand drawn.
- **Silence.** Someone whose presence has said nothing for 2 s fades out over 0.5 s, and is drawn again as their next report arrives; a report alike to the last still counts. What they hold stays in their hand.
- **Leaving.** Someone no longer in the space's participants fades out over 0.3 s where they last were, and is no longer given to an environment's client side. Once the conversation forgets the others, on connecting and on joining or leaving a space, everyone is drawn anew where they first are, and nobody fades.
- **Up close.** Others are drawn fainter, each cube shrinking where it rests, within 0.6 m of this participant's eyes from their head's middle, and not at all within 0.3 m, so that two people arriving at one place are never inside each other's heads; a watcher draws the rays of the participant it follows whole, from inside their head, and nothing else of them. Every fade, a jump's, silence's and leaving's too, shrinks each cube where it rests, so that a person dissolves rather than shrinks.

**Drawing.** The frame step gives the renderer each person as a figure of cubes (`Frame::people`), at most 531 each (`people::CUBES`): the swarm's 399, both hands and both controllers cross-fading at once, and a bar and a dot for each ray; with both hands tracked, 475. How a person looks is made in one place, `people::swarm::look`, from their presence as drawn, their body, kept from frame to frame (`swarm::Body`), and the cues (`swarm::Cues`), so that another look replaces it alone. **A frame with others in the space allocates nothing** once everyone is known: their presence heard, `Conversation::people`, whose voices play, their tracks, their figures and the hands an environment's client side is given are each kept from frame to frame and cleared, never collected anew. The renderer submits each of the first 16 people (`MOST_PEOPLE`) as a dynamic item of the render world of their own, in the scene's coordinates, culled whole and then cube by cube, and draws those seen among the rest of Tomori's cubes, in their one draw, hazed as they are and glowing, by each instance's glow, which the rest have none of: people add no draw call, no pass and no pipeline (see `rendering.md`). A watcher, as the spectator, draws them as a participant does, but for the swarm of the participant it follows, whose rays alone it draws (see `spectator.md`).

**Rays are sent while drawn in full.** A participant's presence carries `pointing`, each of their two rays drawn in full on their headset, left then right (`tomori_protocol::Ray`): its aim pose, pointing along the pose's −Z, how far it reaches, to what it meets or as far as an idle ray drawn in full reaches, and whether it ends on something it meets. A ray is drawn in full while it meets something, while its hand is busy, and for a second after (see `interaction.md`). A stub, a hidden ray, the hand menu's ray and a teleport's aim are never sent, since they are the participant's own: each is `null`. `pointing` is sent whenever one of the rays is shown, and left out while neither is, so that the server tells a current client, whose stubs point at nothing, from an older one, which sends none. The server sounds each ray as it sounds poses, a finite aim within reach with a rotation, made unit, and a finite length of more than nothing and at most `RAY_REACH`, 100 m, drops one that is not, and relays the rest to the others in the space with the poses, leaving `pointing` out where none is left. It adds about 130 bytes to a report for each ray drawn in full, and 24 while only stubs are shown.

**What others see is each participant's choice.** The hand menu's People & security section, and the console's `others`, set **Others see: everything / head only / my name only** on this device, everything by default, kept with its other settings (`feedback::Settings::others_see`), and the next presence sent honours it. *Everything* sends the head, the controllers' grips, tracked hands and rays drawn in full. *Head only* sends the head alone, with no controllers, hands or rays, nor which rays show, so that the others draw the swarm with its hands hanging, environments' objects held follow the head, as for a desktop client, and `security.md`'s reach checks take from the head, and the agent is told of no ray. *My name only* sends the head alone too, since their voice is placed there and the agent follows them by it, and presence's `hidden` asks the others not to draw them: nothing of them is drawn, until labels exist (stage 3), a spectator never follows them, seeing from their head, and the server relays `hidden` with the head. The menu says in one line that this hides them from view, not from the server. Neither changes a role or what anyone may do.

**The agent is told what each ray points at from its aim.** The `presence` tool resolves what each of a participant's rays points at from the aim it was sent with (see `mcp.md`). From an older client, which sends no aims, each ray its client says is shown is resolved from its controller's pose, as before, which is the grip the headset draws the controller at rather than the aim its ray is drawn from.

## Rationale

On #417's review the maintainer noticed that nobody is drawn in a space, and asked for other people's heads, hands and rays to be drawn. Approving the proposal, they asked for the people to be stylised, and a bit playful, rather than human or photo-realistic, and for a cue that someone is looking at you, which helps in conversation; the proposal records the cue's cone, its reach and its easing. The visor block reads at once as a person in a headset facing a way, and is made of the cube primitive, so it is hazed, lit and culled like the rest of Tomori with no assets; a face would be uncanny without expression data. Others' rays are drawn only while drawn in full on their own headset, as the maintainer agreed, so that a room of four is not a room of eight beams.

Seeing stage 1's visor block, the maintainer found it minimal and asked for something more suited to Tomori: a simpler swarm for people than the agent's, roughly showing a body and arms at least, legs being less interesting, with more effort on the head. Of three concepts, a torso and arms of glowing cubes dissolving below the chest, three heads, and eyes that warm as someone looks at you beside the agent's violet cloud, they found all three very cool, even with a few fewer cubes, and chose the second head's rounded cluster, with two soft eyes and an orbit. The body is guessed from the head and hands alone, which three poses cannot pin down, so it is loose: cubes that drift forgive an elbow a little off, and legs, which nothing tracks, are left out rather than guessed. Turning the body with the head's yaw alone, damped, keeps it steady as someone nods or looks about, as a body is. The eyes carry the gaze cue, which the visor did, and the orbit the voice, which the client knows by source, so neither needs anything more sent. The first look built this way was matte, its cubes flat peach, uniform, solid and chunky, where the concepts' magic was light; on review, which the maintainer approved, the cubes became the person's own light, smaller and of many sizes, sparser, with a much stronger dissolve below the chest, and the eyes small, round and soft rather than two large squares, which read as a skull's. Brightness falling off towards each part's outline as seen, rather than blending, makes the edges look translucent with no sorting and no pass, and one glow for each instance, which every other cube has none of, keeps it in the one draw. That second look went too sparse, which the maintainer's review, approved, found: from 3.6 m people barely read as people, their silhouette broke into scattered dots, the head lost head B's dense round shape, and the eyes were two smudged blobs, the shell about them warming and its cubes covering their edges; nor did anything glow, Tomori having no bloom pass. The third look is the middle ground: the head twice as dense, its cubes packed on its outside; the shoulders and upper chest dense, sparse only at the edges and below the chest; the eyes each a crisp round in a socket, which no cube covers; a tidy orbit; and arms of rods, which the maintainer suggested over the small cubes that broke a limb into dots. A larger, dimmer copy behind the eyes and hands glows by brightness and size alone; one about the head's outline was tried and left out, since it only made the head larger and less round. A few hundred cubes, in the batch Tomori's cubes already draw, cost no draw call: on the desktop's RTX 4090, drawing two eyes of 1832 by 1920 with four samples, eight people in view add about 0.07 ms of the GPU's frame, where the second look added 0.06 ms and the first 0.08 to 0.09 ms, and about 38,000 triangles an eye, the second's 29,000; their swarms take about 4 µs of the CPU a person, and the renderer's culling, copying and drawing about 20 µs more, in the test profile, the second look's a little less measured alike. The hands' gleam is 6 of a person's cubes and costs too little to measure. A Quest 3, its GPU perhaps fifteen times slower, would spend roughly 0.75 ms for four and 1 ms for eight, and, its CPU perhaps four times slower, about 0.4 ms of it for four and 0.7 ms for eight, which the frame log's GPU and CPU lines on the headset are to confirm. Fewer cubes beyond 5 m and a head alone beyond 15 m keep a crowded space to a few thousand. On the third look the maintainer found the eyes "a bit scary looking": each a round dot in a dark socket, turning gold with a halo on eye contact, read as a skull. The fourth look, which they approved, follows head B's small, soft eye dots instead: no sockets, the eyes flush on the face, smaller, a warm off-white, a little low and set wide; eye contact a gentle warm brightening with no halo, on the same thresholds; and a natural blink, which a face of cubes otherwise lacks. Its arms' rods are a little thicker and tapered, with a few loose cubes about them, so that figures read less as stick figures; at 399 cubes in full, three more than the third look, its cost is the same.

The proposal first coloured each person from their tag, in a palette of eight, which would have sent the tag to every client. Seeing the swarms on the headset, the maintainer decided instead: "Compute a colour based on their name… set the default based on the name but allow it to be changed, and stored, per person." A name is in the space's state already, so a colour from it needs nothing more sent, and stays the same across reconnects and devices as long as the name does. Keeping a chosen colour with the token, as the name is kept, makes it the person's rather than a device's, and needs only a hue a participant in the state and one message to change it; a chosen colour wins so that what someone chose is what everyone sees, the defaults, which nobody chose, giving way. A continuous wheel rather than a palette gives two names fewer chances to share a colour, and keeping defaults apart within a space, in its participant order, gives everyone the same answer without any client telling another. The arcs keep people clear of the colours that already mean something in Tomori, the agent's and the states', so that a person in a swarm's glow is never read as the agent or as a worker waiting or failing. The transcript's six colours from each public handle are replaced by the same colour, so that a person reads as one colour everywhere: the old palette's violet was the agent's.

Presence carries no time, so people move on #417's timeline, which held a hand still on at most 0.8% of frames with 40 ms of jitter; extrapolating would overshoot at every stop, which shows most on a head. Drawing what someone holds and the hand holding it from one track keeps them together, which two tracks of the same updates could not promise.

Presence sent the headset's controllers' grip poses, which it draws its controllers at, while its rays are drawn from the aim poses, which were never sent, so the server told the agent what a ray pointed at along the grip's −Z, which OpenXR tilts from the aim. Sending each ray's aim while it is drawn in full gives the others what to draw and the server what the participant sees their ray meet. A stub is sent as `null` rather than its aim, since what it points at is nothing the participant can act on, and sending `pointing` whenever a ray is shown costs a few bytes a frame rather than a capability in `Hello` that every connection would carry.

Approving stage 2 after seeing the swarm on the headset, the maintainer said "I'm also okay if we show people's hands, it's probably useful." Joints and bones read a gesture where a cluster at the palm cannot, and are what this headset already draws of its own hands; the arm ends at the wrist, where the hand's joints begin. A controller drawn as the runtime's model would show this participant's hardware rather than theirs, so it is a stylised one. Fading rather than freezing a lost hand, and cross-fading, keep a hand from hanging still where it no longer is or flickering between the two. On the desktop's RTX 4090, two eyes of 1832 by 1920 with four samples, eight people each with both hands tracked are 608 cubes and about 7,300 triangles an eye more than eight without, within the frame's noise on the GPU; the people step takes about 0.75 µs more a person and the renderer about 20 µs more for the eight. A Quest 3 would spend roughly 0.05 ms of its GPU and 0.05 ms of its CPU more for four people with tracked hands, and 0.1 ms each for eight. *Head only* and *my name only* follow the proposal's decision 4; sending the head even for *my name only* keeps voices placed and the agent's following, and the setting says it hides them from view, not from the server, which still sees the head.

## Scope

`tomori_scene::people`, `people::swarm` and `people::hands`, and their tests; each person's colour, `tomori_protocol::hue`, and the conversation's working out of a space's colours, `tomori_scene::speakers::Known`, which the transcript, the queue and the hand menu's swatches share; a chosen colour, `Participant::colour`, `SpaceState::colours` and `set_colour` (`crates/protocol`), kept with a token, `Entry::colour` and `TokenStore::recolour` (`crates/security`), and by the server, `HubState::recolour_request` and `Hub::keep_colour` (`crates/server`); *Others see*, `feedback::OthersSee`, honoured by the frame step's presence and presence's `hidden`; `relayed`, the timeline it and an environment's client side share, whose `Others` the frame step keeps; the frame step's `Frame::people`, its presence's `pointing` and `pointing::Beam::sent`, and the watcher's `Step::follow` (`crates/scene`); the renderer's `Renderer::set_people`, its culling and the glow of Tomori's cubes (`crates/render`); `tomori_protocol::Ray` and presence's `pointing` (`crates/protocol`); `Connection::send_presence`, and whose voices play, `PlayerControl::voices` and `Report::Voices`, which `Conversation::voices` keeps (`crates/client`, `crates/scene`); and the server's relaying and resolving of it (`crates/server`'s `hub/act/presence.rs` and `presence.rs`).
