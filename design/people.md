# People

This describes how the others in a space are drawn, as the first stage of `docs/proposals/drawing-other-people.md`, which the maintainer approved in November 2026: their heads, their rays, a cue when someone looks at you, and what presence carries for them. Hands and controllers, the privacy setting, colours, labels and pointing at people are its later stages.

## Requirement

**A person is drawn as a head and their rays**, built of Tomori's cubes, in the space's scene coordinates, from their presence (`tomori_scene::people`). Nobody is drawn but the others in the joined space whom its state lists, never this participant, and nothing of anyone is drawn outside a space.

- **The head** is a visor block, about 18 by 14 by 16 cm, of three overlapping blocks that round its edges, with a lighter top, a pod on either side, a little antenna off to one side and a dark visor band across its front, standing about where their eyes are, a little behind them, facing where they look. It is stylised for fun, not human, with no face, and its front and the way it faces read at once. In this stage it is one neutral grey for everyone.
- **A ray** is drawn while it is drawn in full on their headset, as presence carries it (below): from its aim as far as it reaches, in this participant's own ray's style, highlighted while it meets something, 0.6 as bright, ending in a small dot where it meets something. Nothing else of their rays is drawn.

**Eye contact.** Someone within 8 m whose head faces this participant's eyes, within 10° of where it looks, lights their visor, its sheen and their antenna's tip a warm glow, and their pods a little, over 0.2 s, the half of the visor towards this participant a little lighter, and it goes out over 0.4 s once their head faces more than 15° away, so that a glance at the edge, or a head wavering there, does not flicker. Each client works it out for itself from the head poses presence already carries, so nothing more is sent and only the one looked at sees it; a watcher sees it towards the participant it follows.

**Motion.** Everyone is drawn from their whole presence, head, controllers, tracked hands and rays, between its updates, on the timeline `relayed::Relayed` follows (see `physics.md`): each update placed when it arrived, drawn about an update behind, at most `MOST`, 50 ms, and holding still beyond the latest, never extrapolated. An environment's client side is given the others' hands from the same tracks, as the people holding them are drawn that frame, so that what someone holds and the hand holding it are the same pose in the same frame.

- **A jump** between two updates of more than `JUMP`, 1.5 m, as a teleport, fades them out over 0.1 s where they were drawn and in over 0.1 s where they are, never gliding between; what they hold stays with the hand drawn.
- **Silence.** Someone whose presence has said nothing for 2 s fades out over 0.5 s, and is drawn again as their next report arrives; a report alike to the last still counts. What they hold stays in their hand.
- **Leaving.** Someone no longer in the space's participants fades out over 0.3 s where they last were, and is no longer given to an environment's client side. Once the conversation forgets the others, on connecting and on joining or leaving a space, everyone is drawn anew where they first are, and nobody fades.
- **Up close.** Others are drawn fainter, shrinking about their head's middle, within 0.6 m of this participant's eyes, and not at all within 0.3 m, so that two people arriving at one place are never inside each other's heads.

**Drawing.** The frame step gives the renderer each person as a figure of cubes (`Frame::people`), at most 15 each: the head's 11, and a bar and a dot for each ray. The renderer submits each of the first 16 people (`MOST_PEOPLE`) as a dynamic item of the render world of their own, in the scene's coordinates, culled whole and then cube by cube, and draws those seen among the rest of Tomori's cubes, in their one draw, hazed and lit as they are: people add no draw call and no pipeline (see `rendering.md`). A watcher, as the spectator, draws them as a participant does, but for the head of the participant it follows (see `spectator.md`).

**Rays are sent while drawn in full.** A participant's presence carries `pointing`, each of their two rays drawn in full on their headset, left then right (`tomori_protocol::Ray`): its aim pose, pointing along the pose's −Z, how far it reaches, to what it meets or as far as an idle ray drawn in full reaches, and whether it ends on something it meets. A ray is drawn in full while it meets something, while its hand is busy, and for a second after (see `interaction.md`). A stub, a hidden ray, the hand menu's ray and a teleport's aim are never sent, since they are the participant's own: each is `null`. `pointing` is sent whenever one of the rays is shown, and left out while neither is, so that the server tells a current client, whose stubs point at nothing, from an older one, which sends none. The server sounds each ray as it sounds poses, a finite aim within reach with a rotation, made unit, and a finite length of more than nothing and at most `RAY_REACH`, 100 m, drops one that is not, and relays the rest to the others in the space with the poses, leaving `pointing` out where none is left. It adds about 130 bytes to a report for each ray drawn in full, and 24 while only stubs are shown.

**The agent is told what each ray points at from its aim.** The `presence` tool resolves what each of a participant's rays points at from the aim it was sent with (see `mcp.md`). From an older client, which sends no aims, each ray its client says is shown is resolved from its controller's pose, as before, which is the grip the headset draws the controller at rather than the aim its ray is drawn from.

## Rationale

On #417's review the maintainer noticed that nobody is drawn in a space, and asked for other people's heads, hands and rays to be drawn. Approving the proposal, they asked for the people to be stylised, and a bit playful, rather than human or photo-realistic, and for a cue that someone is looking at you, which helps in conversation; the proposal records the cue's cone, its reach and its easing. The visor block reads at once as a person in a headset facing a way, and is made of the cube primitive, so it is hazed, lit and culled like the rest of Tomori with no assets; a face would be uncanny without expression data. Others' rays are drawn only while drawn in full on their own headset, as the maintainer agreed, so that a room of four is not a room of eight beams.

Presence carries no time, so people move on #417's timeline, which held a hand still on at most 0.8% of frames with 40 ms of jitter; extrapolating would overshoot at every stop, which shows most on a head. Drawing what someone holds and the hand holding it from one track keeps them together, which two tracks of the same updates could not promise.

Presence sent the headset's controllers' grip poses, which it draws its controllers at, while its rays are drawn from the aim poses, which were never sent, so the server told the agent what a ray pointed at along the grip's −Z, which OpenXR tilts from the aim. Sending each ray's aim while it is drawn in full gives the others what to draw and the server what the participant sees their ray meet. A stub is sent as `null` rather than its aim, since what it points at is nothing the participant can act on, and sending `pointing` whenever a ray is shown costs a few bytes a frame rather than a capability in `Hello` that every connection would carry.

## Scope

`tomori_scene::people` and its tests; `relayed`, the timeline it and an environment's client side share; the frame step's `Frame::people`, its presence's `pointing` and `pointing::Beam::sent`, and the watcher's `Step::follow` (`crates/scene`); the renderer's `Renderer::set_people` and its culling (`crates/render`); `tomori_protocol::Ray` and presence's `pointing` (`crates/protocol`); `Connection::send_presence` (`crates/client`); and the server's relaying and resolving of it (`crates/server`'s `hub/act/presence.rs` and `presence.rs`).

## Validation

- `tomori-scene`'s `people/tests.rs`: a head is a visor block of about 18 by 14 by 16 cm facing where they look, its visor dark at its front, and each ray drawn in full reaches from its aim, fainter than this participant's own, the one meeting something highlighted and ending in a dot; a watcher following someone draws their rays alone; what is not a pose, a hand or a ray is left out, and a head that is not a pose is nobody; drawn from their whole presence, someone walking while their reports arrive unevenly never holds still nor moves two frames' worth at once, and the hand an environment's client side is given is the hand drawn, and the hand alone would be drawn so; a jump fades them out where they were over 0.1 s and in where they are over 0.1 s, never between, and a metre is no jump; silent for 2 s, someone fades out over 0.5 s and back in as they report, a report alike to the last counting; someone who leaves fades out where they were over 0.3 s, and once the others are forgotten everyone is drawn anew; within 0.6 m of the eyes someone is drawn fainter, about their middle, and within 0.3 m not at all; a visor lights within 10° over 0.2 s, holds out to 15°, goes out beyond it over 0.4 s, lights again only within 10°, does not flicker for a head wavering at the edge, lit or not, is lighter on the half towards this participant, and lights for nobody beside them nor anyone farther than 8 m; and a presence drawn between two updates turns and reaches between, taking whatever one lacks from the latest. `relayed.rs`'s tests keep #417's figures for a hand alone, and a number that may be someone else's is drawn where it first is.
- `tomori-render`'s `scene_graphics/tests/people.rs`, on lavapipe in CI: two people at the Tomori wall seen from home, one looking this way, their visor lit, the other pointing at a panel, are drawn in the draw Tomori's cubes already make, while someone behind the eyes is culled whole; and cubes that are not numbers are not drawn, nor more than 16 people. Set `TOMORI_PEOPLE_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to save what it renders, from home and from beside them.
- `tomori-scene`'s `scene/watching/tests.rs`, as `spectator.md` says.
- `tomori-protocol`'s tests: presence round-trips with and without `pointing`, a stub as `null`, a ray meeting nothing without `meets`, and an older peer's presence reads as none; a ray is valid only aimed somewhere, with a rotation, finite and within reach.
- `tomori-scene`'s frame step tests: a stub, the hand menu's ray and a teleport's aim are sent as `null`, a ray drawn in full from its aim as far as it reaches, and one on a worker meeting it.
- `tomori-server`'s tests: a ray drawn in full is relayed sounded and one that is no ray is not, stubs alone relay no `pointing`; the agent is told what a ray points at from its aim rather than its grip, a current client's stub points at nothing, and an older client's shown ray is resolved from its controller.
- On the headset, with two devices: each sees the other's head where they stand, facing where they look, their visor lighting as they meet the other's eyes, and their ray while it is drawn in full; and the frame log's GPU line is no slower with someone in the space.
