# Timers and the Event Queue

This describes how a space's agent sets timers that wake it on a schedule, attached to whether anyone is present, and the space's event queue, through which Tomori tells the agent what its own sources noticed, timers first.

## Requirement

**What a timer is.** A space's agent may set timers in its space with Tomori's `set_timer` tool (see `mcp.md`), so that Tomori wakes it, rather than the agent sleeping in a shell or a sub-agent to wait. Each timer has a name, a note in the agent's own words saying what it is for, a schedule, a condition and a time zone, and optionally an end and a count of firings. When a timer fires, Tomori tells the agent through the space's event queue, below, never mid-turn. A timer belongs to its space, not to the session the space is bound to, and is kept with the space, below.

**Schedules.** `set_timer` takes exactly one schedule, each in a field of its own, so that the tool's schema says which forms there are and each is checked by its type:

- `every_minutes`, from 5 to 10,080, a week: where the number divides a day, as 5, 15, 30, 60, 90, 120 or 1440 do, at every multiple of it after midnight by the clock of its zone, as times of day are, so that every hour falls on the hour, every two hours on the even hours and every day at midnight, before and after daylight saving changes; otherwise every so many minutes of elapsed time from when it was set.
- `daily_at`, `HH:MM` on the 24-hour clock, with optional `days`, a list of day names or their first three letters, `weekdays`, `weekends` or `daily`, by default every day: at that time of day on those days.
- `once_at`: once, at the next `HH:MM`, today if it is still ahead and otherwise tomorrow, or at a `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`, after now and within 366 days.
- `in_minutes`, from 1 to 527,040, 366 days: once, that many minutes from now.
- `cron`: a five-field cron expression, minute, hour, day of the month, month and day of the week, of numbers, `*`, lists, ranges and steps, such as `*/30 9-17 * * 1-5`; Sunday is 0 or 7. As in Vixie cron, where the day of the month or the day of the week begins with `*`, as `*` or `*/2` does, a day must match both, so that `0 9 */2 * *` fires on odd days and `0 9 */2 * 1` on odd days that are Mondays; where both are otherwise restricted, a day matching either fires, so that `0 0 13 * 5` fires on every 13th and every Friday. Names of months and days, `L`, `W`, `#` and `?` are refused. A cron schedule that has no occurrence within four years and a week, such as the 31st of February, is refused.

**Time zones and daylight saving.** A timer's times of day are in its `zone`, an IANA name such as `Europe/London`, by default the zone of the machine the server runs on, or UTC where it has no IANA name; the zone is kept with the timer as it is set, so that moving the server does not move its timers. A time of day that does not exist, as clocks go forward, fires as far past the gap's start as it is, so that 02:30 fires at 03:30 where 02:00 becomes 03:00; a time of day that happens twice, as clocks go back, fires once, at the first. An `every_minutes` that divides a day follows the same rule for each of its times, so that, as clocks go forward, an hourly timer's hour that does not exist fires once, at the next hour, and as they go back, the repeated hour fires once, at its first; one that does not divide a day counts elapsed time, which daylight saving does not touch. This holds in zones whose clocks move by half an hour, as Lord Howe Island's, or whose offset is not a whole hour, as Chatham's.

**Present.** A participant is present in a space while they are in it as an owner or a collaborator, connected, and their client reports them `engaged` or `distracted` (see `spaces.md`): wearing their headset, or with the desktop window visible. A visitor, a participant whose client reports no engagement, such as the spectator or an older client, and a participant who is `away` or has gone, are not present. A space counts as attended while someone is present, and for 2 minutes after the last participant stopped being present, the grace, so that lifting a headset for a moment or reconnecting neither skips a firing nor counts as arriving again. Someone **arrives** when the space was not attended and a participant becomes present.

**Conditions.** A timer's condition says whether it fires while nobody is present:

- `present`, the default: an occurrence fires only if the space is attended as it falls due; otherwise it is skipped, not saved for later, and the next firing says how many were skipped.
- `present_or_arrival`: as `present`, but an occurrence that falls due while the space is not attended waits, and fires once, as the first participant arrives, if someone arrives before the timer's next occurrence, or within 24 hours for its last; otherwise it lapses, counted as skipped. It never fires twice for one occurrence, so leaving and coming back the same morning fires nothing more. This is "every morning at 9 when someone is present, or after 9 when they join for the first time".
- `always`: every occurrence fires, whether or not anyone is present, still never mid-turn. It is bounded more tightly: its occurrences are at least 15 minutes apart, and a space delivers at most 24 firings in any 24 hours while it is not attended.

**Missed occurrences.** The server looks at every timer of a space whose agent can be told, open, bound to its session and connected to it, at each sweep, about every second, by the clock. Occurrences that fell due since a timer was last looked at, as while the server was stopped, the space was closed or not yet bound again after a restart, or Tomori was reconnecting to the agent, coalesce into one: the latest of them is evaluated against the condition, and fires at most once, saying how many before it were missed, counting at most 999. A closed space's timers are not looked at, so that what fell due meanwhile is caught up once it is bound again. After a restart, a space's timers are not looked at for the grace, 2 minutes from when they are first looked at, while nobody is present yet, so that a catch-up as the space is bound again, before headsets reconnect, counts the participants who come back. Occurrences held back by the limits, below, are counted, and the next firing says so.

**Limits.** A space keeps at most 8 timers. A name is 1 to 40 characters on one line, unique in its space in any case, as Unicode lowercases it, the same comparison wherever a name is matched, set, cancelled or pointed at, and setting a timer of a name the space has replaces that timer; a timer set without a name takes `timer N`, the next number whose name no timer has, so that it never replaces one. A note is 1 to 200 characters, on one line. A timer's occurrences are at least 5 minutes apart, which a cron schedule's minute field is checked for, counting from the last of an hour to the first of the next; `always` timers' at least 15. A space delivers at most 96 firings in any 24 hours, of all its timers, and at most 24 of them while it is not attended; a firing remembered as later than now, as after the clock is set back, counts towards neither. `count` is at least 1, and `until` is after now and within 366 days; a timer with no occurrence before its end is refused. A timer that has fired its count, or has no occurrence ahead and none waiting for an arrival, is removed.

**The event queue.** Each space has an event queue of notes from Tomori's own sources, of which timers are the first: a note has its source, a key and its text. A note replaces a note of the same source and key not yet told, counting it, so that an hourly timer that fired twice while the agent worked is told once, saying `and 1 earlier, coalesced`; at most 16 notes wait, the oldest left out beyond that and counted. What waits is told to the agent only while it can be given input: the space is open, the agent's session idle, nothing is waiting for delivery or a decision, and Tomori is not reconnecting. Participants' words queued then take every note waiting with them, before the notes of what participants did, such as ticking an item (see `projection.md`), so that the agent reads both in one turn. Otherwise a prompt of the notes alone is given only once 10 seconds have passed since the agent's turn ended, so that it does not cut in on what was just said, 30 seconds since the last such prompt, so that a burst of events arrives as one, and while nobody in the space is speaking to the agent, since their words will take the notes; words count as being spoken for at most 2 minutes after they began, longer than anyone holds X, and a client that disconnects takes its open words with it, so that a client that drops or never ends its words cannot hold the notes back. A block of notes is at most 2,000 characters, each note at most 600, the latest kept.

**How a note reads.** Each note is in square brackets, on one line, its own square brackets made parentheses, so that it can neither end early nor begin another; a prompt of notes alone has no speaker's name, so it cannot be taken for a participant's words, whose square brackets are already made parentheses (`interaction.md`). A timer's note gives its name and its schedule and condition in short words, when it fired, as `HH:MM`, or a day and time where it was not today, in its zone, whether it fired as someone arrived and when it was due, when it last fired, what was missed, skipped and held back, who is present, whether it was its last firing, and the agent's own note, quoted, double quotes in names and notes made single quotes: `[Timer "status" (every hour, when present): fired 10:00; last fired 09:00; present: Samuel; note: "Give a status update"]`. The note is data, the agent's own reminder: the tool's description and `tomori-tools` tell the agent that it is not a request from anyone, and to decide then what, if anything, is worth doing or saying.

**Whose turn it is.** A prompt of notes alone is no participant's request: it supersedes the words the agent was given last, so that no tool that needs `requested_by` acts on those words in a turn a timer began (see `security.md`), and the agent is asked to have whoever wants it say so again. It is Tomori's own delivery, so the space remembers its key as one it gave, and its delivery is not taken for input that did not come through Tomori. A following agent goes to nobody for it (see `environments.md`). It goes only to the session the space is bound to; a space bound to another session, as when its session is replaced, keeps its timers and tells the session bound now. Notes waiting in the queue are for this run only; a timer's occurrences not yet looked at are kept, and caught up after a restart.

**The tools.** `set_timer`, `timers` and `cancel_timer` are space tools, acting only on the space bound to the calling session (see `mcp.md`). The agent sets a timer when someone asks, or to check back on work it started, and cancels one when someone asks or it is no longer needed; participants cancel a timer by asking the agent, as they have it remove a worker. Neither needs `requested_by`: a timer tells the agent only its own note, within the limits above, and a turn it begins can act on nobody's word.

**Kept with the space.** A space's timers are kept with it in the state directory, in `KeptSpace::timers`, in a format of their own with its version, now 1: each timer, with its next occurrence not yet looked at and an occurrence waiting for an arrival, the times of the space's latest firings, for the daily limits, and the number the next unnamed timer is called by. They are written as a timer is set, cancelled or fires. The record is read leniently, so that it never stops the spaces from loading: a version this server does not read leaves every timer out, and a timer it cannot read, such as one of a schedule kind a newer server knows, a cron it refuses or a field edited by hand, is left out alone, each with a warning naming the space and the timer, the space and its other timers loading; at most 8 are read. A timer kept in a zone this server does not know goes by UTC, with a warning, and `timers` says so of it. Deleting a space deletes its timers, and a space forgotten deletes them with it.

**Drawn in the space.** The space's state carries its timers, `SpaceState::timers`, in the order they were set, each a `tomori_protocol::Timer`: its name, schedule and condition in short words, when it next fires, in seconds since the Unix epoch, for a countdown, and its state, `waiting`, `paused` while a timer that fires only while someone is present has nobody, `awaiting` while an occurrence waits for an arrival, or `fired` for a minute after it fires, each for a distinct look; an older client ignores them. A participant pointing at a timer as they speak names it as `tomori_protocol::Resource::Timer`, by its name, and the server tells the agent of it as it describes it then, `[Pointing at timer "status" (every hour, when present; next in 12 min)]`, as it describes a worker pointed at (`interaction.md`). Drawing each timer as a small dial beside the workers, on the desktop, the headset and, where workers are drawn, the spectator, pointable as they are, is the second increment, below.

## Rationale

In November 2026 the maintainer found that the agent coordinating a space, which answers participants aloud, cannot wake itself, and fakes a watchdog with a background `sleep`. They asked for timers on Tomori's server and tools, attached to presence and cron-like: "fire every morning at 9am when someone is present, or after 9am when they join for the first time", "fire every hour if someone is present", or just "fire every hour"; for a visualisation in the space like the workers'; and for delivery through a generic event queue that other sources will use next, such as a worker that failed, went idle without reporting or stalled, proposed separately as `events.md` on the `worker-events` branch.

**Structured fields and cron both.** Structured fields cover what people say, "every hour", "weekdays at nine", "at half past two", "in twenty minutes", each typed and checked, and are what an agent reads off a request most reliably; a cron string alone would make the agent translate every request into a notation it may get subtly wrong, and refuse nothing helpful. Cron earns a small place for what the fields cannot say, such as every half hour in working hours on weekdays, and its subset is the one every cron reader agrees on. Alignment to the day for an interval that divides it makes "every hour" mean on the hour, which participants expect and a countdown shows plainly; elapsed time keeps an interval from doubling or skipping at daylight saving. Wall-clock forms resolve gaps forward and overlaps to the first, so that a time of day fires once a day whatever the clocks do.

**What present means.** Engagement is the only signal a client gives of someone actually being there (`spaces.md`), and `distracted` is still someone wearing the headset. A client that reports nothing counts as listening for speech, but the spectator, the one client that reports nothing on purpose, watches for an audience and is no one to tell, so timers count only a client that says someone is there. The grace keeps a headset lifted for a moment, or a reconnection, from skipping an hourly firing or counting as a fresh arrival; a skipped occurrence is not saved for later because a stale note on arrival is what `present_or_arrival` is for, and only when the agent asked for it.

**Coalescing and pacing.** An agent woken many times for one thing wastes its turns and talks over people, so occurrences missed while nobody could be told arrive as one, saying how many, and a burst of notes arrives as one prompt. Waiting a quiet moment after the agent spoke, and while anyone speaks, keeps the agent from interrupting itself or a participant; participants' words carry the notes so that the agent hears both at once rather than in two turns.

**Data, not instructions.** The note text is the agent's own, but a timer fires when no one is asking; marking it as Tomori's, with square brackets that participants' words never have, and superseding the words given last, keeps a timer from being taken for, or standing behind, a request. The limits bound what a timer can cost, which is the agent's turns, since an `always` timer can wake it with nobody present.

**A generic queue.** The queue knows sources and keys, not timers, so that the worker events of `events.md` push notes of their own source, one for each condition, and share the pacing, the framing and the supersession above, without another path into the agent's session; they add a pace of their own, at most once every 2 minutes for stalls, a failure told at once, and caps of their own, as `events.md` describes.

**Drawing deferred.** Workers are drawn by a layout the server seats, their stations, signs and swarm flights, in `tomori-scene` and `tomori-render`; a timer's dial follows none of that closely enough to add in the same change without building most of a second layout. This first increment carries everything a client needs, the state and the pointing, so that the dials are a client change on a server that already sends them.

## Scope

`crates/server`'s `timers.rs` and `timers/schedule.rs`, the pure scheduler; `events.rs`, the event queue; `space.rs`, which holds both and gives a prompt of notes; `hub/agent_timers.rs`, the tools and the hub looking at timers; the `set_timer`, `timers` and `cancel_timer` definitions in `mcp.rs`; `kept.rs`'s `timers`; the server's sweep in `server.rs`; and `hub/provenance.rs`'s refusal after a prompt of notes. `tomori-protocol`'s `Timer`, `TimerState`, `SpaceState::timers` and `Resource::Timer`.

## Validation

- Test, in `crates/server/src/timers/tests.rs`, cron's day fields as Vixie cron reads them, `*/2` in either, with a weekday, `*/1`, and both restricted; daylight saving in Pacific/Auckland's September gap and April overlap, daily at 02:30 and hourly, Australia/Lord_Howe's half-hour shift and Pacific/Chatham's; intervals that divide a day staying on the clock through daylight saving; a catch-up after a restart waiting the grace; names matched in any case, beyond ASCII, and default names skipping those in use; firings later than now forgotten; and a record it cannot read leaving out only what it cannot read: a future version, an unknown schedule kind, an invalid cron and a bad field in one of two timers, with `kept.rs` loading the spaces of such a record. Test there each form's next occurrence in Europe/London: aligned and unaligned intervals, an interval over the spring gap, daily, weekdays, weekends and chosen days, a time in the gap and in the overlap, firing once, a one-off and one past, cron in and out of working hours, the 31st skipping a month, the 29th of February, the day of the month or week, Sunday as 7, steps, lists, cron in the gap in order, and a cron that never fires; counting occurrences to a bound; short words for every form and condition; days, cron fields and times of day read and refused; every refusal of `Timer::new`, and names, zones, notes and first occurrences of those it sets.
- Test, there, the conditions: `present` firing while someone is present and skipping, said in the next firing; the grace; `present_or_arrival` waiting, firing once on arrival, not again that period, and lapsing at its period's end, a one-off's after a day; `always` firing with nobody present within its own limit; the space's daily limit for firings and arrivals; occurrences missed while stopped coalescing into one, counting at most 999; a count, an end and a one-off finishing a timer; how a timer looks, waiting, paused, awaiting and fired, and is described pointed at; names, replacing, the most and cancelling; and what is kept, read again, caught up after a restart, and left out for another version.
- Test, in `crates/server/src/events.rs`, that notes coalesce by source and key, counting, a burst is one block, a note cannot end its brackets or begin another and is bounded, the latest are kept and those left out counted, and that a prompt of notes alone waits for a quiet moment after the turn and the gap since the last.
- Test, in `crates/server/src/space.rs`, that notes wait while someone speaks, mid-turn and just after the agent spoke; that a prompt of them is given after the quiet moment, supersedes the words given last as Tomori's own, and is not taken for input from elsewhere; that words take the notes before the notes of ticks; and that a timer the space does not have is not described.
- Test, through the hub with the fake backend and the time given (`crates/server/src/hub_tests/timers.rs`), that a timer fires only while someone is present, a participant reporting no engagement not counting, waits for the agent's turn and a quiet moment, and is told as Tomori's note with no speaker; that a sensitive tool is then refused on the words given before; that firings coalesce while the agent works and ride on a participant's words; that notes wait while someone speaks, but not for words begun longer ago than their bound, and that a client disconnecting takes its open words with it; that pointing at a timer names it; that a timer in a zone the server does not know says so in `timers`; that the tools list, replace, refuse and cancel, only for the space's own session, and say when an occurrence waits for an arrival; and that a closed space's timers wait, are kept as they fire and are restored by another run.
- Test, through the MCP endpoint (`crates/server/tests/mcp_timers.rs`), that `set_timer` sets each form, that everyone in the space sees the timers, that `timers` lists them and `cancel_timer` cancels one, and every refusal of arguments; and that `tools/list` lists the three tools (`tests/mcp.rs`).
- Test, in `tomori-protocol`, that `SpaceState::timers` is carried, absent from an older server's state, and that a timer's state a newer server sends is `Unknown`; and that `Resource::Timer` is read from an utterance's attention.

## Open questions

- The second increment: how a timer's dial looks, its countdown, schedule and condition in short words, and a distinct look while paused, awaiting and just fired; where it stands beside the workers, whether as a member of the home cluster with its own footprint; whether the spectator draws it; and the ray's label for it.
- Whether a participant may cancel a timer from the ray or the hand menu, rather than by asking the agent, and who may: workers have no such control yet either.
- Whether a timer may be one participant's, "remind me", firing only while they are present.
- Whether notes waiting in the queue should survive a restart, rather than only occurrences not yet looked at.
- Whether the limits, 8 timers, 96 firings a day and 24 unattended, suit real use; they are to be tuned from the logs.
