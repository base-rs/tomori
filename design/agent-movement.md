# Agent Movement

This describes where a space's agent and its workers stand and how the agent moves among the participants: its home, following whoever it talks with, being called, pinned, sent and walked with, and how its workers stand at home and at an environment's anchors. The homes and anchors an environment declares are described in `environments.md`; how participants travel, in `locomotion.md`.

## Requirement

- **The agent's home.** The agent's home is the environment's `agent` carried into the space, or else the default. The agent, the swarm or whatever avatar shows it (see `avatars.md`), appears there as the space opens, and again, at the new environment's home, when the space changes environment: it is carried there at once, in the shape it has, rather than gliding across the scene, as the scenery grows in about it; only the swarm's cubes, for the participant joining, fly in from the lobby as it does, a flight of their client's alone (`arrival.md`). It stays there until it goes to someone, as it follows the speaker or is called or asked to go (below), and goes back there. What hangs on it moves with it: its rings, the halo behind it as it faces, the cubes workers fly out from and back to, and its voice, which comes from where it stands, from whatever avatar shows it, as do the points workers fly from and back to (see `spatial-audio.md`). **The ring on the floor is its home ring**, and never moves with it: it stays at home, in every movement mode, as the agent follows, is called or is asked to go, on the space's floor beneath the agent's home, however high the home stands, 1.5 cm up (`FLOOR_LIFT`), as the floor's other markings stand, or, where the environment's `home` anchor asks for it with `Anchor::with_ring(cluster::RingAt::UnderHome)`, under the home, 1.4 m beneath the point it stands about (`cluster::RING_BELOW`, the swarm's usual height, so that it lies at the swarm's foot, as it lay beneath a swarm before there were homes), never below the space's floor, the floor where participants arrive (the client does not look for other surfaces or solids beneath the home, so an environment floating its home over a lower level or a raised object keeps its point at least 1.4 m above it), 1.25 m about it (`anchors::FLOOR_RING`), or, where the home's workers stand in a ring (below), on their ring, about the same point within a millimetre, as much of it as they take, its cubes evenly either side of the way the ring faces, so that the side it leaves open is centred straight behind that; drawn at full brightness while the agent is within half its radius of home, it dims only to 0.85 of that by one radius further out (`HOME_RING_AWAY`), so that it plainly marks home, where the workers stand, while the agent is away, and brightens as it comes back. The roaming agent has nothing beneath it: the satellites already say where it is and what it carries. It moves to a new home at once, whatever avatar shows, as the space opens or changes environment, and stays where the participant saw it, with the environment, while a space being left shrinks away (`arrival.md`). The frame step gives the renderer the ring, `anchors::HomeRing`, with `Renderer::set_home_ring`, and the spectator draws it alike; a renderer given none, as one an older frame step drives, draws it beneath the swarm, and an older client still draws it beneath the agent wherever it goes.
- **Following the speaker.** In a space whose owner chose **Follow the speaker** (below), the agent comes to whoever it is talking with, its partner, as a person would, and the server decides where, from what it already has, sending everyone where it stands as `SpaceState::away`. As a participant presses X for words to the agent, not an answer, a name or a title, it begins turning to face them at once, gently (below), sent as `SpaceState::facing`, a yaw and not a move; a press let go within 0.4 s, a tap, moves nothing more, even once its words are given. Held 0.4 s, it commits, from where their head was as the words began. It stays where it is, only turning, when it already stands within 3 m of their eyes (`follow::NEAR`; 4 m is the alternative the maintainer named), whichever way they face, and even where it stands in their way or behind something: **distance alone moves it**. Someone it stands in the way of asks it to move, and, since coming to them again would only bring it along the same line, it asks where they would like it (see `mcp.md`). Otherwise it comes **straight towards them** from where it is, to their **conversation spot**: on the straight line from their eyes to where it is, **2 m** from their eyes horizontally (`follow::CONVERSATION`, the maintainer's choice, comfortably within 3 m so that it never stands at the edge of moving again; 2.5 m is the alternative to try), whichever way they face or what they look at, behind them if that is where it comes from, its middle 0.1 m above their eyes, never lower than 1.1 m nor higher than 2.4 m above the floor, facing them (`tomori-server`'s `follow.rs`). An environment may declare a **follow height** instead, with `Environment::with_follow_height(metres)`, 1 to 10 m (`FOLLOW_HEIGHTS`; one beyond them, or not finite, is left out with a warning): the agent's middle then hovers that high above the space's floor, the floor where participants arrive, but never more than 3.5 m above their eyes (`follow::HIGHEST_ABOVE`) nor less than 0.1 m above them; where nothing at that height is clear, as beneath a ceiling the environment declares a solid, it stands 0.1 m above their eyes instead, as by default but unbounded by the floor's heights. It is above the space's floor rather than theirs, so that the agent hovers at one height over the scene, above its side walls, for people on the main floor or a low bridge alike, as the environment that asked for it wants; the cap keeps it within talking distance of anyone on a lower level, such as a basement, where it would otherwise hover far above them, in or above its ceiling, and 3.5 m is what 5.1 m leaves above eyes 1.6 m up on the main floor, about 60° up from 2 m away, above them and never in front of their face; and nobody on a deck higher than it has it below their eyes. With a follow height, its nearness, the 3 m within which it only turns, is measured across the floor, horizontally, since the agent hovers well above whoever it comes to and their eyes are never 3 m from it in space: within 3 m across the floor it only turns, however high it hovers. It applies alike for a huddle and for **Call Tomori here**, which share the spot; beside an object it stands 0.5 m above the object (`follow::ABOVE_OBJECT`), but never lower than 0.1 m above the asker's eyes, so that it stands by what it is beside rather than high over it. A client is never sent a follow height. It chooses no side and keeps off no line of sight: someone who turns as it sets off finds it coming straight to them, not to what was their left. A spot is refused where the swarm's box, its reach (half of `stance::SWARM` each way) and 0.1 m more on every side, meets the environment's solids, a wall shown, its slots 5 cm thicker, or a panel floating within 3 m of them, or comes within 0.8 m of anyone's head, or where anything of those stands between their eyes and its middle; then it swings round them at the same distance, 10° at a time, one way and then the other, by the least that is clear, to half a turn, and with none clear it only turns. Its way there goes by a waypoint where the straight way passes through a solid or near a head, as ever. While the agent answers someone else, with their words queued, being given or answered, a question or approval waiting, or another participant speaking, a new speaker's press only glances at them for 2 s, and the agent goes to them as their words are given, in queue order, which is when it begins answering them; so does a pinned agent glance. Once it arrives somewhere it stays at least 10 s before going for another partner, only turning, unless the new one stands more than 6 m from it or cannot see it. Two or more who spoke to it within the last minute, standing within 4 m of the speaker, are a **huddle**, served from one spot: on the straight line from the middle of their heads to where it is, about 2 m from the nearest of them, at their eyes' height on average, facing that middle, clear and seen by each, swung round them as for one where it is not; else from the speaker's own spot. Visitors never turn or move it. Words that come to nothing, cancelled, with no speech in them or failing to transcribe, stop a move begun for them where it has reached, facing them from there, or where it was, if it had barely begun; coming straight towards them, it already faces them, within the 5° it turns to someone by, and does not turn at all. Every move is logged at info with whom it followed and why.
- **On the environment's ground.** Where an environment gives its walkable ground, `Environment::with_navmesh`, the navmesh its clients land participants on, derived alike on the server from the description its crate shares (`tomori-server`'s `follow/ground.rs`), the agent's heights are measured from the floor beneath it rather than the space's floor: its middle stands as high above the floor beneath its spot as the person's eyes stand above theirs, 0.1 m more, within 1.1 to 2.4 m above that floor, so that it is never below someone on a deck or terrace. Their floor is the highest at least 0.3 m beneath their eyes, or, where none is or one within 1 m of them stands more than 1 m higher, as standing in their room just beyond a terrace's edge, that one. A spot over only other levels, more than 1 m from theirs, or over no floor within 1 m of one, as over the yard below a terrace's edge or the gap its rock cuts there, or over no floor at all where theirs is a raised level, more than 1 m above the space's floor, as beyond the farmyard's upper terrace, is refused and it swings round them as for a solid; over no floor near another level on the space's floor's own level, as over a meadow beside a path, it stands as over theirs; only where nowhere about them is on their level does it stand over whatever is there, clear of it, at their eyes' height. Its middle keeps 0.5 m from every floor, above or below, there and 0.3 m about it, never inside a raised level, beneath a floor with none under it, nor with a floor between it and their eyes. It is never near enough, however near, below their floor by less than 0.5 m or stuck in a floor, nor serves a huddle so, and those more than 1.5 m apart up and down are no huddle. An environment that gives no ground is unchanged.
- **Keeping with them through the exchange.** While the agent's partner speaks to it, holding X past a tap or being transcribed, while it thinks about their words, and while it replies, until its turn has ended and its speech has played, held speech included, it keeps with them. Its voice, not its text turn, ends its reply: what it has queued to say counts from as it is queued, through the seconds synthesis takes after its turn has ended, before its voice begins, until the last of it has been sent, at the rate it plays, and played, so that it keeps with them for as long as they hear it. That is the server's own measure of playback, alike for every listener, visitors and desktops too, each frame counted 0.7 s past its end for its lead and the client's buffer (`SPOKEN`), rather than each headset's report of its own playback, which stays on the headset for the swarm's look (see `speech.md`) and is absent where nobody listens; and speech of which nothing is queued, sent or finished for a minute (`STALLED`), as when synthesis hangs, counts no more, so that its reply always ends. It turns to face them as they move, sent as `SpaceState::facing`, once they stand more than 5° from the way it faces, so that it turns with them smoothly without turning at each sway of their head, unless it is on its way somewhere, and the swarm draws in about where they stand now. Once they have moved more than 0.25 m or turned more than 15°, it waits until they have stood still, within those, for 0.75 s (`hub/following.rs`'s `SETTLE`, `STILL` and `STILL_TURN`), so that it never chases each step nor moves while they travel, as by a teleport or flying, only once they arrive, or, where they never stand still so long, as walking about or looking around, 3 s after they began moving (`RESTLESS`), so that it is never left far behind; then, more than 3 m from them, it follows them on, straight towards where they stand now from wherever it has reached, by the same glide, waypoint and stream. Its 10 s dwell is for going to someone else, never for keeping with the partner it went to. Where they stand in a huddle, with others who spoke to it lately within 4 m of them, it stays while it stands within 4 m of the middle of the huddle's heads, however they move within it, and otherwise follows the huddle, served from one spot as ever, or them alone if they have left it. It keeps with nobody while pinned, where it moves only when asked or is kept home, for a tap, once their words come to nothing, after it was sent somewhere or called, and once its reply is over, until their next words or its next reply aloud. **A reply to a note about someone is an exchange with them**: where Tomori's notes alone began its turn (`timers.md`) and one of them is about a participant still in the space, as someone coming near an anchor is (below), it goes to them as its speech is first queued for synthesis, from where they stand now, and keeps with them through its reply as above, by the same trigger, settle, dwell and huddle; a turn with no speech in it moves nothing. Told of several people at once, it goes to whoever the latest such note is about, since the queue keeps notes in the order they came, a note coalescing going to the back. A timer's note, or a worker's (`events.md`), names no participant, even where its text mentions one, since it is about the workers, not someone arriving; nor do notes that ride on someone's words, whose turn is theirs. **A reply aloud to nobody's words is an exchange with whom it talks with**: where nothing about anyone it would go to began its turn, as a sub-agent's report, a message typed into its session outside Tomori, a timer's or a worker's note or a space event, it goes to its partner as its speech is first queued, and keeps with them through its reply, by the same rules, where they are still in the space, have reported where they are, and were last in conversation with it within 10 minutes (`hub/following.rs`'s `TALKED`): they spoke to it, it went to them for their words, a note about them or a walk, they called it or it went to them by its own `move_agent`, or its reply to them sounded. Such a reply is no conversation of theirs, so the 10 minutes still count from their last; with no such partner, as when several are in the space and none spoke to it lately, it speaks from where it is. It does not where it is pinned, moves only when asked or walks with someone, who it keeps with already. Its partner, and when they last talked, outlast its going home. Where its panels go in such a reply follows the same partner (see `projection.md`). Speech queued for the turn that the notes began, and not frames of an earlier reply still playing, starts it, since a note waits a quiet moment after the agent's turn. Visitors are never kept with. Each move is logged as following is, with its reason, such as that they moved while it answered them, how far it stood from them, where it goes, and how, gliding, by a waypoint or streaming, and how far that is from them; a move it gives up, with nowhere beside them clear, is logged as only turning to them, and the end of keeping with them is logged once.
- **Walking with someone.** Asked to walk with someone, it keeps with them throughout, as with a partner through an exchange, whether or not either speaks: once they settle more than 3 m away it comes straight to 2 m from them, facing them, and turns to keep facing them, by the same settle, restless and turning rules, its dwell not applying to them; it does not go home, its return delay counting again from the walk's end. Someone else speaking to it meanwhile is answered from where it is, as while it is busy with another: it glances at them for 2 s (`GLANCE`) as they begin, and stays with whoever it walks with, as a person walking with a friend answers a passer-by without leaving them; a reply to a note about someone else moves it nowhere. A participant starts one by voice, "walk with me" or "come along", which the agent answers with `move_agent with NAME`, gated by `requested_by` as its moves asked for are (see `mcp.md`), so that only a participant's words start one, for themselves, or, asked by an owner, for another participant who has reported where they are; or with **Tomori: walk with me** in the hand menu, `ClientMessage::WalkAgent { walking: true }` (see `hand-menu.md`). A walk unpins it, and is offered and taken in either mode where it may move, never where it stays home, nor by or for a visitor. It ends as they ask it to stay, follow again or go home, by voice through `move_agent stay`, `follow`, also where it moves only when asked, leaving it where it stands, or `home`, or from the hand menu with **Tomori: stay here**, **Tomori: follow again**, **Send Tomori home** or **Tomori: stop walking with me**, `WalkAgent { walking: false }`; as it is called or sent anywhere else; as they leave the space; as the space keeps it home; or after half an hour with nothing between them, neither of them speaking nor they moving (`hub/following.rs`'s `WALK_IDLE`), each logged with its reason. The server sets `SpaceState::walks`, and `walking`, the number of whoever it walks with, which a client reads to offer the walk or its end; `presence`'s `mode` is `walking with Ada#23`. Like following, it is for the run only.
- **How it goes.** Clients glide the agent's place, its rings, halo and voice with it, its ring on the floor staying home, to where the space's state stands it, as a hovering creature moves (`tomori-scene`'s `glide.rs`): a lead sets off along the way at once, at up to 1.06 m/s, slowing to 0.6 of that within 0.6 m of a waypoint, so that it slows round it no harder than it sets off, and the place follows it on a critically damped spring, at 6.25 a second, integrated exactly in steps of at most 1/240 s, so that it eases in, reaching nine tenths of its speed in about 0.6 s, at most at 2.4 m/s², cruises no faster than its lead, settles without overshooting, and moves alike at any frame rate; a new place to go, given on its way, carries on from how it moves, its velocity and acceleration never jumping, turning round smoothly when sent back. Its orientation, a quaternion, follows a lead that turns along the shortest arc at up to 90° a second, on a spring at 6 a second, so that every turn eases in and out, never faster than 90° a second before anyone's face, and a turn of 5°, as it keeps facing someone who moves, is a slight, eased lag of about 0.8 s rather than a twitch. Going further than 0.8 m, it turns towards where it goes as it sets off, never for a way straight up or down, and back to face whoever it faces, or the way it is to face there, as it arrives, blending over its last 1.6 m, so that it never glides sideways or backwards across a room; within 0.8 m it sidesteps, facing them. Each client glides from where it last stood towards where the server stands it, never from sampled positions, so every client converges on the server's place; the swarm's agents fly a quarter faster than it moves, so that they keep up, flocking as before. The participant is never moved, so this is an object moving in the scene, not travel, and `locomotion.md`'s rules for moving the rig do not apply. The server checks the straight way against everyone's heads, kept 0.8 m from it, and the solids and walls shown; where it is blocked, it gives one waypoint, `SpaceState::via`, raised a metre above the tallest head along the way or a metre to either side, which clients glide through; with none clear, or beyond 5 m, the agent **streams**, `SpaceState::stream`, at up to 6 m/s, as a worker's flock flies, its agents regathering at the spot. On the environment's ground, the way keeps above it as well, sampled every 0.2 m, through no floor and 0.5 m from every floor, as the spot is; where the straight way passes below it, as up onto a terrace or down from one, the waypoint is lifted over it: above where it sets off, so that it rises first, above where it goes, so that it travels out then descends, or above the middle, at the higher end's height and then 0.5 m higher each time, up to 6 m. A stream goes by such a waypoint, `SpaceState::via` with `stream`, through the air above the ground to a terrace reached only by teleport, never through a floor nor up from beneath one; and so does the agent's glide home.
- **Going home.** A following agent goes home once the conversation has been quiet for the space's delay, 90 s by default: nobody recording or transcribed for it, nothing queued, no question or approval waiting, its session idle, and no agent speech playing, held or caught up on for anyone, nor still to come from synthesis, checked each second while it is away or turned. Any words begun, and the agent's own speech, as from a worker's report, start the quiet again; a running worker does not hold it. Once its partner has left the space or gone away, the delay is 15 s at most. It goes home as it went, silently, its pin and turn ended, facing as its home faces.
- **Calling it and pinning it.** The trigger does not move the agent: a participant calls it by voice, "come here", which following answers, or, without speech, with **Call Tomori here** in the hand menu, `ClientMessage::CallAgent` (see `hand-menu.md`), which stands it at their conversation spot, in either mode, or, with nowhere clear there, 2.3 m in front of them (`stance::near`), and **pins** it there, for everyone, `SpaceState::pinned`. The agent pins itself with `move_agent stay` (see `mcp.md`), and a participant pins it where it stands with **Tomori: stay here**, `ClientMessage::PinAgent { pinned: true }`, where it follows the speaker. Pinned, it follows nobody and does not go home on its own, though it still turns to whoever speaks, and glances at them while busy, whether it follows the speaker or moves only when asked; unpinned where it moves only when asked, a press neither turns nor moves it; the pin ends when it is sent home, by the agent's `move_agent home` or a participant's **Send Tomori home**, `ClientMessage::SendAgentHome`, as it is let follow again, by the agent's `move_agent follow` or a participant's **Tomori: follow again**, `PinAgent { pinned: false }`, only where it follows the speaker, as the space changes environment, as the server restarts, or after 30 minutes with nobody in the space. Following again, it goes home once the conversation is quiet, and to whoever's words it is answering, if they held X for them. **A participant always wins**: their call, stay, follow again or send home overrides the agent's own stay or follow at once, while a pin a participant made, by calling it or asking it to stay, stays theirs as the agent pins itself again, and the agent's own `follow`, or a move of its own from there, is refused but where `requested_by` names whoever's words asked. A server that takes `PinAgent` sets `SpaceState::pins`; a client offers stay and follow again only to such a server. Visitors call, pin and send home nothing.
- **Asked to go.** The agent goes where someone asks with `move_agent` (see `mcp.md`): to someone's conversation spot, `near` or `beside` them, as following would choose it, straight towards them from where it is, or, with nowhere clear there, in front of them; 2.3 m in front of them, `front`; beside an object its side gives a position, `object ID`, off the line of sight to it from whoever asked, at the conversation distance from where they stand, or, further from it than 2 m, from where they would stand 2 m before it, or at the anchor standing by it; or at an anchor. The mode is unchanged: a following agent is moved on by the next words that need it, and `stay` pins it there. Of its own accord, a move somewhere it does not already stand, but home, is limited to one every 20 s and six in ten minutes, beyond which it is refused and told to stay where it is; one asked for, which `requested_by` names and the server checks as for the gated tools, is not. Going home, staying and following again are never limited. Each move is logged at info with its reason, and who asked.
- **How the agent moves, a space setting.** An owner chooses, in the hand menu's Space section, how the agent moves, kept with the space, `SpaceState::movement`: **Follow the speaker**, all of the above; **Only when asked**, the default, in which it moves only when someone asks, by voice through `move_agent` or with **Call Tomori here**, and stays where it goes until sent home; or **Stay home**, in which it never leaves home, refusing `move_agent` but for home and **Call Tomori here**, and goes home as it is chosen. Following, the owner also chooses how long it waits, quiet, before going home: 30 s, 1, 1½, 2, 5 or 10 minutes, or never. A setting this server does not know, as a newer server kept, leaves the default.
- **Nothing travels with the agent.** Its status wall and its workers stay at home in every mode, as it follows, is called or goes where it was asked, and every client draws them there. The server sends `SpaceState::partner`, the eyes of whoever the agent went to, while it is away, near whom the swarm draws in (see `avatars.md`).
- **Older clients and servers.** Clients and servers from before the agent followed the speaker are not supported: protocol version 3 removed the trigger's summons, `SpaceState::summons` and `summoner`, renamed `summoned` to `away` and `PlaceAgent` to `SendAgentHome`, so the server refuses such a client's `Hello`, saying to update it, in the error an older client shows, and a client lists such a server as incompatible, saying to update the server. A client sends `CallAgent`, `SetAgentMovement` and `SendAgentHome` only to a server that sends `movement`.
- **The agent's form.** The agent shows as the avatar its backend names, the swarm by default, unless the space's environment names one, which overrides it, with `Environment::with_avatar` (see `avatars.md`). Whatever its form, it stands, appears, follows, is called and goes home as above.
- **The workers' arrangement.** The workers stay at home (above). The middle of the workers' arrangement is the environment's `workers` anchor carried into the space, or else the agent's home, and they gather about the eyes at home, 1.6 m above it. The default arc is one curved band about those eyes on which the agent's home stands, in the middle: its radius is the home's horizontal distance from them, at least 2.3 m, so that the arc passes through it, and its workers stand along it to either side of the home, mirrored about it, alternately left and right, all about as far from those eyes as the home, facing them, and none behind the agent at home as they see it. Its columns stand from 40° either side of the agent's bearing from them, 18⅔° apart, where the arc stands 3.2 m or further; nearer, as far apart along the arc as there, from 55⅔° and 26° apart at 2.3 m, so that their signs, a metre wide, keep clear of one another and of the swarm. Two rows to each pair of columns stand about the agent's height, 0.25 m below its middle and 0.6 m above it, then each row 0.85 m above the last, spread in proportion where the arc stands further than 3.2 m, as its signs are drawn larger there, never nearer the floor than 0.3 m, so that the status wall beneath the agent stands beneath the band. Nearer than 3.2 m, the band holds the first two rows of the columns that fit before 170⅔°, five each side at 2.3 m, eighteen workers; the rest stand on the arc 3.2 m from those eyes, at their columns' own angles, above and behind the band, the columns that do not fit on it from two rows up. So whoever stands at home turns their head left and right to see the workers: the first eight within 59° of the agent where its home stands 3.2 m or further from them, as in the farmyard, and within 82° at 2.3 m, as in the default layout. Each worker glides to its new place as the arrangement changes, easing in, at most 2 m/s, its flock, its completed cube and its sign with it, and is pointed at where it is drawn; workers still fly out from the agent, wherever it stands, to their places, and back to it, from and to the departure points of the avatar shown, the swarm's agents by default (see `avatars.md`).
- **The home anchor's cluster.** An environment may give its workers' home room, as a named **cluster anchor**, `tomori_protocol::cluster::Anchor`: a name, here `home`, a pose whose point a single member stands at, facing along its +Z towards whoever watches it, and an extent, width, height and depth, each 0.3 to 12 m, a box centred on the point, with `Environment::with_anchor(Anchor::new("home", pose).with_extent(width, height, depth))`. `with_workers(pose)` declares a `home` anchor without an extent, in place of one before, which takes the room today's arc takes. A name `Anchor::checked` refuses, the rule of an object's reference, an extent out of bounds, or a name an earlier anchor has, is left out with a warning, as are those beyond 64 (below). The server carries the home anchor's pose as the `workers` anchor, which an older client keeps today's arc about, and, where it gives an extent, the anchor itself into `SpaceState::anchors.clusters`, which a client lays its workers out in. One rule, `tomori_protocol::cluster`, shared by the server and every client, lays out whatever stands at an anchor, by the footprint each draws: a worker and its sign 1.05 m across and 0.85 m up, a floating panel 0.85 by 0.6 m, the swarm drawn in to 0.7 m. Members stand in slots, in order of arrival, the first free one taken, which they keep while they stay; the cluster stands as the first of its steps that holds every slot taken: at `home`, today's arc about the eyes at the arrival, while every slot fits the extent, as it always does without one; then one member at the point, and an **arc** through it about the anchor's audience, 2.3 m before it, or for `home` the eyes at the arrival, alternately right and left of the point, a footprint apart, as many as fit across the extent's width, then rows above and below it as its height allows; where the arc does not hold them, as in an extent deeper than it is wide, a **grid** in the extent's face, then further back along its depth, each layer half a column aside; then the same **compact**, footprints and signs at 0.7 of their size; and beyond what that holds, the members from its last slot on gather into one **"+N more"** sign there, its pill saying how many, which lists their titles while it is pointed at. At `home` the point is the agent's own, 0.85 m either side of it kept while it is away, so that workers stand either side of it as today. The steps depend only on the anchor, its audience and the span it is laid out for, the highest slot taken, kept one slot higher as members leave (`Cluster::settle`, `cluster::HYSTERESIS`), so that one coming and going at a step's bound lays the cluster out again once rather than each time, and it steps back only once two fewer stand; so a cluster is laid out alike, to the bit, wherever its slots are the same, and live workers move only as the cluster passes a step, when each glides to its new slot, in slot order; nobody moves as another arrives, leaves, or changes state or title. Gaps are bounded, by `Cluster::reslot`: a worker gathered into the "+N more" sign takes a slot of its own as one frees before the sign, the last gathered first, alone moving; as the cluster passes a step, for the slots taken or for as many workers in the lowest free slots, when every worker moves anyway, those beyond the lowest slots take the gaps, so that gaps left by those who went never keep a cluster compact, or a worker gathered, that would fit at full size; and one arriving far along, as a child beside its parent may, takes a gap rather than pass a step. Otherwise a gap stays until the next to arrive fills it; `Cluster::reseat` closes gaps so only for steps the hysteresis lets it pass. **The server seats every cluster's members**, so that every client stands them alike, however it saw them arrive: each worker in the lowest free slot of its cluster as it arrives, in the order the backend lists them, a worker's own workers in the cluster it stands in, gaps closed by `reseat`, sent in `SpaceState::clusters`, a `cluster::Layout` for each cluster a worker stands in, its anchor, the span it is laid out for and each worker's slot. A client stands each worker where its layout says, gliding as it is seated again, and gives slots itself only for a server that sends none, as an older one; an older client ignores them and keeps its own at home. A registered placement (below) still places the home cluster's workers, each only as it arrives or is seated at home anew.
- **How the home's workers stand: the arc or a ring, the environment's choice.** An anchor says how its cluster stands about its point with `Anchor::with_formation`: `cluster::Formation::Arc`, the default, the steps above, at `home` today's arc about the eyes at the arrival, a theatre about whoever sits building with the agent, as Sky, the grid and the GitHub prototype keep it; or `Formation::Ring`, as the farmyard's `home` is (see `farmyard.md`), its workers hanging about the agent's home on the perimeter of the ring drawn there. The arc is the default because it is what an environment that says nothing has always shown and what an older client draws, and because a ring needs the room all round the home, which only the environment knows is clear and its fit test checks. A ring stands its members on a circle 1.4 m about the point (`cluster::RING`: the agent's 0.85 m and half a worker's reach turned any way, so that neither the swarm nor any sign reaches another), the point kept for the agent at `home`, as many as stand a sign's reach, turned any way, apart, evenly, seven at full size and eleven compact; one place straight behind the point and none before it, so that the agent is seen between them. Its slots fill the sides first as its audience sees them, right then left, nearer before further, so that the first neither hide the agent nor stand behind it; then layers above, 0.85 m apart from 0.25 m below the point, each place straight above the one below, as the arc's rows stand, as many as the extent's height holds, none above the first higher than 2.5 m over the floor (`cluster::RING_HIGHEST`), so that every sign is read and pointed at from the floor and stays below a roof or a board's top: two layers at full size about a home at the swarm's usual height, three compact; then the same compact; then the "+N more" sign at its last slot (`cluster::Step::Ring`). **Each layer is a ring of swarms at one height with all its signs at one height straight above them, below the next layer's swarms**, so that a column reads, from the bottom, swarm, sign, swarm, sign, and no sign meets another or a leader crosses another swarm. So that this holds however far off the ring stands, every sign of a ring is drawn at one size, the ring's (`Cluster::sign_size`): as large as a sign at its nearest place to its audience is drawn, 1 within 3.2 m and in proportion beyond, and its places and layers stand that much further apart; the client gives its stations that size (`Station::size`), so that the signs behind the agent are no longer drawn larger than those before it and never need to rise to clear their neighbours. Signs turn to face whoever looks, as everywhere, so a ring member's reach is the box it takes turned any way. Growing upwards rather than outwards keeps the ring the size it is drawn, so that the drawn ring and the workers' ring always agree, and keeps a ring by a coop or a rack clear of it; a ring that grew outwards would have to clear more of the scene the more workers stood. `Anchor::with_sweep(degrees)`, 90° to 360°, takes only that much of the ring, centred on the way the anchor faces, its +Z, so that a home against a wall or in a corner, turned away from it, leaves that side clear, its drawn ring as much, wherever its audience stands, while its places still fill the sides first as seen from its audience; an anchor's pose may stand the ring elsewhere than the agent's home, its drawn ring with it. Where the extent, or the sweep, holds none of the ring, the arc's steps stand instead. The server sends the home anchor wherever it gives an extent, a formation or a ring under the home, and seats its workers by the same rule; `presence` says `formation: ring` and the sweep; an older client ignores the formation and stands the workers on the arc, as the seats it is sent allow: in the ring's room, where it gives one, by the arc's steps, which that room may not fit, or else today's arc; so a ring that needs no room to stay clear, as the farmyard's, is given none. `Cluster::reslot` moves the last of those gathered into the sign first, one at a time, until none is gathered beside a free slot, so that one standing in the sign's own slot stays where it stood once a far arrival has moved.
- **Workers where their work is.** A worker stands at home unless placed, and a worker's own workers stand with it, wherever it stands, unless placed themselves. The agent places one with `place_worker` (see `mcp.md`): at an anchor, in its cluster; by the object an anchor stands by; beside a wall, in a small cluster of the server's own, named `tomori/wall/WALL`, of the default room, just beyond the wall's end nearer whoever asked, at its slots' height, facing as it faces; or beside someone, in one named `tomori/near/X_Z`, 1.5 m to their right, a little below their eyes, facing them, from where they stood once, shared by those placed beside them within a metre; or home. The server's own clusters go to clients among the anchors, kind `wall` or `person`, labelled with what they stand beside. A placement is kept with the space, for each environment, at most 64 (`places::PLACEMENTS`), by the anchor's name, with who placed it, a participant's by name or else the agent's, and forgotten as its worker leaves the space's complete list of workers. **A participant's placement wins over the agent's**: the agent moves one a participant placed only when `requested_by` names whoever's words asked, checked as for the gated tools, and it stays theirs where it goes, with a Moved notice on the status wall; participants' own placing, by carrying workers, is #259's. The agent goes to an anchor with `move_agent anchor NAME`, into its cluster as any member arrives, facing whoever it gathers about, and is a member while it stands there; that is not kept, and following or going home moves it on.
- **When an anchor goes.** As an environment's side removes an anchor, its workers go home, forgetting where they were placed there, so that one does not follow a flapping alert back and forth; its panels stay floating where they stand, out of its cluster; the agent stays where it is; and one notice on the status wall, titled Places, replacing the last such notice unless a participant put it somewhere, says what went and what moved, such as "The environment closed rack/a12: 2 workers went home, 1 panel was left floating." One removed with nothing at it says nothing. A binding ending, as the space changing environment or the server restarting, is not a removal: nothing is said, and placements are kept. **Placements wait for their anchors**: a worker kept at an anchor not here stands at home meanwhile, and goes there as the side places it again, for up to 60 s (`places::GRACE`) after its environment's side began its binding, as the space opened, changed environment or the server restarted, and never while it is closed; after that its placement is forgotten, silently, since the place may simply be gone. Declared anchors are there at once, so their workers stand there as the space opens, and coming back to an environment puts its workers back where its anchors exist.
- **Where workers stand is the client's.** An environment's client crate may register how its workers are placed, a `tomori-scene` `WorkerPlacement`, beside its scenery, with `Sceneries::set_worker_placement` (see `builders.md`). It applies only in a space bound to that environment while the build draws it, at the version the space names; elsewhere, for a space in an environment drawn at another version or not at all, on the grid, and in a build that registers none, the home cluster stands at the workers anchor, today's arc unless the environment gives it room. Unlike a client side, which waits for the scenery to grow in, it applies from the moment the space is joined, as its scenery grows, so that workers arrive at their places rather than at the arc's and then move. A placement is asked where a worker stands as it arrives, given the arrangement's middle, by default the agent's home, the eyes the arrangement gathers about, `Placing::audience`, those at home, the worker's identity, the worker that delegated to it, its state, its remembered slot and where the workers already standing stand, in the order of their slots; where what it gives is not finite or not within 90 m of the space's middle on each axis, or it panics, the worker stands where the home cluster would put it. It is the build's own code, run on the frame step's thread, at most once for each of at most 64 live workers in a frame, so it must be quick. Whatever a placement does, the default's guarantees hold: a live worker's place never moves when the workers' titles, states, order or membership change, only when the arrangement changes, as when the space changes environment, when each live worker is placed again, in the order of their slots, its place changing at once and its flock following at the particles' usual bounded speed, at most 6 m/s; each identity's slot is remembered as before; the ray points at workers where they stand; and the spectator, given the same sceneries, places them as the headset does (see `spectator.md`). A placement may stand workers together; nothing parts them. A placement that panics is never called again in that client, which says so once in its log, and its workers stand in the home cluster from then on. Tomori's own environments register no placement.
- **A worker's sign stands above it**, wherever it is placed, as it does on the default arc, facing whoever the workers gather about, its bottom edge 0.3 m above its worker, whatever its height, so that a long title grows up from it rather than down over it, its leader hanging between, and moves with it, gliding as it glides, when the arrangement changes: the signs of every worker that moved are placed again together, so that none is pushed aside by where a neighbour's stood before, and a small turn or shift of the arrangement leaves each above its worker. Where another sign is already near that place, as for workers placed together, it rises straight above its worker, only as far as clears every other sign, at most 4.95 m, nine of the default arc's closest rows, so that its leader hangs straight down; only where no height there is clear does it move a column aside, along the arc about whoever it faces, a sign's width apart, as low as is clear there, and only within 30° of the worker's bearing from them, so that a sign moved aside stays near its worker rather than over another cluster, and signs stack sooner; where every such place is taken, as for very many workers at one spot, it stands above its worker, overlapping another, rather than further off. Wherever the sign stands, its leader runs from the middle of its plate's bottom edge to its worker's middle, ending in a dot there (see `interaction.md`). The default arc's signs, whose rows and columns are further apart, each stand above their own worker, and none reaches another worker or sign, even with the longest title.
- **Workers and signs at the distances an environment implies.** A worker the environment stands further from the participant than 3 m is as easy to point at as one 3 m away: the sphere about it that the ray must meet grows with its distance from the ray's origin, from 0.3 m within 3 m, so that it keeps the same angle (see `interaction.md`). A worker's sign further from whoever it faces than the default arc's 3.2 m is drawn larger in proportion, the same image at the same sharpness, so that it reads from home as one on the arc does (see `text.md`); its rows, columns, rise and the room it keeps from other signs grow with it. So workers about an agent's home across a yard or on a balcony are pointed at and read as those on the default arc are, and no environment need keep its workers within 3 m of the arrival.

## Rationale

These requirements moved here from `environments.md`, unchanged, as that document was trimmed to how environments work; Git keeps the narrative of each decision. In November 2026 the maintainer decided that the agent, its status wall and participants' arrival each have a fixed home the environment sets, and that the agent never moves of its own accord. After trying a data-centre environment, they approved the proposal that the agent follows whoever it is talking with (`docs/proposals/agent-follows-the-speaker.md`), built in stages and tried from the headset: it keeps with them through the whole exchange, as its voice rather than its text turn ends it, comes straight towards them, faces the way it moves and turns to them on arriving, goes with whoever a reply aloud is for, and is called, pinned, sent home and walked with from the hand menu or by voice, with how it moves a space setting that defaults to moving only when asked. Trying the farmyard, they asked that the workers stay at home as the agent goes, standing on the farmyard's home ring while Sky keeps its arc about whoever sits building with the agent, that each sign stand straight above its worker, and that the agent be told as someone walks past an exercise station, as one optional radius on any anchor rather than an exercise feature.

Later in November 2026 the maintainer approved dropping compatibility with builds from before the agent followed the speaker, since the installed headset build was about to be replaced: the server's dropping of an older client's summons, a client's gathering of the workers about the agent for an older server and its folding and travelling of the Tomori wall as an older server moved it went with them. `SpaceState::summoned` became `away`, `PlaceAgent` became `SendAgentHome`, without a place, and `tomori_protocol::entourage` became `stance`, names for the agent going alone, and the protocol version rose to 3, so that an older build is refused plainly rather than half working.

## Scope

The agent's home and movement and the workers' arrangement: `tomori-protocol`'s `cluster` and `stance` modules and `SpaceState::away`, `facing`, `pinned`, `partner` and `movement`; `tomori-server`'s `follow.rs` and `follow/`, `places.rs`, `proximity.rs` and the hub's following, keeping, moving and placing, the `move_agent` and `place_worker` tools, and `ClientMessage::CallAgent`, `PinAgent`, `WalkAgent`, `SetAgentMovement` and `SendAgentHome`; `tomori-scene`'s `glide.rs`, `anchors.rs` and `delegation.rs`'s placement and picking of workers, their signs, the "+N more" sign and the satellites; the hand menu's agent controls; and the renderer's drawing of them.
