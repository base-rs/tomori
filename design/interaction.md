# Interaction in a Space

This describes how the headset presents a space and what its controls do there. Arrival, before a space is joined, is described in `arrival.md`.

## Requirement

**No control has a hidden destructive meaning.** In a space:

- **B** closes or backs out of what is open: the hand menu pinned with Y, the right wrist's transcript (see `wrist-transcript.md`), the list of modes, a selected queue entry, or the problem shown, which it dismisses. On its own it never dismisses a projected panel: pointing at one, with none of those open, it says how to throw it away. While a panel is carried with the grip, B is the panel's: a press, let go within 0.4 seconds while the panel is still carried, throws it away, dismissing it for everyone, and a hold, however long, does nothing, and never leaves the space (see `projection.md`). Pointing at a selectable object of the environment's client side, with none of those open, nothing else carried and no problem shown, a press is the environment's, to back out of what it shows, rather than saying that holding B leaves (see `environments.md`). It never decides an approval, cancels queued input or leaves the space. Leaving takes holding **B** for a second, a hold that starts in the space and not on a panel carried; a shorter hold, or a press, never leaves, and while it is held the status line says that holding it leaves. A cancellation still in its undo time is sent before leaving.
- **Deny** is one of an approval's choices, beside *allow once* and each standing option, a button on the right wrist's request card (see `wrist-transcript.md`) confirmed with **A**, like any other choice. Nothing is decided without **A**.
- Cancelling a queued entry takes focusing it in the right wrist's queue with the right stick, then **A**; it is sent only after 3 seconds, during which **A** undoes it. An entry that started sending meanwhile is not cancelled, and the panel says so. Any participant may cancel any waiting entry, as `spaces.md` allows; a visitor is told they only watch.
- A request takes **A** for itself: when an approval or question appears, the list closes, a selected entry is let go, a cancellation still in its undo time is sent at once, and the right wrist's focus moves to the request's title, and **A** does nothing for 0.6 seconds, saying to read the request first, so that an **A** meant for something else decides nothing. Opening the right wrist's transcript while a request waits does the same.
- **A** never resets the swarm in a space, where it confirms the choice shown, chooses from the list, ticks or unticks the checklist item pointed at, or cancels or keeps the selected queue entry.

**Modes are chosen from a list.** On the headset and the desktop window the list is the hand menu's Space section (see `hand-menu.md`); the console opens it with `y`. The list shows every mode the agent offers, by name, with the selected mode's description, and marks the current one; it opens on the current mode. Choosing a mode sends it directly, rather than stepping through the modes in between. The list also offers the space's speech setting, full or brief, which choosing switches for everyone in the space; a space speaks in full unless brief is chosen (see `speech.md`).

**Problems stay visible on their own line.** The panel shows the newest problem, this participant's own error or the space's ongoing problem, on a line of its own of up to two rows, marked `!` with how long ago it appeared. Neither the push-to-talk status, "Sending…" or "Transcribing…", the connection status nor anything else displaces it. It stays until **B** dismisses it or a newer problem replaces it; sending something does not clear it, except that a problem saying that this participant's words were not sent, or their name not changed, is replaced by what becomes of their next words: once those are sent, it clears, as it is no longer news. The live microphone failing, which loses no words, is not replaced this way. A recording with no speech in it is no problem at all, only an outcome on the recording card (see `speech.md`). The space's ongoing problem, dismissed on the headset, shows again if it changes. The status line shows what the microphone is doing, the connection, a notice, a replay, or the counts, and is never used for a problem.

**The queue is always visible.** On the headset and the desktop window it is pinned in the right wrist's transcript, where its entries are removed (see `wrist-transcript.md`). On the panel, entries waiting or being sent are shown in a strip of their own above the footer, oldest first, one row each with the speaker, "You" for the participant's own, their state and the start of their words; the strip takes at most two rows, and a longer queue shows the selected entry and how many are queued. The queue is not an item reached by paging past the last message. While an approval is presented, its choices take the strip's room, except where they are chosen on the right wrist, where the panel says to turn it instead. The console selects an entry in the strip with `^` and `v` and confirms approvals with `<`, `>` and `a`.

**The status pill stands in the world.** On the headset and the desktop window, what is left of the panel in a space is a status pill of two short rows, about 34 columns, low in the view, about 20° below eye level and 1.6 m away, so that what needs the participant is seen without a gesture while the conversation itself is on the right wrist (see `wrist-transcript.md`). Its first row always shows what the agent is doing, with a coloured dot, its mode, `Replaying` while a reply plays again, and the space's title, cut to fit. While agent speech plays on this device, live or played again, for as long as the swarm shows the agent speaking, with its hold (see `speech.md`), the first row says `speaking`, with a violet ringed dot, `◉`, in place of what the agent is doing, whatever that is, as the swarm speaks over it; `speaking, muted` while the participant has muted spoken replies, and `speaking, skipped` once they have spoken over it, since the others still hear it. While the server holds a reply for the participant, as they record and in the pause after they let go of X, until it plays here, the first row says `reply waiting`, with a violet ring, `○`, over what the agent is doing and over speaking, so that they know something is queued for them (see `speech.md`); a space that has closed says so instead. Running is cyan, idle green and waiting amber, each with a plain dot. Once speech has ended, the row shows what the agent is doing again; when it is the participant's turn (see `speech.md`), the dot, settling to idle, brightens towards white and back once, over 0.9 seconds. Its second row shows only when something needs the participant, the most pressing of: a pending approval or question, saying to turn the right wrist; the newest problem not dismissed, with its age; the connection, such as "Offline, retrying in 4 s"; "Leaving: keep holding B"; and a notice. The pill is hidden in arrival, while the hand menu shows and while the transcript shows on the right wrist, and the hand menu's Space section turns it off on this headset. The recording indicator, the `LIVE` badge, the counts and this headset's settings are on the left hand (see `hand-menu.md`) rather than on it. The console prints the panel as before.

**The pill stands where the panel stood.** Inside a space the pill is anchored in the world in front of the participant, upright, rather than fixed to their head: it stays where it is while they look around. It stands in the participant's own room, so travelling (see `locomotion.md`) carries it along with them. Only when their head has turned more than 45° from it, or moved more than half a metre from where it was placed, for longer than a second, does it glide back in front of them, over half a second. Looking nearly straight up or down does not turn it. It is placed in front of them again on entering a space and on a recentre. It is drawn in the scene, translucent and tested against the depth of what is nearer, so that a hand or controller in front of it covers it rather than it covering everything, and it no longer drops while the swarm is busy.

**Pointing at workers (shared attention, stage 1 of #81).** In a space, the right controller's ray, or the desktop's mouse, points at the space's delegated workers. The right trigger, pulled on nothing, draws the swarm towards the hand while held, as in arrival; in a space, a pull begun on nothing of Tomori's nor on an environment's selectable object, held for 0.5 s and let go, summons the agent there, for everyone, until it is sent home, its Tomori wall and its workers with it (see `environments.md`). Workers stand on an arc through the agent, about whoever it faces, either side of it at about its height, the agent in the middle and the Tomori wall beneath them, each with its sign above it (see `environments.md`). Every participant sees every sign facing them: each client turns each sign towards its own viewer's head, the headset's or the desktop window's camera, wherever they walk, while the server's placement of the workers, their anchors and where their signs stand is shared. A sign turns only about its own upright, so that it stays upright and never leans, and follows the head smoothly: a head that moves less than 3 cm from where the signs face, as a still head sways and tracking jitters, turns none, and beyond that they close all but a third of the rest in 0.12 s, settling within a millimetre of it, so that they follow a step without lagging or jittering; a jump of more than 1.5 m, a teleport's, turns them at once, and someone standing within 20 cm of a sign, beneath it, sees it face whoever the agent faces rather than spin. A spectator's signs face the participant it follows; without a viewer, signs face whoever the agent faces. A worker's sign is a dark plate with rounded corners, as wide as its widest row, at least ten cells and at most 28, centred above its worker: its title, wrapped by the one rule for every panel to at most six rows, all in one colour, each row centred; on its last row, its state as a pill, centred, tinted in the state's colour, holding a symbol of the state's own, so that colour is never all that tells states apart, and its word, Running, Waiting, Idle, Finished, Error, Stopped or Unknown; and a thin leader, in the state's colour, faint, from the middle of the plate's bottom edge to its anchor, a small dot 6 cm behind the worker's middle from the viewer, so that the flock reads in front of its anchor and which flock a sign names is plain wherever the sign stands; the leader and its dot face the viewer too. The sign itself stands 6 cm behind its place from the viewer, so that its leader hangs straight down to its dot. Turned to face anyone, signs never reach one another: a sign turns about its own upright within half the room its placement keeps clear about it, as its rows and columns keep apart signs at the same height, so that turning cannot swing it into a neighbour; seen from the side, a nearer sign hides part of one behind it, as anything solid does, rather than signs moving or shrinking as someone walks. Its plate, lines and dot are shared shapes (see `rendering.md`), and its plate hides what is behind it as a panel's does; a worker the arrangement moves, as the agent is summoned or sent home, glides there and is pointed at where it is drawn on the way. The nearest worker whose anchor the ray passes within 0.3 m of, or, for a worker more than 3 m from the ray's origin, within as much more as keeps the same angle, so that a worker an environment stands across a yard is as easy to hold as one 3 m away (see `environments.md`), is highlighted, drawn paler and larger; a worker leaving cannot be pointed at, and nothing in the space is pointed at while the hand menu shows. The headset draws the ray as it does in arrival: on a worker, highlighted and ending among its flock, where it passes nearest the worker's anchor; otherwise, plain and only 1 m long, enough to show where it points without streaking through the space. While the hand menu shows, the ray is the menu's, as in arrival. The desktop draws no ray, as its cursor shows where it points. Pointing only highlights: the trigger still attracts the swarm, or is given to the avatar shown in its place, which decides what to do with it (see `avatars.md`), and no other control changes meaning. The worker pointed at as X is pressed is what the words are about: the client sends it as the utterance begins, and pointing elsewhere, or at nothing, while X is held does not change it, as the server captures attention at the start too. A worker pointed at only before X was pressed, or only after, does not count. Until words are heard, the status line names the worker the words are about, and on the headset and desktop window the recording indicator names it while X is held (see `hand-menu.md`). The client sends only the worker's identifier, in the optional `attention` of `utterance_begin`, which older servers ignore; the server describes the worker to the agent from its own state, before the words (see `spaces.md`). A spoken answer to a question, or a spoken name, attends to nothing. In the console, `point` lists the space's workers, and `point N` points at one until `point off`, so that this can be tried without a headset. In a space that shows the GitHub environment's cards, the ray points at cards too, the nearer of a card and a worker winning, and the right grip picks up the card pointed at. A card pointed at as X is pressed is attended to in the same way, as a `card` resource, which the server describes from the cards it read itself; see `github.md`. The ray points at the space's projected panels too, where nothing else it meets is nearer, and names the panel on a small label; a panel pointed at as X is pressed is attended to as a `panel` resource, which the server describes from its own store, with its id where the agent gave it one, and, where the ray meets a list item of a text panel, such as an item of a checklist, which item, from the line of the body the client says it begins on; see `projection.md`. The trigger still attracts the swarm while pointing at a panel; A ticks or unticks the checklist item the ray meets, for everyone, and the label on the ray says so (see `projection.md`).

**The ray ends on what it meets.** In a space, wherever the right controller's ray meets anything it can point at or take hold of, it ends there, at the point it meets, highlighted, as it ends on an environment's objects, such as the farmyard's chickens: a worker, among its flock where it passes nearest its anchor; a card; a projected panel; a wall, where the participant may take hold of walls, at its board, empty slot or bar; a wall's handles, steppers and knob, where it passes them; and an environment's objects. Each kind reports how far along the ray it meets it, and the ray ends on the nearest, so that it never runs through one thing to end on another behind it. Something held ends it wherever else it meets: a card or a panel carried, a wall carried, and a handle dragged. Meeting nothing, it is plain and 1 m long, as before. While the hand menu shows, the ray is the menu's and ends on the row it meets, a few centimetres from the left hand, as in arrival; while a teleport is aimed, it ends at the landing. Which of the things it meets takes the grip or the words is decided as before; only where it ends is unified.

**What an environment shows carries an id.** Something an environment's client side lets the ray point at, such as a chicken in the farmyard, is attended to as an `object` resource as the words begin, and the server describes it to the agent as the environment's server side described it. Where the side gave it a reference, the description ends with its id, the environment's identifier and the reference, as `[Pointing at Marigold, a hen in the farmyard, wandering by the coop (id farmyard:hen/marigold)]`, and the agent may inspect that id, with its `inspect` tool, for the details the environment gives, such as the exact query and time range behind a metric (see `environments.md` and `mcp.md`). Nothing changes on the headset or in what its controls mean: the id is the server's, from what the side described, never the client's. Only what the participant points at as the words begin carries one; the agent's `presence` tool does not yet name what someone points at in an environment, since only their client knows what its ray meets there, and saying so would need its presence to say it.

**The agent can ask where people look.** Beside what a participant points at as they begin speaking, which reaches the agent with their words, the space's agent may ask, at any time, through its `presence` tool, where each participant is and what they are looking at, along their head, and pointing at, along the right controller's ray: a wall and its slot, a panel by its title, or another participant, as the server resolves them from the walls and panels it lays out and the heads clients report (see `mcp.md`). Nothing changes on the headset or in what its controls mean: the client sends the presence it always sent, and the server works out what it meets. Workers and cards, which each client lays out itself, are resolved only as shared attention resolves them, as the words begin. What the agent projects in answer goes onto the wall the participant who asked was looking at as they began speaking, if they can read it from there, or else floats beside them, to their right, then their left, so that it never hides what they were looking at; or beside, near or in front of whoever the agent names (see `projection.md`). The agent names a participant by their display name, which others may share, or, where two share one, by their handle, such as `Samuel#23`, as `presence` gives it, while it speaks of them by name alone (see `security.md`). A participant whose name is still their device's, such as "Quest 3", says their own in the hand menu: Change your name, then hold X and say it (see `hand-menu.md`).

**Words say which message they answer when the conversation moved on.** As X is pressed, the client names, in the optional `seen` of `utterance_begin`, the newest of the agent's messages that says anything in the conversation it shows, which is also the newest whose speech has played on that device, since speech is of text already shown; older servers ignore it. The server takes only that identity, and captures what it names as the words begin, as it captures attention: a message it does not have as the agent's, or none, as from an older client, is taken as the newest of the agent's messages saying anything that the server has as the words begin. When the words are delivered, if the agent has said anything since that message, in a message with words outside code, as when a sub-agent's report started a turn while they spoke or the agent answered another participant first, Tomori puts a note before the words, after the speaker's name and before what they pointed at: `Ada: [In reply to your earlier message (2 min ago) ending "…should I do A?", before "…should I do B?"] yes, do it`. It names how long before delivery the message seen completed, when the space saw it complete, how that message ends, and how the newest message since ends. Words said before the agent said anything, when it has spoken since, have `[Said before your message ending "…"]`. Otherwise, as when the words follow the agent's latest message, there is no note, so that a conversation that flows carries none. Each ending is the message's words outside code, without Markdown's marks, on one line, as the lobby's excerpt of a reply is, and, when that is longer than 60 characters, its last sentence after "…", or as many of its last words as fit; its double quotes and square brackets are made single quotes and parentheses, so that the agent's own text quoted back to it cannot end the note or read as another. Steps of tool calls alone and messages of code alone say nothing a participant hears, so they are not counted. Each message keeps its place in the conversation, and when it completed, when the session is read again after a reconnection, so that a read again adds a note only for what it lists that the agent said meanwhile; the message seen is named as it was as the words began once the conversation no longer keeps it. The queue shows only the words, and the conversation the delivered text, note included. Tomori's own instructions explain the note, and that without one the words follow the conversation (see `spaces.md`). A spoken answer, name or title is not queued, so it carries no note. While a participant records, a reply that starts is held for them until they stop (see `speech.md`), though the transcript shows it as usual, and the agent's `space` tool says who is recording (see `mcp.md`).

**Travel.** In a space whose environment declares teleport or fly, the left stick travels, as `locomotion.md` describes; while a teleport is aimed, the right controller's ray serves only it, pointing at no worker, card or panel, while a card is carried the left stick does nothing, and while a panel is carried it travels, the panel staying in the hand. The hand menu's Space section turns travel off on this headset. Elsewhere, and in an environment that declares none, as Sky and the Minimal grid do, there is no artificial travel.

**Whose each control is.** Whatever the participant points at gets the input, Tomori's own interface first. Each control has one order of rungs, and the first rung that claims it as it begins takes it: a push of a stick as it leaves the middle, until it returns there; a pull of the trigger as it begins, until it is let go; a press of B as it is pressed. What the ray is on is as the frame before found it, Tomori's own interface first: the hand menu while it shows, the right wrist's transcript while it shows, a teleport being aimed, a projected panel pointed at or carried, a card pointed at or carried and a worker are Tomori's; something the environment's client side picks, nearer than all of those, is the environment's; anything else is nothing. So an environment's client side is given the trigger, B or a stick only after every rung of Tomori's declines it, and only while the ray is on something of its own or on nothing of Tomori's (see `environments.md`). A new use of a control is a new rung, put where it belongs in its order, rather than a change to how the control is claimed; `tomori-scene` keeps each order as one list, `precedence::Right`, `precedence::Left` and `precedence::Trigger`, and B's as the declared order of `conversation::Back`.

- **Right stick:** a projected panel carried, which it pushes, pulls and turns; the right wrist's transcript while it shows, whose focus it moves; a projected panel pointed at with more than it shows, which it scrolls for this participant; anything else of Tomori's the ray is on, such as a panel that shows all it has, where it does nothing more than page result cards; the environment's client side; and otherwise result cards, as ever, which page none in a space. A wall carried shares the first rung with a panel carried.
- **Left stick:** travel, in a space whose environment allows it on this headset (see `locomotion.md`), a panel carried coming along; anything of Tomori's the ray is on, such as a panel carried where there is no travel, where it does nothing; the environment's client side; and otherwise nothing in a space, and outside one moving in what the lobby lists. Turning travel off or on in the middle of a push gives it to nobody new.
- **Trigger:** the hand menu while it shows, or arrival's objects outside a space, choosing the row or object the pull began on, or nothing; the right wrist's transcript while it shows, where it does nothing; a wall carried, where, held, it keeps the wall from snapping to another and attracts nothing (see `projection.md`); anything else of Tomori's the ray is on, such as a panel, a card or a worker, where it attracts the swarm as ever and never reaches what the environment shows behind it; a selectable object of the environment's client side, which it selects, without attracting the swarm; and otherwise the swarm, which it attracts, and which, held for 0.5 s and let go in a space, it summons there (see `environments.md`). A summons is the swarm's own rung: a pull begun on anything of Tomori's attracts the swarm but lets it go back however long it is held, and one begun on a selectable object never draws it, so that reading a panel or holding a worker with the trigger never moves the agent.
- **B:** a projected panel carried, which a quick press throws away for everyone; the hand menu pinned with Y, which it lets go of; the right wrist's transcript while it has something to back out of; the list of modes; a selected queue entry; waiting for a new name; the card saying no speech was heard; the participant's own problem; the space's problem not yet dismissed; a projected panel pointed at, whose notice says how to throw it away; a selectable object of the environment's client side pointed at, to back out of what the environment shows; and otherwise the notice that holding B leaves. Every press but one on a panel carried also starts holding to leave, which leaves after a second whichever rung took the press and is never shortened, so in an environment whose every object is selectable a press while pointing at one shows no notice that holding B leaves, yet holding it still leaves, and the status line says so while it is held. A wall carried, which a quick press removes for an owner, and then a wall held but not carried, which a press lets go of, come straight after a panel carried, before the pinned hand menu, a wall carried starting no hold to leave either.
- **A:** a hand menu row the ray is on, which it chooses; anything else while the hand menu shows, where it does nothing; the right wrist's transcript while it shows, expanding or collapsing a turn, confirming the focused choice, or removing the focused queue entry or undoing that; otherwise choosing from the list; otherwise, once a request that has just appeared has been there 0.6 seconds, a checklist item on a text panel pointed at, not carried, which it ticks or unticks for everyone, for a participant, in a space whose server takes it, a visitor being told they only watch, saying the panel's revision the server last sent, so that the server refuses a tick racing a change of the panel, cued as not done, rather than tick another item (see `projection.md`); and otherwise nothing, deciding nothing elsewhere, as saying to turn the right wrist to decide a request. A never reaches an environment. The transcript's and the hand menu's rungs are the frame step's; the list's and the item's, the conversation's `accept`.

The grip, X, Y and the stick clicks never reach an environment.

**Feedback.** Hovering onto something, choosing it, recording, the live microphone, holding to leave and requests waiting are heard as short sound cues and felt as controller vibrations, as `feedback.md` describes.

**Paging uses the rows that are left.** On the console's panel, the body pages at the rows the problem line, the queue strip and an approval's choice leave it, so that following a message shows its last page as drawn and paging reaches every page. The list of modes keeps the selected entry and its description in view whatever room is left. Arrival keeps its own world-anchored labels and shows no panel.

## Rationale

In November 2026, speaking from the headset, the maintainer found "…4 more lines" on a code panel with no way to see them. Pointing at a panel without carrying it left the right stick free but for paging result cards, while carrying already gave it to the panel and the transcript takes it while it shows, so a push that leaves the middle pointing at a panel with more than it shows scrolls it, after those two and before the cards, without a new button or a mode. Each participant's scroll is their own, since it is a way of reading rather than a change to the space.

In November 2026 the user found the panel "just a wall of text" and asked for the transcript, approvals and the queue on the right wrist instead; the controller-anchored interface proposal (PR #89) moved deciding approvals and removing queued entries to the right wrist's transcript, keeping these rules, and the user accepted it. The user also accepted shrinking the panel to the status pill, with an off switch, rather than keeping the full panel as an option: the transcript is out of sight by design, and something needing the participant must still be seen without a gesture, while two full presentations of the conversation would double the layout and its tests. The pill drops no longer while the swarm is busy, since its place should not depend on what the agent is doing. Y pins the hand menu, as the user accepted, since a menu shown by pose cannot simply be opened by a button, and pinning keeps Y meaning modes and speech.

In November 2026 the user reviewed the headset's interaction and found it barely workable (the interaction proposal, PR #65): errors were hidden behind "Sending…", the queue was hard to reach, and the head-locked panel swam against the world.
