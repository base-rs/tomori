# Interaction in a Space

This describes how the headset presents a space and what its controls do there. Arrival, before a space is joined, is described in `arrival.md`.

## Requirement

**No control has a hidden destructive meaning.** In a space:

- **B** closes or backs out of what is open: the hand menu pinned with Y, the right wrist's transcript (see `wrist-transcript.md`), the list of modes, a selected queue entry, or the problem shown, which it dismisses; and, with none of those open, the projected panel the ray points at, which a press, let go within 0.4 seconds, dismisses for everyone, as the participant chose it by pointing at it, while a hold to leave dismisses nothing (see `projection.md`). It never decides an approval, cancels queued input or leaves the space. Leaving takes holding **B** for a second, a hold that starts in the space; a shorter hold, or a press, never leaves, and while it is held the status line says that holding it leaves. A cancellation still in its undo time is sent before leaving.
- **Deny** is one of an approval's choices, beside *allow once* and each standing option, a button on the right wrist's request card (see `wrist-transcript.md`) confirmed with **A**, like any other choice. Nothing is decided without **A**.
- Cancelling a queued entry takes focusing it in the right wrist's queue with the right stick, then **A**; it is sent only after 3 seconds, during which **A** undoes it. An entry that started sending meanwhile is not cancelled, and the panel says so. Any participant may cancel any waiting entry, as `spaces.md` allows; a visitor is told they only watch.
- A request takes **A** for itself: when an approval or question appears, the list closes, a selected entry is let go, a cancellation still in its undo time is sent at once, and the right wrist's focus moves to the request's title, and **A** does nothing for 0.6 seconds, saying to read the request first, so that an **A** meant for something else decides nothing. Opening the right wrist's transcript while a request waits does the same.
- **A** never resets the swarm in a space, where it confirms the choice shown, chooses from the list, or cancels or keeps the selected queue entry.

**Controls in a space:**

| Control | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | on the right wrist's transcript, expand or collapse a turn, confirm the focused choice, or remove the focused queue entry, or undo that; otherwise choose from the list; it decides nothing elsewhere |
| B | on the right wrist's transcript, back out of it first (see `wrist-transcript.md`); otherwise close the list, stop waiting to say a new name, or dismiss the problem; with nothing else to back out of, a press dismisses the projected panel the ray points at, for everyone (see `projection.md`); hold for a second to leave |
| X (hold) | push-to-talk to the agent; while a question Tomori can answer is presented, the answer to its current field (see `spaces.md`); once after choosing Change your name in the hand menu, the participant's new display name, and again after a name that was not changed, until B cancels (see `security.md`) |
| Y | pin the hand menu open at its Space section, whatever the left hand's pose, until Y or B (see `hand-menu.md`); the console's `y` opens or closes the list of the agent's modes, with their descriptions, and the space's speech setting, and a space with neither fetches its state again |
| Left stick | on the headset and desktop window, in a space whose environment allows travel (see `locomotion.md`), forward, while the right controller points, aims a teleport and returning the stick to the middle lands there, left and right turn 30°, and back steps back a short way as it returns to the middle, or, held, rises where the environment allows flying, each push owned by the direction it first went until the stick returns to the middle, and a projected panel carried comes along; otherwise nothing there, where the transcript is on the right wrist; the console pages through text (up and down), moves between messages (left and right), or moves in the list |
| Right stick | while a projected panel is carried, push it further away or pull it closer (forward and back) and turn it 10° a flick (left and right) (see `projection.md`); while the right wrist's transcript shows, move its focus (up and down) or between turns (left and right); otherwise page result cards; each push belongs to whichever of these took it as it left the middle, until it returns there |
| Right grip | detach the right wrist's transcript into the world, or reattach it; while the transcript does not show, pick up the card pointed at, carry it along the ray and put it down (see `github.md`), or pull off the projected panel pointed at, carry it and let it go, floating, or into the wall's slot the ray points at or the panel is carried near, which is outlined meanwhile (see `projection.md`) |
| Right stick click | while a projected panel is carried, turn it back square, facing the participant; otherwise play the latest reply again, or stop it (see `speech.md`) |
| Left stick click | mute or unmute spoken replies locally |
| Left grip | toggle the live microphone (see `live-audio.md`) |

**Modes are chosen from a list.** On the headset and the desktop window the list is the hand menu's Space section (see `hand-menu.md`); the console opens it with `y`. The list shows every mode the agent offers, by name, with the selected mode's description, and marks the current one; it opens on the current mode. Choosing a mode sends it directly, rather than stepping through the modes in between. The list also offers the space's speech setting, full or brief, which choosing switches for everyone in the space; a space speaks in full unless brief is chosen (see `speech.md`).

**Problems stay visible on their own line.** The panel shows the newest problem, this participant's own error or the space's ongoing problem, on a line of its own of up to two rows, marked `!` with how long ago it appeared. Neither the push-to-talk status, "Sending…" or "Transcribing…", the connection status nor anything else displaces it. It stays until **B** dismisses it or a newer problem replaces it; sending something does not clear it, except that a problem saying that this participant's words were not sent, or their name not changed, is replaced by what becomes of their next words: once those are sent, it clears, as it is no longer news. The live microphone failing, which loses no words, is not replaced this way. A recording with no speech in it is no problem at all, only an outcome on the recording card (see `speech.md`). The space's ongoing problem, dismissed on the headset, shows again if it changes. The status line shows what the microphone is doing, the connection, a notice, a replay, or the counts, and is never used for a problem.

**The queue is always visible.** On the headset and the desktop window it is pinned in the right wrist's transcript, where its entries are removed (see `wrist-transcript.md`). On the panel, entries waiting or being sent are shown in a strip of their own above the footer, oldest first, one row each with the speaker, "You" for the participant's own, their state and the start of their words; the strip takes at most two rows, and a longer queue shows the selected entry and how many are queued. The queue is not an item reached by paging past the last message. While an approval is presented, its choices take the strip's room, except where they are chosen on the right wrist, where the panel says to turn it instead. The console selects an entry in the strip with `^` and `v` and confirms approvals with `<`, `>` and `a`.

**The status pill stands in the world.** On the headset and the desktop window, what is left of the panel in a space is a status pill of two short rows, about 34 columns, low in the view, about 20° below eye level and 1.6 m away, so that what needs the participant is seen without a gesture while the conversation itself is on the right wrist (see `wrist-transcript.md`). Its first row always shows what the agent is doing, with a coloured dot, its mode, `Replaying` while a reply plays again, and the space's title, cut to fit. Its second row shows only when something needs the participant, the most pressing of: a pending approval or question, saying to turn the right wrist; the newest problem not dismissed, with its age; the connection, such as "Offline, retrying in 4 s"; "Leaving: keep holding B"; and a notice. The pill is hidden in arrival, while the hand menu shows and while the transcript shows on the right wrist, and the hand menu's Space section turns it off on this headset. The recording indicator, the `LIVE` badge, the counts and this headset's settings are on the left hand (see `hand-menu.md`) rather than on it. The console prints the panel as before.

**The pill stands where the panel stood.** Inside a space the pill is anchored in the world in front of the participant, upright, rather than fixed to their head: it stays where it is while they look around. It stands in the participant's own room, so travelling (see `locomotion.md`) carries it along with them. Only when their head has turned more than 45° from it, or moved more than half a metre from where it was placed, for longer than a second, does it glide back in front of them, over half a second. Looking nearly straight up or down does not turn it. It is placed in front of them again on entering a space and on a recentre. It is drawn in the scene, translucent and tested against the depth of what is nearer, so that a hand or controller in front of it covers it rather than it covering everything, and it no longer drops while the swarm is busy.

**Pointing at workers (shared attention, stage 1 of #81).** In a space, the right controller's ray, or the desktop's mouse, points at the space's delegated workers. The nearest worker whose anchor the ray passes within 0.3 m of is highlighted, drawn paler and larger; a worker leaving cannot be pointed at, and nothing in the space is pointed at while the hand menu shows. The headset draws the ray as it does in arrival: on a worker, highlighted and ending among its flock, where it passes nearest the worker's anchor; otherwise, plain and only 1 m long, enough to show where it points without streaking through the space. While the hand menu shows, the ray is the menu's, as in arrival. The desktop draws no ray, as its cursor shows where it points. Pointing only highlights: the trigger still attracts the swarm, and no other control changes meaning. The worker pointed at as X is pressed is what the words are about: the client sends it as the utterance begins, and pointing elsewhere, or at nothing, while X is held does not change it, as the server captures attention at the start too. A worker pointed at only before X was pressed, or only after, does not count. Until words are heard, the status line names the worker the words are about, and on the headset and desktop window the recording indicator names it while X is held (see `hand-menu.md`). The client sends only the worker's identifier, in the optional `attention` of `utterance_begin`, which older servers ignore; the server describes the worker to the agent from its own state, before the words (see `spaces.md`). A spoken answer to a question, or a spoken name, attends to nothing. In the console, `point` lists the space's workers, and `point N` points at one until `point off`, so that this can be tried without a headset. In a space that shows the GitHub environment's cards, the ray points at cards too, the nearer of a card and a worker winning, and the right grip picks up the card pointed at. A card pointed at as X is pressed is attended to in the same way, as a `card` resource, which the server describes from the cards it read itself; see `github.md`. The ray points at the space's projected panels too, where nothing else it meets is nearer, and names the panel on a small label; a panel pointed at as X is pressed is attended to as a `panel` resource, which the server describes from its own store; see `projection.md`.

**Travel.** In a space whose environment declares teleport or fly, the left stick travels, as `locomotion.md` describes; while a teleport is aimed, the right controller's ray serves only it, pointing at no worker, card or panel, while a card is carried the left stick does nothing, and while a panel is carried it travels, the panel staying in the hand. The hand menu's Space section turns travel off on this headset. Elsewhere, and in an environment that declares none, as Sky and the Minimal grid do, there is no artificial travel.

**Feedback.** Hovering onto something, choosing it, recording, the live microphone, holding to leave and requests waiting are heard as short sound cues and felt as controller vibrations, as `feedback.md` describes.

**Paging uses the rows that are left.** On the console's panel, the body pages at the rows the problem line, the queue strip and an approval's choice leave it, so that following a message shows its last page as drawn and paging reaches every page. The list of modes keeps the selected entry and its description in view whatever room is left. Arrival keeps its own world-anchored labels and shows no panel.

## Rationale

In November 2026 the user found the panel "just a wall of text" and asked for the transcript, approvals and the queue on the right wrist instead; the controller-anchored interface proposal (PR #89) moved deciding approvals and removing queued entries to the right wrist's transcript, keeping these rules, and the user accepted it. The user also accepted shrinking the panel to the status pill, with an off switch, rather than keeping the full panel as an option: the transcript is out of sight by design, and something needing the participant must still be seen without a gesture, while two full presentations of the conversation would double the layout and its tests. The pill drops no longer while the swarm is busy, since its place should not depend on what the agent is doing. Y pins the hand menu, as the user accepted, since a menu shown by pose cannot simply be opened by a button, and pinning keeps Y meaning modes and speech.

In November 2026 the user reviewed the headset's interaction and found it barely workable (the interaction proposal, PR #65): errors were hidden behind "Sending…", the queue was hard to reach, and the head-locked panel swam against the world. B meant deny, cancel or leave depending on hidden state, so a press meant as "never mind" could deny a tool call not yet read, cancel the wrong entry or leave the space. Modes were cycled blind, several presses away, with their descriptions never shown. The user accepted the proposal's first stage, keeping the live microphone on the left grip. Holding to leave, an explicit deny and an undo for cancelling make each consequential action deliberate. A world-anchored panel that follows lazily is the usual remedy for head-locked text in VR; the thresholds keep a glance from moving it, and are to be tuned on the headset.

Later in November 2026 the user, speaking from the headset, found the panel hard to read and asked for replies to be spoken in full by default rather than ending with "More on the panel.", so brief speech is a space's choice from this list rather than the default.

Also in November 2026 the user, speaking from the headset, asked to point at the sub-agents projected into the space and ask, for example, for the status of one. Pointing at workers is a deliberately narrow prototype of shared attention (issue #81, proposed in `docs/proposals/environment-framework.md`): workers are the only things that can be pointed at, what was pointed at travels with the utterance rather than in presence, and other participants do not see it. The prototype first sent the worker pointed at most recently while X was held, since people point as they say "this one", not only before they start talking. For the first stage of #81 the maintainer decided that attention is captured as the utterance begins, as #81 proposes, by the client and the server alike, so that what the agent is told is fixed when the question starts and does not depend on where the ray drifts while talking. Like the framework, the client reports only an identity, and the server describes it. The resource descriptions, shared hover and selections of #81's later stages are to extend this.

## Scope

The headset client's conversation model and controls inside a space, and the placement of its panel; the right wrist's transcript is in `wrist-transcript.md`. The desktop console mirrors the controls as commands.

## Validation

- Test that B in a space never sends a decision, a cancellation or a leave, that holding it for less than a second does not leave, and that holding it for a second leaves once.
- Test that deny is the last approval choice, survives updates to the space and is sent only with A; that A cancels a selected queue entry only after the undo time, that A within it keeps the entry, that an entry sent meanwhile is not cancelled, that leaving sends a pending cancellation, and that an approval arriving sends it and closes the list.
- Test that following and paging reach a message's last page with the queue strip and a problem shown, including a problem that arrives without the space changing, and that the list keeps its selection and description in view.
- Test that A does nothing just after a request appears.
- Test that a problem is shown while recording and while sending, stays when something is sent, and is dismissed by B; that words not sent are a problem only until the next words are sent, and a recording with no speech in it none; and that every panel line fits.
- Test that the queue strip shows waiting and sending entries, marks the participant's own, keeps the selected entry in view, and is not reached by paging.
- Test that on the headset A away from the right wrist decides no approval and removes no queued entry, and that the panel says to turn the right wrist.
- Test that Y opens the list on the current mode with its description, that A sends the chosen mode directly and the speech setting toggled, and that Y or B closes it.
- Test that the panel's quads stay within its bounds for the longest text, and that characters are drawn as `text.md` says, as themselves where a bundled font has them and `?` where none does; on the headset, confirm that a hand or controller in front of the panel covers it.
- Test the status pill: its first row shows the activity, the mode, a replay and the title cut to fit; its second row shows only what needs the participant, a request before a problem, a problem before the connection, then holding B and a notice; and the Space section turns it off. On the headset, confirm that the pill is noticed when an approval arrives while looking away.
- Test that Y in a space pins the hand menu at its Space section, and that Y or B lets it go.
- Test the panel anchor: it stays put while the head turns less than 45° or nods, a glance away of under a second does not move it, a longer turn or a walk brings it back over half a second, and reset places it in front at once.
- On the headset, confirm the panel stays put while looking at the swarm and follows when turning away.
- Test that a ray meets a worker within the pointing radius of its anchor and highlights it, that a leaving worker cannot be pointed at, that the worker pointed at as X is pressed is sent as the words begin, that pointing elsewhere while X is held changes nothing and the words end naming nothing, and that pointing only before or after the press, or at a worker the space lacks, sends nothing. Test that the ray is drawn highlighted when it meets a worker, and that every pass draws it at 1× and 4× multisampling. On the headset, confirm that the ray is visible in a space and reaches the worker it highlights, that a worker 3 m away is easy to hold with the ray, and that the trigger still attracts the swarm.
