# Physics

This describes `tomori-physics`, the rigid-body physics an environment's loose objects are simulated with, and how they are held, let go, simulated and drawn alike on every client. It is the first two stages of the physics proposal (`docs/proposals/physics-and-stacking.md`), which the maintainer approved in November 2026, the beach's stones and then the campsite's sticks on the same model, and which its later stages extend: cairns kept across restarts, handovers and knocking cairns over, and the dam.

## Requirement

**One model for loose objects.** An environment declares the objects participants may pick up as bodies of `tomori-physics`, each the convex hull of its points (`Body::convex`), drawn and collided from the same points, so that a body never visibly floats or sinks, with its density, friction, restitution and damping, granite's by default (2,650 kg/m³, a friction of 0.9, restitution 0.05, damping 0.1 linear and 0.5 angular), and either `Dynamic`, simulated once let go, or `Kinematic`, only grabbable, resting where it is let go. What they rest on are static colliders in the environment's own coordinates: triangles of what the scenery draws (`Static::mesh`), a grid of heights (`Static::heights`), convex hulls (`Static::convex`) and boxes (`Static::cuboid`); and its water (`Water::below`), everything below a height within an outline, which damps a body that enters it up to six times as much, three times as much turning, and buoys it by the weight of the water it displaces, each as much of its height as is below the surface, so that a body lighter than the water, as a stick of wood, floats as deep as it is lighter, and which may take a body that comes to rest in it back to where it began after a while (`Water::returning`). Nothing in the crate knows of stones or sticks: an environment's own crates declare those, as the farmyard's beach and campsite do (see `farmyard.md`), a stone and a stick alike a `Body::convex`, of granite and of dry wood. A face that another body is to rest on has at most four corners: Rapier rests a face of more than four on another unsteadily, so that a stack of them shakes itself apart, which the stacking harness showed.

**Authority follows the hand.** Whoever holds a body controls it. Their own client draws it from their own hand every frame, at the offset they took it at, so that it never lags the hand; every other client draws it from the holder's hand in the presence it already receives, by the offset the side announced as it was taken (`Held`), so that holding costs nothing on the wire. Let go, the environment's server side simulates it, once, and says where it is as it moves and where it rests. At rest it is the server side's, and costs nothing until something wakes it. The server is never round-tripped while a body is held, and clients never simulate.

**Taking and letting go are intents.** A participant takes a body with `{"take": {"body", "epoch", "hand", "offset"}}` and lets go with `{"let_go": {"body", "epoch", "at", "velocity", "spin", "clock"}}` (`message::Intent`), as the environment's intents, so that the server's rules for intents apply: visitors send none, at most 8 at once and 4 a second each, and the side validates what remains (see `security.md`). Each body has an **epoch**, which each taking and letting go raises and every intent names, as a panel's version is named in an edit: a taking at an epoch that has moved on is refused, so that of two taking one body at once the first to arrive holds it, and the other's client sees it in the winner's hand and lets go of its own; a letting go is taken only from the holder, at the epoch their taking made. A body the side says a participant holds, they take again, in the hand that takes it, at the epoch they hold it at or the one their letting go would have made, its epoch then the one after: their client let go of it, but the letting go never arrived, as one intent too many, so that otherwise they could not take it again until their client noticed. The side checks each against where the server last saw the participant (`Seen`, from `tomori_environment::Space::presence`):

- a body is taken only within `PULL`, 3 m, of the hand that takes it, beyond its hull, and half a metre more for how far it moved since, or, where the server has not seen that hand, as on the desktop, within that and an arm's length of the head; held no farther than `HOLD`, 0.5 m, from the hand; and by a participant holding fewer than `HELD_LIMIT`, two, and nothing in that hand, so one in each hand; never by someone the server has not seen;
- a body is let go where its holder says, unless that is farther than `REACH`, 1.5 m, from their head as the server last saw it, or, unseen, from where it was held, when it is let go where the server saw their hand hold it, unmoving; no faster than `RELEASE_SPEED`, 8 m/s, nor than the body itself may be, as its environment declares a heavy one (`Body::thrown_at_most`), nor spinning faster than `RELEASE_SPIN`, 20 rad/s, cut to them, a speed that is not finite being none; and lifted, a centimetre at a time for up to half a metre, out of anything it was let go inside, so that a stone pressed onto a cairn is let go on it rather than in it;
- what a participant holds is let go where the server last saw their hand hold it, unmoving, when they leave the space, as `Space::present` says, or their presence has said nothing for `SILENT`, 3 seconds.

The farmyard's side logs, at info, each taking and letting go, taken or refused and why, and what was let go for someone who left or went quiet, at most 30 lines at once and 2 a second after, saying how many it did not log, so that a report of a stone that would not come can be checked against the server's log.

A side is given only the poses the server sounded, finite and with a rotation, and each check fails closed on what is not a number, so that no presence a client sends puts a body within reach.

**The world.** `world::World`, behind the crate's `simulation` feature, on Rapier, runs an environment's bodies: it takes at most `BODY_LIMIT`, 64, bodies, so that a snapshot of every one of them moving fits one 4 KiB event, each hull of 4 to `HULL_POINTS`, 32, points with volume, and static colliders of at most `STATIC_TRIANGLES`, 16,384, a hull or a box counting as twelve, refusing, with `Refused`, saying why, anything else. It steps at 60 Hz with four substeps, only while something moves, so that a beach at rest costs no CPU, and a few dozen bodies moving step in well under a millisecond. A side settles its world as it starts (`World::settle`), so that bodies placed roughly rest where they fall, which is where each begins and returns to. A body falling a metre below the lowest static collider, or farther than the settings say from home, 30 m by default, goes back where it began; one at rest in water that takes back what rests in it glides back over two seconds (`GLIDE`), rising on the way, and is let go just clear of whatever body has come to lie where it began, resting there on what it rested on before. A side may send a body nobody holds back where it began itself, over as long as it says, a short glide rising less (`World::send_home`), as the farmyard's campsite puts a stick back on its log, and ask whether one rests there (`World::at_home`). A body gliding back is out of the simulation, pushing nothing it passes through, its epoch unchanged, so that it may be caught, when it falls as any body let go does.

**Sleeping.** A body sleeps once Rapier's own rule says it has barely moved for half a second, firmer than Rapier's default, at 5 mm a second (`Settings::sleep_speed`); and, since Rapier's rule can be kept awake at the solver's noise by a contact where the ground bends, once everything that moves has moved no more than 3 mm nor turned more than about a degree for two seconds, all of it is put to sleep together, never part of a settling cairn alone. A sleeping island wakes only when something awake touches it, so a cairn that stands, stands for as long as nobody touches it. Contacts between bodies are as firm as Rapier makes those against the ground, 60 Hz, so that a cairn of ten settles no more than a fraction of a millimetre a contact under its own weight; meshes fix their internal edges, so that a body resting across two triangles meets their face; and contacts are foreseen 15 cm ahead (soft CCD), as far as a stone thrown at 8 m/s travels in a step. **Contacts against a mesh** are clustered by default, as Rapier's are, those of its triangles within about 5° of each other solved as one plane, which is cheaper; a world whose broad, flat, heavy bodies rest across gentle bends in a mesh solves each against its own triangle, in two passes of the solver a substep (`Settings::unclustered`), since one plane pushed such a body sideways a few millimetres a second, for ever, faster than the stillness rule's 3 mm in two seconds, as the farmyard's building stones lying across bends of a degree or two in its shingle did; it costs about a tenth more, and the second pass lets a heavy body on a small one settle as soon as it did clustered.

**What is said.** The side shares every body for whoever joins (`message::State`): its number, epoch, where it is, whether it moves, and who holds it. Each time something changes, it announces an `message::Update`, gathered and said at most `SNAPSHOT_RATE`, 20, times a second: where each body that moves is, its number and seven whole numbers, its position to the millimetre and its rotation's quaternion to a thousandth, about a tenth of a degree (`Precision::Moving`), about 40 bytes a body; where each that came to rest rests, once, to a tenth of a millimetre and a ten-thousandth, about a hundredth of a degree (`Precision::Rest`); what changed hands; and what struck something, how hard and whether into water, so that clients clack and splash. A body at rest sends nothing. The encoding reads back the same on every platform, its quaternion made unit by plain arithmetic in a fixed order, so that what is kept and what every client draws agree to the bit; a body at rest is said, kept and shared where it was said to rest. Rapier's own body is not moved to that encoded pose, since moving a body in Rapier wakes it: the two are within a twentieth of a millimetre and a few thousandths of a degree, and a sleeping body does not move. A state from before the stones reads as none, and each message's field is optional, so that what an older client does not know it ignores.

**The client's half.** A client depends on the crate without its `simulation` feature, and so builds no Rapier: `mirror::Mirror` keeps every body as the side shares and announces it, draws one at rest where it rests and one moving between its snapshots `DELAY`, 100 ms, behind the environment's clock, carried on for at most that beyond the last, and gives a held one to its holder's hand. A body this participant lets go is foreseen, under gravity, from where and as fast as they let it go, until the server's snapshots arrive, then blended into them over `BLEND`, 150 ms, drawn from the latest carried to the clock rather than behind it, so that the holder sees no hitch; a foresight nothing answers is given up after half a second. A letting go the side never answers, as one intent too many, refused, or a body the side says this participant holds that no hand of theirs does, is let go of again, unmoving, where it is drawn, each second until the side's epoch moves on, so that it is never left in their hand for everyone else. A body this participant let go of whose letting go the side has not yet answered, or that the side says they hold though no hand of theirs does, is theirs to take again at once, at the epoch their letting go makes, which the side takes whether the letting go arrived or not; their taking waits through the side saying the letting go, and is agreed at the epoch after it. A test in `tomori-bake` requires that no client crate builds Rapier, parry or nalgebra, with any feature, but an environment's common crate's server side, which a server enables.

**Interface of the core.** `tomori-physics` depends on no Tomori crate, and nothing in the core depends on it. The core gives environments what it needs, each general (see `environments.md`): the left grip for client sides, and the others' left hands; `Space::presence` for server sides, at most 20 times a second for each participant; and a bound on how often a side shares and announces, 30 a second after a burst of 60.

## Rationale

In November 2026, from the headset, the maintainer found that a stone they had picked up and dropped could not be picked up again. Taking one again before the side had said it was let go, which a hand does at once, named the epoch from before the letting go, which the side refused as moved on; and a letting go dropped as one intent too many, at 4 a second, two to each stone picked up and put down, left the stone in their hand on the server, each taking of it refused as held and each letting go as at the wrong epoch, for as long as they kept trying, since the letting go sent again each second shared the same intents. Neither was logged above debug. Taking again at the epoch the letting go makes, which the side takes either way, and taking again what the side says one holds, end both.

The authority, the engine and the stages are the proposal's, which compares server authority throughout, which drags a held stone a round trip behind the hand, and deterministic lockstep, which buys nothing a few dozen stones need and makes every divergence permanent, with holding by the hand and simulating once let go; and Rapier, Avian, Jolt and a solver of Tomori's own, Rapier being pure Rust, standalone, glam-based since 0.32, Apache-2.0, and maintained. `rapier3d` 0.36.1 needs glam 0.33.7 or later, through `glamx` 0.3.1, so the workspace moved to glam 0.33 first, in a pull request of its own; it brings `parry3d` 0.31.1, `nalgebra` 0.35, `simba` and `wide`, all under permissive licences, Apache-2.0, MIT or Zlib, which compile in about 23 s together in a release build, the server's release build about 8 s longer, and its binary 3.1 MB, 3.4%, larger, 93.6 MB against 90.5 MB, in November 2026; the headset builds none of it. The client mirrors rather than querying `parry3d` for the held body's slide, as the proposal suggested, so that the APK does not grow: a held stone is lifted above the environment's own ground as its client draws it, and the server lifts what is let go inside something clear of it.

In November 2026 the maintainer asked whether the campsite's marshmallow sticks should use the same physics, to standardise and generalise the interactions, and approved it as the proposal's second stage: each stick is a body of the same kind as a stone, taken, held and let go by the same intents and checks, its toasting the server's from the hand presence brings, so that the campsite's own grab, toast and release intents, and its trust in the client, went. The farmyard's side and client keep a body of either kind by the same code, `side::loose` and the client crate's `loose`, the kinds differing only in their shapes, how a hand holds them and how they sound. A stick's hull is its rod and its marshmallow, each six-sided and turned alike, since a five-sided rod and a seven-sided marshmallow left slivers of faces between them that a stick lying on the ground rocked on for seconds; sticks are lighter than water, as wood is, so the water's buoyancy follows how much of a body is under the surface rather than its middle alone, without which a stick would bob about its surface for ever; and a body gliding home touches nothing, since one pushed through its neighbours on the log as it glided, and is cleared of other bodies only, since clearing it of the log it rests on lifted it off its rest.

The harness came first, as the proposal asked, and found what Rapier needs to hold a cairn still: faces of at most four corners, as above; contacts between bodies as firm as against the ground, without which a cairn of ten sank several millimetres and its top crept; flat bottoms, without which a stone rocks on its uneven foot for as long as anyone watches; and the stillness rule, without which a stone lying across a bend in a mesh rocked at the solver's noise and was sent for ever. Snapping a resting body to its encoded pose, as the proposal suggested, wakes it in Rapier, which would make every rest a cycle; keeping the encoded pose as what is said and kept keeps everything that is seen agreeing to the bit.

## Scope

`crates/physics`: `lib.rs`, its limits and refusals; `placement.rs`, placements and their encoding; `body.rs`, bodies, static colliders, water and settings; `message.rs`, what a side shares, announces and is asked, and its checks; `mirror.rs`, the client's half; `world.rs`, the simulation, and `world/tests.rs`, the harness. The farmyard's beach and campsite (see `farmyard.md`) are its users, through its common crate's `side/loose.rs` and its client crate's `loose.rs`.

## Validation

- The harness: cairns of 3, 5, 7 and 10 stones, stacked a couple of millimetres apart, a little turned and off centre, settle and sleep within 4 s, none creeping 1.5 cm nor sinking more than the gaps it was placed with and a fraction of a millimetre a contact, and then nothing moves, nothing is said and every stone stays put to the bit for ten simulated minutes; one more stone let go on cairns of 3, 6 and 9 wakes each without bursting it, every stone within 3 cm of where it stood and the new one on top; sixty cairns of 3 to 7 placed less carefully, up to 4 mm off centre and tipped up to a degree, from seeds, stand and sleep within 5 s, and woken and left to Rapier alone stand still, as does a stone overhanging its support within its top face, while one well beyond it falls, so that the stillness rule never holds up what would fall; stones thrown at 1 to 8 m/s and spinning up to 20 rad/s from either hand rest on the ground within 15 s; structures of dressed blocks and flagstones, a dolmen, a flagstone on a cairn, a short wall with its coping and a tower capped with a flagstone, clustered or not, settle within 5 s, stay put to the bit for ten simulated minutes, and woken and left to Rapier alone stand still, while a lintel beyond its one pillar's edge falls; a block or a flagstone lying across a mesh bent a degree or two, unclustered, rests within 3 s; the farmyard's beach, its 28 river stones of four kinds and two sizes and its 8 building stones settling within 4 s, and cairns of six of its own slabs on its shingle, turned or not, each of its stones on a big slab, and big slabs, small ones and a cobble, standing and asleep within about 5 s, never moving asleep, and its heaviest on its lightest settling, standing or fallen, as soon; and its dolmen, flagstone on a cairn, wall and tower of its building stones on its shingle standing and asleep within 5 s for ten simulated minutes; with all 36 moving, a step takes about 0.2 ms on average, and at most 0.8 ms, on the development machine.
- Unit tests check placements' encoding at both precisions, read back the same however often, either quaternion written alike; that a body is taken once at the epoch seen, within reach, by a free hand, one in each hand; that a body whose letting go never arrived is taken again by its holder alone, at the epoch they hold it at or the one after; that only its holder lets it go, where they say within reach, no faster than the bounds or a heavy body's own, lifted clear of what it was let go inside, or where the server saw it held; what a leaver or someone silent held is let go where their hand was; that what moves is said at most 20 times a second and where it rests once, exactly; that a lost body goes back where it began; that water slows, buoys and takes back what rests in it; that taking a stone from beneath others wakes them; that a grabbable body rests where it is let go, and glides home and rests there; that a body sent home glides there through what it passes, pushing nothing, rests where it began, keeps its epoch and may be caught on the way, falling once let go; that a body lighter than water floats on it and rests there; and that the mirror interpolates behind the clock, carries on beyond its last snapshot, rests where it is said to, and blends a foresight into the server's placements.
- On the headset: take a stone from the beach with either hand, within reach and by pointing at it from a few metres; carry it, throw it into the river and watch it come back a minute later; build a cairn of five or more on the flat shingle and watch it stand; with two headsets, both reach for one stone, and confirm the second sees it in the first's hand.
