# Physics

This describes `tomori-physics`, the rigid-body physics an environment's loose objects are simulated with, and how they are held, let go, simulated and drawn alike on every client. It is the first two stages of the physics proposal (`docs/proposals/physics-and-stacking.md`), which the maintainer approved in November 2026, the beach's stones and then the campsite's sticks on the same model, and which its later stages extend: cairns kept across restarts, handovers and knocking cairns over, and the dam.

## Requirement

**One model for loose objects.** An environment declares the objects participants may pick up as bodies of `tomori-physics`, each the convex hull of its points (`Body::convex`), drawn and collided from the same points, so that a body never visibly floats or sinks, with its density, friction, restitution and damping, granite's by default (2,650 kg/m³, a friction of 0.9, restitution 0.05, damping 0.1 linear and 0.5 angular), and either `Dynamic`, simulated once let go, or `Kinematic`, only grabbable, resting where it is let go. What they rest on are static colliders in the environment's own coordinates: triangles of what the scenery draws (`Static::mesh`), a grid of heights (`Static::heights`), convex hulls (`Static::convex`) and boxes (`Static::cuboid`); and its water (`Water::below`), everything below a height within an outline, which damps a body that enters it up to six times as much, three times as much turning, and buoys it by the weight of the water it displaces, each as much of its height as is below the surface, so that a body lighter than the water, as a stick of wood, floats as deep as it is lighter, and which may take a body that comes to rest in it back to where it began after a while (`Water::returning`); where two waters overlap, the first in the list is the one, so that water that keeps what rests in it, as the beach's dam reach, may lie within water that returns it. Nothing in the crate knows of stones or sticks: an environment's own crates declare those, as the farmyard's beach and campsite do (see `farmyard.md`), a stone, a stick and a log of the campsite's woodpile alike a `Body::convex`, of granite and of dry wood. A face that another body is to rest on has at most four corners: Rapier rests a face of more than four on another unsteadily, so that a stack of them shakes itself apart, which the stacking harness showed.

**Authority follows the hand.** Whoever holds a body controls it. Their own client draws it from their own hand every frame, at the offset they took it at, so that it never lags the hand; every other client draws it from the holder's hand in the presence it already receives, drawn between its updates (see `environments.md`), by the offset the side announced as it was taken (`Held`), so that holding costs nothing on the wire. Let go, the environment's server side simulates it, once, and says where it is as it moves and where it rests. At rest it is the server side's, and costs nothing until something wakes it. The server is never round-tripped while a body is held, and clients never simulate.

**Taking and letting go are intents.** A participant takes a body with `{"take": {"body", "epoch", "hand", "offset"}}` and lets go with `{"let_go": {"body", "epoch", "at", "velocity", "spin", "clock"}}` (`message::Intent`), as the environment's intents, so that the server's rules for intents apply: visitors send none, at most 8 at once and 4 a second each, and the side validates what remains (see `security.md`). Each body has an **epoch**, which each taking and letting go raises and every intent names, as a panel's version is named in an edit: a taking at an epoch that has moved on is refused, so that of two taking one body at once the first to arrive holds it, and the other's client sees it in the winner's hand and lets go of its own; a letting go is taken only from the holder, at the epoch their taking made. A body the side says a participant holds, they take again, in the hand that takes it, at the epoch they hold it at or the one their letting go would have made, its epoch then the one after: their client let go of it, but the letting go never arrived, as one intent too many, so that otherwise they could not take it again until their client noticed. The side checks each against where the server last saw the participant (`Seen`, from `tomori_environment::Space::presence`):

- a body is taken only within `PULL`, 3 m, of the hand that takes it, beyond its hull, and half a metre more for how far it moved since, or, where the server has not seen that hand, as on the desktop, within that and an arm's length of the head; held no farther than `HOLD`, 0.5 m, from the hand; and by a participant holding fewer than `HELD_LIMIT`, two, and nothing in that hand, so one in each hand; never by someone the server has not seen; and one they hold in their other hand, at the epoch they hold it at, passed to the hand that takes it, by the same checks against where that hand holds it, still held, its epoch raised (`World::passing`), while one someone else holds is refused, a handover being stage 3's;
- a body is let go where its holder says, unless that is farther than `REACH`, 1.5 m, from their head as the server last saw it, or, unseen, from where it was held, when it is let go where the server saw their hand hold it, unmoving; no faster than `RELEASE_SPEED`, 8 m/s, nor than the body itself may be, as its environment declares a heavy one (`Body::thrown_at_most`), nor spinning faster than `RELEASE_SPIN`, 20 rad/s, cut to them, a speed that is not finite being none; and lifted, a centimetre at a time for up to half a metre, out of anything it was let go inside, so that a stone pressed onto a cairn is let go on it rather than in it;
- what a participant holds is let go where the server last saw their hand hold it, unmoving, when they leave the space, as `Space::present` says, or their presence has said nothing for `SILENT`, 3 seconds.

**Passing from hand to hand.** A participant's free hand that grips a body their other hand holds, touching any part of it within `PASS_REACH`, 18 cm, more than a body lying loose is reached from, or with its ray on it, takes it at once: their client asks the side to take it in that hand at the epoch the other hand's taking made, and draws it there from that moment, held where the hand closed on it, no farther than `HOLD`, or else pulled to the hand as along the ray, so that it never falls nor jumps, and nothing is thrown. The other hand holds nothing, though its grip is still closed, until it lets go and grips again; what the body carries, as a stick's toasting, is the body's and stays. The two-handed proposal's third stage holds panels and walls with both hands, but not a body: the latest grip on one wins. The hand that took it feels a light tap.

The farmyard's side logs, at info, each taking, passing and letting go, taken or refused and why, and what was let go for someone who left or went quiet, at most 30 lines at once and 2 a second after, saying how many it did not log, so that a report of a stone that would not come can be checked against the server's log.

A side is given only the poses the server sounded, finite and with a rotation, and each check fails closed on what is not a number, so that no presence a client sends puts a body within reach.

**The world.** `world::World`, behind the crate's `simulation` feature, on Rapier, runs an environment's bodies: it takes at most `BODY_LIMIT`, 64, bodies, so that a snapshot of every one of them moving fits one 4 KiB event, each hull of 4 to `HULL_POINTS`, 32, points with volume, and static colliders of at most `STATIC_TRIANGLES`, 16,384, a hull or a box counting as twelve, refusing, with `Refused`, saying why, anything else. It steps at 60 Hz with four substeps, only while something moves, so that a beach at rest costs no CPU, and a few dozen bodies moving step in well under a millisecond. A side settles its world as it starts (`World::settle`), so that bodies placed roughly rest where they fall, which is where each begins and returns to. **Returning.** A body falling a metre below the lowest static collider, or farther than the settings say from home, 30 m by default, or at rest in water that takes back what rests in it, its middle in it or, lighter than the water, floating in it, its middle within the water's outline and some of it below the surface, for as long as the water says, goes back where it began; and a side may send a body nobody holds back itself (`World::send_home`), as the farmyard's campsite puts a stick back on its log, and ask whether one rests there (`World::at_home`). Each goes back alike, and is never seen on its way: it fades out where it is over half a second (`FADE`), is put where it returns to at once, and fades in there over half a second more, before it is let go there, just clear of whatever body has come to lie there since, resting on what it rested on before. It is out of the simulation the whole second, touching nothing and pushing nothing, its epoch unchanged, and nobody may take it until it is there (`Refused::Returning`), so that it is never half faded in a hand. It returns to its home, unless another body lies more than 5 mm within it or is returning there; otherwise to the nearest home of another that is free, taking that home as its own, and giving that body its own, if the two are alike, as the campsite's sticks are, so that neither lies away from its home, or, if not, as the beach's stones are not, keeping its own and lying there in its own turn, lifted clear of what it rests on; with nowhere free, it returns to its home all the same, let go on whatever lies there.

**Sleeping.** A body sleeps once Rapier's own rule says it has barely moved for half a second, firmer than Rapier's default, at 5 mm a second (`Settings::sleep_speed`); and, since Rapier's rule can be kept awake at the solver's noise by a contact where the ground bends, once everything that moves has moved no more than 3 mm nor turned more than about a degree for two seconds, all of it is put to sleep together, never part of a settling cairn alone. A sleeping island wakes only when something awake touches it, so a cairn that stands, stands for as long as nobody touches it. Contacts between bodies are as firm as Rapier makes those against the ground, 60 Hz, so that a cairn of ten settles no more than a fraction of a millimetre a contact under its own weight; meshes fix their internal edges, so that a body resting across two triangles meets their face; and contacts are foreseen 15 cm ahead (soft CCD), as far as a stone thrown at 8 m/s travels in a step. **Contacts against a mesh** are clustered by default, as Rapier's are, those of its triangles within about 5° of each other solved as one plane, which is cheaper; a world whose broad, flat, heavy bodies rest across gentle bends in a mesh solves each against its own triangle, in two passes of the solver a substep (`Settings::unclustered`), since one plane pushed such a body sideways a few millimetres a second, for ever, faster than the stillness rule's 3 mm in two seconds, as the farmyard's building stones lying across bends of a degree or two in its shingle did; it costs about a tenth more, and the second pass lets a heavy body on a small one settle as soon as it did clustered.

**What is said.** The side shares every body for whoever joins (`message::State`): its number, epoch, where it is, whether it moves, and who holds it. Each time something changes, it announces an `message::Update`, gathered and said at most `SNAPSHOT_RATE`, 20, times a second: where each body that moves is, its number and seven whole numbers, its position to the millimetre and its rotation's quaternion to a thousandth, about a tenth of a degree (`Precision::Moving`), about 40 bytes a body; where each that came to rest rests, once, to a tenth of a millimetre and a ten-thousandth, about a hundredth of a degree (`Precision::Rest`); what changed hands; what struck something, how hard and whether into water, so that clients clack and splash; and what began its way back, once, from where to where (`message::Returning`), which is said neither moving nor resting until it is back, and is shared for whoever joins where it returns to. A body at rest sends nothing. An update is said as of when what moves was where it says, since the simulation reaches only whole steps, short of the time it is stepped to (`World::behind`, `side::Loose::behind` in the farmyard), and a side steps on an even pace whatever intents arrive, the farmyard's every 50 ms, so that snapshots come evenly and clients draw what moves between them on time. The encoding reads back the same on every platform, its quaternion made unit by plain arithmetic in a fixed order, so that what is kept and what every client draws agree to the bit; a body at rest is said, kept and shared where it was said to rest. Rapier's own body is not moved to that encoded pose, since moving a body in Rapier wakes it: the two are within a twentieth of a millimetre and a few thousandths of a degree, and a sleeping body does not move. A state from before the stones reads as none, and each message's field is optional, so that what an older client does not know it ignores.

**The client's half.** A client depends on the crate without its `simulation` feature, and so builds no Rapier: `mirror::Mirror` keeps every body as the side shares and announces it, draws one at rest where it rests and one moving between its snapshots `DELAY`, 100 ms, behind the environment's clock, its position and its rotation, carried on as it last moved and turned for at most that beyond the last, and gives a held one to its holder's hand; one beginning to move from rest moves from where it rested, a snapshot's interval before its first. What arrives that moves where a body is drawn, as a snapshot later than the delay or the first after a foresight, is blended in over `BLEND`, 150 ms: the body is drawn off from where it now is by as far as it moved it, eased away, so that nothing jumps. A body on its way back is drawn where it was, shrinking about its middle to nothing over `FADE`, eased, then where it returns to, growing from nothing, on the same delayed clock (`mirror::Fade`), never between, and is neither pointed at nor taken until it is whole, a taking of it the side has not answered being given up as its way back is said. A body this participant lets go is foreseen, under gravity, from where and as fast as they let it go, until the server's first snapshot arrives, and is then drawn from the server's snapshots carried to the clock rather than behind it, as the foresight moved until there are two, blended in from the foresight as above, until it rests, so that the holder sees no hitch; a foresight nothing answers is given up after half a second. A body someone else lets go is drawn alike, foreseen from where it was last drawn in their hand, as fast and spinning as it moved there over the last 80 ms (`Mirror::drawn_held`), so that it neither jumps to the delayed snapshots nor waits for them; one never drawn in their hand is drawn from its first snapshot; and one they pass to their other hand is drawn on from where it was, as it moved, eased into that hand over `BLEND`. A letting go the side never answers, as one intent too many, refused, or a body the side says this participant holds that no hand of theirs does, is let go of again, unmoving, where it is drawn, each second until the side's epoch moves on, so that it is never left in their hand for everyone else. A body this participant let go of whose letting go the side has not yet answered, or that the side says they hold though no hand of theirs does, is theirs to take again at once, at the epoch their letting go makes, which the side takes whether the letting go arrived or not; their taking waits through the side saying the letting go, and is agreed at the epoch after it. A test in `tomori-bake` requires that no client crate builds Rapier, parry or nalgebra, with any feature, but an environment's common crate's server side, which a server enables.

**Interface of the core.** `tomori-physics` depends on no Tomori crate, and nothing in the core depends on it. The core gives environments what it needs, each general (see `environments.md`): the left grip for client sides, and the others' left hands; `Space::presence` for server sides, at most 20 times a second for each participant; and a bound on how often a side shares and announces, 30 a second after a burst of 60.

## Rationale

In November 2026, from the headset, the maintainer found that a stone they had picked up and dropped could not be picked up again. Taking one again before the side had said it was let go, which a hand does at once, named the epoch from before the letting go, which the side refused as moved on; and a letting go dropped as one intent too many, at 4 a second, two to each stone picked up and put down, left the stone in their hand on the server, each taking of it refused as held and each letting go as at the wrong epoch, for as long as they kept trying, since the letting go sent again each second shared the same intents. Neither was logged above debug. Taking again at the epoch the letting go makes, which the side takes either way, and taking again what the side says one holds, end both.

Later in November 2026, from the headset, the maintainer asked for passing an object from one hand to the other to be easier: it took letting go and taking again, the object falling between. The side already took a body its holder held again, in another hand, so a pass is that taking, at the epoch held, named in the log; the client, which had refused a grip on what either hand held, now lets the free hand take it, from farther than a loose body, since two hands meeting rarely touch exactly. Two-handed carrying is designed with panels in the two-handed proposal, so the latest grip winning is the interim rule.

The authority, the engine and the stages are the proposal's, which compares server authority throughout, which drags a held stone a round trip behind the hand, and deterministic lockstep, which buys nothing a few dozen stones need and makes every divergence permanent, with holding by the hand and simulating once let go; and Rapier, Avian, Jolt and a solver of Tomori's own, Rapier being pure Rust, standalone, glam-based since 0.32, Apache-2.0, and maintained. `rapier3d` 0.36.1 needs glam 0.33.7 or later, through `glamx` 0.3.1, so the workspace moved to glam 0.33 first, in a pull request of its own; it brings `parry3d` 0.31.1, `nalgebra` 0.35, `simba` and `wide`, all under permissive licences, Apache-2.0, MIT or Zlib, which compile in about 23 s together in a release build, the server's release build about 8 s longer, and its binary 3.1 MB, 3.4%, larger, 93.6 MB against 90.5 MB, in November 2026; the headset builds none of it. The client mirrors rather than querying `parry3d` for the held body's slide, as the proposal suggested, so that the APK does not grow: a held stone is lifted above the environment's own ground as its client draws it, and the server lifts what is let go inside something clear of it.

In November 2026 the maintainer asked whether the campsite's marshmallow sticks should use the same physics, to standardise and generalise the interactions, and approved it as the proposal's second stage: each stick is a body of the same kind as a stone, taken, held and let go by the same intents and checks, its toasting the server's from the hand presence brings, so that the campsite's own grab, toast and release intents, and its trust in the client, went. The farmyard's side and client keep a body of either kind by the same code, `side::loose` and the client crate's `loose`, the kinds differing only in their shapes, how a hand holds them and how they sound. A stick's hull is its rod and its marshmallow, each six-sided and turned alike, since a five-sided rod and a seven-sided marshmallow left slivers of faces between them that a stick lying on the ground rocked on for seconds; sticks are lighter than water, as wood is, so the water's buoyancy follows how much of a body is under the surface rather than its middle alone, without which a stick would bob about its surface for ever; and a body on its way home touches nothing, since one gliding home pushed through its neighbours on the log, and is cleared of other bodies only, since clearing it of the log it rests on lifted it off its rest.

In November 2026, from the headset, the maintainer found that a stone or a stick thrown into the river or the pool flew back through the air to where it began when it returned, which they found weird. A body now fades out where it is and in where it returns to, with no travel between, which reads well in the flat-shaded style, costs a scale of each body's model, and is the same however far it goes; shrinking was chosen over sinking below the water, since a stick put back by its log or lost on dry ground returns the same way. A taking of a fading body is refused rather than cancelling its return, so that nothing is ever held half faded, and a stick's fresh marshmallow, said as it begins, never needs taking back; it waits a second at most. A body alike to the one lying in its home swaps homes with it, since the campsite would otherwise send a stick lying by its log, away from its home, back again for ever.

In November 2026, from the headset, the maintainer found that a marshmallow stick let go seemed to move at a low frame rate, though it was smooth in the hand. The holder's client drew it from the latest snapshot carried to the clock with its rotation held, so that it turned only as each snapshot arrived, twenty times a second, and jumped as each did; the side said each snapshot at the time it said it rather than when the simulation had the stick there; and an intent put off the side's next step, up to doubling the gap between snapshots. A harness of a stick thrown, through a side's loop, a network whose latency varies and a client drawing at 72 Hz, found the holder's view holding still on 21 to 30 of about 150 frames, its turning changing about 180% from frame to frame, against the simulation's own 15%. Carrying rotation as well as position, blending in what arrives rather than jumping to it, and saying snapshots on time at an even pace leave no frame held, and its steps within 15 points of the simulation's own. Twenty snapshots a second, drawn between, are smooth, so the rate was not raised: two kinds of body moving at once already come near the 30 updates a second the core allows a side.

Reviewing that, an agent found what others saw of a stick thrown: drawn in the thrower's hand as their presence last said, then, let go, from the snapshots a delay behind, so that it sat on the first for 55 to 85 ms. Presence comes as often as the thrower's frames, 72 a second, not 20, but over a network whose latency varies a frame often brings none and the next two, of which a client keeps the latest: modelled with up to 5, 20 and 40 ms of jitter, a hand so drawn held still on 2%, 17% and 23% of frames. Drawn between its updates, by when each arrived, a frame behind, it held still on at most 0.8% of frames, its steps changing 28 to 35% from frame to frame against 34 to 48%, for 13 ms more lag; a hand others see only through what it holds, as on the headset, which draws nobody's hands, does not show that. Stamping presence with its sender's time would make its steps as even as the hand's, at a change to the protocol, and is left until people's hands are drawn. The harness, extended to the other watching the stick swung in the thrower's hand and let go, with the thrower's presence over the same network, found the stick held still on 6 to 19 frames, its step changing by up to 366 mm from one frame to the next where the stick's own changed 22 mm at most; drawn between the hand's updates and on from the hand, none, and at most 64 mm, as the stick catches up with the server's snapshots carried to the clock, at up to twice its speed over `BLEND`. Neither ever moved it back: in the harness the hand slows after letting go, so the first snapshot is ahead of where the stick was drawn.

The harness came first, as the proposal asked, and found what Rapier needs to hold a cairn still: faces of at most four corners, as above; contacts between bodies as firm as against the ground, without which a cairn of ten sank several millimetres and its top crept; flat bottoms, without which a stone rocks on its uneven foot for as long as anyone watches; and the stillness rule, without which a stone lying across a bend in a mesh rocked at the solver's noise and was sent for ever. Snapping a resting body to its encoded pose, as the proposal suggested, wakes it in Rapier, which would make every rest a cycle; keeping the encoded pose as what is said and kept keeps everything that is seen agreeing to the bit.

## Scope

`crates/physics`: `lib.rs`, its limits and refusals; `placement.rs`, placements and their encoding; `body.rs`, bodies, static colliders, water and settings; `message.rs`, what a side shares, announces and is asked, and its checks; `mirror.rs`, the client's half; `world.rs`, the simulation, and `world/tests.rs`, the harness; and `tests/smoothness.rs`, what is drawn of a stick thrown. The farmyard's beach and campsite (see `farmyard.md`) are its users, through its common crate's `side/loose.rs` and its client crate's `loose.rs`.
