# Proposal: One Model of Geometry and Materials

Status: **a proposal**, for the maintainer's review, November 2026. It refines stages 7, 8 and 10 of `rendering-architecture.md` (approved, #551) and takes precedence over them where they differ. Nothing here is built. It was written against `origin/main` at `b2c841aa` (#566), with step 3's open pull requests #568 to #570 read as they stand.

It builds on the render world and its materials (`tomori-world`, `design/rendering.md`, step 2: #563 to #565), the tiers Low, High and Ultra, the content store by digest (`design/content.md`), and step 3's texture generator, KTX2 in ASTC and BC7, and per-face planar projection (#568 to #570).

## The request

The maintainer, reviewing step 3's first textures (tiling earth, stone and bark laid on the farmyard's faces along the world axis each face's normal is nearest), November 2026:

> We need a more holistic approach… The texture mapping is a bit basic… I think we need to consider how this fits into a proper material system. Even if we implemented megatexture lite, this is something that should be controlled by materials, and the geometry data set should feed into that (e.g. vertex positions, texture coordinates, normals, etc). It feels like we need to come up with a cohesive model first.

Step 3's look showed the limits of one tiling texture a material, projected the same way on everything: the earth repeats as the eye moves over it, with nothing at a larger scale than a tile; the bark's furrows run across the faces of a trunk rather than up it; the stone is stretched where a face leans between two axes. Each is a symptom of the same gap: the material cannot say where its texture coordinates come from, cannot combine more than one surface, and has nothing unique to each place.

## Recommendation in brief

- **Three pieces, each with one job.**
  - **Geometry** says what each vertex carries: positions, normals, texture coordinate sets, colours, layer weights, skinning. Each mesh declares its attributes, and nothing it does not use is stored or fetched.
  - **A material** says how a surface is found from those attributes and from textures. It holds a shading model, a *surface* of one or more *layers*, and *inputs*, each a constant, a texture read by declared *coordinates*, or a procedural function. Whatever its inputs, a material produces the same thing at every pixel: one glTF metallic-roughness surface (base colour, normal, occlusion, roughness, metalness, emission, alpha), which its shading model lights.
  - **The tier** is applied to the material, as data: `Material::features(tier)` reduces what the material declares to what that tier samples and computes. That reduced set of features is the key of a shader variant. Tiers become properties of materials, not code paths of their own.
- **Megatexture lite is one kind of input,** a *unique texture*: a small texture of each chunk (or instance) of its own, baked on the server, holding its macro colour, layer weights, wetness or wear, and occlusion, and later its baked light. It is addressed by a texture coordinate set that chunking generates, and stored as one layer of a texture array, so no page tables, feedback pass or bindless textures are needed. Tiling layers add the fine detail, blended by its weights.
- **Coordinates are the material's choice, per input:** a mesh's own sets (authored, or generated by the code that builds the shape where it knows a natural one, such as up a trunk); *top-down* for ground; the plane of *each face*; the *world axis* nearest each face's normal (#569's); *triplanar*; and *solid*, in world space, for procedural or 3D textures.
- **Faceted geometry makes this cheaper, not harder.** Its faces share no vertices, so every attribute can be per face for free. Each face's tangent frame is exact from screen-space derivatives, so no tangents are needed. Its seams fall on edges where the shading already breaks.
- **On wgpu:** one shader source per shading model, composed by features; a pipeline cache keyed by (shading model, vertex streams, features, alpha, sidedness); materials' parameters in one storage buffer; each environment's tiling textures in texture arrays by format and size, and its unique textures in another. These work on the Quest without bindless. Tomori's own swarm, people, panels and text keep their own pipelines, described by the same material data.
- **Migration in five small stages,** each with a gate measured on the Quest 3. The first is the model as data, with no change in the pictures, and #568 landed renamed `tomori-texture-generator`. #569 is redone as the coordinates modes of a material pipeline, and #570 is closed and redone on it.

## The model at a glance

```text

                                                     │
                                                     ▼
              per pixel: one metallic-roughness surface ──► shading model ──► hazed colour
```

## 1. Geometry

### Attributes

A mesh declares the attributes it carries, by glTF's names where glTF has them. Application-specific ones take glTF's leading underscore, so an authored file can carry them too.

| Attribute | glTF | Type on the GPU | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Position` | `POSITION` | `f32×3` | everything; required |
| `Normal` | `NORMAL` | `snorm8×4` | lighting; faceted meshes carry each face's own, at each of its corners. Absent, a face's normal comes from screen-space derivatives, as today |
| `Tangent` | `TANGENT` | `snorm8×4`, handedness in `w` | normal maps read by texture coordinates on *smooth* meshes; MikkTSpace at import where a file has none. Faceted meshes need none (below) |
| `TextureCoordinates(0)` | `TEXCOORD_0` | `f32×2`, or `unorm16×2` when quantised | authored mapping; generated where the builder knows one (a trunk's around and along, a plank's grain) |
| `TextureCoordinates(1)` | `TEXCOORD_1` | `unorm16×2` | the *unique* set: a chunk's or an instance's own texture, where baked light also goes, as glTF, Bevy, Godot and Unreal put lightmaps in their second set |
| `TextureCoordinates(2..3)` | `TEXCOORD_2..3` | as set 0 | rare; decals or a second authored mapping |
| `Color` | `COLOR_0` | `unorm8×4`, linear | each face's variation, as `Shapes::polygon` varies it today; multiplies the base colour |
| `LayerWeights` | `_LAYER_WEIGHTS` | `unorm8×4` | how much of each of up to four layers, at each corner; per face or graded across one |
| `Joints`, `Weights` | `JOINTS_0..1`, `WEIGHTS_0..1` | as today | skinning: the hens |
| `Flow`, `Sway` | `_FLOW`, `_SWAY` | `f32×2`, `f32` | a custom shading model's own: the water's flow, the ferns' sway |

- **Streams, not one fat vertex.** Today every model vertex carries all eight of `model.wgsl`'s attributes, joints included. Instead, attributes are stored in up to four vertex buffers: *position*; *shading* (normal, colour); *surface* (texture coordinates, weights, tangents); *skin*. A pipeline binds only the streams its variant reads. Low skips the surface stream, and a later depth-only pass (Ultra's shadow maps) binds the position stream alone.
- **Per instance, not per vertex:** an instance carries its transform, a *variation* seed (as #569's turn and shift for each piece), its *unique texture layer* (which layer of the environment's unique array is its own), and its light list. A chunk is an instance of its own, so its unique layer needs no vertex attribute.
- **Budget:** the farmyard's 47,000 triangles, unshared, are 141,000 vertices. With position (12 bytes), normal (4), colour (4), weights (4) and the unique set (4), that is 28 bytes each, about 4 MB.

### Faceted geometry

The look stays faceted (#551, section 7), and the model keeps it cheap:

- **Each face has its own vertices,** as `Shapes::face` builds them, so normals, colours, weights and coordinates are per face at no extra cost. A hard boundary between two materials, or a seam in a unique texture, can fall on any edge.
- **Tangent frames come from derivatives.** Within a flat face, the screen-space derivatives of position and texture coordinates are constant, so the cotangent frame built from them in the fragment shader is exact. A faceted mesh needs no `Tangent` attribute for normal maps, whatever its coordinates. Smooth authored meshes keep tangents, as glTF expects.
- **Triplanar adds little to a facet.** With one normal over a face, triplanar's three weights are constant across it, so it blends three copies of the texture into a ghosted mix. The world-axis projection of #569 is triplanar's limit at infinite sharpness, and the face-plane projection has no stretch at all. Triplanar stays for smooth meshes and Ultra.

### Unique texture coordinates for chunks

A static mesh that a material gives a unique texture is chunked, as today, at most 7 m across. Each chunk gets `TextureCoordinates(1)` into its own square, with two kinds of chart:

- **The ground chart:** every face of the chunk within about 45° of up, projected top-down onto one chart, so the ground's unique texture is continuous across faces, and across chunks with a texel of border.
- **Face charts:** each steeper face (terrace walls, rocks, cliffs, banks), or each set of coplanar faces, projected onto its own plane and packed beside the ground chart, with a gutter of two texels at the size of the smallest mip sampled. Its edges are the face's edges, where the shading already breaks.

Chunking and its charts move from the client's upload (`Model::chunked`) into `tomori-world`, deterministic and GPU-free, so that the server's baker and every client compute the same chunks and coordinates from the same mesh. The first unique stage can bake the ground chart alone, leaving steep faces without unique data until face charts come (section 6).

### How geometry is produced

| Producer | Attributes it gives | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environment generators in code (`tomori-environment-*`), on both server and client | position, face normals, colour; layer weights by the generator's own rules (the trail's track is earth, its verges grass); texture coordinates where the shape has a natural mapping | the farmyard's ground, trunks, fence, rocks |
| glTF import (`tomori-assets`) | whatever the file has; flat normals where it has none (as glTF requires); tangents by MikkTSpace where a normal map needs them and the mesh is smooth | the hens, the coop and the workbench when authored |
| Procedural at runtime, by Tomori's own code | position and normal of a unit shape, everything else per instance | the swarm's and people's cubes, diagrams' solids |
| Text and shapes (`tomori-scene`) | position in the panel's plane, colour, glyph atlas coordinates, weight | panels, walls, signs, labels |
| Chunking (`tomori-world`) | the unique set, `TextureCoordinates(1)` | any static mesh with a unique texture |

## 2. Materials

### What a material is

```text
Material
  shading:  Unlit | Own | Stylised | PhysicallyBased | Custom(pipeline)
  surface:  Single(Layer) | Layered { layers: up to 4 Layer, weights: WeightSource, transition: Transition }
  unique:   Option<UniqueTexture>         -- which unique set, and what each channel means
  alpha, sides, hazed, lamps              -- as today
Layer
  base_color, normal, occlusion_roughness_metalness, emissive, height : Input
Input
  Constant(value)
  Texture { texture: TextureReference, coordinates: Coordinates, scale: metres a tile, variation: Variation } × factor
  Procedural { function, parameters }     -- evaluated in the shader
Coordinates
  Set(n, transform)          -- the mesh's own; transform as KHR_texture_transform
  TopDown                    -- world x and z: ground, continuous over faces and chunks
  FacePlane                  -- each face's own plane, upright where it stands
  WorldAxis                  -- the world axis nearest each face's normal (#569)
  Triplanar { sharpness }    -- three reads, for smooth meshes
  Solid                      -- world position in 3D: procedural, or a small 3D texture
WeightSource
  Vertex | Unique(channels) | Rule(slope, height, …) | a product of these
```

- **The shading model** is the existing `tomori_world::Shading`, with `PhysicallyBased` added, glTF's metallic-roughness on Filament's mobile BRDF as #551's stage 8 describes it. `Stylised` reads the surface's base colour, normal and occlusion and ignores its roughness and metalness. `Unlit` reads base colour and emission. `Own` is Tomori's own light and glow. `Custom` is an environment's pipeline, which may still read the material's inputs through the same bindings (section 4).
- **The surface is glTF's,** so that every glTF material is a `Single` layer read by `Set(n)` coordinates and nothing is lost on import. What glTF lacks, layers, unique textures, and coordinates other than a mesh's own sets, is Tomori's addition. It always resolves to the same surface glTF defines.
- **Layered surfaces.** Up to four layers, weighted by vertex weights, a unique texture's channels, rules (slope from the face's normal, height, distance from water) or their product. The transition is height-aware: each layer's height (the base colour texture's alpha) is added to its weight, and the result is sharpened, `max(h_i + w_i − max_j(h_j + w_j) + depth, 0)`, so that grass gives way to gravel between the stones, not as a cross-fade. A layer's index is a texture array's layer (section 4), so a pixel can choose its own two strongest layers without bindless textures.
- **Unique textures** carry, by declared channel: macro colour, which multiplies the layers' colour (the variation at 1 to 20 m that tiling cannot give); occlusion; layer weights; wetness or wear (darker and smoother); and later baked light. A material names a *unique set* (such as the farmyard's ground). The environment maps that set to the digests of its current bake, so a new bake changes bindings, never the material.
- **Variation:** each piece's turn and shift (#569), and, in Ultra, stochastic tiling (three reads blended by a hash of the tile), so that no tile repeats visibly.
- **A texture's mean** is recorded with the texture by whoever makes it: the generator, the importer or the baker. It is a property of the texture, not of the material (`flat_color` in #569). It is what a tier that does not sample the texture uses in its place.

### What each tier does with a material

The reduction is one GPU-free function, `Material::features(tier) -> Features`, tested on CI for every material an environment declares. Its result is both the shader variant's key and the census's count of reads.

| | **Low** | **High** | **Ultra** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shading | `PhysicallyBased` drawn `Stylised`; others as declared | as declared | as declared, with Ultra's light (#551, section 8) |
| Tiling textures | none: each read replaced by its texture's mean | base colour and normal; occlusion, roughness and metalness where the material has a texture for them | all, with stochastic tiling where declared |
| Layers | weights × each layer's mean, computed per vertex; no reads | the two strongest per pixel, height-aware | all four, height-aware |
| Unique texture | its macro colour and occlusion, one read at a coarse mip, where the bake has arrived | all its channels, two reads | as High |
| Coordinates | none needed | as declared; `Triplanar` drawn as `WorldAxis` | as declared |
| Procedural detail | none | cheap: slope rule, wetness by height, ripples | full: solid noise, parallax where declared |
| Reads a scenery pixel, at most | 1 | 6 | 16 |

So Low draws today's faceted look, enriched by the unique macro colour when a bake exists. A device stepping down a tier changes variants and resident mips, not the scene.

| **id Tech** | MegaTexture in *Enemy Territory: Quake Wars*, then virtual texturing of every surface in *Rage*: a unique texel everywhere, with baked light, streamed in pages by a feedback pass through a page table; costly in storage, with pop-in; later id Tech dropped it | unique data for what tiling cannot give, with baked light in it, but small: low-frequency only, a fixed layer for each chunk instead of pages, fetched whole by digest, with tiling detail on top. No feedback pass, page table or streaming by view |

Ideas only: no dependency is proposed.

## 3. Where inputs come from

| Source | What | Named by | Made |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Authored assets** | hero objects' textures: the coop, the workbench, the hens, the controllers | the glTF's digest and the image's index in it, or a KTX2 file's digest | offline, with provenance and licence (`assets.md`) |
| **Offline generators** | tiling layers: earth, grass, gravel, moss, stone, bark, wood; normal maps for water | digest; the recipe and seed in the environment's common crate, regenerated and compared in its tests (#570) | by `tomori-texture-generator`; the server generates its environments' sets as its content store reads them (0.66 s for the farmyard's twelve) |
| **Server bakes** | unique textures (macro colour, weights, wetness, wear, occlusion; later baked light); probes | a *unique set*, which the environment maps to the digests of its current bake | by the server's core, from the static scene `tomori-world` describes: deterministic, incremental by chunk (#551, section 6) |
| **On the device** | procedural detail: slope rules, wetness, ripples, flicker, solid noise; the glyph atlas | the function and its parameters in the material | in the shader, at the tier's fidelity |

- **Everything that is bytes goes through the content store by digest,** as `image/ktx2` (#568). A unique set is a KTX2 file for each chunk, 10 to 20 KB, so that an incremental bake refetches only the chunks it changed. Each is uploaded into its layer of the array.
- **A material never names a bake's digest.** Bakes change and materials stay the same: the environment's state names the current digests for each unique set, and the client swaps them in with a fade as they arrive, drawing the tier's fallback until then.
- **Determinism:** the generator's and the baker's outputs are functions of code, seeds and the scene, so that every server makes the same bytes and caches agree.

## 4. Rendering on wgpu

### Variants and pipelines

- **One shader source for each shading model,** composed from modules by feature, as Tomori already composes `haze.wgsl` into every shader (`tomori_assets::haze::shader`): one module for each coordinates mode, layering, the unique texture, each procedural function. Features that change bindings or streams are composed in; numbers are WGSL `override` constants (`PipelineCompilationOptions::constants`) or entries in the material buffer.
- **The pipeline key:** (shading model, vertex streams, `Features`, alpha, sides, pass). An environment's materials have few distinct keys: the farmyard about a dozen in each tier.
- **Compiled when an environment is loaded,** for its tier, never at first draw, with wgpu's pipeline cache on Vulkan kept in the app's cache directory between sessions, so that the Quest compiles each variant once.
- **The shader rule holds:** no uniform struct or array passed by value (`rendering.md`). Materials live in a storage buffer, outside that rule, and are read field by field anyway.

### Bindings and batching

| Bind group | Holds | Changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0, the view | the eye's camera, haze, environment light | once a pass |
| 1, the environment | its materials' storage buffer; its tiling textures in 2D texture arrays, one for each format and size (1,024² ASTC 4×4 sRGB base colours; 1,024² normals; 512² of each); its unique array (128² layers, linear and sRGB); samplers | when the environment, its textures or a bake change |
| 2, a material's own | authored textures of arbitrary size, which fit no array | per authored material only |
| instances | transforms, variation, unique layer, material index, light list range, in one storage buffer with offsets | each frame for what moves |

- **Arrays rather than bindless.** A 2D array's layer is a texture coordinate, so a pixel can pick any layer with no non-uniform descriptor indexing. That works on every adapter Tomori meets, the Quest's included, and lets most of an environment's draws share bind group 1. wgpu's binding arrays (`TEXTURE_BINDING_ARRAY` with non-uniform indexing) are an Ultra optimisation, adopted only if measured to help. The default limit of 256 layers holds the farmyard's 147 chunks.
- **Batching:** opaque lists sort by pipeline, then by bind group 2 (mostly none), then front to back, as #551 plans. Static chunks of one material merge into one draw where they are adjacent in the index buffer.

### Budgets for each tier

| | Low | High (Quest 3) | Ultra |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reads a scenery pixel, at most | 1 | 6 | 16 |
| Textures resident, an environment | unique textures' coarse mips only, about 1 MiB | 64 MiB, #551's budget, of which unique textures at most 4 MiB | as High |
| Variants an environment, at most | 16 | 32 | 64 |
| Gate | Low's (#551, section 8) | the eyes at most 11.5 ms at the 95th percentile at 72 Hz, and each stage within its own increase (section 6) | within the window's refresh |

At 128² per 7 m chunk, about 5.5 cm a texel, two ASTC 6×6 textures with mips come to about 20 KB a chunk, so 3 MB for the farmyard's 147 chunks.

## Risks

- **Variants multiply.** Features combine. The census counts each environment's variants for each tier on CI, with the budgets above, and compiling at load with a persistent cache keeps hitches out of the headset.
- **Seams in unique textures.** Face charts have gutters, and their seams fall on facet edges. The ground chart is continuous within a chunk, with a texel's border to the next. The render tests check a chunk's border at every mip that is sampled.
- **The server bakes before the client draws.** Every tier draws without a bake, so a slow or missing bake is never worse than stage 3.
- **Texture arrays need one size and format each.** The generator makes sets in two size classes. Authored textures that fit neither take bind group 2, one bind each.

## Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Unique texture density:** 128² a 7 m chunk (5.5 cm a texel, 3 MB for the farmyard) for macro colour, weights and occlusion, or 256² (2.7 cm, about 12 MB), which baked light may later want?
2. **Rocks:** each facet textured in its own plane (`FacePlane`: no stretch, seams at edges, like cut stone), or along the nearest world axis (#569: continuous across facets, stretched where they lean)? This proposal recommends the first, with solid textures in Ultra.
3. **Layers:** at most four layers a material, and two sampled a pixel in High. Enough for the farmyard's ground?
4. **#570:** close it and redo the farmyard's textures in stage 2, as recommended, or merge it as an interim look until then?
